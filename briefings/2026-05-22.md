## CLUSTER 1 — AI Visibility & Discovery: Hotels Race to Exist Inside LLMs

The single clearest theme of the past 72 hours is hotel brands and infrastructure vendors scrambling to ensure they are *findable and bookable* inside AI assistants before those assistants become the dominant booking funnel.


Cloudbeds unveiled **Ask Signals** on May 21 — a new conversational AI interface designed to help hotel teams interact with operational, guest, and revenue data through natural conversation. Built on Signals, Cloudbeds' unified hospitality intelligence architecture, it connects reservations, revenue, channels, payments, guests, and marketing within a single data environment. Unlike AI tools layered onto fragmented hospitality systems, Ask Signals is designed to "see the whole hotel, not a slice of it."



CEO Adam Harris framed the competitive moat bluntly: "Every hotel technology company will have AI. What will matter is the quality and connectedness of the data underneath it. Most hospitality AI tools only see one part of the business because they sit on disconnected systems assembled over time."
 Ask Signals is in early pilot; no outcome data yet.

On the distribution side, 
Wyndham launched a native app within ChatGPT, marking what it describes as the first native hotel app from a major U.S. economy and midscale franchisor available inside OpenAI's ecosystem. The experience allows users to explore Wyndham's portfolio of approximately 8,400 hotels through map-based browsing, conversational prompts, amenity filters, and interactive hotel cards.
 
This is Wyndham's second major LLM integration: in 2025 it became the first major hotel company to launch on Anthropic's Claude, and it is actively preparing an integration with Google's AI Mode. Since 2018, Wyndham has invested more than $450 million in technology modernization, and in 2020 became the first major hotel company to fully migrate its systems to the cloud.


Wyndham disclosed specific operational metrics: 
AI-powered tools deployed in its call centers have aided in reducing average handle times by 7%. The most engaged hotels on the Wyndham Connect platform last year averaged more than $60,000 in incremental revenue, with the highest-performing property exceeding $200,000.


On the infrastructure side, a startup called **Agentic Hospitality** launched the TravelOS Model Context Protocol (MCP) Server. 
The platform connects directly to a hotel's central reservation system and PMS, which remain the system of record, rather than duplicating inventory. It delivers availability, room details, policies, and rate information in real time into AI environments in a structured format, and also includes an Agentic Booking Engine that enables conversational booking flows across websites and AI environments while routing transactions through the hotel's existing CRS.
 Chief AI Officer Brad Brewer: "We extend those systems into AI platforms rather than replacing them. Hotels keep their pricing control, inventory authority and direct relationship with the guest." [PAYWALLED/BLOCKED — details from search snippet only]

Meanwhile, 
PhocusWire reported on May 11 that in the same week Wyndham unveiled its ChatGPT app, Choice Hotels unveiled a series of internal AI tools.
 And Skift's May 21 front page carried the headline: "Expedia to Launch Agentic AI Tools for B2B Partners," with an executive confirming its B2B MCP server is expected within months — a key step toward letting partners' AI agents connect directly to Expedia's travel inventory. [Skift paywalled — details from search snippet.]

**So what:** Hotels that do not have structured data, real-time rate APIs, and explicit LLM distribution strategies within 12 months will be invisible to the next generation of travel planners. This is an OTA disintermediation battle being fought on a new front. The MCP protocol is becoming the de facto connectivity layer — operators should be asking every PMS and CRS vendor when they ship an MCP endpoint.

## CLUSTER 2 — Data & Integration: BI as the New PMS Battleground

The analytical layer is where PMS vendors are competing hardest. 
Mews launched **Mews Business Intelligence**, embedded directly into its operating system, offering hoteliers a "single source of truth" for tracking revenue, occupancy, and bookings. Features include AI-powered performance summaries, custom dashboards, automated reporting, and external source data that combines Mews' own data with Google Ads and OTA data. Mews BI has been adopted by 1,000 early customers.
 The 42-room Adara Hotel in Whistler used the room performance dashboard to recategorize inventory and boost ADR — a small but concrete case study. GM Rhys Davies: "We had data before, but it was manual and not detailed, just basic stats." 
A recent Abode Worldwide report found that PMS and AI-led platforms led hospitality tech investment in the past year; investors are "concentrating capital in platforms that hospitality businesses increasingly depend on every day."


Separately, Hotel Dive's editors flagged a **Marriott CIO** piece from May 18 (full article paywalled): Global CIO Naveen Manga said during a virtual event that 2026 is "a year for scale," as Marriott continues a multiyear technology transformation with "ambition and caution." Also noted in the search results: **Actabl secured a patent for hotel data normalization technology** powering AI-driven analytics — a quiet but significant IP move in the race to build defensible data infrastructure.

**So what:** The PMS-as-BI-platform move (Mews, Cloudbeds, Otelier, Cloudbeds all in the same week) is real and accelerating. For PE-backed operators, the data normalization layer is where the investable moat is being built right now, not the chatbot frontend. Ask vendors: what is the normalization methodology, and can the data be exported cleanly?

**Strongest sources:** [Hotel Dive on Mews BI](https://www.hoteldive.com/news/mews-launches-ai-powered-business-intelligence-product/817672/) · [Abode Worldwide report via Hotel Dive] · [Hotel Dive Marriott CIO brief]

## CLUSTER 3 — Operational AI (Restaurant): NRA Show Floor Reality Check

The 2026 National Restaurant Association Show (May 16–19, Chicago, 2,200+ exhibitors) delivered the most concentrated restaurant AI signal of the year, and the ground-level operator reaction was notably skeptical.


The dominant new AI application at the Show was AI assistants that sit atop restaurants' data and provide operational insights and action items — telling an operator that a location is busier than usual, or that they've run out of ketchup. Several of the big POS companies were offering a version of this.
 Toast CMO Kelly Esten: 
"Restaurant people wear a ton of hats. How can we start to take some of that work that's not core to why they got into running a restaurant off their plate with AI?"


But operator pushback was pointed. 
Khara Mangiduyos, owner of Kalei's Kitchenette in San Diego, said: "The AI that we're seeing is not necessarily solving the immediate problem. That's why you end up creating your own."
 Separately, Amir Harpaz of The Mudpuppy (Vonore, TN) has trained AI to run all social media accounts and built a program that optimizes menu layouts and sends designs to the printer — operator-built solutions bypassing the vendor ecosystem entirely.

Pre-show, RTN reported that 
nearly 90% of operators expect food and labor costs to continue increasing this year, while 80% of consumers say overall value remains their top priority when dining out.
 Voice AI was a standout category: SoundHound AI showcased its platform for restaurant ordering and guest interaction. 
Voice AI was the biggest trend, with some vendors making the tech more approachable by placing old-fashioned landlines in their booths.
 Miso Robotics' Flippy fry cook was cited at $75,000 with $3,000/month service — roughly equivalent to a single fast-food employee's fully-loaded cost.

Fast Company's Most Innovative Companies list (2026) included: 
Toast's Toast IQ, introduced in 2025, which lets operators ask questions like "Which items aren't selling well?" In its first four months, more than half of Toast's 120,000+ restaurants used Toast IQ, collectively sending over 1 million queries.
 
OpenTable expanded its reach to include popular AI platforms like ChatGPT, adding restaurant inventory in more places to connect diners with available tables.
 
DoorDash spent $1.2 billion to acquire restaurant reservations platform SevenRooms, adding reservations to its app months later; 80% of DoorDash customers who tried its "going out" feature visited a restaurant they'd never ordered from before.


Also noted from SevenRooms' own blog: 
American Express owns both Resy and Tock, and a merger under Resy has been announced for summer 2026, which would roughly double Resy's venue inventory to over 25,000 listings and bring Tock's pre-paid booking and tiered experience features into the Resy platform.


**So what:** The POS data intelligence layer (Toast IQ, Square's AI tools, Oracle Restaurants) is the restaurant equivalent of the hotel BI battle above. The operator-built AI trend is significant: vendors who can't demonstrate ROI within a clear payback period will lose ground to operators piecing together solutions themselves. The Resy/Tock merger creates a formidable alternative to OpenTable with zero per-cover fees and $1.2B DoorDash muscle behind SevenRooms.

**Strongest sources:** [Restaurant Business Online NRA Show floor](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/technology/6-tech-trends-restaurant-show) · [RTN NRA Show preview](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/05/ai-automation-and-cost-pressures-take-center-stage-at-the-2026-national-restaurant-association-show/) · [Fast Company Most Innovative Companies 2026]
