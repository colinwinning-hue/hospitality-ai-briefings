## DATA & INTEGRATION

Unified data architectures are taking centre stage as the industry's defining vendor battleground.


**Cloudbeds** dropped its most significant product news of the year on 21 May, unveiling Ask Signals, a conversational AI interface designed to help hotel teams interact with operational, guest and revenue data through natural conversation. Built on Signals, Cloudbeds' unified hospitality intelligence architecture, which connects reservations, revenue, channels, payments, guests and marketing within a single data environment, Ask Signals is designed to "see the whole hotel, not a slice of it."
 The system compiles booking source, channel history, payment records, ancillary spend, ADR, review history and recent guest communications — and can surface contextual details such as a guest visiting to celebrate a birthday, helping front-desk teams personalise stays before arrival. 
"Every hotel technology company will have AI," said Adam Harris, co-founder and CEO of Cloudbeds. "What will matter is the quality and connectedness of the data underneath it."
 Cloudbeds is opening signups for an early pilot; the product is in development, not generally available.


**Mews** launched Mews Business Intelligence on 16 April, a new AI-powered product embedded directly into its operating system, offering hoteliers a "single source of truth" for tracking revenue, occupancy and bookings across their portfolios. The launch follows its $300 million Series D capital raise in January. Mews BI features AI-powered performance summaries, custom dashboards, automated reporting, and external source data combining Mews data with Google Ads and OTAs. The product has been adopted by 1,000 early customers.
 The 42-room Adara Hotel in Whistler, British Columbia is an early case study: 
"We had data before, but it was manual and not detailed, just basic stats like overall ADR and date," said Rhys Davies, general manager. "With Mews BI, everything is in one place."


**Sources:** [Cloudbeds (GlobeNewswire, May 21)](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/05/21/3299668/0/en/Cloudbeds-Introduces-Ask-Signals-a-Conversational-AI-Interface-Built-on-Unified-Hotel-Data.html) · [Hotel Dive – Mews BI (Apr 16)](https://www.hoteldive.com/news/mews-launches-ai-powered-business-intelligence-product/817672/)

**So what:** Mews and Cloudbeds are racing to make the argument that AI is only as good as its data substrate. Operators evaluating vendor AI claims should demand to know whether the model sees a slice of the business or the whole thing. A tool that talks to one system is a chatbot. A tool that talks to all systems is infrastructure.

## AI VISIBILITY & DISCOVERY

The "front door" of hotel booking is migrating from OTA search pages to conversational AI interfaces — and brands are moving fast to secure their place.


**Wyndham Hotels & Resorts** is the latest major brand to make a significant move in that direction, launching a native app within ChatGPT on May 6. The experience allows users to explore Wyndham's portfolio of approximately 8,400 hotels worldwide directly within ChatGPT through map-based browsing, conversational prompts, amenity filters, and interactive hotel cards.
 
Since 2018, Wyndham has invested more than $450 million in technology modernisation. In 2020, it became the first major hotel company to fully migrate its systems to the cloud.
 The operational results are already measurable: AI-powered tools deployed within Wyndham's call centres have reduced average handle times by 7%. The most engaged hotels using Wyndham Connect averaged more than $60,000 in incremental revenue last year, with the highest-performing property exceeding $200,000. 
Other hotel companies are quietly experimenting with similar technologies behind the scenes; across the sector, executives are increasingly evaluating how conversational AI could influence distribution economics, loyalty engagement, staffing efficiency, guest personalisation, and search visibility.



**Sabre** — the GDS written off for years as legacy infrastructure — shipped first-to-market agentic APIs and an MCP server in September 2025, then published the industry's first Agentic Blueprint three months later. The Hotels Network, the direct-booking specialist owned by Lighthouse, launched the first direct booking app for hotels inside ChatGPT in March 2026.
 
In the past few months, Stripe, Visa, Mastercard, AWS, OpenAI, and Google have all launched payment infrastructure specifically for AI agents. Amazon's announcement named hotel bookings as a primary use case.



**MSC Cruises** added an AI concierge to its mobile app on May 7. The tool, available in more than 90 languages, can answer guest questions and handle tasks such as restaurant reservations, spa bookings and shore excursions.



In the same week, **Choice Hotels** unveiled a series of internal AI tools — a move PhocusWire summarised as the industry actively deciding between app-first and AI-ready strategies.


**Sources:** [Hotel Technology News – Wyndham (May 7)](https://hoteltechnologynews.com/2026/05/wyndham-launches-native-chatgpt-app-as-competition-intensifies-around-ai-powered-hotel-discovery/) · [Hospitality.today – PMS Era Is Ending](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/opinion/4132382/the-pms-era-is-ending) [PAYWALLED/BLOCKED] · [PhocusWire Latest (May 7–11)](https://www.phocuswire.com/Latest-News)

**So what:** For a mid-market hotel not yet in ChatGPT, Claude, or Perplexity, the risk is structural invisibility — not just a missed channel, but complete exclusion from the recommendation set. Operators should audit whether their property data is machine-readable, current, and rich enough for LLMs to surface it. This is the new SEO.

## OPERATIONAL AI

The 2026 National Restaurant Association Show (May 16–19, Chicago) delivered a ground-level temperature check of where AI is actually landing with operators.


The new hot application at the Show appeared to be AI assistants sitting atop restaurants' data and providing insights and action items — tools that might tell an operator that a certain location is busier than usual, or that they've run out of ketchup. Several of the big POS companies were offering a version of this. "Restaurant people wear a ton of hats," said **Toast** CMO Kelly Esten. "How can we start to take some of that work that's not core to why they got into running a restaurant off their plate with AI?"


Crucially, the floor also revealed a resistance undercurrent. 
Voice AI was a major trend, with some vendors putting old-fashioned landlines in their booths to make the tech feel approachable. But operators expressed fatigue with AI hype. "The AI that we're seeing is not necessarily solving the immediate problem," said Khara Mangiduyos, owner of Kalei's Kitchenette in San Diego. "That's why you end up creating your own."
 Multiple attendees reported building their own AI apps to avoid vendor costs.


According to research cited by the National Restaurant Association Show, nearly 90 percent of operators expect food and labor costs to continue increasing this year, while 80 percent of consumers say overall value remains their top priority when dining out.


On the hotel side, 
**Scandic** is rolling out **Quinyx** (an AI workforce management tool) to forecast demand and align schedules and labor costs with occupancy; other hospitality operators are tapping platforms such as Nowsta to screen and onboard temporary workers rapidly.
 
**Yum Brands** has introduced the Byte AI Restaurant Coach under its Byte platform. The system analyses operational data and recommends decisions related to staffing, scheduling and restaurant performance. It can track employee attendance, help plan shift patterns and assist with drive-thru management. It has been deployed across more than 28,000 restaurants within the Yum system.



**IDeaS** (a SAS company) and **Stayntouch** announced on May 5 the integration of IDeaS Last Room Value–powered availability and length-of-stay decisions within Stayntouch's cloud PMS — bringing advanced revenue decision-making within reach for lean teams across independent hotels and growing portfolios.


**Sources:** [Restaurant Business Online – NRA Show Tech Trends (May 18)](https://restaurantbusinessonline.com/technology/6-tech-trends-restaurant-show) · [PYMNTS – Fast Food Chains Put AI to Work](https://www.pymnts.com/artificial-intelligence-2/2026/fast-food-chains-put-ai-to-work/) · [Hotel Online – IDeaS/Stayntouch (May 5)](https://www.hotel-online.com/news/ideas-expands-integration-with-stayntouch-pms-to-power-smarter-value-driven-availability-decisions)

**So what:** The "AI assistant atop your data" pattern has become the default vendor pitch — from POS giants at the NRA Show to hotel BI tools. The operators who cut through the noise are the ones demanding a specific operational outcome (labour hours saved, waste reduced, RevPAR uplift) with a named attribution methodology, not just a product demo.

## VENDOR MOVES


**Wonder** (founded by e-commerce veteran Marc Lore) announced Wonder Create on May 5 — an initiative that lets anyone use AI to design and launch their own restaurant brand in under a minute, deploying it across Wonder's growing network of 120 tech-enabled kitchen locations. "You type in what kind of restaurant you want to build. It builds the restaurant — AI does — in under a minute. It does the name, branding, description, pictures, pricing, health information, and all the recipes for your restaurant," Lore explained at the WSJ Future of Everything conference.
 
The company has 120 locations today and aims to have 400 next year and 1,000 by 2035.
 The ghost kitchen model has a bruised track record (MrBeast Burger being the canonical cautionary tale), and Wonder's approach is unproven at scale — but the combination of proprietary robotic kitchen infrastructure, the Grubhub distribution network (250 million deliveries/year), and AI brand generation is architecturally distinct from prior attempts.


**Mews** raised $300 million in January 2026 at a $2.5 billion valuation — the largest round ever for a hotel PMS company. The company now powers 12,500 properties across 85 countries, processing $19.7 billion in transaction volume annually. Its 55% SaaS gross profit growth signals strong unit economics.
 
Mews calls its approach "agentic orchestration" — the idea that AI should coordinate workflows across departments rather than act as a narrow assistant in a single application. Near term, that means expanding capabilities including Smart Tips and Atomize, the revenue management system. Longer term, it means AI that can handle common operational loops end-to-end: receive a guest request, identify the right action, assign it to the right team, confirm completion, and update the guest automatically.



**AI-led guest experience platforms** were a standout investment category, with Duve, Chatlyn, Conduit and Canary Technologies raising a combined $152.6 million. Mews' acquisitions of Flexkeeping and DataChat in late 2025 are one example of broader platform build-through-acquisition strategy. These platforms are targeting one of the industry's biggest pressures: the need to deliver fast, personalized service with leaner teams.


**Sources:** [TechCrunch – Marc Lore/Wonder (May 5)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/05/marc-lore-says-that-ai-will-soon-enable-anyone-open-a-restaurant/) · [Hotel Technology News – Mews $300M (Jan 2026)](https://hoteltechnologynews.com/2026/01/mews-secures-300-million-to-accelerate-agentic-ai-for-autonomous-hotel-management/) · [Hotel Online / Abode Worldwide Investment Index (Apr 2026)](https://www.hotel-online.com/news/hospitality-tech-drew-over-1-billion-in-the-last-year-with-pms-and-ai-led-platforms-capturing-biggest-share)

**So what:** The PMS war is becoming a platform war. Mews is acquiring its way to vertical integration; Cloudbeds is betting on data unification; Oracle is defending its enterprise base. For PE-backed hospitality SaaS: the differentiated position is anything that adds a layer of proprietary data or workflow automation that the platform giants can't easily replicate.

The 90-day burst between December 2025 and February 2026 saw three of the sector's biggest raises land back-to-back: Mews ($300 million), Kindred ($125 million across two simultaneous rounds) and Limehome (€75 million).



Seven PMS companies raised a combined $408.1 million. "Crossing the $1 billion mark matters, but the real story is where the money is going. **Dishio** raised $2.5 million to help restaurants turn guest data into repeat revenue.
 Small raise, but representative of the broader seed-stage bet on first-party data ownership as restaurants try to reduce dependence on third-party platforms.

## GOVERNANCE & SECURITY

Two developments this week signal the governance consequences of rushed AI deployment are arriving in earnest.


**Hoteliers have reached a breaking point with vendors who can't explain what their technology does beyond a buzzword**, according to PhocusWire analysis from Cloudbeds VP Sebastien Leitner. Revenue management systems that have existed for 20 years are suddenly "AI-enabled" without any significant technical changes. The properties that will succeed are those demanding transparency from technology partners and insisting on concrete explanations of what the tech does and how it solves specific problems.



Human-driven web traffic is declining across the hospitality industry. As organic visits fall, automated traffic from AI agents, OTA bots and scrapers now represents a growing share of what platforms like Google Analytics record. The result: analytics aren't measuring what they used to. Signal integrity is collapsing.
 When bot activity is misclassified as genuine interest, properties may overestimate demand and make pricing or marketing decisions based on patterns that don't reflect real traveller intent.

From the restaurant side, the Restaurant Dive sidebar revealed a timely governance case study: a **Pizza Hut franchisee filed a lawsuit claiming AI-driven delivery aggregator deployment caused $100 million in damages** — an early indicator of what franchisee-brand AI disputes may look like when technology mandates outpace operator consent.

**Sources:** [PhocusWire – 6 AI Trends for Hotels 2026](https://www.phocuswire.com/6-ai-trends-hotels-2026) · [Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/)

**So what:** Analytics integrity is a board-level risk for any hotel that prices or staffs off digital demand signals. Operators should be auditing their analytics pipelines for bot contamination before making major revenue management or CapEx decisions off that data.
