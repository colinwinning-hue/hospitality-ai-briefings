## Data & Integration

The most consequential theme across both sectors this cycle is the argument that AI is only as good as the data layer beneath it — and vendors are racing to own that layer.


**Cloudbeds** (May 21) unveiled Ask Signals, a conversational AI interface designed to help hotel teams interact with operational, guest and revenue data through natural conversation. Introduced as part of Cloudbeds' Spring Release, it gives hoteliers a faster way to surface insights, understand performance trends, and deliver more personalized guest experiences without navigating multiple systems or manually pulling reports. Ask Signals is built on Signals, Cloudbeds' unified hospitality intelligence architecture, which connects reservations, revenue, channels, payments, guests and marketing within a single data environment.
 The company's positioning is pointed: 
"Every hotel technology company will have AI," said Adam Harris, co-founder and CEO of Cloudbeds. "What will matter is the quality and connectedness of the data underneath it. Most hospitality AI tools only see one part of the business because they sit on disconnected systems assembled over time."
 The launch is in early-access pilot; no outcome data yet.

**Mews** (April 16) pursued the same thesis on the restaurant side of its stack. 
Mews has launched Mews Business Intelligence, an AI-powered product giving hoteliers "a single source of truth" for tracking revenue, occupancy and bookings. Mews BI features built-in analytics including AI-powered performance summaries, custom dashboards, automated reporting, and external source data combining Mews' figures with Google Ads and OTA data. Mews BI has been adopted by 1,000 early customers
, including the 42-room Adara Hotel in Whistler, whose GM noted: "We had data before, but it was manual and not detailed, just basic stats like overall ADR and date. With Mews BI, everything is in one place."

Sources: [Cloudbeds GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/05/21/3299668/0/en/Cloudbeds-Introduces-Ask-Signals-a-Conversational-AI-Interface-Built-on-Unified-Hotel-Data.html) · [Hotel Dive — Mews BI](https://www.hoteldive.com/news/mews-launches-ai-powered-business-intelligence-product/817672/)

**So what:** The data-layer battle is becoming the real PMS war. Any operator evaluating AI tools should first ask: does this system see the full operation or only a slice? If a vendor cannot demonstrate how their AI accesses and integrates data across reservations, revenue, and guest touchpoints in a single environment, the AI layer will underperform regardless of model quality.

## AI Visibility & Discovery

The fight over where guests discover and book hotels and restaurants is accelerating, with conversational platforms threatening to create a new intermediary tier above OTAs.


**Wyndham Hotels & Resorts** (May 7) launched a native app within ChatGPT — the first from a major U.S. economy and midscale franchisor — allowing users to explore Wyndham's portfolio of approximately 8,400 hotels worldwide directly within ChatGPT through map-based browsing, conversational prompts, amenity filters, and interactive hotel cards.
 This follows Wyndham's 2025 Claude integration and a forthcoming Google AI Mode integration. Importantly, Wyndham cited measurable outcomes from adjacent AI tools: 
since 2018, Wyndham has invested more than $450 million in technology modernization; in 2020 it became the first major hotel company to fully migrate to the cloud.
 Its AI-powered call-centre tools have reduced average handle times by 7%, and the most-engaged hotels on its Canary-powered Wyndham Connect platform averaged more than $60,000 in incremental revenue last year, with the top-performing property exceeding $200,000.

**IHG and Marriott** are taking a deliberately different approach. 
While Wyndham launched a ChatGPT app, Choice Hotels unveiled internal AI tools including "Charlie" to guide employees through core platforms. Marriott is working toward conversational search on its website and mobile app. IHG CEO Elie Maalouf said in Q1 2026 earnings that it's more important to ready technology and content for AI searches to get "the right visibility with the right content." IHG is also implementing a Salesforce cloud-based CRM enabling more personalized experiences, with Maalouf saying it will "bolster our top-of-funnel visibility, make us even readier for GenAI booking."


**Google** (April 10) introduced agentic restaurant booking within AI Mode in the UK. 
The new functionality allows users to ask Google's AI to handle the entire reservation process, including identifying suitable restaurants, checking availability and confirming bookings.
 
Listings are drawn from partners including TheFork, SevenRooms and DesignMyNight, yet the interface, and crucially the customer relationship, will sit squarely with Google. The move sets Google on a direct collision course with OpenTable, whose business has long depended on playing intermediary between restaurants and hungry customers.
 OpenTable responded by positioning this as a channel expansion: "OpenTable is a partner in this launch with Google, and our inclusion is the latest example of how we're meeting diners wherever they are." Google AI Mode had 
reached 75 million daily active users by January 2026.


Sources: [Hotel Technology News — Wyndham](https://hoteltechnologynews.com/2026/05/wyndham-launches-native-chatgpt-app-as-competition-intensifies-around-ai-powered-hotel-discovery/) · [Restaurant Technology News — Google](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/04/google-introduces-ai-powered-restaurant-booking-as-search-evolves-into-action/) · [PhocusWire — Hotel groups app vs AI-ready strategies](https://www.phocuswire.com/news/technology/hotels-ai-apps-content-ai-ready) [PAYWALLED/BLOCKED]

**So what:** There is now a meaningful strategic fork for hotel and restaurant brands: build native LLM integrations to own the discovery moment, or focus investment on structured data and content so AI search surfaces you accurately. IHG's content-first posture may prove more defensible for mid-market independents than racing to ship a ChatGPT app. The risk either way is that AI intermediaries extract the customer relationship the way OTAs did before them.

## Operational AI

The 2026 NRA Show (May 16–19, Chicago) delivered a clear floor signal: AI data-assistant tools sitting atop POS systems are the most densely marketed category of the moment — and operators are beginning to push back on the hype.


At the Show, the new hot application was AI assistants that sit atop restaurants' data and provide insights and action items — telling operators that a certain location is busier than usual, or that they've run out of ketchup. Several big POS companies offered a version of this, with the goal of saving operators the manual labor of keeping tabs on all that information. "Restaurant people wear a ton of hats," said Toast CMO Kelly Esten. "How can we start to take some of that work that's not core to why they got into running a restaurant off their plate with AI?"


**PAR Technology** (April 10) launched PAR Intelligence, featuring an Insights Agent, Offers Agent, and Developer Assist Agent. 
Because they are directly tied to PAR's other products like POS and marketing, PAR says the agents can provide operators with "one source of truth." Taco Bell franchisee Charter Foods began testing one of PAR's agents last year and asked it to identify stores that could benefit by staying open later. Late-night sales increased 20% as a result of its recommendations.
 That is the strongest operator outcome figure of this cycle — real attribution, real uplift.

**Square** (April 10) launched Managerbot. 
While the old Square AI assistant was reactive, Managerbot is proactive: it surfaces insights and metrics it believes the restaurant needs to know, like that it's running low on milk, or that several menu items have duplicate names in the POS. It can also perform tasks such as drafting employee schedules, creating and running email campaigns, and generating purchase orders for supplies.
 Notably, Square urged operators to check its responses for accuracy — an appropriate caveat.

**Operator fatigue and homegrown AI** were the counter-narrative on the NRA Show floor. 
"The AI that we're seeing is not necessarily solving the immediate problem," said Khara Mangiduyos, owner of Kalei's Kitchenette in San Diego. "That's why you end up creating your own."
 Multiple independent operators reported building their own AI tools for social media management and menu optimization rather than paying vendor premiums — a signal that the mid-market is ahead of some vendors in practical AI use.

Sources: [Restaurant Business — 6 NRA Show Tech Trends](https://restaurantbusinessonline.com/technology/6-tech-trends-restaurant-show) · [Nation's Restaurant News — PAR and Square AI Agents](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology/the-hot-new-restaurant-tech-trend-ai-agents)

**So what:** The AI-assistant category is crowding fast. The operators who will see ROI are those who demand: (a) proactive, not reactive, surfacing of problems; (b) action capability, not just dashboards; and (c) a clear answer to "what does this replace or measurably improve?" PAR's 20% late-night sales lift at Charter Foods is the kind of evidence that should be the minimum bar for any vendor conversation.

## Vendor Moves

Several significant platform plays moved this cycle, pointing toward consolidation around unified commerce stacks.

**Shake Shack** (April 11) announced Project Catalyst, a coordinated technology initiative targeting growth to 1,500 company-operated locations. 
At the center is a modernization of in-store technology including POS and kitchen display systems; Shake Shack has partnered with Qu, a cloud-native unified commerce platform, to upgrade its POS and kitchen orchestration capabilities.
 
Beyond operations, Project Catalyst places strong emphasis on guest engagement: the company is developing its first loyalty platform integrated across POS and digital channels. Artificial intelligence is another core component — Shake Shack is building proprietary AI capabilities aimed at delivering real-time operational insights and supporting decision-making at both the restaurant and enterprise level.


**DoorDash** shut down its standalone Zesty AI discovery app and folded its recommendation and conversational search capabilities into its core platform — consolidating discovery closer to ordering and reservations. This followed its $1.2 billion acquisition of SevenRooms in 2025, giving DoorDash a direct reservation infrastructure. 
DoorDash's 20 million-plus DashPass members get preferred access to hot tables and delivery credits simply for completing a reservation; in the last year, 80% of DoorDash customers who tried its "going out" feature visited a restaurant they had never ordered from before.


**Canary Technologies** (March 3) launched what it described as the industry's first hospitality-specific AI agent builder: 
Hospitality AI Agent Studio lets hotels create, customize and deploy AI agents across their organization. Hoteliers can configure pre-built agent templates — Front Desk, Concierge, Central Reservations, and more — or custom-build agents. Canary's existing connectivity across the hotel tech ecosystem provides the ability to quickly create end-to-end agentic workflows.
 Canary is trusted by over 20,000 hotels in 100+ countries, including Marriott, Four Seasons, and IHG.

Sources: [Restaurant Technology News — Shake Shack Project Catalyst](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/04/shake-shack-unveils-restaurant-technology-initiative-focused-on-ai-loyalty-and-unified-commerce/) · [Fast Company — Most Innovative 2026](https://www.fastcompany.com/91497285/restaurants-dining-food-services-most-innovative-companies-2026)

**So what:** The consolidation pattern is identical across both sectors: operators want fewer systems that share data natively, not more specialist tools requiring integration work. For PE-backed SaaS companies, the window for standalone point solutions is closing. The acquirable assets are those with deep operational data and genuine switching costs.

## Funding & M&A


Hospitality technology startups raised more than $1 billion across 40 companies between April 2025 and March 2026, with PMS and AI-led platforms attracting the largest share of investment. That shift was most visible in a 90-day burst between December 2025 and February 2026, when the sector's three biggest raises landed almost back-to-back: Mews ($300 million), Kindred ($125 million across two simultaneous rounds) and Limehome (€75 million).



PMS was the clear funding leader: seven PMS companies raised a combined $408.1 million, more than any other category in the report, spanning established players and newer challengers including Amenitiz, Arbio and Boom.
 
AI-led guest experience platforms were another standout category, with Duve, Chatlyn, Conduit and Canary Technologies raising a combined $152.6 million.



Jessica Gillingham, CEO and Founder of Abode Worldwide, said: "Investors are concentrating capital in the platforms that hospitality businesses increasingly depend on every day, especially PMS and AI-led systems that unify operations, improve automation and strengthen the data layer underneath the business. Unified systems generate more data, better automation and higher switching costs — a compounding dynamic that is proving more appealing to investors."


On the restaurant side, **Dishio** raised $2.5 million to help restaurants turn guest data into repeat revenue, and restaurant guest-data startup **Guestologie** launched in May to help operators act on guest data in real time.

Sources: [Hotel Technology News — Abode Worldwide Report](https://hoteltechnologynews.com/2026/04/research-hospitality-technology-tops-1-billion-across-40-startups-with-pms-and-ai-platforms-capturing-the-largest-share/) · [Hotel Dive — Mews BI](https://www.hoteldive.com/news/mews-launches-ai-powered-business-intelligence-product/817672/)

**So what:** The investor thesis is now clearly articulated: back the control layer, not the feature. PMS and unified-data platforms command the highest multiples because they generate compounding data advantages. For PE operators evaluating hospitality SaaS, ask whether the asset sits at the centre of operator workflows or at the edge.

## People & Skills

This cycle surfaced two emerging talent signals.


A PhocusWire report highlighted the emergence of a new kind of professional, the "hospitality engineer," who can blend an appreciation of service and guest experience with a practical understanding of technology, integrations and data. Sandrine Zechbauer, CMO at RMS, said many hoteliers know they need to adopt AI but struggle to understand how to implement it — and hiring that knowledge costs more money. HRS's Biermann said AI-related talent costs are rising at about 30% each year, and people are increasingly hard to find; "recruiting specialist firms say these in-demand roles now take 50 times longer to recruit."


**Choice Hotels International CEO Patrick Pacious** departed on May 20, 2026. The company appointed Chief Growth and Strategy Officer Dominic Dragisich as interim CEO. With Choice mid-stream on several AI initiatives (CHARLIE, RAISE, EasyBid), leadership continuity at the commercial and technology layer is worth monitoring.


Hotels also face staffing challenges with the tourism sector lagging behind other industries in AI talent: just 2.9% of full-time travel and tourism employees are skilled in AI, compared to 21% in technology and media. However, the share of full-time hospitality workers with AI skills is increasing nearly 5% year over year.


**So what:** The talent gap is real and worsening. Operators who cannot hire hospitality engineers should identify vendor partners who can effectively serve that function — and evaluate vendors partly on how much AI expertise they embed in customer success and implementation teams.

## Governance & Security

*Limited direct signal this cycle; flagged for awareness.*


Wyndham spent $100,000 connecting its data to several LLMs — a cost CEO Geoffrey Ballotti described as nominal — with CFO Mahar noting: "The MCP itself is not an expensive lift." However, a broader PhocusWire piece on AI cost traps noted that many entrepreneurs complain about "subscription creep," where AI service costs rise over time as usage scales. A Gartner report predicts more than 40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by the end of 2027 due to escalating costs.


The privacy and data obligation angle is growing on the restaurant side. Legal commentary from Modern Restaurant Management noted that personalization via AI — reservation data, payment data, loyalty data — is "no longer just a marketing advantage — it's a compliance and cybersecurity obligation that restaurants have to treat as core risk management."

**So what:** Model Context Protocol (MCP) integrations are cheap to stand up but expensive to run at scale. Budget owners approving agentic AI pilots should build in realistic cost modelling for 12–24 months of usage, not just pilot-phase token costs.
