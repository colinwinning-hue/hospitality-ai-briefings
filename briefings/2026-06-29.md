# HOSPITALITY AI BRIEFING — 29 June 2026

**Lead story this cycle:** American Express announces a $700 million acquisition of TheFork, Europe's largest restaurant booking platform, completing one of the most consequential weeks of consolidation in reservation-tech history. Alongside that, SevenRooms (DoorDash-owned) launched a free, open-licence reservation aggregator — a direct volley at OpenTable's recent exclusivity push. Both moves cleared in the past two weeks; they are the freshest items from the watchlist sweep.

## FUNDING & M&A

The reservation layer of the restaurant industry is being carved up by three powerful financial actors — and the pace has accelerated sharply.


**American Express** announced on June 14, 2026 a proposed $700 million all-cash acquisition of TheFork from Tripadvisor. TheFork connects millions of diners with more than 50,000 restaurants across 11 European countries. The deal is expected to close before year-end, subject to regulatory approvals, and would expand American Express' dining network to approximately 75,000 bookable venues when combined with Resy and Tock.



TheFork generated $232 million (€199 million) in revenue for the year ended March 31, 2026, a 25% jump year-over-year, according to Tripadvisor's earnings report. Activist investor Starboard Value had in October 2025 pressed for TheFork's sale as Tripadvisor struggled to recover from pandemic-era disruptions.



"Dining is one of the most important ways people engage with our brand," said Rafa Marquez, President of International Card Services at American Express.



**Resy and Tock** are simultaneously completing their own integration: American Express announced the Tock venue network will move under the Resy brand, combining over 25,000 bookable venues. The Tock name and platform will be phased out.
 
Resy also plans to implement Tock's tiered and prepaid experience features, and build a tech stack integrating AI-driven restaurant discovery and booking, table management, ticketing, POS data, and CRM.
 
In 2026, Resy plans to release an upgraded set of APIs for third-party partners, including Gen AI platforms, exposing Resy's reservation inventory across a diverse set of digital channels to drive discovery and demand.


**DoorDash** executed its own reservation play earlier in the cycle. 
DoorDash launched its in-app "Going Out" reservations tab on September 30, 2025, powered by SevenRooms. In testing, 80% of customers who tried Going Out visited a restaurant they had not previously ordered from. On average, diners using in-app rewards earn $9 per order.
 
Via SevenRooms, DoorDash is also offering restaurants voice AI that can take reservations over the phone and event management capabilities including group bookings and private dining.



On the broader platform side, **Olo** was taken private by Thoma Bravo in a $2 billion acquisition. By going private, Olo gains flexibility for longer-term strategic bets such as deeper integrations with POS systems and loyalty platforms, and positions itself to roll up smaller tech providers.



According to PitchBook, mergers and acquisitions involving restaurant technology providers rose by 45% in the first half of 2025 compared to the same period in 2024.
 
Over 60% of recent restaurant tech acquisitions were motivated by product-line expansion, as platforms aim to offer complete suites — POS, scheduling, ordering, inventory, and analytics — under one roof.


**Smaller raises this cycle:** 
**Chowbus**, an AI-powered restaurant platform designed for Asian restaurants, closed an $81 million fundraising round led by Prysm Capital and Left Lane Capital, with plans to expand beyond its POS platform into marketing, accounting, supply ordering, and insurance. **SlangAI** closed a $36 million Series B led by US Venture Partners, raising $68 million in total, to build out voice AI capabilities.


Sources: [BusinessWire — AmEx/TheFork](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260614939103/en/American-Express-Announces-Proposed-Acquisition-of-TheFork-a-Leading-European-Restaurant-Booking-Platform) | [Hospitality Ireland — TheFork revenue](https://www.hospitalityireland.com/technology/american-express-to-buy-tripadvisors-restaurant-booking-platform-thefork-215932) | [Restaurant Technology News — consolidation](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2025/07/restaurant-technology-consolidation-accelerates-as-investors-favor-scale-over-specialization/)

**So what:** The reservation layer is no longer owned by pure-play reservation companies. AmEx (Resy + Tock + TheFork = 75,000 venues), DoorDash (SevenRooms), and Booking Holdings (OpenTable) now control the primary booking infrastructure for most high-value restaurants in the US and Europe. For mid-market operators, the leverage calculus has shifted: whoever controls your reservation data increasingly controls your marketing relationships, your upsell pipeline, and your credit-card rewards economics. Evaluate existing reservation contracts now, before renewals.

## VENDOR MOVES

The reservation data war opened a new front this week with SevenRooms moving directly against OpenTable's exclusivity policy.


On June 17, 2026, **SevenRooms** unveiled Channel Connect, a desktop app that aggregates reservations from various booking channels — including major rivals — into a single system, while automatically updating a restaurant's inventory across all those channels simultaneously.
 
"More tables for diners, one book for restaurants," SevenRooms co-founder and CEO Joel Montaniel said. He noted the program will bring restaurants up to speed with hotels and airlines, which have been handling bookings this way for years.



The move comes directly after OpenTable began requiring restaurant clients to make it their system of record and make all of their inventory available exclusively on OpenTable. Channel Connect is able to access that rival data "through mechanisms those platforms already provide, using credentials and the data the restaurant controls," a SevenRooms spokesperson said.
 SevenRooms is offering Channel Connect for free under an open licence, available to any restaurant, even those that don't use SevenRooms.


**Toast** launched a major expansion of Toast IQ as a full conversational AI assistant in October 2025. With access to real-time and historical data across approximately 148,000 customer locations, Toast IQ can flag top-selling items by daypart, add a menu item with a single prompt, automatically update menus across every service channel, and answer questions like "Who's working Friday night?" — all from a chat interface.
 
Toast also announced a collaboration with The Coca-Cola Company to create an exclusive AI-powered feature within Toast IQ to help operators boost beverage sales through menu optimization.



**Mews** launched at its Mews Unfold event, presenting what it calls "hospitality's iPhone moment" — five products completing an AI-native Mews Operating System. Inside Mews, PMS, RMS, CRS, CRM, and POS operate as "one workflow, on one data model with AI working across every layer." Mews RMS runs 150 million pricing calculations a day, executing rate changes against live demand without manual intervention, and reports an average 13.7% lift in total revenue per square metre for hotels using the RMS versus those with none.



**PAR Technology** launched its Engagement Cloud in June 2025, consolidating marketing, loyalty, guest data, and ordering tools into a single platform — reflecting growing belief in the "super app" model for restaurants.



**Olo** is developing an AI agent inspired by the Shopify universal commerce protocol, where customers can ask questions like "Is the store open?" and "Is this product available?" Olo CEO Noah Glass told NRN the goal is an open platform accessible to non-Olo restaurants as well. **Miso Robotics** acquired AI operations platform Zignyl and integrated it into its Zippy product — an LLM-powered tool offering agentic assistance, revenue metrics, labor scheduling, and forecasting for operators of Flippy kitchen robots.



**Lighthouse** launched Connect AI on the MCP standard in June 2025, and its sister company The Hotels Network shipped the first direct booking app for hotels inside ChatGPT in March 2026.


Sources: [NRN — SevenRooms Channel Connect](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology/sevenrooms-launches-a-reservations-aggregator-for-restaurants) | [Restaurant Technology News — Toast IQ](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2025/10/toast-launches-conversational-ai-assistant-to-help-restaurant-operators-work-faster-and-smarter/) | [HospitalityNet — Mews OS](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4132634/mews-unveils-the-operating-system-for-hospitality)

**So what:** SevenRooms Channel Connect is a sharp competitive instrument — free, open-licence, and explicitly designed to break OpenTable's new exclusivity hold. For operators caught between platform mandates, Channel Connect is a credible hedge. Toast IQ's demonstrated ability to take action (not just surface insights) is the clearest signal of a restaurant AI product graduating from dashboard to co-pilot.

## AI VISIBILITY & DISCOVERY

The shift from AI as a search layer to AI as a booking actor is happening faster in restaurants than in hotels.


On April 13, 2026, **Google** introduced agentic restaurant booking within AI Mode in the UK, enabling users to describe detailed dining preferences and receive curated, bookable options in real time. Reservations are completed through integrations with platforms such as TheFork, SevenRooms, ResDiary, and OpenTable.



Two weeks before the UK restaurant launch, Google had confirmed it is developing similar agentic booking capabilities for hotel and flight reservations. "Our goal is that you'll be able to also book flights and hotels directly in AI Mode," said Julie Farago, Google's VP of engineering for travel and local search. Google is working with Booking.com, Expedia, Marriott International, IHG Hotels & Resorts, Choice Hotels International, and Wyndham Hotels & Resorts. Google said specifics regarding the booking flow, payments, and other details are still being worked out.
 
As of June 2026, agentic hotel and flight booking in AI Mode has not launched.



Google built this in stages. Agentic booking went live first for restaurant reservations, event tickets, and beauty and wellness appointments in the US in November 2025 through partners such as OpenTable, Resy, and Ticketmaster.
 
An OpenTable spokesperson stated: "OpenTable is a partner in this launch with Google, and our inclusion is the latest example of how we're meeting diners wherever they are. This expansion with Google builds on our existing partnerships with OpenAI, Perplexity, and Alexa+ to make it easier than ever for diners to find and secure the right table."



**TheFork** was among the first companies to join OpenAI's Operator as an early research preview contributor in February 2025. UK diners can now use OpenAI's Operator to automate bookings, receive personalised dining recommendations, and manage reservations on TheFork. Special offers available at restaurants are automatically applied.
 
"With OpenAI's Operator, we're harnessing the best of AI-powered efficiency and personalisation to learn more about how agents can enhance every aspect of the dining experience," said Almir Ambeskovic, CEO of TheFork.



The broader adoption of the Model Context Protocol lets agents query hotel inventory and rates programmatically. Phocuswright reports that more than 60% of travel businesses are experimenting with or scaling agentic AI. Agentic bookings remain a small share of total volume, with most agent flows still requiring human approval at the booking step.



The asymmetry agents create for hotels is significant: the agent's logic is opaque, the signals it weights are unpublished, and the hotel cannot see how its property is being represented to the traveller before the booking happens — or whether it is being represented at all. The first signal a hotel may receive that an agent considered them is the booking that arrives — or, more often, the booking that doesn't.



Industry tracking indicates that roughly 40% of US travelers used generative-AI tools to plan a trip during 2025 — an 11-point year-over-year increase — while the share starting trip planning at a conventional search engine fell from about half to roughly a third over the same period.


Sources: [Hospitality.today — Google UK restaurant booking](https://www.hospitality.today/article/google-brings-ai-powered-restaurant-booking-to-search-in-the-uk) | [PhocusWire — Google hotel/flight AI Mode](https://www.phocuswire.com/google-agentic-travel-booking-ai) | [Hospitality.today — Agentic discovery analysis](https://www.hospitality.today/article/the-hotel-discovery-layer-agentic-discovery-and-the-next-intermediary)

**So what:** Google's decision to sequence restaurant bookings before hotel bookings is not accidental — restaurants are lower-value, higher-frequency transactions that let Google calibrate consumer trust at lower risk. Hotels are next. The window to get your inventory readable by AI agents (via MCP, clean structured data, API-first booking engines) is closing. Properties that aren't accessible to these systems won't lose bookings to AI — they'll lose them to OTAs *through* AI.

## GOVERNANCE & SECURITY

Two overlapping regulatory signals landed this month that hospitality operators cannot ignore.


On June 2, 2026, the White House issued an Executive Order titled "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security." A hospitality-sector analysis published on Hospitality Net argues this signals that AI has crossed from an experimental productivity tool into "a critical layer of operational infrastructure that governments now treat as a matter of national security." The competitive question for hotels is shifting from "How many AI features do we have?" to "Can we govern AI responsibly, secure our connected systems, and turn operational trust into guest experience?"



One of the most overlooked shifts is the convergence of hospitality infrastructure with national infrastructure. Large hotels, integrated resorts, convention centers, and gaming properties increasingly intersect with transportation, financial networks, public utilities, and emergency response — meaning expanded cybersecurity expectations will cascade to hospitality operators over time.



A separate compliance risk is already active: the DOJ's Data Security Program (DSP), which became effective April 8, 2025, restricts or prohibits certain data transactions that could give countries of concern access to Americans' bulk sensitive personal data. Hospitality companies are uniquely exposed because AI deployments — from dynamic pricing to biometric check-in — typically require massive volumes of customer and device data and often rely on third-party vendors, offshore development teams, and cloud-hosted model pipelines.



Hospitality organizations should conduct an inventory of AI systems likely to contain covered data categories (geolocation, biometrics, payments, health accommodations), screen vendors for offshore access, and update contracting playbooks to include strict prohibitions on onward transfers or independent AI training use.


Sources: [Hospitality Net — White House EO analysis](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/opinion/4132771/when-national-ai-security-becomes-a-hospitality-imperative) | [Hospitality Technology — DOJ DSP](https://hospitalitytech.com/beyond-hipaa-navigating-dojs-new-bulk-data-thresholds-hospitality-ai)

**So what:** The era of "casual AI" procurement — dropping a vendor in without governance review — is closing. PE-backed hospitality SaaS companies selling into enterprise hotel groups now face procurement questionnaires on AI governance as rigorous as uptime SLAs. Build the AI governance documentation now; it will be a sales-blocking issue within 18 months.
