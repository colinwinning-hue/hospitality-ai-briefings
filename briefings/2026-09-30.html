Vendor Moves

The window's headline cluster is four distinct product and model launches on September 29, spanning OTA-backed AI assistants, cruise-line ChatGPT app debuts, restaurant POS platform expansion, and a delivery-platform push into physical dining.

Lola — the Booking Holdings-backed AI travel assistant built by Kayak co-founders Steve Hafner and Paul English — unveiled its membership model on September 29. Booking Holdings CEO Glenn Fogel is running at least five AI projects across Priceline's Penny assistant, Booking.com's planning tools, an Agoda trip planner, an incubated startup, and an unnamed West Coast effort, alongside an ad deal with OpenAI and an integration with Google's hotel booking tool in AI Mode. Lola is the incubated startup now going public. Lola, Booking Holdings' new AI assistant, books hotels, tables, and tickets in one chat and charges members up to $25 a month for rates up to 15% below the public price. The subscription model is structurally distinct from every competitor in the market: instead of monetising through OTA commissions or sponsored placement, Lola charges the traveller directly and promises ad-free recommendations. Whether it can sustain an "unbiased" positioning while Booking Holdings controls its inventory supply chain is the central credibility question.

Princess Cruises launched a dedicated ChatGPT app on September 29 in partnership with Mobi. The Princess ChatGPT app enables cruise customers to explore destinations, check pricing and availability, and get help with planning a cruise. It joins Wyndham, IHG, Accor, and Marriott in building brand-specific ChatGPT surface area — a race to own the distribution relationship before generalist agents colonise it. No booking-completion or conversion data was disclosed at launch.

Toast launched Toast Fuel on September 29. Restaurant and retail digital technology platform Toast debuted Toast Fuel, new capabilities for gas stations and convenience stores. Toast believes artificial intelligence can strengthen customer relationships, produce new revenue streams and make its software indispensable to restaurateurs.

"Convenience stores sell an estimated 80% of the fuel purchased by consumers in the United States," Toast said, citing National Association of Convenience Stores data. "Many of those convenience store operators run their in-store retail and fuel businesses on two disconnected systems and have had little access to the cloud-based tools that have helped reshape restaurants and retailers over the past decade." Toast Fuel connects pump control, food service, retail, payments, loyalty, inventory, and team management in one system — the same platform architecture Toast deploys across its roughly 180,000 existing restaurant customer locations. Since Toast's market debut in 2021, its market share has grown from about 1 in 20 small and midsize restaurants in the US to 1 in 5. Restaurants on the platform are paying more, on average: revenue per restaurant is up from about $8,000 to $13,000 over the last five years.

Grubhub announced on September 29 the launch of a dine-in rewards program — a direct move by the delivery platform into the physical dining occasion. No programme details were disclosed in trade coverage at close of the window, but the strategic signal is plain: delivery platforms are competing for the full dining relationship, not just the off-premise transaction.

Sources: PhocusWire — Lola membership launch · PhocusWire — Princess Cruises ChatGPT app · PYMNTS — Toast Fuel, Sep 29 · NRN — Grubhub dine-in rewards

So what: The Lola subscription model is the most structurally significant launch of this cycle. A paid-membership AI travel tool backed by the world's largest OTA is not neutral — it is Booking Holdings' hedge against AI agents that disintermediate OTAs. For independent hoteliers and restaurant operators, Lola creates a new distribution surface with opaque ranking criteria that cannot be purchased. Treat it as a new GEO channel to audit, not a future abstraction. Toast Fuel signals the QSR–c-store format convergence is now a platform battleground: operators running both food service and fuel who are not yet on a unified cloud stack are increasingly disadvantaged on pricing transparency, loyalty tie-in, and labour efficiency.

Funding & M&A

Two September 29 funding announcements expand the hotel-technology investment picture.

Dextr AI emerged from stealth with $6.7 million in seed funding for an agentic hotel platform. When Sajid Shariff returned to the Bay Area in 2024, he reconnected with people he knew from his years working in hospitality. So Shariff offered to build an AI agent for a couple of properties. The first, called Alfred, answered guest questions and helped with check-ins and check-outs. Within 90 days, he said, online reviews improved at both properties.

The startup works directly with the owners and operators of branded properties and is beginning its first direct relationship with a hotel brand. It also works with hotel and property management companies, as well as reservation centres, vacation rentals, and outdoor hospitality venues such as campgrounds, RV parks, lodges, and golf courses. He said annual recurring revenue has been growing about 20% to 30% month over month, though he did not disclose hard figures. Dextr plans to use its new funding to hire more engineers who work directly with customers, expand its software integrations, and develop additional agents. The PhocusWire report confirms the platform covers voice AI, guest management, back-office operations, staff management, and sales and marketing.

HyperGuest secured a $25 million investment from Apax Partners — a firm with prior hospitality-tech investments in Guesty and a minority stake in IBS Software — to fund international growth for its B2B hotel connectivity platform. HyperGuest positions as infrastructure for direct hotel distribution, connecting hotels with travel agencies, OTAs, and corporate buyers through a single API layer. As AI agents increasingly require machine-readable, real-time inventory to transact on hotels' behalf, distribution infrastructure quality is becoming a direct competitive variable.

Sources: PhocusWire — Dextr AI $6.7M seed, Sep 29 · PhocusWire — HyperGuest $25M, Sep 29

So what: Dextr AI's 20–30% monthly ARR growth is the strongest operational signal from a hotel AI startup this cycle — but it is self-reported, covers fast early expansion from a small base, and carries no independent attribution. The 90-day review improvement claim at pilot properties is specific enough to be a testable hypothesis for operators evaluating similar tools: request review-score trajectory data before and after deployment, control for seasonality. HyperGuest's Apax backing reflects PE conviction that hotel connectivity infrastructure — not just the AI layer on top — is where durable value is being built as agentic booking scales.

Data & Integration

September 30 marks a hard operational deadline in hotel distribution as Google ends auto-rate scraping, transferring pricing visibility to OTAs by default for any hotel without a direct feed.

Google stopped filling hotel price boxes through its own rate-scraping on September 30, per the Google Hotel Center help page. Until now, Google filled that gap for hotels that advertised without a feed — it found their rate itself. According to its Hotel Center help page, that ends on September 30. No feed, no hotel ads.

Booking.com, Expedia, and the other sites that sell your rooms send Google their prices for your hotel every day. Your own price sits beside theirs only if you send Google your rate too, through Hotel Center or your booking engine.

The AI-mediated pricing picture reinforces the structural problem. When someone asks ChatGPT what a hotel costs, it goes to the web almost every time. Nicolas Sitter, who heads AI research at the hotel data firm Lighthouse, captured 240 of those questions on June 25 and logged every page ChatGPT fetched to answer them. It searched in 98.8% of cases. Booking.com, Expedia and other online travel agencies made up 46% of the pages it read, more than every other kind of source combined.

Booking.com and Expedia run their own apps inside ChatGPT, built on OpenAI's tools, that show hotels, prices, and booking links inside the conversation. ChatGPT has sold ads in the US since February and in Europe since August. When SE Ranking put 50,006 US questions to it in July, every ad it found was a single sponsored offer. In travel, Booking.com held that one seat 62.57% of the time.

The Google AI Mode hotel booking surface, live since late August with ten named partners, underlines the exposure for independents. No independent is on that list. An independent gets into the answer through a booking site, at the booking site's price. Beside it, Choice, Hilton, and Wyndham can show a points price.

Sources: Hospitality.today — Hotels losing pricing control, Sep 30 · Google Hotel Center help page (referenced therein)

So what: The Google Hotel Center feed deadline is the single most actionable item in this briefing — not a future risk but an operational change effective today. Any property without a live, accurate Hotel Center feed has now ceded its Google pricing slot to OTAs. For PE-backed hotel groups and management companies: audit feed compliance across the portfolio immediately. For SaaS vendors selling to hotels: direct-feed connectivity to Hotel Center became materially more valuable on September 30 than it was on September 29. The broader takeaway is that AI pricing surfaces (ChatGPT, Google AI Mode) now systematically amplify OTA pricing power because both are trained on or fed by OTA inventory. Hotels without machine-readable direct-rate distribution are structurally invisible in the channels that increasingly shape guest decision-making.

Operational AI

FS/TEC 2026 (September 24–26) produced a practitioner framing that cuts against the dominant AI voice-ordering narrative.

FS/TEC delegates coalesced around a key principle captured in NRN's September 28 post-conference reporting: next-generation restaurant technology should "automate the task, not the moment." NRN's coverage on September 28 frames the distinction: the moments that define hospitality are inherently human, while the surrounding logistical infrastructure — scheduling, ordering accuracy, inventory signals, kitchen sequencing — is the correct automation target. This sits in direct tension with the AI voice ordering push that Toast, Burger King (now walking it back), and Presto are all pursuing: those deployments target precisely the guest-facing ordering moment.

Toast's late-September momentum reinforces the company's position as the most-watched mid-market restaurant AI platform. Coverage centred on the firm's push into artificial intelligence ordering tools, a fresh restaurant partnership in mid-September, and a raised full-year view following a strong quarter. CEO Aman Narang pointed to the company's growing customer base while stressing that AI is moving beyond experimentation into tools that restaurants are already paying to use. The September 29 Toast Fuel launch extends the platform into a new vertical on the same day analyst attention peaks around its AI story.

Sources: NRN — Automate the task, not the moment, Sep 28 · Kalkine Media — Toast AI push, Sep 28 · Expedite — Toast five years public, Sep 23

So what: "Automate the task, not the moment" is worth operationalising as a procurement filter. Before any AI tool purchase: map which moments in your service model are human-differentiated and which are purely logistical. Tools that target logistical tasks — scheduling, order accuracy, inventory signals, reporting — carry a cleaner ROI case and lower CX risk than those that insert AI into the service moment. Toast Fuel is a concrete example of the former applied to a new vertical. The FS/TEC consensus also reframes the Burger King drive-thru reversal (Sep 24, just outside the window but the most-cited practitioner datapoint of the trailing period) not as an isolated failure but as a sector-wide calibration away from moment-level automation.
