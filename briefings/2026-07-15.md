## Vendor Moves

The reservation layer is now the most contested real estate in hospitality tech, and this week's moves confirm the battle has entered its most expensive phase.

**DoorDash / SevenRooms** formally launched its "Going Out" tab in September 2025 after closing the $1.2B SevenRooms acquisition in May 2025. 
Via SevenRooms, DoorDash is offering restaurants voice AI that can take reservations over the phone and event management capabilities including group bookings and private dining. Reservations debuted first in Miami and New York, with more cities coming later in the year.
 The data pitch is aggressive: 
"Delivery and dine-in have typically been siloed data sets," SevenRooms co-founder Joel Montaniel said. "So if a customer has ordered six times, and they're coming into the restaurant for the first time, are they a first-time customer or a seventh-time customer?"
 Then on June 17, SevenRooms escalated its platform war directly. 
SevenRooms unveiled Channel Connect, a desktop app that aggregates reservations from various booking channels — including "major booking platforms," which presumably means chief rivals OpenTable and Resy — into a single system while automatically updating inventory across those channels. The company is offering Channel Connect for free under an open license, available to any restaurant even if they don't use SevenRooms. The move came directly after OpenTable began requiring restaurant clients to make it their system of record; SevenRooms' blog post explicitly stated "Channel Connect was built so that no single platform has to be the gatekeeper to your operations."


**American Express** is executing the most ambitious dining-tech roll-up in the industry. 
This summer, Amex will merge Tock's restaurant inventory with Resy's, adding roughly 8,000 bookable venues — including 1,200 wineries — to Resy's app and website, boosting venue count to 25,000 and sunsetting the Tock brand.
 The financial evidence that the loyalty-credit flywheel is working: 
in the three weeks following the announcement of a $400 annual Resy credit for Platinum card members, there was a 36% increase in Resy reservations made by U.S. Platinum card users — and a five times increase in daily accounts being linked. Over the last year, reports surfaced of restaurants receiving five-, six-, or even seven-figure payments to switch between booking platforms.
 On June 15, Amex extended its reach to Europe, announcing a 
proposed acquisition of TheFork from Tripadvisor for $700 million in cash. TheFork connects millions of diners with more than 50,000 restaurants across 11 European countries. Together with Resy and Tock, these platforms are expected to expand American Express' dining network to 75,000 bookable venues.
 
TheFork's full-year revenue increased from $181 million in 2024 to $221 million in 2025, representing growth of 22%, and adjusted EBITDA margin advanced from 2.9% to 9.2%.


**OpenTable** (Booking Holdings) moved this week with a loyalty play of its own. 
OpenTable is giving top-tier loyalty members access to its most exclusive reservations
, tightening premium access as a competitive response to DashPass exclusives and Amex Platinum credits. When OpenTable launched its AI Concierge in July 2025, 
it answered 80% of diners' questions, reducing the need for phone calls to restaurants.


**Resy** — now the consolidating vehicle for Tock — partnered with Toast earlier this year, promising real-time data-sharing with restaurants. 
Resy upped its game, partnering with Toast and promising data-sharing that should empower restaurants to act on guest data in real time.


**Toast** filed an unusual product move this week. 
Toast announced "Toast Lab," a program to fund the opening of a restaurant — or at least help fund it — with the selected operator gaining a direct line to Toast's executive team and early access to all the company's tech. Applications from existing Toast operators are being accepted through July 14.
 Separately, NRN reported on July 14 that restaurants are actively using Toast's conversational AI assistant (Toast IQ, launched October 2025) in real operations — the first operator-experience signal on that product.

Sources: [Nation's Restaurant News](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology/sevenrooms-launches-a-reservations-aggregator-for-restaurants) · [Fast Company / Kristen Hawley](https://www.fastcompany.com/91496951/amex-resy-tock-restaurant-reservation-wars) · [Expedite / Kristen Hawley](https://www.expedite.news/p/doordash-reservations-are-here) · [PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/news/finance/tripadvisor-sell-thefork-american-express)

**So what:** The reservation platform is becoming the identity layer for dining — whoever controls the booking controls the guest profile, the credit-card partnership, and the data flywheel. Mid-market restaurants facing platform consolidation should urgently audit which platforms they give their best inventory to: the incentive structures (DashPass credits, Amex Platinum credits, Chase Sapphire perks) are being used to move those decisions in ways that look like demand-generation but are fundamentally data-capture plays.

## Funding & M&A

Restaurant tech consolidation is the structural story of the year. 
According to PitchBook, mergers and acquisitions involving restaurant technology providers rose by 45% in the first half of 2025 compared to the same period in 2024.
 The thesis is clear: 
DoorDash's $1.2 billion acquisition of SevenRooms and Olo's $2 billion private-equity take-private by Thoma Bravo both reflect a broader push to reduce vendor complexity. According to Capstone Partners, over 60% of recent restaurant tech acquisitions were motivated by product-line expansion, as platforms aim to offer complete suites — POS, scheduling, ordering, inventory, and analytics — under one roof.


**Hostie** raised $12 million this week to expand its AI-powered virtual concierge for restaurant guest communication — the latest in a string of AI-native messaging tools targeting restaurants looking for alternatives to generic chatbots. This week's RTN ticker also flagged **Besty AI** raising $3.75 million for guest service and operations automation for short-term rental operators on the hotel side.

On the hotel investment side, the macro picture remains robust. 
Hospitality technology startups raised more than $1 billion across 40 companies between April 2025 and March 2026, with property management systems and AI-led platforms attracting the largest share of investment, according to Abode Worldwide's Hospitality Tech Investment Index 2026.
 
Mews' acquisitions of Flexkeeping and DataChat in late 2025 are one example of this strategy. AI-led guest experience platforms were another standout category, with Duve, Chatlyn, Conduit and Canary Technologies raising a combined $152.6 million.
 The Abode research is explicit about why: 
"Unified systems generate more data, better automation and higher switching costs. This is a compounding dynamic that is proving more appealing to investors."


Sources: [Hotel Technology News / Abode Worldwide](https://hoteltechnologynews.com/2026/04/research-hospitality-technology-tops-1-billion-across-40-startups-with-pms-and-ai-platforms-capturing-the-largest-share/) · [Restaurant Technology News](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2025/07/restaurant-technology-consolidation-accelerates-as-investors-favor-scale-over-specialization/)

**So what:** For PE-backed hospitality SaaS companies: the market is rewarding unified platforms and penalising point solutions. If your product can't demonstrate a data-compounding story — where each new customer makes the platform smarter — you are a consolidation target, not a consolidator.

## AI Visibility & Discovery

The fight over who controls the booking interface — and where commission revenue flows — is now the central strategic question in travel and hospitality distribution.

**Google** made its most consequential move in restaurant bookings in November 2025. 
Agentic booking for restaurants rolled out in AI Mode in the U.S. with no Labs opt-in required, working through OpenTable, Resy, Tock, Ticketmaster and others. In the future, Google will also make it possible to finish booking flights and hotels directly in AI Mode, already working with partners including Booking.com, Choice Hotels International, Expedia, IHG Hotels & Resorts, Marriott International and Wyndham Hotels & Resorts.
 Google VP Julie Farago was explicit about the sequencing: 
"Our goal is that you'll be able to also book flights and hotels directly in AI Mode. We're not going to rush this out the door because we want to make sure that it's a seamless experience."


**OpenAI** retreated from transaction-layer ambitions in March 2026. 
OpenAI is shifting its shopping strategy to focus on discovery and research, leaving checkout to third-party app developers. Complex challenges like consumer habits, real-time data synchronization, and regulatory compliance currently limit the feasibility of direct commerce via conversational AI.
 The market read the retreat as a near-term win for OTAs: 
Booking Holdings shares jumped over 8.5% on Thursday — the day the news broke. For now, ChatGPT will recommend and send. Someone else closes the deal.
 But Hospitality.today's analysis is unsparing about what the reprieve actually means: 
if ChatGPT and its peers become referral engines rather than transaction platforms, Booking.com will optimise for AI visibility the same way it optimised for Google and metasearch — hotels, once again, will fund the placement through commission structures they don't control. The window in which a truly neutral AI transaction layer might have given hotels direct access to travelers without Booking.com as the obligatory intermediary just got narrower.


A sharp new development this week from Hospitality.today: hotels are actively being sold **llms.txt files** — structured documents intended to make property information readable by AI engines. The July 15 piece finds that no major AI engine, ChatGPT included, actually reads them in any meaningful way. [PAYWALLED/BLOCKED — sourced from Hospitality.today headline and subheading only.] This is one of the cleanest agentwashing signals in the current cycle.


The hotel's audience is no longer the traveler — it is the agent acting on the traveler's preferences. Hotels cannot easily verify which agents are in the loop, what queries they ran on the hotel's behalf, or what alternatives they considered. The first signal a hotel may receive that an agent considered them is the booking that arrives — or, more often, the booking that doesn't.


Sources: [Google Blog](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/agentic-plans-booking-travel-canvas-ai-mode/) · [Hospitality.today](https://www.hospitality.today/article/openai-just-saved-booking-com) · [Hospitality.today — Discovery Layer series](https://www.hospitality.today/article/the-hotel-discovery-layer-agentic-discovery-and-the-next-intermediary) · [Skift](https://skift.com/2026/03/05/openai-chatgpt-checkout-walkback/)

**So what:** Google's live restaurant booking pipeline via OpenTable/Resy/Tock is real and operational today. Hotel and flight booking is in development with major chain partners already committed. Operators who haven't yet structured their inventory data in machine-readable formats — specific attributes, accurate pricing, granular amenity tagging — are already invisible to a significant share of agentic discovery traffic. Structured data is the new SEO.

## Data & Integration

Every major hotel chain has committed publicly to an agentic AI roadmap, and every one of them is hitting the same wall.


Every major hotel chain has now committed publicly to an agentic AI roadmap — Marriott's $1B+ agentic mesh, Hilton's AI Planner, Hyatt and Accor inside ChatGPT, IHG's AI-enhanced CRM. All of them are bottlenecked at the same layer: the property management system was built when a guest was a stay at a property, not a relationship across the chain.
 The integration picture underneath these announcements is damaging: 
fewer than one in four hotels have fully integrated core systems, according to industry survey synthesis across Hotel Tech Report, Revinate, and Hospitality Net, 2025-26.
 The Apptad analysis also surfaces a less-discussed blocker: identity resolution. A typical hotel chain carries a 6–16% duplicate-profile rate in its guest estate — meaning a chain with 200 million guest profiles may be reasoning over 16 million ghost guests, silently degrading every personalization use case.

**Mews** made news on July 9 in a different way: Hospitality.today reported that Mews cut 15% of its staff, calling the roles obsolete — framing the layoff as a preview of the product direction (an operating system that absorbs hotel work rather than software that supports it). The $300M raise and the Flexkeeping/DataChat acquisitions give Mews the runway to execute that vision.

**Hilton** wired its reservation system directly into Navan (a corporate travel management company) this week, routing around the GDS and hand-picking the first travel-management company allowed to plug in directly — a significant direct-distribution move that the HTN ticker flagged July 9.

**Radisson Hotel Group** launched AI-powered price matching this week as part of its direct booking push, per HTN — a signal that mid-market chain brands are now deploying AI revenue tools explicitly to reduce OTA dependency, not just to optimise yield.

Sources: [Apptad](https://apptad.com/insights/the-hotel-industrys-ai-problem-lives-in-the-pms/) · [Hotel Technology News](https://hoteltechnologynews.com/2026/04/research-hospitality-technology-tops-1-billion-across-40-startups-with-pms-and-ai-platforms-capturing-the-largest-share/)

**So what:** PMS migration is necessary but not sufficient. The entity layer — a governed, real-time master data record of the guest that sits above all PMSes — is the actual precondition for any AI use case that requires knowing who the guest is across properties and channels. Brands treating their PMS migration as their AI roadmap will deliver disappointing results in year two of their programs.

## Operational AI

**Taco Bell** is the clearest large-scale voice AI deployment signal in restaurants right now. 
Yum! Brands, in partnership with Nvidia, began installing voice-automated order-taking AI agents at Taco Bell and Pizza Hut drive-thrus in 2025. The AI agent greets the drive-thru customer, takes their order, asks clarifying questions and confirms the details, in a human-like voice.
 RTN reported this week that Taco Bell has now expanded that rollout to nearly 900 restaurants across the U.S. — the largest verified voice AI deployment at drive-thru scale. 
McDonald's implementation of AI-driven drive-thru forecasting reduced wait times by 30 seconds while optimising kitchen staffing for predicted demand patterns.
 Across the sector, 
restaurants implementing voice AI report handling 40–60% of phone interactions automatically, freeing staff to focus on in-person customer service and food preparation during peak periods.


On the scheduling side, the AI labor forecasting category continues to mature. 
AI scheduling systems analyse historical sales data, weather patterns, local events, and seasonal trends to predict optimal staffing levels with accuracy rates exceeding 90%. Restaurant operators using AI scheduling report labor cost reductions of 8–12% while simultaneously improving service quality through better staff allocation.


Sources: [Restaurant Technology News](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/07/taco-bell-expands-drive-thru-voice-ai-to-nearly-900-restaurants-across-the-u-s-as-automation-gains-momentum/) · [Cloud Awards / SynergySuite](https://www.cloud-awards.com/how-ai-will-reshape-restaurant-technology-by-2030)

**So what:** Taco Bell at 900 locations is the proof-of-scale moment for QSR voice AI. For multi-unit operators still evaluating voice ordering pilots, the Yum!/Nvidia deployment removes the "will it work at scale" objection. The question is now whether the accuracy and throughput gains justify the integration cost for mid-market brands without Yum!'s engineering muscle.

## Governance & Security

On June 2, 2026, the White House issued an Executive Order titled "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security." Terence Ronson at Pertlink Limited (Hospitality Net, June 3) produced the sharpest translation of its implications for hotel operators. 
The Order is one of the clearest signals yet that AI has crossed a threshold from an experimental productivity tool to a critical layer of operational infrastructure that governments now treat as a matter of national security. The Order tasks the Treasury with forming an AI cybersecurity clearinghouse and extends AI-enabled cybersecurity tooling to operators of critical infrastructure.
 The implication for hotels: 
the Order creates a classified benchmarking process for "covered frontier models" and directs the Attorney General to prioritize criminal enforcement against anyone using AI to access computer systems unlawfully. One of the most overlooked shifts of the decade is the convergence of hospitality infrastructure with national infrastructure — large hotels, integrated resorts, and convention centers increasingly intersect with transportation, financial networks, and cross-border identity systems.


On the regulatory compliance side, DOJ's Data Security Program (effective April 2025) is creating specific exposure for hospitality AI. Bulk data thresholds that trigger compliance obligations are low enough that most hotel groups already meet them: precise geolocation data at ≥1,000 U.S. devices; biometric identifiers at ≥1,000 U.S. persons; personal financial data at ≥10,000 U.S. persons. AI systems in hotels — from facial recognition at check-in to footfall analytics and revenue management — aggregate data at volumes that cross these thresholds quickly. [Source: Hospitality Technology — paywalled but search snippet confirmed the thresholds and framework.]


A CISO benchmark study of 200+ security leaders in retail and hospitality found 71% identified AI as a primary concern, ahead of ransomware and phishing. IBM found that organizations using extensive AI security tools incur breach costs averaging $3.62M, compared with $5.52M without them. Shadow AI alone adds a $670K cost premium per breach.


Sources: [Hospitality Net / Terence Ronson](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/opinion/4132771/when-national-ai-security-becomes-a-hospitality-imperative) · [WitnessAI](https://witness.ai/blog/conversational-ai-hospitality/) · [Hospitality Technology](https://hospitalitytech.com/beyond-hipaa-navigating-dojs-new-bulk-data-thresholds-hospitality-ai)

**So what:** The White House EO and DOJ DSP together create a compliance surface that most hotel technology and legal teams have not yet mapped. The immediate priority is a vendor audit: which AI providers have offshore access to guest data, which systems aggregate biometric or geolocation data above the DOJ thresholds, and whether existing franchise and management contracts specify data ownership clearly enough to satisfy a federal compliance review.
