## Vendor Moves

The restaurant reservation market is being structurally redefined by three well-capitalised platforms converging on the same real estate: high-demand dining rooms.

**American Express** announced the proposed $700 million acquisition of TheFork from Tripadvisor on June 15, 2026. 
TheFork connects millions of diners with more than 50,000 restaurants across 11 European countries through its restaurant management, booking, and customer engagement platform. The transaction is expected to close before end of 2026, subject to regulatory approvals.
 
Together with Resy and Tock, these platforms are expected to expand American Express' dining network to 75,000 bookable venues.
 In a separate move, American Express integrated Resy into Anthropic's Claude platform. 
When a user asks Claude where to eat, Claude surfaces relevant restaurant recommendations; if they want to check availability or make a reservation, Claude can prompt them to connect to the Resy app within Claude to view available tables and be redirected to Resy.com or the mobile app to book.
 Resy CEO Pablo Rivero framed the combined roadmap in a February letter: 
"The unification of Resy and Tock venues — all on Resy — this summer represents more than a merger of platforms; we are creating a go-to destination for so many of the industry's most ambitious operators."
 
The merger will bring Resy's total venue count to about 25,000 and bring Tock's pre-paid booking and tiered experience features into the Resy platform.


**DoorDash** launched "Ask DoorDash" on June 11, a conversational AI chatbot allowing customers to order food, build grocery carts, and book restaurant reservations using text prompts, photos, or voice. 
The chatbot pulls from a combination of each user's ordering history and outside sources such as blog posts and social media reviews; it is available in select markets on iOS for restaurant search and grocery shopping, with reservations and broader U.S. availability rolling out in the coming weeks.
 
Early testing showed nearly half of all restaurant orders placed through the tool were from a place the customer had never ordered from before; carts assembled through the chatbot ran more than 35% higher in value than a standard grocery order, with users completing them five times faster. DoorDash co-founder Andy Fang said the product draws on AI models built by OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google, supplemented by open-source options.
 DoorDash also wound down its standalone AI discovery app Zesty in April, with the RTN analysis noting that 
the Zesty functionality is being repositioned within the core DoorDash platform, where it connects to SevenRooms reservation data and DoorDash's delivery transaction layer to influence decisions earlier in the funnel; this increases the degree to which restaurants rely on DoorDash to access and influence their customers.
 Separately, 
DoorDash intends to license the Ask DoorDash technology to outside partners — grocers, restaurants, and other retailers — who would deploy it under their own branding, sharpening its rivalry with Instacart.


**SevenRooms** (now DoorDash-owned) launched Channel Connect in June, described in the RTN ticker as bringing hotel-style distribution management to restaurants, further embedding SevenRooms into the multi-channel discovery stack.

**Google** expanded agentic restaurant booking via AI Mode to eight new countries in April 2026. 
Users in Australia, Canada, Hong Kong, India, New Zealand, Singapore, South Africa, and the UK can now book restaurant tables directly through Search without leaving the AI interface; the feature reaches users in eight new markets for the first time, without a Labs opt-in requirement.
 
Eight reservation platform partners power the feature: TheFork, SevenRooms, ResDiary, Mozrest, Foodhub, Dojo, DesignMyNight, and OpenTable — turning existing reservation infrastructure into a Google Search feature without additional integration work for restaurants already listed.
 
Google Trends data shows UK searches for "when to book a table" surged 140% in 2026.


Sources: [American Express Newsroom](https://www.americanexpress.com/en-us/newsroom/articles/travel-and-dining/american-express-announces-proposed-acquisition-of-thefork--a-le.html) | [CNBC / PYMNTS on DoorDash](https://www.pymnts.com/news/2026/doordash-debuts-conversational-ai-to-streamline-search/) | [RTN on Zesty](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/04/doordash-shuts-down-zesty-and-brings-restaurant-ai-discovery-into-core-app/) | [Winbuzzer / Google AI Mode](https://winbuzzer.com/2026/04/13/google-ai-mode-rolls-out-restaurant-booking-to-8-countries-xcxwbn/)

So what: The reservation layer is now controlled by three entities — American Express (Resy+Tock+TheFork: 75,000 venues across three continents), DoorDash (SevenRooms + direct booking + AI chatbot), and Booking Holdings (OpenTable). Google sits above all of them as an inbound discovery gateway. Mid-market independents need to audit which of these reservation rails they are connected to, because restaurants absent from AI Mode's booking partners and Resy's expanded network risk being functionally invisible to a growing proportion of high-intent diners.

## AI Visibility & Discovery

An acute visibility gap is opening in restaurant discovery as AI-mediated search replaces traditional Google SERP results for a fast-growing segment of diners.

**Uberall** released the industry's first benchmark measuring AI restaurant recommendation coverage in May 2026. 
The central finding: 83% of restaurant locations are entirely invisible in AI-generated recommendations. When a consumer asks ChatGPT "where can I get a good pizza near me tonight," only 17% of restaurants ever appear in the answer — despite 86% maintaining some presence on Google.
 The concentration is severe: 
the top three brands per QSR category capture 53.4% of total Share of Voice; in burger chains, the leader alone captures 10x the Share of Voice of the average brand.
 The discovery mode is also different from traditional search: 
informational and comparative prompts — "what's the healthiest breakfast I can grab on the go" or "which coffee chain has the best mobile rewards program" — drive nearly 79% of AI-generated restaurant responses; brands must win preference *before* the moment of decision.
 Platform ratings thresholds are punishing: 
ChatGPT primarily recommends businesses averaging 4.3 stars or higher, Perplexity 4.1+, and Gemini 3.9+ — a restaurant with a 4.0 average can still rank on Google but fall below the threshold AI platforms use to recommend.


**Meltwater** data confirms OTA dominance in AI-mediated hotel visibility: 
analysis of eight leading LLMs reveals that Booking.com and Expedia overwhelmingly lead AI-generated recommendations for European travel; OTA visibility is highly concentrated on LLMs, with model differences significant and consequential, and prompt framing producing different OTA rankings.


**Booking Holdings** / **OpenTable**: 
OpenTable launched an AI Concierge that initially answered diner questions and is now evolving into a broader discovery tool; when launching in July 2025, OpenTable said it answered 80% of diners' questions, reducing the need for phone calls to restaurants.


Sources: [Uberall / BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260507962493/en/83-of-Restaurants-Are-Invisible-in-AI-Search-New-Uberall-Report-Reveals-the-Discovery-Gap-Reshaping-the-Quick-Service-Restaurant-Industry) | [Meltwater GenAI Lens](https://www.meltwater.com/en/blog/online-travel-platform-ai-visibility-index) | [RTN on Google AI booking](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/04/google-introduces-ai-powered-restaurant-booking-as-search-evolves-into-action/)

So what: Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) is the new local SEO — and operators are roughly 2 years behind the curve. For multi-location QSR brands, the first mover advantage in AI visibility is compressing fast: Uberall's data suggests the top-three-per-category pattern is already consolidating. For independent restaurants, the minimum viable action is Google Business Profile hygiene, structured schema markup, and connection to at least one AI Mode reservation partner. For PE-backed groups, a GEO audit should be part of every portfolio company's 100-day plan.

## Operational AI

### Restaurant

The mature AI deployments in 2026 are invisible back-office tools, not robotic kitchens, and the most credible new entrants connect directly to existing POS data.

**Toast** made two significant moves in Q2. It launched Toast Drive-Thru in April, 
combining POS-native software, AI voice ordering integrations with partners like Incept AI, Speed of Service reporting, and Drive-Thru KDS enhancements — running the entire drive-thru operation through one integrated ecosystem rather than a patchwork of disconnected vendors for POS, menu boards, and audio systems.
 Toast President Steve Fredette stated: 
"AI is giving brands new opportunities to impact this equation. By seamlessly integrating POS, KDS, outdoor digital menu boards, and AI voice ordering partnerships, Toast Drive-Thru gives operators the tools they need to keep pace with peak demand today."
 On June 16, Toast announced Toast Lab, 
a new initiative combining funding and expertise to collaborate with a Boston-area restaurant operator, opening a new location together; the selected operator will receive early access to new Toast products, direct mentorship from Toast's executive leadership team, and collaborate directly alongside Toast's product teams as new tools are built and rolled out.
 Applications close July 14, 2026. Toast IQ, the company's conversational AI assistant, is also in active deployment: 
recent Toast research shows that over 83% of food and beverage retail operators plan to increase their use of AI tools in 2026.


**Restaurant365** reported operational AI evidence at scale: 
according to Restaurant365's recent AI Strategy Survey, 61% of restaurant operators say AI has reduced food costs and 62% report lower labor costs, demonstrating that AI is already delivering measurable value across restaurant operations.


**Saleem S. Khatri** (CEO, Lavu) wrote the sharpest agentwashing critique of the cycle in RTN: 
"A generic AI wrapper is not proprietary intelligence. A general-purpose language model doesn't know what a void spike at 3 PM on Bar 2 means, or why overtime being negatively correlated with revenue at half your locations should alarm you."
 His diagnostic checklist — *does it connect to all your data simultaneously, does it get smarter over time, does it tell you something you didn't already know* — is the most actionable operator heuristic published this cycle.

**GoTab** acquired Fishbowl this week (RTN ticker, July 2026) to bring guest marketing and loyalty deeper into restaurant commerce, continuing the platform consolidation theme. **inKind** secured $320 million in financing from Liberty Mutual (RTN, July 2026) to help restaurants access capital alongside its guest demand tools.

## Data & Integration

The Model Context Protocol is emerging as the genuine infrastructure answer to hospitality's N×M integration problem.

**Grevon's** MCP-based launch at HITEC is the most concrete hospitality deployment of the protocol to date. 
The architectural response taking shape in 2026 has two parts: first, an entity layer or governed master data management layer sitting above the PMS, holding the trusted version of guest, rate, loyalty, and supplier data; second, the Model Context Protocol, which Databricks describes as a way to solve the N×M integration problem so each AI agent doesn't need a custom connector to every other system.
 The same analysis frames the competitive imperative clearly: "The competitive edge in 2026 hospitality AI isn't model novelty. It's interoperability discipline. Buy access architecture before you buy features."

**OpenAI's Plaid partnership** (launched mid-May 2026 for ChatGPT Pro subscribers in the US) adds financial transaction infrastructure to ChatGPT for the first time. 
To date, ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini are positioning themselves as the new front door of the customer journey — inspiring travelers and guiding destination and property research — but none has yet crossed the threshold of the transaction itself, which implies financial liability and mastery of secure financial data flows. ChatGPT may have found the missing building block.
 No hotel booking use case has been announced, but the Plaid connection to 12,000+ financial institutions removes the core technical barrier.

**OpenAI** separately walked back its "Instant Checkout" ambitions inside ChatGPT in March 2026: 
OpenAI abandoned plans for enabling direct purchases within ChatGPT, instead prioritizing the platform's role in search and product discovery and leaving transaction processing to external apps — a strategic pivot that relieved competitive pressure on online travel agencies like Expedia and Booking Holdings, though the threat has only been delayed, not removed.


Sources: [alphacorp.ai](https://alphacorp.ai/blog/ai-for-hospitality-2026-what-actually-works-now) | [Hospitality-ON on OpenAI/Plaid](https://hospitality-on.com/en/distribution/partnering-plaid-openai-gives-itself-means-process-financial-transactions-inside) | [Skift on ChatGPT walkback](https://skift.com/2026/03/05/openai-chatgpt-checkout-walkback/)

So what: The OpenAI/Plaid move is the single most consequential infrastructure development for hospitality distribution in this cycle — not because it is deployed in travel yet, but because it removes the primary technical objection. Hotels and restaurant groups that have been waiting for "agentic commerce" to become real should treat this as a six-to-twelve-month signal to begin preparing structured, machine-readable rate/availability/inventory feeds now, or risk being unable to participate when AI agents can actually transact.

## Governance & Security

AI adoption in hotels is outpacing security controls, and AI content pipelines are generating accuracy problems that erode guest trust.

**Guardian / TripAdvisor investigation** (flagged in Hotel Dive's "What We're Reading," July 2026): AI summaries of TripAdvisor hotel reviews are systematically downplaying serious guest complaints — a finding that directly implicates every hotel that relies on AI-generated review responses or AI-summarised guest feedback as a source of operational intelligence.

**Alphacorp's hospitality AI analysis** frames the structural risk clearly: 
hospitality has become what one LinkedIn analysis calls a "cyber front desk" — the more third-party tools you connect, the larger your attack surface gets. Every vendor integration is a potential breach vector.
 HTN's ticker carried a piece titled "Why Hotel AI Adoption Is Moving Faster Than Security Controls and Increasing Risk Exposure" this week — the headline itself is the data point.

**Emotional AI / biometric sensing** in luxury properties raises consent issues that ESSEC's Metalab has flagged: reading facial expressions, voice tone, and biometric cues to infer mood has obvious personalization value and equally obvious ethical risk when guests have not explicitly consented.

Sources: [alphacorp.ai](https://alphacorp.ai/blog/ai-for-hospitality-2026-what-actually-works-now) | [Hotel Technology News (ticker)](https://hoteltechnologynews.com/2026/07/) | [Guardian via Hotel Dive](https://www.theguardian.com/business/2026/jul/02/ai-summaries-tripadvisor-hotel-reviews-downplay-serious-complaints)

So what: Any hotel or restaurant group that uses AI-generated review summaries for SOPs or guest intelligence needs an accuracy audit immediately. The TripAdvisor finding is a canary: if AI summaries are systematically suppressing severity in public reviews, the same distortion may be occurring in internal guest feedback loops. Governance here means human-in-the-loop review of AI content quality, not just AI content volume.
