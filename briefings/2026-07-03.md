## AI Visibility & Discovery

The funnel for finding restaurants and hotels is being rebuilt inside AI interfaces, and the pace of platform moves this cycle is the clearest evidence yet.


**DoorDash** launched Ask DoorDash on June 11, a new AI chatbot that lets users order food and groceries with text prompts and photos in its latest AI push. The chatbot, called "Ask DoorDash," allows users to search the app for what they're looking for in their own words instead of having to scroll through restaurants and stores to build a cart.
 
Users can describe a craving, upload a cookbook photo, or snap a picture of a grocery list, and the app builds a cart from the results. Early testing showed nearly half of all restaurant orders placed through the tool were from a place the customer had never ordered from before; carts assembled through the chatbot ran more than 35% higher in value than a standard grocery order, with users completing them five times faster through the regular interface. Co-founder Andy Fang said the product draws on AI models built by OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google, supplemented by open-source options.


**DoorDash** had already signalled the direction of travel in April. 
DoorDash wound down Zesty, its AI-powered restaurant discovery app, less than a year after its launch. In an email to users, DoorDash said that Zesty's core features — personalised restaurant recommendations and conversational search — will be integrated into the main DoorDash app. The standalone product went offline April 17.
 
DoorDash's existing delivery business provides it with a large base of transaction-level data, while SevenRooms extends its reach into dine-in behavior and guest preferences. Adding AI-driven discovery into that mix allows the company to influence decisions earlier in the funnel and guide users toward actions that remain within its ecosystem.


**Google** is further advanced than most operators realise. 
Google expanded its agentic restaurant reservation offering to more users, with agentic booking for restaurant reservations available on AI Mode in the U.S. without Labs opt-in.
 
Two weeks after launching agentic bookings in AI Mode to reserve restaurants, buy event tickets and make beauty and wellness appointments, Google confirmed it is developing similar capabilities for hotel and flight reservations, working with Booking.com, Expedia, Marriott International, IHG Hotels & Resorts, Choice Hotels International and Wyndham Hotels & Resorts. Google said specifics regarding the booking flow are still being worked out and it did not provide a timeline.
 Google VP Julie Farago told PhocusWire: "We're not going to rush this out the door because we want to make sure that it's a seamless experience and that people have all the control that they need and expect."

The **DoorDash 2026 Restaurant Industry Trends Report** provides the consumer data underpinning all of the above. 
22% of consumers have used an AI tool like ChatGPT or Google Gemini to help choose a restaurant, making AI a new discovery signal to watch.
 
Three-quarters of consumers say they're comfortable using AI for reservations, but only 28% of operators are using AI to manage calls and customer service. With an estimated 40% of reservation calls going unanswered industry-wide, AI gives restaurants another way to capture demand and keep front-of-house teams focused on the guests in front of them.
 
According to Yext research, restaurant listing sites like DoorDash account for more than 41% of the sources AI tools cite when recommending restaurants.


For hotel chains, the equivalent battle is playing out around AI search apps. 
Q2 2026 was dominated by chain-level distribution moves: Wyndham, Hilton, Marriott, and Accor each invested in AI-powered discovery layers, while Choice Hotels expanded its enterprise AI partnership with AWS.
 
None of the announcements meaningfully closed the property-level guest-facing AI gap, leaving room for dedicated third-party AI layers.


Sources: [DoorDash Ask DoorDash launch (TechCrunch)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/11/doordashs-new-ai-chatbot-lets-you-order-with-prompts-and-photos/), [DoorDash 2026 Trends Report](https://about.doordash.com/en-us/news/doordash-restaurant-industry-trends-report-2026), [Google agentic travel booking (PhocusWire)](https://www.phocuswire.com/google-agentic-travel-booking-ai)

So what: The "discovery funnel" is shifting from keyword search to conversational AI. Restaurants that don't actively structure their digital presence for LLM ingestion — clean schema, current menus, robust reviews — will become invisible in the fastest-growing discovery channel. For hotels, chains are building AI distribution layers that independents won't have access to; the answer is a separate GEO/AI-visibility strategy, not waiting for the PMS vendor.

## Vendor Moves

The reservation and dining technology market is consolidating fast, with American Express and DoorDash making the most consequential structural moves.


**American Express** proposed a $700 million acquisition of TheFork from Tripadvisor. The proposed transaction would add TheFork's network of more than 50,000 restaurants across 11 European countries to American Express' growing dining ecosystem. Combined with existing platforms Resy and Tock, the acquisition would expand American Express' network to approximately 75,000 bookable venues worldwide, creating one of the largest restaurant reservation and dining engagement platforms in the industry.
 
The acquisition of Tock in 2024 had already significantly broadened American Express' capabilities in reservations, ticketed events, prepaid dining experiences and hospitality operations. The proposed addition of TheFork extends that strategy internationally while strengthening the company's position in Europe.
 Separately, 
Resy is integrating 5,000 eateries, bars and wineries from Tock onto its platform this summer, bringing its total to about 25,000 venues. Resy CEO Pablo Rivero noted that American Express cardholders "spend close to $90 billion a year on dining."


**Toast** is executing the most comprehensive platform build in restaurant technology. 
By combining POS, payments, online ordering, delivery, kiosks, drive-thru technology, kitchen display systems, AI, guest engagement, marketing, payroll, scheduling, inventory, cost analytics, finance tools and commercial-grade hardware, Toast is building a broad operating platform for restaurants.
 
That direction became more visible with the launch of Toast IQ Grow, a marketing-focused solution introduced as part of Toast's Spring 2026 product release, described as more than 20 updates across marketing, payroll, inventory and operations. At the centre of the launch is an AI-powered Marketing Agent that can build audiences and automate campaigns across channels.
 
The company launched Toast Drive-Thru in 2026 as a unified solution for enterprise quick-service operations, bringing together hardware, POS-native software, AI voice ordering integrations and services.


**SevenRooms**, now owned by DoorDash following the $1.2 billion acquisition, launched Channel Connect to bring hotel-style distribution management to restaurants — a direct signal that the platform is evolving beyond its CRM roots. 
SevenRooms co-founder Joel Montaniel described the DoorDash integration: "Delivery and dine-in have typically been siloed data sets. So if a customer has ordered six times, and they're coming into the restaurant for the first time, are they a first-time customer or a seventh-time customer?"


**OpenTable** under **Booking Holdings** is also in product motion. 
OpenTable launched an AI Concierge that initially answered diner questions and is now evolving into a broader discovery tool. The platform is also developing capabilities to help OpenTable's restaurant partners streamline their operations and gain actionable insights.
 
Booking Holdings CEO Glenn Fogel said the company has partnered externally with OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and Amazon, and framed AI as a competitive moat: "We continue to believe AI-enabled productivity and efficiencies are an area of notable opportunity." Early testing of Priceline's Penny AI showed "a noticeable uplift from users who engage with Penny compared to non-Penny users."
 
Agoda found that its AI-assisted automation drove a double-digit year-over-year reduction in customer service costs per booking.


Sources: [American Express/TheFork acquisition (RTN)](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/06/american-express-expands-restaurant-technology-ambitions-with-700-million-thefork-acquisition/), [Toast platform overview (RTN)](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/06/toast-advances-restaurant-operations-with-connected-pos-ai-and-guest-engagement-technology/), [Booking Holdings Q1 2026 (PhocusWire)](https://www.phocuswire.com/news/online/booking-holdings-earnings-q1-2026)

So what: The reservation stack is consolidating around two dominant ecosystems — AmEx/Resy/Tock/TheFork globally and DoorDash/SevenRooms in the U.S. For mid-market operators, the question is no longer which reservation system to pick but which data ecosystem to anchor to. Switching costs are rising fast.

## Funding & M&A

The hospitality tech investment market crossed a significant threshold this cycle.


Hospitality technology startups raised more than $1 billion across 40 companies between April 2025 and March 2026, with property management systems and AI-led platforms attracting the largest share of investment, according to Abode Worldwide's Hospitality Tech Investment Index 2026.
 
PMS was the clear funding leader: seven PMS companies raised a combined $408.1 million, more than any other category in the report. The list includes Amenitiz, Arbio and Boom. That matters because PMS is increasingly becoming the control layer of the hospitality tech stack.
 
AI-led guest experience platforms were another standout category, with Duve, Chatlyn, Conduit and Canary Technologies raising a combined $152.6 million. These platforms are targeting one of the industry's biggest pressures: the need to deliver fast, personalised service with leaner teams.



Abode Worldwide CEO Jessica Gillingham said: "The companies attracting the strongest investor backing share a common trait. They sit close to essential operator workflows and become more valuable over time. Unified systems generate more data, better automation and higher switching costs. This compounding dynamic is proving more appealing to investors."


**Mews** dominated the PMS fundraising period. 
With 15,000 customers across 85 countries and a $2.5 billion valuation, Mews raised $300 million in January 2026 to develop advanced automation capabilities, and in February 2026 was named AAHOA's official PMS, representing roughly 60% of US hotels.


On the restaurant side, 
**Nesto**, founded in 2017 by engineers from the Karlsruhe Institute of Technology, raised $12 million in growth equity from Expedition Growth Capital. Its platform combines demand forecasting, shift planning, time tracking, and payroll into a single system, using AI to connect data points that are often managed separately.
 
The company reports productivity gains of more than 10%, reductions in planning time of up to 80%, and forecast accuracy approaching 90% under optimal conditions, across more than 3,000 locations.
 Separately, RTN's sidebar headlines on the same day as the Toast article reveal **Blink** raised $17 million and partnered with Shake Shack to advance frontline workforce technology; **inKind** secured $320 million in financing from Liberty Mutual; and **GoTab** acquired Fishbowl to bring guest marketing and loyalty deeper into restaurant commerce.

Sources: [Abode Worldwide Hospitality Tech Investment Index (Hotel Dive)](https://www.hoteldive.com/news/hospitality-tech-attracts-1b-in-funding-pms-ai-leaders/817439/), [Nesto $12M raise (RTN)](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/04/nesto-lands-12-million-to-bring-ai-driven-workforce-planning-into-the-core-of-restaurant-operations/)

So what: Investor capital is concentrating in platforms that own workflow data and generate switching costs — PMS at the hotel layer, and integrated restaurant management platforms at the F&B layer. Pure-play point solutions without clear data moats face a tougher funding environment in the next cycle.

## Operational AI

AI is moving from dashboard to action layer in restaurant operations, with real usage data now available.

**Toast** published its first substantive usage report on Toast IQ. 
In just 90 days, operators used Toast IQ to ask questions about their sales, labor, menus, guests, and growth opportunities across more than 125,000 restaurant locations. Fine dining locations used Toast IQ 29% more than fast-casual restaurants. Operators most frequently initiated conversations about sales and revenue (47% of restaurants using the product), menu and inventory (34%), and guest and marketing (32%).
 
The signal suggests restaurant AI is moving beyond experimentation and into everyday operations. Restaurants were "remarkably polite" with their AI assistant — 33,000 instances of "please" and "thank you."


**Restaurant365** survey data corroborates that real value is flowing through. 
According to Restaurant365's recent AI Strategy Survey, 61% of restaurant operators say AI has reduced food costs and 62% report lower labor costs, demonstrating that AI is already delivering measurable value across restaurant operations.
 
CPO Steve Demchuk said: "The conversation around AI has moved beyond experimentation and into measurable business outcomes. Restaurant operators want practical answers about what AI does well, where it falls short, and how to apply it responsibly to drive profitability."


For hotels, 
the Mews and Canary Technologies May 2026 industry survey put hotel AI adoption at roughly 98%, meaning nearly every property surveyed reported using AI somewhere in its operation, ending the 'should hotels adopt AI?' debate. The more interesting result is the survey's nuance: hoteliers strongly believe certain interactions should remain human-led, and they want AI to handle volume so staff can focus on the moments that matter.


Sources: [Toast IQ 90-day report (BusinessWire)](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260610036506/en/90-Days-with-Toast-IQ-How-Restaurant-Operators-Are-Using-Toast-IQ-to-Find-Time-Protect-Margins-and-Grow), [Restaurant365 Transform 2026 (PRNewswire)](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/restaurant365-announces-transform-2026-helping-restaurant-leaders-drive-profitability-in-the-age-of-ai-302813553.html)

So what: Fine dining's outsized adoption of Toast IQ (29% above fast-casual) is a counterintuitive signal worth watching. Premium operators — the ones with the most to protect in guest experience — are leaning hardest into AI for operational intelligence. If that pattern holds, the mid-market will follow within 12-18 months.

## Data & Integration

The single-data-source limitation is becoming the defining constraint on AI value in restaurant operations.


Lavu published a 2026 Restaurant POS AI Capabilities Report, a feature-by-feature comparison of seven major POS platforms, finding that most restaurant AI on the market today is built on a single data source — leaving multi-unit operators with serious blind spots in the areas where restaurants lose the most money. CEO Saleem Khatri said: "Everyone is building AI chat on top of their own POS data, and that is useful. But the problems that cost multi-unit operators the most money — the ones worth seven figures a year — are cross-system problems. You will not find them inside any single platform."


In hotels, the equivalent constraint is the PMS data model. 
Apptad's May 2026 analysis framed it directly: legacy PMS platforms have limited APIs, inconsistent schemas, and maintenance-heavy direct integrations. Bolt enough AI tools onto that foundation and you get duplicate guest records, broken workflows, and what the industry has started calling 'integration debt.' The architectural response taking shape in 2026 has two parts: first, an entity layer or governed master data management layer sitting above the PMS; second, the Model Context Protocol (MCP), which Databricks describes as a way to solve the N×M integration problem so each AI agent doesn't need a custom connector to every other system.



In 2026, Model Context Protocol (MCP) marks a turning point by connecting AI tools directly to live hotel systems and data. By adopting MCP early, hotels can surface real-time data in AI tools, strengthen direct booking strategies, and prepare for agent-driven bookings. MCP is no longer optional; it is foundational.


Sources: [Lavu POS AI Capabilities Report (GlobeNewswire)](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/03/03/3248661/0/en/Lavu-Publishes-2026-Restaurant-POS-AI-Capabilities-Report), [MCP for hoteliers (Revfine)](https://www.revfine.com/2026-is-the-year-of-mcp-what-hoteliers-need-to-know/)

So what: For multi-unit restaurant operators evaluating AI vendors, ask one question: does your AI connect POS, payroll, and scheduling simultaneously, or does it only read one of those? That distinction separates genuine operational intelligence from a chatbot bolted onto a POS dashboard. For hotels, MCP readiness is now a vendor evaluation criterion alongside uptime and integration count.

## Governance & Security

Two regulatory developments this cycle are directly shaping how hospitality operators must govern their AI deployments.


On June 2, 2026, the White House issued an Executive Order titled "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security." On its face, the Order is about federal systems, frontier-model security, and national cyber defense. Read carefully, it is one of the clearest signals that artificial intelligence has crossed a threshold — from an experimental productivity tool to a critical layer of operational infrastructure that governments now treat as a matter of national security.
 Hospitality technology analyst Terence Ronson (Pertlink Limited) argues the implications for hotels are direct: 
the Order tasks the Treasury with forming an AI cybersecurity clearinghouse, creates a classified benchmarking process for "covered frontier models," and directs criminal enforcement against anyone using AI to access or damage computer systems unlawfully. Critically, it explicitly states it does not impose mandatory licensing requirements for developing or releasing AI models.
 
The strategic call is: "Know where your AI already lives, govern it deliberately, and make trust a feature you can prove."


On data sovereignty, 
hospitality companies are accelerating adoption of AI — from dynamic pricing and biometric check-in to fraud detection and digital key platforms — creating fast-emerging compliance risk under the Department of Justice's Data Security Program rules. Hospitality companies routinely touch at least four categories of sensitive personal data (identifiers, geolocation, biometrics, and financial data). AI magnifies this exposure by aggregating and reusing data for training and inference across systems.


EU operators face an immediate deadline: 
EU AI Act Article 50 transparency requirements for chatbots apply from August 2, 2026. High-risk AI obligations under the Act apply from August 2026 to Annex III systems. Hospitality operators need to map their AI systems to these frameworks.


Sources: [White House EO hospitality implications (Hospitality Net)](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/opinion/4132771/when-national-ai-security-becomes-a-hospitality-imperative), [DOJ Bulk Data thresholds (Hospitality Technology)](https://hospitalitytech.com/beyond-hipaa-navigating-dojs-new-bulk-data-thresholds-hospitality-ai)

So what: August 2, 2026 is a hard date for EU-facing operators with AI chatbots in production. Any deployment that collects guest data or makes automated recommendations needs a disclosed AI policy before that date. For U.S. operators, the White House EO's cybersecurity clearinghouse signals that vendor scrutiny on AI security is about to intensify — run a vendor AI governance audit now before procurement pressure makes it reactive.

## WHO TO WATCH

**Andy Fang (Co-founder, DoorDash)** — His statement that "more options shouldn't mean more work" is the clearest articulation of why conversational AI matters for discovery-to-order platforms. The real test is whether Ask DoorDash's early metrics (49% of orders from never-tried restaurants, 35%+ cart uplift) hold at scale — if they do, DoorDash has built a structural advantage over OpenTable and Resy that no reservation-only platform can replicate.

**Glenn Fogel (CEO, Booking Holdings)** — His framing of AI as a moat against "generic horizontal agents" is the most strategically honest thing said by a public-company CEO in this cycle. "Google knows everything about you — they don't know what I can offer because you're a Genius Level 3" is the competitive argument for loyalty-data-driven AI in a nutshell. Whether Booking's loyalty graph actually outperforms Google's intent graph is the defining question for OTA distribution over the next three years.

**Pablo Rivero (CEO, Resy / American Express Global Dining)** — With Tock absorbing into Resy this summer and the proposed TheFork addition creating a 75,000-venue global network, Rivero is running the most ambitious restaurant reservation integration project in the industry. The AI play here — connecting AmEx cardholder spending data to reservation behavior across three platforms — is potentially more valuable than any of the individual platforms alone. Execution risk is high; worth tracking whether the data integration delivers or creates a fragmented UX.

**Steve Fredette (President & Co-Founder, Toast)** — Toast's simultaneous moves into Drive-Thru, AI-powered marketing (Toast IQ Grow), and consumer discovery (Toast Local) represent a deliberate attempt to own both sides of the restaurant transaction loop. Fredette's public statement that "AI saves time, and in this industry, speed of analysing data is real money" is borne out by the Q1 IQ usage data. The question is whether a platform that started as a POS can credibly compete with DoorDash for the consumer discovery and reservation layer.

**Terence Ronson (Founder, Pertlink Limited)** — His June 3 white paper translating the White House AI Executive Order into hospitality consequences is the most practically useful governance document published this cycle for hotel operators. His core argument — that the competitive question is shifting from "how many AI features do we have?" to "can we govern AI responsibly and turn operational trust into guest experience?" — is exactly the framing that PE-backed hotel SaaS companies should be putting in front of their boards.
