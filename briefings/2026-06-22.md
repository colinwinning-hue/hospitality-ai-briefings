## AI VISIBILITY & DISCOVERY

The dominant story of this cycle is the acceleration of conversational AI into the booking and discovery stack, with DoorDash, Google, OpenAI, and American Express each making distinct moves that collectively reshape how diners find and transact with restaurants.

**DoorDash** launched Ask DoorDash on June 11, the most consequential single product announcement of this cycle. 
The chatbot — called "Ask DoorDash" — allows users to search the app for what they're looking for in their own words instead of scrolling through restaurants and stores to build a cart. Users can describe a craving, share a recipe link or photo, or describe the reservation they're looking for. It is rolling out on iOS in select regions for restaurant search and grocery shopping, and within DoorDash Reservations, with broader U.S. availability in the coming weeks.
 The operator quote: 
"We've spent over a decade building an app that puts everything in your city at your fingertips, but more options shouldn't mean more work," said Andy Fang, Co-founder of DoorDash.
 Early results reported by Bloomberg are commercially meaningful: 
nearly half of all restaurant orders placed through the tool were from a place the customer had never ordered from before; carts assembled through the chatbot ran more than 35% higher in value than a standard grocery order, with users completing them five times faster than through the regular app interface.
 Critically, DoorDash is not keeping the technology to itself: 
DoorDash intends to license the technology to outside partners — grocers, restaurants, and other retailers — who would deploy it under their own branding, sharpening its rivalry with Instacart as that company expands its own enterprise-focused AI business. The chatbot rollout arrives as DoorDash works to unify the technology underpinning its portfolio, which has grown to include SevenRooms, acquired for $1.2 billion, and Deliveroo, bought for close to $4 billion.
 The model stack: 
it draws on AI models built by OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google, supplemented by open-source options.


**Google** has moved its agentic restaurant booking from UK pilot to U.S. general availability (no Labs opt-in required), and is now developing hotel and flight booking in AI Mode. 
Two weeks after launching agentic bookings in AI Mode to reserve restaurants, buy event tickets and make beauty and wellness appointments, Google confirmed it is developing similar capabilities for hotel and flight reservations, working with partners including Booking.com, Expedia, Marriott International, IHG Hotels & Resorts, Choice Hotels International and Wyndham Hotels & Resorts.
 Google's VP of engineering: 
"We're not going to rush this out the door because we want to make sure that it's a seamless experience and that people have all the control that they need and expect."
 Critically, 
Google confirmed that partner companies will manage transactions and service bookings made in AI Mode — Google will not serve as a merchant of record.


**OpenAI** moved in the opposite direction in March. 
OpenAI apparently no longer wants to handle commerce payments, cancellations, refunds, customer complaints and travel bookings. ChatGPT will redirect shoppers to merchant apps when they want to make purchases. OpenAI's strategy had originally allowed users to buy products and book hotels in the chatbot; now analysts are calling it a gateway to recommendations, competing with Google Gemini, rather than a tool that processes purchases.
 The visibility gap matters for restaurants: 
about 83% of restaurants are invisible on ChatGPT, according to LocalFalcon, which monitors visibility for engines such as Google, ChatGPT, Apple Maps and others.


**American Express** embedded Resy into Claude via an Anthropic partnership. 
Claude users can now explore U.S. Resy restaurants directly in their conversations when deciding where to eat — Claude connects to Resy to help diners browse available tables in real time and secure a table seamlessly.


DoorDash's own consumer research adds quantitative grounding: 
22% of consumers have used an AI tool like ChatGPT or Google Gemini to help choose a restaurant. Restaurant listing sites like DoorDash account for more than 41% of the sources AI tools cite when recommending restaurants. Operators are responding by updating menu information (39%), managing reviews (34%), and improving photo quality (32%).
 The operator gap is stark: 
three-quarters of consumers say they're comfortable using AI for reservations, but only 28% of operators are using AI to manage calls and customer service. An estimated 40% of reservation calls go unanswered industry-wide.


Sources: [DoorDash (official)](https://about.doordash.com/en-us/news/ask-doordash) | [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/11/doordash-ai-ordering-automation.html) | [PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/google-agentic-travel-booking-ai) | [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/413293/openai-retreating-from-agentic-commerce.html) | [American Express](https://www.americanexpress.com/en-us/newsroom/articles/travel-and-dining/american-express-partners-with-anthropic-to-make-u-s--resy-resta.html)

**So what:** Restaurants that do not have structured, machine-readable content on DoorDash, Yelp, and Google will be systematically bypassed by conversational AI discovery. The 83% ChatGPT invisibility figure is an operational problem, not a marketing one — and it is solvable with metadata discipline, not ad spend.

## VENDOR MOVES

The reservation and dining platform landscape is consolidating at speed, driven by American Express and DoorDash on opposite sides of the Atlantic.

**American Express** proposed a $700 million acquisition of TheFork from Tripadvisor (announced June 15). 
The proposed transaction would add TheFork's network of more than 50,000 restaurants across 11 European countries to American Express' growing dining ecosystem. Combined with existing platforms Resy and Tock, the acquisition would expand American Express' network to approximately 75,000 bookable venues worldwide, creating one of the largest restaurant reservation and dining engagement platforms in the industry.
 
American Express first entered the reservation technology market through its acquisition of Resy in 2019; the acquisition of Tock in 2024 significantly broadened its capabilities; the proposed addition of TheFork would extend that strategy internationally while strengthening the company's position in Europe, one of the world's largest restaurant markets.


**Resy/Tock brand merger** is proceeding this summer: 
the move boosts Resy's venue count to 25,000, but will sunset the Tock brand, formally uniting two onetime startups against reservations market leader OpenTable. In Q4 2025, spending by American Express card members at Resy restaurants was up more than 20% versus 9% for all global restaurant spend by Amex members.


**SevenRooms** launched Channel Connect this week — a distribution management tool bringing hotel-style channel management to restaurants. [PAYWALLED/BLOCKED — source: RTN sidebar, no article fetched.] SevenRooms is now operating as part of DoorDash post-acquisition, adding distribution integration to its CRM capabilities.

**Booking Holdings / OpenTable** framed its AI strategy explicitly as a moat against generalist models. 
Booking Holdings is positioning its AI efforts as a moat against general AI models. Early testing showed lifts in user conversion, reduced customer service interaction and other positive metrics across brands.
 
OpenTable launched an AI Concierge that initially answered diner questions and is now evolving into a broader discovery tool; the platform is also developing capabilities to help OpenTable's restaurant partners streamline their operations and gain actionable insights.
 On performance: 
when launching its AI assistant in July 2025, OpenTable said it answered 80% of diners' questions, reducing the need for phone calls to restaurants. Agoda found that its AI-assisted automation drove a double-digit year-over-year reduction in customer service costs per booking.


**Toast** launched Toast Lab on June 16. 
The POS provider launched Toast Lab, an incubator that will provide funding and tech for a new restaurant in Boston that it will help operate, allowing Toast to pressure-test new technology.
 Applications run through July 14. 
Kelly Esten, Toast CMO: "Toast Lab is a formal return to that instinct, inspired by our belief that the best restaurant technology gets built when operators and product teams are working side-by-side to create something new together."


Sources: [Restaurant Technology News](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/06/american-express-expands-restaurant-technology-ambitions-with-700-million-thefork-acquisition/) | [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91496951/amex-resy-tock-restaurant-reservation-wars) | [PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/news/online/booking-holdings-earnings-q1-2026) | [Food on Demand](https://foodondemand.com/06172026/toast-seeks-restaurant-operator-to-test-tech-open-restaurant/)

**So what:** The reservation stack is bifurcating into two global ecosystems: AmEx (Resy+Tock+TheFork, 75,000 venues, cardmember spending moat) versus DoorDash (SevenRooms+Deliveroo, delivery/dine-in data flywheel). Independent operators need to decide which ecosystem gives them more data ownership — and price that against discovery volume.

## OPERATIONAL AI

The restaurant POS layer is moving from reactive chatbots to proactive agentic systems that act without being asked.

**PAR Technology and Square** both launched agentic tools in the same week in April, signalling a market inflection. 
PAR Technology and Square unveiled AI products that can assist operators with their business and even complete tasks on their behalf, joining Toast and Thanx which have launched similar tools over the past year.
 PAR's Intelligence system includes an Insights Agent, Offers Agent, and Developer Assist Agent tied directly to its POS. 
Taco Bell franchisee Charter Foods began testing one of PAR's agents last year and asked it to identify stores that could benefit by staying open later — late-night sales increased 20% as a result.
 PAR's stated framing: "PAR Intelligence is designed to make your best people even better — faster decisions, clearer priorities, less time in dashboards and spreadsheets." 
Square's Managerbot is proactive: it surfaces insights and metrics the restaurant needs to know, and can perform tasks such as drafting employee schedules, creating and running email campaigns, and generating purchase orders.


**Toast IQ** usage data from Q1 2026 shows rapid adoption. 
Toast IQ launched in October 2025. Over 125,000 U.S. restaurant locations used Toast IQ in Q1 2026. Fine dining used Toast IQ 29% more than fast-casual. The top categories: sales and revenue (47% of restaurants), menu and inventory (34%), and guest and marketing (32%). The single most popular prompt: "Create a short, easy-to-read daily briefing for my restaurant."


**Shake Shack Project Catalyst** — the chain's integrated technology initiative targeting 1,500 locations — embeds AI as an operating layer rather than a feature. 
At the centre is a partnership with Qu, a cloud-native unified commerce platform, to upgrade POS and kitchen orchestration. Beyond operations, Shake Shack is developing its first loyalty platform integrated across POS and digital channels.
 
Rather than treating AI as a standalone innovation, the company is positioning it as an operating layer that sits across systems and processes, moving from experimental use cases to more practical, operational applications.


**Agentic Hospitality** launched the TravelOS MCP Server — an infrastructure layer connecting hotel CRS/PMS directly to AI platforms via the Model Context Protocol. 
The platform enables hotels to participate directly in an AI environment without duplicating inventory, scraping rates or creating separate booking systems. It supports conversational search, structured room selection, loyalty-aware rate personalization and policy transparency.
 Brad Brewer, Chief AI Officer: "Hotels have invested heavily in the systems that run their operations. Our approach is simple. We extend those systems into AI platforms rather than replacing them. Hotels keep their pricing control, inventory authority and direct relationship with the guest."

Sources: [Nation's Restaurant News](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology/the-hot-new-restaurant-tech-trend-ai-agents) | [Rutland Herald / Toast](https://www.rutlandherald.com/news/business/90-days-with-toast-iq-how-restaurant-operators-are-using-toast-iq-to-find-time/article_d512dcaa-62be-5e85-9d30-78ad13d724ca.html) | [Restaurant Technology News](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/04/shake-shack-unveils-restaurant-technology-initiative-focused-on-ai-loyalty-and-unified-commerce/) | [Hotel Management](https://www.hotelmanagement.net/tech/agentic-hospitality-launches-infrastructure-connect-hotels-ai-booking-platforms)

**So what:** The line between "AI assistant" and "AI agent" is now commercially meaningful — PAR's 20% late-night sales lift is the clearest operator-verified outcome this cycle. For mid-market multi-unit operators, the Lavu report finding that most restaurant AI is built on single POS data alone (missing payroll and scheduling cross-system problems worth "seven figures a year") is worth stress-testing against your current stack.

## FUNDING & M&A


Forty global hospitality technology startups raised more than $1 billion in the past year, with property management systems and artificial intelligence-led platforms attracting the most investment, according to Abode Worldwide's Hospitality Tech Investment Index 2026.
 
The shift was most visible in a 90-day burst between December 2025 and February 2026: Mews ($300 million), Kindred ($125 million across two simultaneous rounds), and Limehome (€75 million). Seven PMS companies raised a combined $408.1 million, more than any other category.
 
AI-led guest experience platforms were a standout category — Duve, Chatlyn, Conduit, and Canary Technologies raising a combined $152.6 million. Canary notably acquired OpenKey in February to expand its access to door lock providers.
 
Jessica Gillingham, CEO of Abode Worldwide: "The companies attracting the strongest investor backing share a common trait. They sit close to essential operator workflows and become more valuable over time. Unified systems generate more data, better automation and higher switching costs."


The AmEx $700M TheFork deal (see Vendor Moves) is the largest disclosed restaurant tech transaction of this cycle.


Mews' acquisitions of Flexkeeping and DataChat in late 2025
 illustrate the PMS-as-platform strategy: acquiring operational and analytics tools to reduce third-party integration dependency.

Sources: [Hotel Dive](https://www.hoteldive.com/news/hospitality-tech-attracts-1b-in-funding-pms-ai-leaders/817439/) | [Hotel News Resource](https://www.hotelnewsresource.com/article140878.html) | [Restaurant Technology News](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/06/american-express-expands-restaurant-technology-ambitions-with-700-million-thefork-acquisition/)

**So what:** Investor logic is converging on the same thesis: unified systems compound in value. PE-backed SaaS buyers should apply Gillingham's "close to essential workflows" test. A PMS that consolidates data, automates operations, and generates the data foundation for AI is now more defensible than a best-of-breed point solution.

## GOVERNANCE & SECURITY


On June 2, 2026, the White House issued an Executive Order titled "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security." Terence Ronson of Pertlink Limited, writing in Hospitality Net, translates the Order into practical hotel priorities: "Treat this Order as an early-warning system, not a foreign-policy footnote. The smartest move available to any hotel, vendor or integrator today is unglamorous: know where your AI already lives, govern it deliberately, and make trust a feature you can prove."



The paper argues the competitive question for hospitality is shifting from "how many AI features do we have?" to "can we govern AI responsibly, secure our connected systems, and turn operational trust into guest experience?" A modern hotel is a digitally orchestrated environment, a data-rich ecosystem, an operational-intelligence platform, and increasingly an autonomous service infrastructure — the same intelligence that makes a property efficient also makes it exposed.


The EO has specific downstream implications for hotel vendor procurement: 
the average cost of a data breach in hospitality was $3.86 million in 2024.
 The AI-specific threat vector is escalating: 
malicious actors are increasingly using AI to craft sophisticated, contextually relevant messages that closely mimic internal communication styles — in the hospitality industry, which relies immensely on emails for everything from coordinating bookings to guest communications, this evolution significantly heightens the risk of social engineering.


Sources: [Hospitality Net](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/opinion/4132771/when-national-ai-security-becomes-a-hospitality-imperative) | [Hotel Business](https://hotelbusiness.com/the-year-ahead-2026-cybersecurity-predictions-for-the-hotel-industry/)

**So what:** The EO is not a compliance mandate — it creates soft procurement pressure. Hotels and vendors that cannot answer "where does your AI live, who governs it, and what is the rollback if an agent fails?" will lose enterprise and flagged-brand deals as insurance underwriters and procurement officers align with the EO's framework.
