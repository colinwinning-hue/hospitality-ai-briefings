## Funding & M&A

American Express is assembling the most consequential dining-technology empire the industry has seen.

**American Express** announced a proposed $700 million acquisition of **TheFork** from Tripadvisor on June 14 — its third major dining-platform acquisition after Resy (2019) and Tock (2024). 
Under the terms, Amex will acquire TheFork from Tripadvisor for $700 million in cash. TheFork connects millions of diners with more than 50,000 restaurants across 11 European countries; combined with Resy and Tock, the deal expands American Express' dining network to approximately 75,000 bookable venues.
 
The sale follows Tripadvisor's February 2026 announcement that it was exploring strategic alternatives for TheFork, marking the end of its more than decade-long ownership, and allowing Tripadvisor to sharpen its focus on its growing Experiences business.
 Critically for operators, 
reservation platforms have evolved into comprehensive customer acquisition, guest engagement and revenue optimization systems. Every reservation generates valuable information about dining preferences, spending habits, visit frequency and customer lifetime value.


**Resy + Tock merger** (announced February 24, completing summer 2026) reshapes the North American fine-dining stack. 
American Express is combining its two reservation platforms into one under Resy this summer, bringing Tock's inventory of fine-dining restaurants and wineries to Resy for the first time. The move will approximately double Resy's library to more than 25,000 venues, creating a single platform to compete against market leader OpenTable, which lists more than 60,000 restaurants worldwide.
 Performance data is already significant: 
in the fourth quarter of 2025, global restaurant spend by American Express card members was up 9% overall, but spending by American Express card members at Resy restaurants was up more than 20%.


**DoorDash** closed its $1.2 billion acquisition of **SevenRooms** in 2025, reshaping the reservation wars. 
DoorDash announced its $1.2 billion acquisition of SevenRooms, a reservation platform focused on direct bookings through a restaurant's own website. As it enters the bookings game, DoorDash is looking to capture the range of dining possibilities, whether it's delivery, takeout or table. "Delivery and dine-in have typically been siloed data sets," SevenRooms co-founder Joel Montaniel said. "So if a customer has ordered six times, and they're coming into the restaurant for the first time, are they a first-time customer or a seventh-time customer?"


In the hotel sector, **hospitality tech startups** crossed the $1 billion investment milestone. 
Hospitality technology startups raised more than $1 billion across 40 companies between April 2025 and March 2026, with property management systems (PMS) and AI-led platforms attracting the largest share of investment. That shift was most visible in a 90-day burst between December 2025 and February 2026, when the sector's three biggest raises landed almost back-to-back: Mews ($300 million), Kindred ($125 million across two simultaneous rounds) and Limehome (€75 million).
 
AI-led guest experience platforms were another standout category, with Duve, Chatlyn, Conduit and Canary Technologies raising a combined $152.6 million, targeting the need to deliver fast, personalised service with leaner teams.


Sources: [BusinessWire – Amex/TheFork](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260614939103/en/American-Express-Announces-Proposed-Acquisition-of-TheFork-a-Leading-European-Restaurant-Booking-Platform) | [Fast Company – Resy/Tock](https://www.fastcompany.com/91496951/amex-resy-tock-restaurant-reservation-wars) | [Hotel Dive / Hospitalitynet – $1B Index](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/report/4131877/hospitality-tech-drew-over-1-billion-in-the-last-year-with-pms-and-ai-led-platforms-capturing-biggest-share)

**So what:** The dining reservation layer is now being contested by three financial giants — American Express, DoorDash, and Chase/Visa via OpenTable. For restaurant operators, this means more leverage in platform negotiations and a genuine financial incentive to choose the platform whose card members actually spend more. For PE-backed hospitality SaaS, watch for Amex to apply its Resy playbook (API-first, loyalty-integrated) to TheFork's European restaurants.

## AI Visibility & Discovery

The battle for where a guest first encounters your property has decisively moved to conversational AI.

**DoorDash** launched "Ask DoorDash" on June 11 — a conversational AI chatbot for food ordering, grocery shopping, and restaurant reservations. 
DoorDash announced it's launching a new AI chatbot that lets users order food and groceries with text prompts and photos. The chatbot allows users to search the app for what they're looking for in their own words instead of having to scroll through restaurants and stores to build a cart. In February, Uber Eats launched an AI-powered "Cart Assistant," while Instacart has rolled out an AI shopping assistant; DoorDash's app can build a grocery cart based on a photo from a cookbook, a picture of a grocery list, or a recipe.
 Results in limited iOS rollout are notable: 
DoorDash co-founder Andy Fang said that during its limited rollout to select iOS users, nearly half of takeout orders via the tool were made by people who had never ordered from that restaurant before.
 
Grocery baskets built with the tool are more than 35% larger than typical grocery orders. Fang suggested the company is ready to sell its AI technology, including the chatbot, for corporate customers to use under their own brands.
 
Ask DoorDash uses a combination of AI models from OpenAI, Anthropic and Google, as well as some open-source models to help reduce costs.


**IHG Hotels & Resorts** launched an IHG app in ChatGPT on June 3. 
As people rapidly turn to AI to plan trips — now used by more than half of U.S. travelers — IHG has launched an IHG app in ChatGPT and will soon add AI-powered conversational search within IHG.com and the IHG One Rewards app. IHG's Chief Product & Technology Officer Jolie Fleming said: "Guest expectations are evolving quickly, and we're evolving with them. With more than one million guests staying with us every night around the world, we're using AI to make the travel experience more seamless from planning and booking through to their stay."


**American Express / Resy** partnered with Anthropic in April to make U.S. Resy restaurants discoverable inside Claude. 
Claude users can now explore U.S. Resy restaurants directly in their conversations when deciding where to eat — Claude connects to Resy to help diners browse available tables in real time and secure a table seamlessly. The partnership reflects American Express's focus on creating AI-powered experiences for Card Members and embedding its unique assets, like Resy, into leading AI platforms.
 Resy also plans 
an upgraded set of APIs for third-party partners, including Gen AI platforms, exposing Resy's reservation inventory in a diverse set of digital channels to drive discovery and demand for venues.


**Google AI Mode** is developing full hotel and flight agentic booking, building on restaurant reservation capabilities already live without Labs opt-in. 
Two weeks after launching agentic bookings in AI Mode to reserve restaurants, buy event tickets and make beauty and wellness appointments, Google confirmed it is developing similar capabilities for hotel and flight reservations, working on building out the experience with industry partners such as Booking.com, Expedia, Marriott International, IHG Hotels & Resorts, Choice Hotels International and Wyndham Hotels & Resorts. "We're not going to rush this out the door because we want to make sure that it's a seamless experience," said Julie Farago, Google's VP of engineering for travel and local search.


**OpenAI's** direct transaction ambitions quietly collapsed in March, with shares of Expedia and Booking Holdings rising on the news. 
OpenAI is shifting its shopping strategy to focus on discovery and research, leaving checkout to third-party app developers. This strategic pivot has relieved concerns for online travel agencies like Expedia and Booking Holdings, as AI-driven checkout was seen as a potential threat to their business models.


Sources: [TechCrunch – Ask DoorDash](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/11/doordashs-new-ai-chatbot-lets-you-order-with-prompts-and-photos/) | [IHG press release](https://www.ihgplc.com/en/news-and-media/news-releases/2026/ihg-hotels-and-resorts-advances-conversational-ai-search-capabilities) | [Skift – OpenAI walkback](https://skift.com/2026/03/05/openai-chatgpt-checkout-walkback/) | [PhocusWire – Google AI Mode](https://www.phocuswire.com/google-agentic-travel-booking-ai)

**So what:** Any hotel or restaurant not actively managing its Generative Engine Optimisation (GEO) is already ceding discovery to competitors who are. DoorDash's data that 47% of Ask DoorDash orders go to previously unvisited venues is the clearest signal yet that AI recommendation engines are driving genuine incremental traffic — not just repurchases. Operators without structured, AI-parseable content (menus, descriptions, availability APIs) won't surface.

## Operational AI

The agentic layer is arriving in restaurant operations — with real usage data, not just promises.

**Toast IQ** published its first 90-day usage report on June 10, covering 125,000 U.S. restaurant locations. 
Fine dining locations used Toast IQ 29% more than fast-casual restaurants. Operators most frequently initiated conversations about sales and revenue (47% of restaurants), menu and inventory (34%), and guest and marketing (32%). Restaurants are moving from AI curiosity to AI-powered operations with millions of threads from Toast IQ in Q1 2026.
 The top prompt was: *"Create a short, easy-to-read daily briefing for my restaurant."* This is genuine signal — operators are treating Toast IQ as a management co-pilot, not a toy.

**Toast** also launched **Toast Drive-Thru** in April — a unified hardware/software/AI voice ordering platform for enterprise QSR. 
Toast Drive-Thru is an enterprise-grade solution designed to help modern quick-service restaurants increase throughput and order accuracy at scale. The solution includes AI voice ordering integrations with partners like Incept AI to automate order taking, Speed of Service (SoS) reporting to help identify lane bottlenecks, and Drive-Thru KDS enhancements.
 Steve Fredette, President and Co-Founder of Toast, said: 
"AI is giving brands new opportunities to impact this equation. By seamlessly integrating POS, KDS, outdoor digital menu boards, and AI voice ordering partnerships, Toast Drive-Thru gives operators the tools they need to keep pace with peak demand today, and the flexible foundation required to adopt new innovations in the future."


**PAR Technology** and **Square** both unveiled agentic AI products in April. 
PAR Technology and Square unveiled AI products that can assist operators and even complete tasks on their behalf, joining Toast and Thanx that have launched similar tools over the past year. Square's Managerbot is proactive — surfacing insights, drafting employee schedules, creating and running email campaigns, and generating purchase orders for supplies.
 Results from the field: 
Taco Bell franchisee Charter Foods began testing PAR's Insights Agent and asked it to identify stores that could benefit by staying open later. Late-night sales increased 20% as a result of its recommendations.
 Charter Senior Director John Rankin said: 
"In this industry, speed of analyzing data is real money."


**Booking Holdings** reported its AI initiatives are showing measurable operational ROI. 
When launching its OpenTable AI Concierge in July 2025, OpenTable said it answered 80% of diners' questions, reducing the need for phone calls to restaurants. Agoda found that its AI-assisted automation drove a double-digit year-over-year reduction in customer service costs per booking. CEO Glenn Fogel said Booking Holdings is also partnering with OpenAI, Google, Anthropic and Amazon to position itself wherever AI-driven travel demand appears.


Sources: [BusinessWire – Toast IQ 90 Days](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260610036506/en/90-Days-with-Toast-IQ) | [BusinessWire – Toast Drive-Thru](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260414359751/en/Toast-Launches-Unified-Drive-Thru-Solution) | [Nation's Restaurant News – PAR/Square agents](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology/the-hot-new-restaurant-tech-trend-ai-agents) | [PhocusWire – Booking Holdings Q1](https://www.phocuswire.com/news/online/booking-holdings-earnings-q1-2026)

**So what:** Toast IQ's 90-day data is the most important operator-facing AI benchmark of this news cycle — it proves that AI assistants embedded in the POS are used daily, not occasionally. The Charter Foods / PAR case (20% late-night revenue lift attributable to an AI recommendation) is the type of attributable ROI evidence that justifies tech-stack investment conversations at the board level.

## Data & Integration

The PMS and POS are consolidating into control layers — and the vendors who own that position are pulling away.

**Mews** exemplifies the acquisition-as-integration strategy. 
Mews' acquisitions of Flexkeeping and DataChat in late 2025 point to the broader pattern: the best-funded PMS businesses are building platforms through acquisition rather than integration dependency.
 
Jessica Gillingham, CEO of Abode Worldwide, said: "Investors are concentrating capital in the platforms that hospitality businesses increasingly depend on every day, especially PMS and AI-led systems that unify operations, improve automation and strengthen the data layer underneath the business."


**Resy + Toast integration** is the most operationally significant data-plumbing move in the restaurant sector this cycle. 
Resy and Toast plan to launch an integration connecting Toast's Digital Chits feature to Resy's guestbook, allowing Resy to surface information like guest notes for that restaurant directly on Toast POS and handhelds during service. Resy also announced the next phase of its dining platform linking AI-powered discovery and booking, table and guestbook management, ticketing and transactions, check-level point of sale data, customer relationship management, and American Express membership.
 Resy CEO Pablo Rivero framed the vision: 
"No more whispering to the server at the host stand that there's a birthday at the table — the server will already know and have a candle ready."


**Lavu's** independent POS AI benchmark (March) documented a structural problem: 
its 2026 Restaurant POS AI Capabilities Report, a feature-by-feature comparison across seven widely deployed point-of-sale platforms, found that most restaurant AI on the market today is built on a single data source. Several major POS providers offer AI assistants that answer plain-English questions about their own POS data, but they are limited to their own platform's data — they cannot pull in external payroll or scheduling systems, where the most expensive problems for multi-unit operators actually live.


Sources: [Hospitalitynet – Abode Worldwide Index](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/report/4131877/) | [Amex/Resy press release](https://www.americanexpress.com/en-us/newsroom/articles/travel-and-dining/resy-announces-next-phase-of-its-reservation-and-dining-platform.html) | [GlobeNewswire – Lavu POS Report](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/03/03/3248661/0/en/Lavu-Publishes-2026-Restaurant-POS-AI-Capabilities-Report.html)

**So what:** The Resy+Toast integration is the first time a major reservation CRM and a dominant POS have committed to sharing check-level guest data in real time. This is the data foundation that makes genuine personalization at scale possible. Multi-unit operators still running AI on POS data alone are missing the highest-value signals — cross-system payroll, scheduling, and delivery reconciliation data.
