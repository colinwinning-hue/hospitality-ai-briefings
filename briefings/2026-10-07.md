## Vendor Moves

This window's vendor action is dominated by two product launches from watchlist names and one genuine infrastructure reveal.

**Booking.com** disclosed AgentHub, an internal platform it has built to construct and iterate on modular AI agents. 
PhocusWire reported October 5 that Booking.com said AgentHub's configuration-driven setups and shared infrastructure allow teams to build and iterate on agents faster.
 This is not a consumer-facing feature — it is internal AI development infrastructure. The disclosure matters because it signals Booking Holdings is building agent capability in-house rather than buying or partnering, which implies proprietary data advantages as agents mature. No commercial rollout date or outcome data has been released.

**Toast** launched Toast IQ Team and expanded Toast IQ Grow on October 6. 
The BusinessWire release introduced Toast IQ Team as a new labor management solution for restaurant and retail operators, alongside a significant expansion of Toast IQ Grow, described as the complete marketing solution Toast introduced earlier in 2026.
 Toast IQ Team targets schedule-building and labor cost management; Toast IQ Grow covers marketing automation and review generation. No specific performance benchmarks were included in the launch announcement.

**TrustYou** announced on or around October 6 that it is replacing its traditional analytics dashboard with Ask TrustYou, 
a conversational analytics tool replacing traditional dashboards, included on all hotel plans from Lite to Enterprise starting today.
 No outcome data from early users was published.

**Presto** raised $10 million to expand its drive-thru voice AI, 
with POS integration shaping the competitive landscape.
 [PAYWALLED — sourced from RTN news ticker only.] Presto has had a turbulent operating history; the funding round's lead investors and terms were not disclosed in available snippets.

Sources: [PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/news/technology/booking-develops-internal-platform-build-modular-ai-agents) · [BusinessWire/Toast](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20261006538986/en/New-Toast-IQ-Team-and-Expanded-Toast-IQ-Grow-Help-Businesses-Drive-Profits-Attract-Guests-and-Manage-Labor) · [HospitalityNet/TrustYou](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4134687/trustyou-replaces-the-dashboard-with-a-conversation)

## Funding & M&A

The window's dominant M&A story is a large restaurant-sector deal that arrived outside the usual hospitality trade press cycle.

**Uber Eats** announced it will acquire **ezCater** for $2.3 billion. 
Restaurant Dive reported October 6 that the catering platform will expand Uber Eats' large order business, which has become a significant opportunity in recent quarters.
 ezCater is the leading US platform for workplace and corporate food ordering, connecting employers with restaurants for group meals. The acquisition folds a B2B ordering layer — one with predictable, high-ticket orders and recurring corporate accounts — into Uber Eats' consumer delivery infrastructure. Uber Eats does not yet have an equivalent of DoorDash's DoorDash for Business / MCP connector, but this deal provides the customer relationships and enterprise distribution to build one.

**Presto** closed a $10 million raise to expand drive-thru voice AI, as noted above.

Sources: [Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/uber-eats-acquires-ezcater-2-billion/832219/) · [NRN](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology/uber-to-acquire-ezcater-for-2-3b)

So what: The Uber Eats/ezCater deal is a direct competitive response to DoorDash's DashOS and MCP connector plays (previously covered). Restaurant groups with corporate catering operations should expect Uber Eats to push ezCater integration into its broader platform over the next 12 months. For PE-backed restaurant groups with B2B revenue streams, this consolidation compresses the number of independent corporate-ordering channels.

## Governance & Security

**McDonald's** faces what appears to be the first major class-action lawsuit targeting AI pricing recommendations at a large QSR chain. 
NRN reported October 6 that a customer in Illinois filed a potential class action lawsuit over the brand's use of AI to recommend prices; the fast-food chain says franchisees set prices.
 McDonald's has publicly disputed that it uses AI for dynamic pricing, but the legal claim specifically targets "AI pricing recommendations" — distinct from outright dynamic pricing — and tests whether AI-assisted menu or promotional suggestions constitute a form of price manipulation requiring consumer disclosure.

The case will likely take years to resolve, but the discovery phase alone could force disclosure of how McDonald's AI pricing systems actually function, creating precedent for the industry. No comparable lawsuit has been filed against a hotel brand using AI for rate recommendations, but the legal theory is directly transferable.

Sources: [NRN](https://www.nrn.com/quick-service/mcdonald-s-now-faces-a-lawsuit-over-its-pricing-recommendations)

So what: Any multi-unit restaurant brand using AI to inform pricing, promotional targeting, or menu sequencing should have legal review the disclosure language on its menus, apps, and terms of service before this case generates discovery templates. Hotel brands using AI revenue management are not immediately exposed by the same claim but should watch how the "AI recommendation versus AI decision" distinction gets litigated.

## AI Visibility & Discovery

Two pieces published October 5–6 surface a structural problem with AI discovery for both hotels and restaurants.

**AI hotel recommendation inconsistency** was documented in a study published October 6 on Hotel Speak. Kollective Agency queried five AI engines for the same hotel properties 15 times over a month and found that recommendations were noisy but not random — the same property appeared in some sessions and disappeared in others without any change to underlying data. 
Hotel Speak reported October 6: "Fifteen times over a month, we asked five AI engines [for hotel recommendations]. AI Hotel Recommendations are Noisy, But Not Random."
 The study found that review recency and volume were the most consistent factors in whether a property appeared, but that even high-rated properties with strong structured data dropped in and out of recommendation sets.

**Google's Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP)** for AI-mode hotel booking was flagged in the October 7 Hospitalitynet editorial briefing as having a significant omission: 
the HN Brief noted a "critical finding that Google's Universal Commerce Protocol for AI Mode hotel booking has no fields for travel agencies, commissions, or group terms."
 This matters because any booking executed through UCP-connected AI agents would effectively route direct — with no mechanism to attribute commission to a travel agent, no handling for corporate negotiated rates, and no group-terms negotiation capability. It is a design choice, not an oversight, and it shapes which intermediaries survive the agentic booking shift.

Sources: [Hotel Speak](https://www.hotelspeak.com/2026/10/ai-hotel-recommendations-are-noisy-but-not-random/) · [HospitalityNet HN Brief](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/editorial/4134768/the-ai-booking-protocol-has-no-field-for-your-travel-agent-will-agentic-bookings-flourish-or-fizzle-inaccurate-hotel-content-costs-travelers-1900-on-average)

So what: Hotel revenue managers optimising for AI discoverability cannot rely on a one-time audit. Review recency is an ongoing operational requirement, not a one-off SEO task. On UCP: the absence of agency fields is not a bug to petition Google to fix — it is the default architecture. Hotels and travel-management companies that want agency commission to survive agentic booking need to escalate this gap through AHLA and GDS partners now, before UCP becomes the de facto standard.

## Data & Integration

**Mews** received its most rigorous independent financial analysis yet. 
Skift reported October 6 that Mews has nearly $20 billion flowing through its platform; the opportunity — and harder part — now is to capture more value from the money and operations it already touches.
 At a $2.5 billion valuation, Mews is priced for a platform that monetises far beyond SaaS subscription revenue. The Skift analysis focuses on the gap between the volume of transactions on Mews and the actual revenue per transaction the company captures, flagging payments, distribution, and financial services as the logical expansion paths.

Sources: [Skift](https://skift.com/2026/10/06/inside-mews-2-5-billion-valuation-can-the-economics-catch-up/)

So what: For operators evaluating PMS contracts in 2027, Mews' investor pressure to monetise transaction flow means the SaaS subscription is no longer the full commercial picture. Understand payment routing terms, commission structures on future distribution products, and data licensing before signing multi-year deals.

His next strategic move — whether further Wonder brand integration, an acquisition, or a differentiated B2B play — will define Grubhub's relevance in the agentic ordering era.
