## VENDOR MOVES

Agentic commerce crossed a visible threshold this cycle, with multiple watchlist names shipping concrete infrastructure rather than roadmap slides.


**DoorDash** launched the most discussed product move of the cycle. On July 16, co-founder and CTO Andy Fang announced that DoorDash is opening its CLI in limited beta — `dd-cli`, a command-line tool that lets developers order DoorDash directly from their AI agent: search stores, find the best deals, check out, and more. A demo showed Anthropic's Claude picking a restaurant, adding items, and completing real payments from a terminal session.
 
For DoorDash, this is a platform-expansion play that fits a broader infrastructure strategy. Earlier in 2026, CEO Tony Xu outlined plans to consolidate DoorDash, Deliveroo, and Wolt onto a single unified technology platform. dd-cli extends that logic outward, positioning DoorDash's ordering network as a service layer that third-party developers and AI systems can plug into, rather than a destination consumers navigate to directly.
 
DoorDash has built several agent-facing integrations ahead of dd-cli — a Claude connector for menu browsing, cart building, and restaurant reservations; an integration with OpenAI's ChatGPT; a grocery-focused integration launched in December 2025; and Ask DoorDash, an in-app conversational ordering assistant.



**DoorDash** also published the architecture behind Ask DoorDash this week in a three-part engineering deep dive, describing how it built the system using LLMs, specialized AI agents, Model Context Protocol (MCP)-based tooling, persistent consumer memory, and automated evaluation infrastructure. Early production results showed that computed consumer memory improved grocery checkout conversion by approximately 24%, increased basket sizes by 17%, and reduced conversational turns by 7% during a seven-day evaluation. For restaurant discovery, the assistant achieved a 15% higher conversion rate on open-ended queries. The automated evaluation framework scaled quality measurement to more than 2,000 evaluations daily, reduced regression testing from six hours to twenty minutes, and validated a model migration that reduced latency by 35%.



The stakes are high for DoorDash, which is in the middle of a massive investment cycle involving the creation of a unified tech platform to house all its brands following a string of big acquisitions — including a $1.2 billion deal for restaurant booking platform SevenRooms and the nearly $4 billion acquisition of Deliveroo. DoorDash's stock is down 33% compared to the Nasdaq's roughly 8% gain.


**Mews** launched its most substantial product release of the year at Unfold 2026. 
Richard Valtr, Founder of Mews, said: "The hotel industry has been running on a category map drawn forty years ago. PMS, RMS, CRS, CRM, POS — these aren't real things. They're acronyms invented by software companies to sell more software. Inside Mews, those categories don't exist as separate systems. They are one workflow, on one data model with AI working across every layer."
 
Mews launched five new products at Unfold 2026, completing an AI-native operating system that unifies PMS, RMS, messaging, automations, and accounts receivable on a single data model, citing 476% three-year ROI per IDC research.
 
Accounts Receivable replaces the seven-to-eight-day invoicing delay typical in the industry with automatic issuance at billing closure, enabling 100% automated reconciliation. Independent IDC research shows hotels running on the Mews Operating System achieve 8.7% revenue growth, €350,000 in annual benefit per 100 rooms, and a 476% three-year ROI with a four-month payback.


**Oracle Hospitality** announced Oracle OPERA Cloud Assistant, embedding AI-powered capabilities directly into OPERA Cloud workflows. 
Because the PMS remains one of the most important systems in hotel operations, Oracle's move matters. When AI becomes embedded in the system of record used by front desk teams, revenue managers and property leaders, it has the potential to shape everyday decisions rather than automate only a narrow task at the edge of the operation.
 **Revinate** introduced Ivy, 
an AI decision-intelligence layer built across its platform and designed to understand a hotel's guests, property and brand voice. Revinate also used HITEC to highlight Ivy-powered capabilities for the reservations call floor and guest inbox, including automated call scoring and Revinate Chat.


**American Express** partnered with Anthropic to make U.S. Resy restaurants discoverable in Claude. 
Claude users can now explore U.S. Resy restaurants directly in their conversations when deciding where to eat — Claude connects to Resy to help diners browse available tables in real time and secure a table seamlessly. This partnership also reflects American Express's focus on creating AI-powered experiences and embedding Resy into leading AI platforms.


**Resy and Tock** are merging under American Express. 
A merger under Resy has been announced for summer 2026, which would roughly double Resy's venue inventory to over 25,000 listings and bring Tock's pre-paid booking and tiered experience features into the Resy platform. Resy runs on a flat-rate monthly subscription with no per-cover reservation fees.
 Per the NRN Tech Tracker, 
Resy plans to move forward with creating a tech stack that integrates AI-driven restaurant discovery and booking, table management, ticketing and transactions, point-of-sale data, and a CRM platform.


**Grevon** debuted at HITEC 2026 with 
Grevon Kore, an MCP-based connectivity layer for hospitality that powers Pulse, an AI booking agent for hotel websites; Echo, an AI voice agent for inbound calls; and Ops, a staff intelligence platform. Grevon's launch points to an emerging competitive front in hotel distribution: making official hotel content, rates, availability and booking paths usable by AI agents.

## OPERATIONAL AI

The first real-world usage data from an embedded AI assistant confirms that operators are using these tools for bread-and-butter business intelligence — not the speculative workflows vendors demo on stage.


**Toast** published data about how operators used its AI assistant, Toast IQ, in the first quarter of the year. Toast launched Toast IQ in October 2025. The chatbot connects to the rest of Toast's tech ecosystem, allowing operators to ask questions about their business and receive quick answers. In Q1, operators at more than 125,000 restaurants started millions of threads with Toast IQ. Their most common questions were about sales and revenue (47%), followed by menu and inventory (34%) and guests and marketing (32%). Far fewer used it for local events or weather (5%), catering and reservations (1%), and forecasting and planning (1%).



More than a quarter asked about menu optimization, and 13% asked for help with labor costs and efficiency. Some restaurant types relied on AI more than others — fine-dining locations had 29% more threads with Toast IQ than fast casuals. Usage also differed by geography: restaurants in Florida, Arizona, and Georgia averaged the highest number of threads per location.



**Toast** also launched Toast IQ Grow as part of its Spring 2026 release — a marketing-focused solution described as more than 20 updates across marketing, payroll, inventory and operations. At the center is an AI-powered Marketing Agent that can build audiences and automate campaigns across channels, supported by a reimagined Toast Local app.
 
Toast IQ Grow has already reached 40,000 weekly active locations.


**Taco Bell** expanded drive-thru voice AI to nearly 900 U.S. restaurants. 
Through a partnership with Omilia, which has brought voice AI to nearly 900 U.S. restaurants, the Mexican chain will continue to add the tech across its domestic system.
 
Yum! Brands has processed more than two million drive-thru orders via Voice AI across 300-plus Taco Bell locations.



**PAR** published analysis showing that AI tools with a holistic view of restaurant operations can make operators more efficient, improve margins and even drive same-store sales growth, per PAR's head of AI.
 The framing — holistic view vs. siloed point-tools — is the sharpest commercial argument PAR has made for its integrated data model.

Miso Robotics and Olo are building agentic tools at different ends of the restaurant stack. 
Miso Robotics acquired Zignyl to create Zippy, a large language model-powered tool offering data, revenue metrics, labor scheduling, forecasting, and task management in a ChatGPT-like agentic AI experience. Olo CEO Noah Glass told NRN the company is building an AI agent inspired by Shopify's universal commerce protocol that would be available to any restaurant operator regardless of their POS.


Sources: [NRN — Toast IQ Usage Data](https://www.nrn.com/independent-restaurants/here-s-how-restaurants-are-using-toast-s-ai-chatbot), [Restaurant Dive — PAR](https://www.restaurantdive.com/topic/technology/), [NRN — AI Chatbots](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology/tech-tracker-ai-chatbots-are-the-next-frontier-in-restaurant-technology)

So what: Toast IQ usage data is the most credible window into how mid-market restaurant operators actually use embedded AI: they want a smarter business dashboard, not an autonomous agent. "Create a daily briefing" and "show me sales variances" are the real use cases at scale. Vendors pitching autonomous AI to these operators are ahead of where adoption actually sits — and risk buyer fatigue.

## AI VISIBILITY & DISCOVERY

The biggest structural shift of the quarter is not inside the restaurant — it's happening upstream, at the discovery layer.


**Uberall** released *Fast Food, Faster Discovery: The 2026 GEO Playbook for Multi-Location QSRs* — the industry's first benchmark report measuring how AI assistants recommend restaurants. Its central finding: as consumer restaurant discovery rapidly shifts from traditional search to AI assistants, the majority of QSR locations are effectively absent from AI-generated recommendations. The playbook introduces Location Performance Optimization (LPO) as the strategic framework — connecting SEO and Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) into a single operating model built on four pillars: Visibility, Reputation, Engagement, and Conversion.


Key numbers from the Uberall benchmark: 
83% of restaurant locations are entirely invisible in AI-generated recommendations despite 86% maintaining some presence on Google. ChatGPT primarily recommends businesses averaging 4.3 stars or higher, Perplexity 4.1+, and Gemini 3.9+. AI typically recommends only 3–5 brands per query.
 
As consumer restaurant discovery rapidly shifts from traditional search to AI assistants, the majority of QSR locations are effectively absent from AI-generated recommendations — at the exact moment AI is becoming consumers' primary discovery channel.


**Uberall CMO Stephanie Genin** said: 
"AI now decides which restaurants get discovered, and most QSR brands aren't structured for the signals AI relies on. The gap between average and best-in-class is wide enough to represent a real competitive advantage — and the window to claim it is narrowing fast."



The adoption curve is the steepest in restaurant discovery since the smartphone. BrightLocal's 2026 consumer survey found 45% of consumers now use AI tools for local business recommendations — up from 6% one year earlier. DoorDash's 2026 Restaurant Industry Trends Report found 22% have used AI to choose a restaurant, statistically even with Yelp (24%).


**Google** confirmed hotel and flight agentic booking is coming in AI Mode. 
Two weeks after launching agentic bookings in AI Mode to reserve restaurants, buy event tickets and make beauty and wellness appointments, Google confirmed it is developing similar capabilities for hotel and flight reservations. Google is working with Booking.com, Expedia, Marriott International, IHG Hotels & Resorts, Choice Hotels International and Wyndham Hotels & Resorts.
 Google VP of Engineering Julie Farago told PhocusWire: 
"We're not going to rush this out the door because we want to make sure that it's a seamless experience and that people have all the control that they need and expect."



Those hesitations around treading on the toes of Booking.com and Expedia are now firmly behind Google. At Google I/O 2026, the tech giant pivoted decisively into agentic hotel booking, involving a complete overhaul of Search and its deep integration with Gemini, deployed across Gemini Flash and Gemini Spark. Google is stepping out of its traditional role as a search engine and repositioning as a direct transaction layer under an "execution-first" model.



Its hospitality-specific implementation, UCP for Lodging, enables any AI interaction to be converted into an instant room reservation, executed directly within Google surfaces. Crucially, unlike a booking made through an OTA, the hotel retains Merchant of Record status and full ownership of the guest relationship.



Restaurant listing sites like DoorDash account for more than 41% of the sources AI tools cite when recommending restaurants, per Yext research. Operators are already responding by strengthening the basics — updating menu information (39%), managing reviews (34%), and improving photo quality (32%).


**Booking Holdings** is running its AI strategy as a distribution moat. 
OpenTable launched an AI Concierge that initially answered 80% of diners' questions. Priceline's AI travel agent Penny is getting more interactive and more personalized, and in early testing Booking Holdings is "seeing a noticeable uplift from users who engage with Penny compared to non-Penny users." Agoda found its AI-assisted automation drove a double-digit year-over-year reduction in customer service costs per booking.
 CEO Glenn Fogel said: 
"As gen AI reshapes how travelers discover and plan trips, we are focused on meeting them wherever that journey begins."


Sources: [BusinessWire — Uberall GEO Playbook](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260507962493/en/83-of-Restaurants-Are-Invisible-in-AI-Search-New-Uberall-Report-Reveals-the-Discovery-Gap-Reshaping-the-Quick-Service-Restaurant-Industry), [PhocusWire — Google Agentic Travel](https://www.phocuswire.com/google-agentic-travel-booking-ai), [PYMNTS — Booking Holdings](https://www.pymnts.com/earnings/2026/booking-holdings-ai-assistants-slash-costs-and-boost-bookings/)

So what: GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) is now a budget-line item, not an experiment. Restaurant operators with multi-location footprints need to audit their AI discovery profile across ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity this quarter — not next year. For hotels, Google's UCP for Lodging is the most structurally significant distribution development since Google Hotel Ads: it preserves Merchant of Record for hotels, which is strategically valuable, but demands that your CRS expose machine-readable live inventory or you won't exist in agentic search results.

## GOVERNANCE & SECURITY


A white paper from Terence Ronson of Pertlink Limited translates the White House's June 2, 2026 Executive Order titled "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security" into operational priorities for hotels. The Order is, on its face, about federal systems, frontier-model security, and national cyber defense — but read carefully, it is one of the clearest signals that AI has crossed a threshold from an experimental productivity tool to critical operational infrastructure that governments treat as a matter of national security.



The market is likely to fragment. Trusted infrastructure vendors and governance-mature providers will consolidate enterprise and flagged-brand deployments. Commodity "AI-overlay" vendors — those that bolted generative features on for marketing — will be the first to fail procurement and insurer review. The era of superficial "AI feature" marketing is beginning to fade, replaced by demands for accountability, clarity on liability, assurances of resilience, and security validation.



Hotels sit on uniquely valuable behavioral data: guest identities, movement patterns, spending behavior, wellness and dining preferences, loyalty activity, and emotional-preference signals. The Order's emphasis on protecting intellectual property foreshadows broader geopolitical tension around sovereign AI, data localization, AI supply chains, and cross-border data governance. Operators with international footprints should prepare for regional AI fragmentation and conflicting compliance requirements.



Hospitality companies are accelerating AI adoption — dynamic pricing, marketing personalization, fraud detection, digital key platforms, call-center transcription, and biometric check-in. These deployments typically require massive volumes of customer and device data and often rely on third-party vendors, offshore development teams, and cloud-hosted model pipelines. That combination creates a fast-emerging compliance risk under the Department of Justice's Data Security Program (DSP) rules implementing Executive Order 14117, which restricts or prohibits certain "covered data transactions" that could give countries of concern access to Americans' bulk sensitive personal data. Hospitality routinely touches at least four categories of sensitive personal data: identifiers, geolocation, biometrics, and financial.



Vietnam's comprehensive AI law became effective March 1, 2026, positioning it as Southeast Asia's first comprehensive AI regulator. South Korea has introduced enforceable AI governance and transparency obligations.


Sources: [Hospitality Net — White House AI EO & Hotels](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/opinion/4132771/when-national-ai-security-becomes-a-hospitality-imperative), [Hospitality Technology — DOJ DSP](https://hospitalitytech.com/beyond-hipaa-navigating-dojs-new-bulk-data-thresholds-hospitality-ai)

So what: Any hotel or restaurant group running AI tools that process guest biometrics, geolocation, or financial data via third-party vendors with offshore development needs a DOJ DSP compliance review now — not at contract renewal. The hospitality industry's default of delegating compliance to PMS/POS vendors is no longer legally defensible.

## PEOPLE & SKILLS


At the National Restaurant Association Show, Square's head of food and beverage James Schonzeit called AI "the biggest thing of this entire show this year." But almost universally, restaurant tech leaders said AI was the one thing people were getting wrong. Popmenu CEO Brendan Sweeney cautioned that overadoption of large language models rests on shaky fundamentals — the cost of AI tokens required to operate coding services can exceed the salaries of the workers the technology purports to replace.


Toast CMO Kelly Esten framed the real constraint: 
"It can't be another thing to do. It has to actually do some of the work for you."
 Popmenu's Sweeney added: 
"Being first is not always the best. Being right and doing it the right way is the best."



Twenty-six percent of restaurant operators say they are using AI-related tools, per the National Restaurant Association's State of the Restaurant Industry 2026 report. Marketing is the top area: 19% of full-service operators and 15% of limited-service operators use AI to assist with marketing. Only 6% of restaurants are using AI for customer orders. However, roughly six in 10 millennials and Gen Z adults said they would place an order with an AI-generated bot.


Sources: [Restaurant Dive — NRA Show Skepticism](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/restaurant-technology-executives-warn-against-ai-overdependence/821024/), [Restaurant Dive — NRA Adoption Stats](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/national-restaurant-assocation-operator-artificial-intelligence-adoption/812418/)

So what: The gap between consumer willingness to use AI ordering (60% of Gen Z/Millennials) and operator deployment for ordering (6%) is the largest single growth market in restaurant technology. Vendors who can close that gap reliably — with documented accuracy, handoff protocols, and transparent pricing — have a clear runway.
