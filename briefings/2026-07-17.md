## Vendor Moves

The restaurant reservation market has fractured into three distinct ecosystems controlled by deep-pocketed parents — and each made significant moves this cycle.


**American Express** is folding Tock into Resy, boosting Resy's venue count to 25,000, and sunsetting the Tock brand, formally uniting two former startups against OpenTable.
 The commercial logic is straightforward: 
in Q4 2025, global restaurant spend by American Express card members was up 9% overall, but spending at Resy restaurants was up more than 20%.
 The trigger was the Platinum Card's new $400 annual Resy dining credit. 
In the three weeks following the credit announcement, there was a 36% increase in Resy reservations made by Platinum cardholders, a five-times increase in daily account-linking, and users spending on average 25% more, according to Resy CEO Pablo Rivero.
 Tock restaurants begin joining the Resy credit network this summer, with full migration of Tock's restaurant-facing tech expected in 2027. 
Reports and rumors have surfaced of restaurants receiving five-, six-, and even seven-figure payments to switch between booking platforms including Resy, Tock, OpenTable, and SevenRooms.


**DoorDash** is running the most aggressive AI product expansion of any delivery or reservation company in the cycle. 
The company launched Ask DoorDash, a chatbot allowing customers to order food and groceries and book restaurant reservations using text prompts, photos, or voice inputs — part of a massive investment cycle that includes a $1.2 billion acquisition of SevenRooms and a nearly $4 billion acquisition of Deliveroo, with finance chief Ravi Inukonda telling investors DoorDash plans to carry out most of the tech stack overhaul this year.
 Early testing was substantive: 
nearly half of all restaurant orders placed through Ask DoorDash were from a place the customer had never ordered from before; carts assembled through the chatbot ran more than 35% higher in value than a standard grocery order, with users completing them five times faster. The product draws on AI models built by OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google, supplemented by open-source options.


The more significant move came on 16 July. 
DoorDash CTO Andy Fang unveiled dd-cli, a command-line interface tool that allows AI agents to autonomously search restaurants, compare deals, and complete checkout on DoorDash without any manual input from a human — processing real payments rather than simulated transactions. A public demonstration showed Anthropic's Claude completing a full end-to-end DoorDash order autonomously.
 
Access is invite-only, restricted to macOS developers in the US and Canada. DoorDash is positioning its ordering network as a service layer that third-party developers and AI systems can plug into — part of CEO Tony Xu's plan to consolidate DoorDash, Deliveroo, and Wolt onto a single unified technology platform.


**Toast** expanded Toast IQ from a set of smart features into a full conversational AI assistant. 
Toast IQ can flag top-selling items by daypart, add a menu item with a single prompt, automatically update menus across every service channel, and answer questions like "Who's working Friday night?" — available to all U.S. customers on iOS and Android.
 
With access to real-time and historical data across approximately 148,000 customer locations, Toast IQ is designed as an operator's "right hand," going beyond answering questions to proactively surfacing insights and executing tasks directly within the Toast platform.
 At the May 2026 NRA Show, Toast added Toast IQ Grow, a marketing agent that identifies opportunities, then creates and deploys campaigns. 
Separately, Marqii launched an MCP (Model Context Protocol) server so restaurants can use ChatGPT or Claude to manage their reviews, menus, and online information. Chowly developed AI agents that will automatically update restaurants' web presence and ad campaigns — and if its SEO agent detects a pizza place's website is not surfacing for "pizza near me," it rewrites the site with more relevant keywords.


**SevenRooms** (DoorDash-owned) launched Channel Connect in June 2026, bringing hotel-style distribution management to restaurants. It is also powering Going Out inside the DoorDash app, where 
restaurants can promote events, manage tables, and personalise hospitality with enriched guest profiles, with SevenRooms Voice AI helping streamline operations.


Sources: [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91496951/amex-resy-tock-restaurant-reservation-wars) · [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/doordash-to-let-ai-agents-place-real-food-orders-4796131) · [Restaurant Business Online](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/technology/tech-tracker-host-new-ai-tools-hit-market) [PAYWALLED]

So what: The reservation market is now three ecosystems — DoorDash/SevenRooms, Amex/Resy+Tock, and Booking Holdings/OpenTable — with Chase/Visa orbiting all three. A mid-market operator choosing a reservation platform is now also choosing a card-member spend programme, a delivery integration, and a data-sharing arrangement with a public company. Evaluate on those terms, not just on cover fees or UI.

## AI Visibility & Discovery

Google has converted restaurant booking from a search result into an agentic action at significant scale. 
Google rolled out AI Mode's restaurant booking feature to eight countries, letting users in the UK, Australia, Canada, and five others reserve tables directly through Search for the first time. Eight reservation platform partners power the feature — TheFork, SevenRooms, ResDiary, Mozrest, Foodhub, Dojo, DesignMyNight, and OpenTable — requiring no extra integration from restaurants. AI Mode had grown to 75 million daily active users since its May 2025 launch.
 The consumer now moves from a natural-language query ("dog-friendly Italian in Shoreditch, Saturday 7 p.m., two people") to a confirmed reservation without leaving Search.

Consumer adoption of AI for restaurant discovery is measurable and growing. 
DoorDash's 2026 Restaurant Industry Trends Report found 22% of consumers have used an AI tool like ChatGPT or Google Gemini to help choose a restaurant. Restaurant listing sites like DoorDash account for more than 41% of the sources AI tools cite when recommending restaurants, per Yext research. Three-quarters of consumers say they're comfortable using AI for reservations, but only 28% of operators are using AI to manage calls and customer service.


Two new entrants are attacking the AI-native ordering layer. **Bites** launched its ordering experience on ChatGPT, 
enabling diners to discover, browse, and place orders from restaurants directly within ChatGPT, built in partnership with POS companies using the same pricing as in-store menus — no markups, no commissions, no additional operational overhead.

a standalone app in public beta in San Francisco and New York City where users search with prompts like "cozy pasta spots on the Lower East Side with outdoor seating under $100." The app aggregates data from DoorDash, Google Maps, TikTok, and other sources, reasons through requests, and allows conversational follow-ups — emphasising vibe and explainability rather than star ratings.

Operators who have not audited their listings for machine-readability — correct menus, accurate hours, consistent photo quality — are invisible to AI recommendation layers, not just to traditional search. Claiming that Google handles this automatically is wrong: structured, operator-controlled data still wins over scraped content.

## Data & Integration

Every major hotel chain has announced an agentic AI roadmap, and every one is running into the same bottleneck. 
Marriott's $1B+ agentic mesh, Hilton's AI Planner, Hyatt and Accor inside ChatGPT, IHG's AI-enhanced CRM — all of them are bottlenecked at the same layer: the property management system was built when a guest was a stay at a property, not a relationship across the chain.


The structural problem is that the PMS holds only a fraction of available guest signal. 
A brand can commission an agentic concierge in the boardroom and discover, in the second sprint, that the system cannot reliably identify the same guest across the PMS, the loyalty platform, and the mobile app. The team will spend the next six months on what looks, from the outside, like AI development. It is almost entirely data integration and identity resolution. The model is rarely the bottleneck. The data foundation is.
 Synthesised industry data from Hotel Tech Report, Revinate, and Hospitality Net shows 
fewer than one in four hotels have fully integrated core systems — approximately 23% fully integrated, 47% partially integrated, and 30% largely siloed.
 Duplicate guest profile rates run at 6–16% across major chains.

The complicating factor is franchise structure. 
Most major hotel brands do not own the properties that fly their flag — the relationship is governed by a franchise or management agreement. This means guest data captured at a property does not always flow cleanly back to the brand, and when a franchise relationship ends, the parties frequently end up litigating over who owns what. The PMS data model still treats the reservation as the primary key and the guest as an attribute of that reservation — a design that served the industry in the era when a guest was a stay at a property.



The 2025 IDC PMS MarketScape presents a different context from the 2022 edition — cloud is now assumed rather than debated, and AI has moved from the margins to the centre of product strategy. Modern property management systems have shifted from systems of record to systems of intelligence, with predictive insights supporting room assignment, maintenance planning, and demand forecasting.


Sources: [Apptad](https://apptad.com/insights/the-hotel-industrys-ai-problem-lives-in-the-pms/) · [Hotel Online](https://www.hotel-online.com/news/ai-is-coming-for-the-pms-but-not-the-way-you-think) · [Hospitality Net](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4129961.html)

So what: PE-backed hotel SaaS companies building AI features on top of fragmented data estates are building on sand. The durable investment thesis is the entity layer above the PMS — master data management for guest, property, and rate-plan entities — not the AI features themselves. For mid-market hotel operators, this translates practically: before signing any AI guest-experience contract, demand a clear answer on how the vendor resolves duplicate guest profiles across your PMS, loyalty programme, and CRM. If they can't explain it, the AI is hallucinating about who your guests are.

## Funding & M&A


Forty global hospitality technology startups raised more than $1 billion across 40 companies between April 2025 and March 2026, with property management systems and AI-led platforms attracting the largest share of investment, according to Abode Worldwide's Hospitality Tech Investment Index 2026.
 
Seven PMS providers including Amenitiz, Arbio, and Boom raised a combined $408.1M, and nearly half of all tracked rounds were pre-seed, seed, or Series A — a sign capital is still chasing newer entrants over incumbents.



PMS businesses are building broader platforms through development and acquisition rather than relying on third-party integrations. Mews' acquisitions of Flexkeeping and DataChat in late 2025 are one example. AI-led guest experience platforms were another standout category, with Duve, Chatlyn, Conduit, and Canary Technologies raising a combined $152.6 million.
 Canary separately acquired OpenKey in February 2026 to expand door-lock access. 
Abode CEO Jessica Gillingham summarised the thesis: "The companies attracting the strongest investor backing sit close to essential operator workflows and become more valuable over time. Unified systems generate more data, better automation and higher switching costs — a compounding dynamic that is proving more appealing to investors."


The most recent signal: Hotel Dive's sidebar this week shows **Mews has laid off 15% of staff** in a pivot to an "AI-native future." This sits in tension with the company's $300M raise and its recent M&A spree — watch whether the headcount reduction reflects genuine AI-driven productivity or cost pressure following overexpansion.

On the hotel brand side: 
the global travel, leisure, and hospitality sector saw $10.8 billion in M&A activity in H1 2025, a 17.3% year-over-year increase in deal value.
 However, 
private equity-sponsored hospitality deals declined 85% year-over-year in H1 amid high borrowing costs, Trump tariff policy shifts, and worsening global travel sentiment.
 The capital is concentrating in luxury and technology assets, not scale.

Sources: [Hotel Dive](https://www.hoteldive.com/news/hospitality-tech-attracts-1b-in-funding-pms-ai-leaders/817439/) · [Hotel Dive M&A](https://www.hoteldive.com/news/global-hospitality-travel-merger-acquisition-activity/759314/)

So what: Investors are rewarding platform businesses closest to daily operator workflows — not point-solution AI features. If you are a PE-backed hospitality SaaS company, the question is whether you are on a path to becoming the operational substrate or whether you are a feature that a better-funded PMS will absorb.

## Operational AI

Voice AI for restaurant phone reservations has moved from novelty to operational infrastructure. 
In recent weeks, three separate announcements involving OpenTable — from Maple, Loman AI, and SoundHound AI — indicate voice-driven reservations are becoming more tightly connected to core reservation infrastructure. Maple automates inbound calls; Loman AI handles the full reservation lifecycle (book, modify, confirm, cancel) via natural language; SoundHound AI extends reservations to in-vehicle voice assistants, capturing guest intent before they reach a restaurant's own channels.



Rather than offering a single proprietary voice AI solution, OpenTable supports integrations with multiple voice AI providers. Yelp expanded aggressively in Fall 2025, launching Yelp Host at $149/month (or $99 for existing Guest Manager users) and Yelp Receptionist at $99/month — pre-trained on Yelp's business data, operating as full replacement or overflow for manual call handling.


The scale is real. 
ConverseNow facilitates over 2 million conversations per month and repurposes over 83,000 labor hours monthly. Dine Brands (Applebee's and IHOP) announced plans in June 2025 to implement Voice AI Agents to handle customer orders and reservations over the phone. By 2027, there could be a 69% increase in the use of AI and robotics in fast food restaurants.


Sources: [Restaurant Technology News](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2025/12/how-voice-ai-is-changing-the-way-restaurants-handle-phone-reservations/) · [PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/news/technology/AI-booking-push-continues-despite-openai-strategic-shift)

So what: A restaurant spending $200–800/month on voice AI for reservations recovers it in reduced missed calls alone — 
modern voice AI responds in under 300ms, processes bookings in 22 seconds, and can reduce missed calls by 87%, with an estimated 40% of restaurant calls currently going unanswered industry-wide.
 The strategic risk is data: voice reservation conversations are a rich source of guest intent signals. Operators should verify who owns those transcripts.

## Governance & Security


In March 2026, CyberNews discovered that an attacker had compromised 527 credentials from accounts on Chekin and Gastrodat, hospitality platforms based in Spain and Austria, extracting the names, booking details, and even ID documents of over 5 million guests — streaming everything directly into the attacker's Telegram via simple Python scripts.
 This is the incident that defines the current risk frame.


Between January 2025 and February 2026, at least 20 separate security incidents occurred across AI-powered applications, with nearly all having preventable causes like mishandled credentials or lax authentication.
 The financial exposure is calibrated: 
MGM's 2023 breach cost $100 million from operational disruption in a single quarter with another $45 million in settlement costs; Marriott paid $52 million in penalties and entered a 20-year consent order; IBM found in 2025 that the average data breach costs $4.44 million when accounting for forensics, legal fees, notification, and lost business.


The structural problem is that AI agents are being handed the same access levels that attackers work hard to obtain. 
In 2025, Microsoft researchers identified screenshot processing as one of seven major risk classes associated with Computer Use Agents — giving an agent access to private guest and company information with no guarantee of where that data ends up.


On the governance side, the White House issued an Executive Order on 2 June 2026 titled "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security." 
The modern hotel has quietly become a dense, interconnected intelligent environment — binding together identity, payments, guest data, building systems, IoT, energy orchestration, and AI-driven workflows. The more intelligent a property becomes, the more strategically exposed it becomes.
 
A modern hotel system already exposes APIs into PMS and POS systems, digital locks, IoT, and guest-room management, CRM, payment ecosystems, HVAC, and guest-identity databases. An improperly governed AI agent with access to those interfaces could, in principle, unlock guestrooms, alter reservations, manipulate pricing, issue refunds, expose guest data, or trigger operational disruption.


Sources: [Hotel Technology News](https://hoteltechnologynews.com/2026/05/why-hotel-ai-adoption-is-moving-faster-than-security-controls-and-increasing-risk-exposure/) · [Hospitality Net](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/opinion/4132771/when-national-ai-security-becomes-a-hospitality-imperative)

So what: "Ask your AI vendors where your data goes, how their audit trail works, and who controls your information. If they can't answer clearly, pause and assess before that agent gets your PMS login." — Daniel Hersey, CEO of Caleta. For PE-backed hospitality SaaS vendors: cyber governance is becoming a procurement prerequisite, not a feature. Underwriters are raising premiums and tightening requirements. Vendors who cannot demonstrate clean data flow and agent auditability will fail enterprise RFPs.

## AI Visibility & Discovery — OpenAI / Booking Holdings


OpenAI abandoned its plans for enabling direct purchases within ChatGPT, shifting to product discovery and leaving checkout to third-party app developers. An OpenAI spokesperson confirmed: "We're prioritizing making ChatGPT search and product discovery great, with the Agentic Commerce Protocol serving as the infrastructure that connects users to merchants." This pivot relieved competitive pressure on travel booking giants Expedia and Booking Holdings, whose shares rose following the announcement.



Ira Vouk, founder of Hospitality 2.0 Consulting, said OpenAI's focus on enterprise business "suggests that owning the transaction layer may not be their immediate priority" and that "each AI platform will make different decisions about whether to own, facilitate, or bypass the transaction layer."
 Meanwhile, Google's position is assessed by Phocuswright as structurally strongest: 
Google has the most impressive end-to-end AI tech stack — Gemini, AI chips, cloud computing, Google Wallet, Google Pay, and several open-source agentic standards, with years of investment in hotel ARI integrations across over a million properties.



Booking Holdings CEO Glenn Fogel, during Q3 earnings, said Booking.com is measuring "faster search, better conversion, lower cancellation rates and high customer satisfaction" from its OpenAI ChatGPT app integration, though he called these "very early signals."
 
OpenAI's retreat from direct booking benefits Booking.com in the near term — without in-chat transactions, AI platforms still need someone else to complete the booking, and Booking.com's payments, inventory systems, merchant model, and global infrastructure are difficult to replicate.


Sources: [Skift](https://skift.com/2026/03/05/openai-chatgpt-checkout-walkback/) · [PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/news/technology/AI-booking-push-continues-despite-openai-strategic-shift) · [PhocusWire Q3](https://www.phocuswire.com/booking-holdings-q3-2025-earnings)

So what: Hotels should not mistake OpenAI's transaction pullback for a long-term win for OTAs. AI will still reshape distribution — the delay gives Booking.com more time to dominate another referral channel, and it gives operators with weak direct-booking capabilities even less runway to act.
