Funding & M&A

The restaurant reservation stack is consolidating around two financial-services empires — and the speed is accelerating.

American Express announced on June 15 a proposed $700 million all-cash acquisition of TheFork from Tripadvisor, connecting 50,000 restaurant venues across 11 European countries to the Amex dining portfolio that already includes Resy and Tock. Combined, the platforms would create a network of approximately 75,000 bookable venues. The transaction is expected to close before end of 2026, subject to regulatory approvals.

TheFork generated $221 million in revenue in 2025, up 22% year-over-year, with adjusted EBITDA improving from $5M to $21M in the same period.

The TheFork move crucially bolsters Amex's position amid heightened competition: DoorDash spent $1.2 billion to acquire SevenRooms in 2025 and offers diners delivery credit for booking tables, while Chase Sapphire linked with OpenTable as part of its Visa partnership for exclusive restaurant bookings.

On the Resy/Tock side, this summer Amex merges Tock's restaurant inventory into Resy, adding roughly 8,000 bookable venues — including 1,200 wineries — boosting Resy's venue count to 25,000 and formally sunsetting the Tock brand. In Q4 2025, Amex cardmember spend at Resy restaurants was up more than 20%, versus 9% for overall restaurant spend. Pablo Rivero, CEO of Resy and Tock, said: "Resy will be the singular app for the best culinary experiences used by the hospitality world's most ambitious operators, with a membership benefit for card members."

Sources: BusinessWire (AmEx/TheFork) · Fast Company (Resy/Tock) · Reuters/AOL

So what: Two of the world's largest financial-services companies are now competing to own restaurant booking infrastructure. Reservation platforms are no longer neutral tools — they are the front-ends of cardholder loyalty ecosystems. Data portability and direct channel ownership are the real negotiating assets; operators should review contracts before lock-in provisions tighten further.

Vendor Moves

DoorDash is reshaping its stack from delivery app to agentic commerce OS; Toast and OpenTable are embedding AI across their full operating surfaces.

DoorDash launched Ask DoorDash on June 11, a chatbot allowing customers to order food and groceries with text prompts, photos, or voice inputs. "Traditional search works best when you know the exact restaurant or table you're looking for," DoorDash wrote in the launch post. "Ask DoorDash is designed for the moments when you don't."

Early testing showed nearly half of all restaurant orders placed through the tool came from a restaurant the customer had never ordered from before; grocery carts assembled through the chatbot ran more than 35% higher in value than standard orders and were completed five times faster. Andy Fang said the product draws on AI models built by OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google.

On July 16, DoorDash CTO Andy Fang unveiled dd-cli, a command-line interface tool opening in limited beta, with a blunt pitch: let your AI agent search stores, find the best deals, and check out without opening the consumer app. Demos in the announcement thread showed Anthropic's Claude picking a restaurant, adding items, and completing real payments from a terminal session.

DoorDash's CEO Tony Xu had earlier in 2026 outlined plans to consolidate DoorDash, Deliveroo, and Wolt onto a single unified technology platform; dd-cli extends that logic outward, positioning DoorDash's ordering network as a service layer that third-party developers and AI systems can plug into directly.

SevenRooms (DoorDash-owned) announced on July 21 — published July 28 — a Global Guest capability enabling partner restaurants to search hundreds of millions of DoorDash and Deliveroo customer records at check-in, just over a year after being acquired by DoorDash. The feature enables restaurant staff to recognise guests at check-in, even for first-time visitors. Allison Page, founder and CPO of SevenRooms, said: "These tools turn fragmented guest information into something a host or server can actually use, such as recognizing a regular by sight, knowing their usual order, or catching that their last few visits all ran slow."

Toast launched Toast Drive-Thru on April 14, an enterprise-grade solution designed to help QSRs increase throughput and order accuracy across the approximately 140,000 U.S. drive-thru locations. The platform unifies hardware, POS-native software, and AI voice ordering integrations — including Incept AI for automated order-taking — with Speed of Service reporting and Drive-Thru KDS enhancements for kitchen load-balancing.

On May 5, Toast launched Toast IQ Grow, a $499/month marketing solution pairing an AI Marketing Agent with a dedicated human Marketing Success Manager. The Marketing Agent builds audiences and automates campaigns across email, SMS, social media, and paid advertising. Toast also unveiled a reimagined Toast Local consumer app featuring integrated Resy reservations and expanding bookable locations to more than 20,000.

An early adopter, Keita Okugawa of Matoi in San Diego, said the platform generated $3.4K in attributed email revenue over two months — more than three times the cost of Toast IQ Grow — alongside a 4.5x return on ad spend.

OpenTable on July 16 upgraded its AI Concierge from per-restaurant profiles to homepage-level cross-network discovery across 65,000+ restaurants, drawing on Perplexity for real-time web grounding and OpenAI as the LLM inference engine. The RAG architecture grounds responses in live reservation inventory — preventing hallucinated availability. The same day, OpenTable published its 2026 Top 100 Hotel Restaurants, built from more than 10 million verified diner reviews; hotel restaurant dining by travelers rose 13% year-over-year, and 60% of respondents planning summer travel said they booked a hotel because of its on-site restaurant.

Sources: TechCrunch (Ask DoorDash) · Food On Demand (SevenRooms) · BusinessWire (Toast Drive-Thru) · Pulse 2.0 (Toast IQ Grow) · TechTimes (OpenTable)

So what: DoorDash is becoming a commerce infrastructure layer, not just an ordering channel. If dd-cli reaches scale, it bypasses the consumer app entirely, routing agent-mediated orders through a CLI that sits inside AI assistants, productivity tools, and calendar apps. Operators should understand that their DoorDash listing may soon be accessed by AI agents acting autonomously on behalf of users. For operators evaluating Toast IQ Grow: the 8% pilot GMV lift is real but was measured in a controlled beta with MSM support — independent replication across a broader operator cohort will be the signal to watch.

AI Visibility & Discovery

83% of restaurants are invisible to AI search; the window to claim position is narrowing.

Uberall published on May 7 the industry's first benchmark measuring how ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Copilot, and Google AI Overviews actually recommend restaurants. Its central finding: as consumer restaurant discovery rapidly shifts from traditional search to AI assistants, the majority of QSR locations are effectively absent from AI-generated recommendations. The playbook introduces Location Performance Optimization (LPO) as the strategic framework, connecting SEO and Generative Engine Optimization into a single operating model built on Visibility, Reputation, Engagement, and Conversion.

AI restaurant discovery is research-heavy: informational and comparative prompts drive nearly 79% of AI-generated restaurant responses; ChatGPT primarily recommends businesses averaging 4.3 stars or higher, Perplexity 4.1+, and Gemini 3.9+. AI typically recommends only 3–5 brands per query. Stephanie Genin, CMO at Uberall: "AI now decides which restaurants get discovered, and most QSR brands aren't structured for the signals AI relies on. The gap between average and best-in-class is wide enough to represent a real competitive advantage — and the window to claim it is narrowing fast."

Google introduced what it described as "agentic capabilities" within AI Mode, beginning with restaurant reservations in the UK on April 10, allowing users to complete the entire reservation process — including identifying restaurants, checking availability, and confirming bookings — without leaving Search.

Google has since named hotel booking as the next vertical for its Universal Commerce Protocol, with an Agent Payments Protocol enabling AI agents to complete purchases on users' behalf.

OpenTable is a named Google launch partner; a spokesman said: "OpenTable is a partner in this launch with Google, and our inclusion is the latest example of how we're meeting diners wherever they are, and on whatever platform they're using. This expansion with Google builds on our existing partnerships with OpenAI, Perplexity, and Alexa+."

A DoorDash survey of 3,001 U.S. consumers found 22% have used AI to choose a restaurant. Restaurant listing sites like DoorDash account for more than 41% of the sources AI tools cite when recommending restaurants. Three-quarters of consumers say they're comfortable using AI for reservations, but only 28% of operators are using AI to manage calls and customer service — with an estimated 40% of reservation calls going unanswered industry-wide.

Sources: BusinessWire (Uberall) · RTN (Google) · TechTimes (OpenTable)

So what: A restaurant with 200 reviews that hasn't optimised for GEO is now invisible to a discovery channel capturing 22% of dining decisions — growing fastest in the highest-spending demographic. The corrective action is not expensive: consistent NAP data, schema markup, structured menu data, and review volume above the AI recommendation floor (4.3 stars for ChatGPT). Multi-location operators should prioritise this before chain brands lock in the top AI citation positions, which 5WPR data suggests are hardening now into durable visit-frequency economics.

Operational AI (Hotels)

Post-HITEC, AI has moved from the exhibition floor into core hotel workflows — the strongest use cases disappear into the stack rather than wearing a badge.

At HITEC 2026 in San Antonio, the most credible AI demonstrations were the least theatrical. The stronger use cases did not ask hoteliers to imagine a distant future — they focused on familiar work that already consumes too much time: assigning rooms, responding to guest questions, scoring reservation calls, qualifying group leads, and reconciling invoices. Oracle Hospitality introduced Oracle OPERA Cloud Assistant, with embedded intelligence for AI-assisted room assignments, staff guidance, rate-description generation, and multilingual translation — AI sitting inside the PMS environment, close to the workflows where front desk, reservations, and operations teams already spend their day.

A July 3 HTN analysis described the post-HITEC landscape as a market in which vendors are less focused on isolated tools and more focused on intelligence layers connecting the PMS, guest communications, network infrastructure, revenue management, procurement, access control, building operations, and commercial sales workflows. Innspire launched Guest Flows, a zero-download guest journey solution supporting mobile check-in, digital key, AI concierge, F&B ordering, upgrades, ancillary revenue, and automated checkout through a guest's smartphone. Grevon debuted with Grevon Kore, an MCP-based connectivity layer with an AI booking agent for hotel websites (Pulse), an AI voice agent for inbound calls (Echo), and a staff intelligence platform (Ops).

BCG's February 2026 "AI-First Hotels" report found that Marriott's AI-driven room-assignment engine now processes more than 1.2 million room assignments per second across the chain. Ritz-Carlton San Francisco implemented an AI housekeeping synchronisation system that sped up room preparation by 20%; IHG followed with predictive housekeeping models that anticipate peak cleaning times. In North America, 65% of hotels reported staffing shortages in 2025; labor costs jumped 11.2% year-over-year.

Sources: HTN July 3 · HTN June 23 · BCG

So what: The HITEC signal for mid-market operators: vendor selection is increasingly about whether AI intelligence sits inside your existing system of record or as a standalone layer requiring manual integration. Oracle's OPERA Cloud move and Mews's semantic data layer are differentiated precisely because the AI reads operational data natively. For PE-backed hotel SaaS: Cendyn's Wayfinder launch signals that GEO/AI visibility monitoring for hotels is a distinct, emergent product category — not a marketing module add-on.

Governance & Security

Regulatory exposure for AI in hospitality is arriving on three simultaneous tracks — and most operators haven't mapped their AI systems to any of them.

The EU AI Act's Article 50 transparency requirements for chatbots apply from 2 August 2026 — four days from the date of this briefing. High-risk AI obligations under the Act apply from August 2026 to Annex III systems, with product-related high-risk obligations phased in from August 2027. California's Automated Decisionmaking Technology access and opt-out rights take effect January 1, 2027.

On June 2, 2026, the White House issued an Executive Order titled "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security." The Order's significance lies less in any single clause than in what it collectively recognises: that artificial intelligence is now a national strategic capability, a cybersecurity priority, and a critical infrastructure dependency. A modern hotel is no longer merely a physical property — it is a digitally orchestrated environment, a data-rich ecosystem, and an increasingly autonomous service infrastructure. "The same intelligence that makes a property efficient also makes it exposed."

The RH-ISAC 2026 CISO Benchmark Report, drawing on insights from more than 200 CISOs across retail and hospitality, found that AI tops the list of friction points — ahead of ransomware and phishing. Seventy-one percent identified AI as a primary concern, citing data leakage, insider misuse, and insufficient governance controls. Seventy percent of CISOs said AI governance has been added to their scope of responsibility. [PAYWALLED/BLOCKED — sourced from search snippet only]

Sources: Witness.ai · Hospitalitynet.org · PRNewswire/RH-ISAC

So what: August 2 is now. Operators running AI chatbots for EU guests who have not added disclosure labelling are in breach of Article 50 as of this week. The compliance ask is not complex — a clear disclosure that the user is interacting with an AI — but it needs to happen immediately. For hospitality SaaS vendors: customers will increasingly require documented AI governance as part of procurement, not just GDPR data-processing agreements.

People & Skills

Hospitality is the most AI-exposed industry for workforce readiness — and the adoption-to-skill gap is widening.

BCG's February 2026 report found that only 2.9% of full-time employees in travel and tourism possess AI skills, compared with 21% in the tech and media sector. AI-skilled workers in hospitality are growing at roughly 5% year-over-year — but from an extremely low base. Marriott framed its pilot of an AI-driven room-assignment engine as "empowerment, not replacement," with frontline staff co-designing the tool alongside developers and maintaining override authority.

A 2026 workforce analysis by Resume Now found that AI tools are rolling out faster than workers can be trained, creating a growing mismatch between current skills and what employers now need. Hospitality ranks as the most exposed industry, ahead of healthcare, financial services, and logistics. AI tools are being rolled out faster than workers can be trained to use them effectively, creating friction and increasing the risk of errors.

Hireology's 2026 research found hospitality applicants are the most AI-sceptical workforce across all verticals: 24% are very uncomfortable with AI in hiring — the highest rate of any industry — with over 36% having significant reservations.

Sources: BCG · Allwork.Space

So what: The skills gap is primarily a change-management and trust problem, not a technology problem. Marriott's co-design model is the available evidence for what drives adoption. For operators deploying AI hiring tools: Hireology's data suggests hospitality is the industry most likely to lose experienced candidates when AI screening is perceived as depersonalising the process — a meaningful risk in a sector still reporting 65% staffing shortages.

Data & Integration

The delivery-to-dining data merge has arrived: operators inside the DoorDash/SevenRooms ecosystem now have a first-party guest view that rivals any loyalty programme.

SevenRooms' Global Guest capability — the most tactically significant data development of this cycle — lets restaurant staff search hundreds of millions of DoorDash and Deliveroo customer records at the point of check-in, enabling recognition of a delivery-only customer on their first dine-in visit. This is the first public evidence of delivery data flowing into dine-in operations at scale, arriving just over a year after DoorDash's acquisition. SevenRooms also launched AI-powered automatic capacity management, tightening and loosening table availability based on real-time traffic, and targeted incentive insertion within booking flows.

The broader pattern, as described in HTN's July 3 analysis: Oracle Hospitality's OPERA Cloud Assistant brings AI into the system of record used by front desk teams, revenue managers, and property leaders — meaning it has the potential to shape everyday decisions rather than automate only a narrow task at the edge. The post-HITEC wave points to a market in which vendors are less focused on isolated tools and more focused on intelligence layers that connect the PMS, guest communications, revenue management, procurement, access control, and commercial sales workflows.

Sources: Food On Demand · HTN

So what: If your restaurant uses SevenRooms and has significant DoorDash volume, your front-of-house team now has delivery-behaviour data that was previously invisible at check-in. The immediate ROI is recognition and personalisation; the medium-term play is using delivery frequency and order history to predict dine-in preferences and fill off-peak slots. For operators not on SevenRooms: this is the clearest current example of why the reservation platform choice is now partly a data infrastructure decision.
