## Vendor Moves

The reservation platform landscape has undergone the most concentrated ownership shift in its history, and the past 72 hours added a new layer to the delivery-side consolidation.

**American Express** is acquiring **TheFork** from Tripadvisor for $700 million in cash, with the deal expected to close before the end of 2026 subject to regulatory approval. 
TheFork partners with more than 50,000 restaurants across 11 European countries and serves millions of diners through its mobile app and website.
 
Together with Resy and Tock, these platforms are expected to expand American Express' dining network to 75,000 bookable venues.
 Rafa Marquez, President of International Card Services at American Express, stated: 
"Dining is one of the most important ways people engage with our brand."
 
Separately, American Express is folding Tock's 5,000 eateries, bars and wineries into Resy this summer, doubling its venue library to over 25,000 restaurants
 — still below OpenTable's 65,000 but now closing the gap on a global basis once TheFork closes.

**DoorDash** made two significant restaurant moves this week. 
On September 15, DoorDash invested $125 million in Wonder as part of a strategic partnership
, following Wonder's September 14 deployment of its first automated makeline in Manhattan and a separate robotic bowl maker debut. 
Separately, DoorDash also acquired the Grubhub campus division for $300 million
, deepening its on-campus delivery position. This follows the earlier Deliveroo integration: 
DoorDash announced in February 2026 it is exiting four countries across its Deliveroo and Wolt brands — Qatar, Singapore, Japan, and Uzbekistan — following a multi-month review of country-specific conditions reflecting DoorDash's continued focus on investing where it sees the clearest path to sustainable scale.


**SevenRooms** (DoorDash-owned since its $1.2 billion acquisition in 2025) launched **Channel Connect** in June, 
which consolidates a restaurant's booking data from other platforms into SevenRooms as a backend data tool, allowing it to be used alongside other booking platforms without requiring total exclusivity.
 Meanwhile, 
OpenTable's spring 2026 user agreement update required that subscribers agree that OpenTable is their primary reservations platform
 — a contractual moat move that is driving the platform war into restaurants' legal agreements.

**Toast** continued its platform expansion. 
The company launched Toast Drive-Thru in 2026 as a unified solution for enterprise quick-service operations, bringing together hardware, POS-native software, AI voice ordering integrations and services.
 
The accompanying Spring 2026 release introduced Toast IQ Grow, a marketing-focused solution with an AI-powered Marketing Agent that can build audiences and automate campaigns across channels.
 At the Goldman Sachs Communacopia + Technology Conference on September 9, Toast reported $1.91 billion in Q2 2026 revenue and 23% year-over-year growth. 
NRN noted Uber laid off 10% of its workforce on September 2, while Wonder separately laid off 7% of staff in a streamlining effort on September 1.


**Mews** launched its POS in North America (per Hotel Dive's September 11 Lodging Lowdown). At its Unfold 2026 event, 
Mews launched five new products completing an AI-native operating system that unifies PMS, RMS, messaging, automations, and accounts receivable on a single data model, citing a 476% three-year ROI per IDC research. Mews RMS runs 150 million pricing calculations a day, lifting total revenue per square meter 13.7% on average against hotels without an RMS.
 Founder Richard Valtr declared: "PMS, RMS, CRS, CRM, POS — these aren't real things. They're acronyms invented by software companies to sell more software. Inside Mews, those categories don't exist as separate systems."

Sources: [BusinessWire — Amex/TheFork](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260614939103/en/American-Express-Announces-Proposed-Acquisition-of-TheFork-a-Leading-European-Restaurant-Booking-Platform) · [NRN Technology](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology) · [Boston Globe — Reservation Wars](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/08/17/lifestyle/reservation-platforms-sevenrooms-opentable-resy/) · [Restaurant Technology News — Toast](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/06/toast-advances-restaurant-operations-with-connected-pos-ai-and-guest-engagement-technology/)

So what: The reservation platform is now a multi-year data and ecosystem partnership, not a booking widget. Any mid-market operator or multi-unit restaurant group that has not audited what data flows to their platform's parent company — and what their agreement says about primary-platform status — is carrying unpriced risk. The DoorDash/Wonder and DoorDash/Grubhub moves on the same day signal DoorDash is accelerating its "operating system for local commerce" strategy: delivery, reservations, and now automated food production are being brought under a single commercial umbrella.

## AI Visibility & Discovery

The front door of hotel and restaurant discovery is being structurally rebuilt, and the last two weeks produced the clearest evidence yet of how permanent that shift is.

**Google** officially launched hotel booking through AI Mode in Search on September 1. 
Google has introduced hotel booking through AI Mode in Search, allowing travelers to discover and book their stay "in one seamless conversation." English-speaking users in the U.S. can review and compare hotels, choose a room from integrated partners, and complete the reservation with Google Pay. Google is partnering with 10 travel websites and hotel chains for the initial rollout, including Choice Hotels International, Hilton, and Wyndham Hotels & Resorts.
 The Skift analysis of the architecture reveals a significant distribution consideration: 
Google shares only the minimum data needed to complete a booking — name, payment, occupancy, property — withholding broader chat context, inspiration, and decision signals from partners, even in aggregate, which reduces their visibility into the path to conversion.
 
Bookings coming through conversational AI agents currently represent less than 1 percent of total room nights industry-wide, according to Booking Holdings' own CFO, meaning this remains an early-stage shift rather than a fully transformed booking landscape.


Anna Scozzafava, Choice Hotels' Chief Data, AI and Technology Officer, stated: 
"Travel discovery is evolving rapidly, and Choice Hotels is actively shaping what comes next. By working with Google as an early partner for hotel booking through AI Mode in Search, we're helping make it easier for travelers to find the right Choice hotel for their trip while positioning our franchisees to compete and win in the age of agentic commerce."


**OpenTable** moved its AI Concierge to the homepage in July, a step-change in ambition. 
The double announcement marks OpenTable's most aggressive move yet to position itself not just as a reservation management tool, but as the AI discovery layer that intercepts the "where should I eat?" query before travelers ever reach a search engine or review site.
 
Survey data commissioned by OpenTable found that 60% of respondents planning summer travel say they booked a hotel because of its on-site restaurant. Hotel restaurant dining by travelers rose 13% year-over-year, and overall seated diners at hotel restaurants climbed 7%.
 OpenTable CTO Sagar Mehta framed the goal as meeting diners "where they are, whether researching or booking." 
OpenTable publicly identified Perplexity as the real-time web grounding layer and OpenAI as the underlying LLM inference engine, with Concierge's real-time availability grounding as the architecturally significant differentiator — the system cannot tell a user a restaurant is open when reservation data shows it is full.


For restaurants, the AI visibility problem is now quantified as severe. 
BrightLocal's 2026 consumer survey found 45% of consumers now use AI tools for local business recommendations — up from 6% one year earlier.
 
AI-recommended businesses average 4.3 stars on ChatGPT, 4.1 on Perplexity, and 3.9 on Gemini — a structural rating floor below which restaurants are effectively invisible regardless of other optimisation efforts.


Also on the **Google/restaurant** front, NRN reported on August 7 that Google brought restaurant ordering to Google Maps via its Ask Maps feature. 
Expedia also acquired Layla, a planning app built on conversational AI, to capture travelers as early as the research phase rather than only at the point of final booking.


Sources: [Hotel Dive — Google AI Mode hotel booking](https://www.hoteldive.com/news/google-introduces-ai-mode-hotel-booking/829265/) · [Skift — Google's Agentic Hotel Booking](https://skift.com/2026/08/27/googles-agentic-hotel-booking-tool-comes-to-ai-mode/) · [TechTimes — OpenTable AI Concierge](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320769/20260716/best-hotel-restaurants-2026-opentable-list-goes-live-ai-concierge-moves-homepage.htm)

So what: Google is not yet an OTA, but it is becoming the ambient discovery layer that sits above OTAs and hotel brand sites alike. Mid-market hotel operators should treat Google Business Profile optimisation and Google Hotel Center price-feed connectivity as a distribution priority today — Google's October 1 cutoff for third-party rate sourcing in Hotel Ads creates an urgent compliance deadline. For restaurant groups, the 45% consumer AI adoption for local discovery is the figure that should drive a GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) audit before year-end.

## Funding & M&A

The hospitality tech investment cycle is producing its clearest consensus yet: capital is concentrating in platforms that sit at the core of daily operations, not at the edges.


Forty global hospitality technology startups raised more than $1 billion in the past year (April 2025 to March 2026), with property management systems and artificial intelligence-led platforms attracting the most investment, according to U.K.-based Abode Worldwide's Hospitality Tech Investment Index 2026. The three companies with the largest raises included Mews at $300 million, Limehouse at €75 million, and home-swapping platform Kindred at $125 million across two funding rounds.



PMS was the clear funding leader. Seven PMS companies raised a combined $408.1 million, more than any other category. The list spans established and newer challengers including Amenitiz, Arbio, and Boom.
 
AI-led guest experience platforms were another standout category, with Duve, Chatlyn, Conduit and Canary Technologies raising a combined $152.6 million. Canary, the largest at $80 million, also acquired mobile key platform OpenKey in February 2026.


Jessica Gillingham, CEO of Abode Worldwide, said: 
"Investors are concentrating capital in the platforms that hospitality businesses increasingly depend on every day, especially PMS and AI-led systems that unify operations, improve automation and strengthen the data layer underneath the business. The companies attracting the strongest investor backing share a common trait. They sit close to essential operator workflows and become more valuable over time. Unified systems generate more data, better automation and higher switching costs."


On the restaurant side, 
the restaurant reservation market went through the biggest shake-up in its history: American Express now owns both Resy and Tock (Tock acquired for $400 million in 2024), and DoorDash acquired SevenRooms for $1.2 billion in 2025. OpenTable, long the default, saw its US market share slide from 51 percent to 46 percent between 2022 and 2024, though it still leads in 13 of the 15 top US cities.


Sources: [Hotel Dive — $1B funding report](https://www.hoteldive.com/news/hospitality-tech-attracts-1b-in-funding-pms-ai-leaders/817439/) · [Abode Worldwide Investment Index](https://abodeworldwide.com/hospitality-tech-investment-index/) · [NRN Technology](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology)

So what: For PE-backed hospitality SaaS companies, the investment thesis is consolidating clearly: unified platforms with compounding data advantages attract capital; standalone point-solution tools face a harder sell to operators already drowning in fragmented stacks. The $1B milestone confirms hospitality tech is no longer a niche investor category. For operators on the buy side, the key question is: does this vendor sit close enough to my daily workflows that their data advantage compounds, or are they solving an edge problem I can patch elsewhere?

## Operational AI

The most important AI signal from the restaurant sector this cycle comes not from a product launch but from senior technology executives publicly warning against the hype they helped create.


At the 2026 National Restaurant Association Show, almost universally, restaurant tech leaders answered "artificial intelligence" when asked what the sector is getting wrong — while simultaneously reporting that "AI is the biggest thing of this entire show this year." Shake Shack is using AI to identify and alert managers to potential inefficiencies. Culver's began rolling out computer vision to measure service execution and vehicle flow in its drive-thrus. In April, Dairy Queen expanded a voice AI drive-thru test to more locations in partnership with Presto.


Brendan Sweeney, CEO of Popmenu, offered the sharpest critique: 
the cost of AI tokens required to operate coding services can exceed the salaries of the workers the technology purports to replace. The slow pace of data structure construction and rising cost of electricity make it unlikely that AI services will see a decline in cost. With OpenAI pursuing an IPO, AI developers could face greater pressure from investors to turn a profit — something OpenAI has failed to do.
 Kelly Esten, CMO at Toast, drew the operational line: "There's some parts of running a restaurant that are all about reliability. AI can't replace the core aspects of restaurant operations or restaurant technology. Instead, AI can augment robust underlying programs — improving workflows and speed of service."

For hotels, the shift is in a different direction: from advice to agency. 
The Q2 2026 Hotel Tech Innovation Report from HITEC San Antonio found that the software that used to summarize reviews and suggest a rate now answers the guest, works the lead, and moves the rate on its own. The work that stays with the team is the part that needs a human: the exception, the relationship, the call that carries real risk. Every product in the report cleared one bar: recent, public feedback from working hoteliers showing the AI is moving their business, not only demoing well.


In hotel revenue management, the human-AI boundary is being drawn with unusual precision. 
Jeff Wermager, VP of sales and revenue management at My Place Hotels of America, described the operating model as: "AI owns the high-volume, repeatable pricing decisions; our revenue management team owns the strategic exceptions — new property ramp-up, market disruptions and negotiated rates."
 Robert Matsuoka, CTO at Duetto, clarified: 
"At Duetto we draw a hard line between automated execution and autonomous decision-making. Our forecasting and pricing run on machine-learning models, not agents. The revenue manager sets the guardrails: rate floors and ceilings, competitive positioning. The system optimizes within those bounds, not outside them. Where AI earns its place today is the work around the decision. That's the busywork, not the pricing call itself."


Sources: [Restaurant Dive — NRA Show](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/restaurant-technology-executives-warn-against-ai-overdependence/821024/) · [Hotel Tech Report Q2 2026](https://hoteltechreport.com/news/q2-2026-hotel-ai-trends-report) · [Hotel Management Aug/Sep 2026](https://hotelmanagement.mydigitalpublication.com/publication/?i=868825&article_id=5195912&view=articleBrowser)

So what: The practical operator consensus is now the same on both sides of the industry: narrow, specific AI deployments tied to measurable operational pressure points survive; broad AI transformation promises rarely make it past early pilots. For mid-market restaurant groups, the question to ask every vendor is "what specific operational outcome does this produce, and what is your attribution methodology?" For hotel operators, the 59% preference for human-led check-in (Mews survey) is a guide to deployment priority, not a barrier.

## Data & Integration

Every major hospitality AI product announcement in this cycle eventually runs into the same constraint: a broken data layer underneath.


PMS was the single largest holder of guest signal, but rarely more than 20% of the total signal across a chain. Full-service luxury brands have a smaller PMS share because spa, F&B, and concierge systems carry more of the signal. Fewer than one in four hotels have core systems — PMS, CRS, RMS, loyalty, and CRM — fully integrated in a way that supports a unified guest profile.


**PAR Technology** launched **Guest360** on September 1, addressing the equivalent problem in restaurants. 
Built alongside PAR Punchh, Guest360 uses Profile Stitching to consolidate a guest's activity across POS, kiosk, drive-thru, and mobile app into a single lasting profile rather than fragmented records, with the unified view then powering PAR Intelligence for audience, campaign, and next-step recommendations. CEO Savneet Singh stated: "Most restaurants know their loyalty members well, but that's only a fraction of their guests."


**Shiji** published a detailed position paper on hotel data architecture arguing that 
competitive advantage will depend less on the number of systems a property deploys and more on its ability to integrate, govern and secure data across the entire technology ecosystem. Integration strategies and security strategies cannot be developed independently — every new connection between the PMS, POS, and the wider technology ecosystem must be designed with data governance, access controls, and cybersecurity principles in mind.


**MCP (Model Context Protocol)** has emerged as the de-facto integration standard for agentic hospitality AI in H1 2026. 
OpenTable has built connections to Microsoft Copilot, Amazon Alexa+, Google's Model Context Protocol (which enabled the Gemini Spark integration), Salesforce Agentforce for autonomous agent workflows, and voice AI platforms Slang AI and PolyAI for handling restaurant phone inquiries.


Sources: [Hospitality AI Intelligence Daily Brief](https://briefings.inverisla.com/) · [Restaurant Technology News — PAR Guest360](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/09/par-technology-launches-guest360-to-bring-unified-guest-understanding-to-every-restaurant-transaction/) · [Shiji — PMS/POS Data Integration](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4133265/beyond-pms-and-pos-building-a-connected-hotel-data-ecosystem)

So what: As Abode Worldwide's report noted, "AI can't compensate for scattered data, disconnected systems or poor processes — if a hotel's underlying systems are fragmented, AI will only make operational problems more visible." CIOs evaluating agentic AI vendors should run a 90-day data-layer audit before committing to contracts — specifically mapping which system is authoritative for the guest entity, and what the vendor's data-portability terms say at contract termination. For restaurant multi-unit operators, the PAR Guest360 launch is the template: the 5-10% of transactions captured through loyalty are not the full guest signal; Profile Stitching across all touchpoints is the next competitive layer.

## Governance & Security


A white paper translating the White House's June 2, 2026 AI Executive Order into operational priorities for hotels argues the Order signals that AI has crossed from an experimental productivity tool to a critical layer of operational infrastructure that governments now treat as a matter of national security. For hospitality, the modern hotel has quietly become a dense, interconnected intelligent environment binding identity, payments, guest data, building systems, IoT, and energy orchestration.


The regulatory calendar is compressing: 
The Colorado 2026 Automated Decision-Making Technology Act takes effect January 1, 2027. PCI DSS v4.0.1 has new requirements active since March 2025. EU Regulation 2024/1028 on short-term rentals is active from May 2026. Local tax authorities are requiring more granular reporting. Compliance isn't overhead anymore — it's a barrier to entry. Vendors that handle it natively have a structural advantage that no amount of AI marketing can replicate.



A Mews May 2026 survey found 98% of hoteliers had used AI across operations in the prior six months, yet 41% have no formal AI policy.
 That gap — ubiquitous deployment, minimal governance — is exactly what the regulatory wave is targeting.

Sources: [Hospitality AI Intelligence Daily Brief](https://briefings.inverisla.com/) · [Chekin — Hotel PMS Systems 2026](https://chekin.com/en/blog/hotel-pms-systems/)

So what: The practical first step is an AI system inventory — mapping every place AI is already operating inside the property before a board-level AI strategy is drafted. For PE-backed hospitality SaaS vendors, buyer procurement teams are beginning to ask AI governance questions that previously belonged only to enterprise healthcare and finance. Vendors without audit trails and explainability documentation are increasingly losing procurement reviews to those who have them.

## People & Skills


The HSMAI Foundation's 2026 AI Talent Pipeline Report found a "Confidence-Preparedness Paradox": students rated their confidence in applying AI to work tasks at 3.24 out of 5, while rating their academic program's preparation at just 2.78 out of 5 — implying students are self-teaching AI through experimentation more than structured curriculum.


Brian Hicks, President and CEO of HSMAI, stated: 
"Students are entering the workforce with established AI habits and confidence, but employers must equip future talent with the training, governance, and decision-making frameworks needed to apply AI tools successfully in real-world hotel operations."



The State of Distribution 2025 hotel benchmark report underscores that AI adoption fails without staff enablement. According to the report, only 15% of hotels call their stack "fully automated," largely due to training gaps.


Sources: [HSMAI Foundation via Hospitality Net](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4132558/hsmai-foundation-releases-new-ai-talent-pipeline-report-examining-the-future-of-hospitality-workforce-readiness) · [RateGain — AI in Hospitality](https://rategain.com/blog/ai-in-hospitality/)

So what: The workforce AI gap is a two-sided problem: incoming talent has habits but lacks governance frameworks, and existing operators have AI tools but lack the structured onboarding to use them well. Hospitality operators who build structured AI onboarding — covering validation practices, escalation protocols, and decision-making frameworks — before deploying AI in guest-facing roles will compound advantage as adoption becomes universal.

## WHO TO WATCH

**Rafa Marquez (President, International Card Services, American Express)** — His stated rationale that "dining is one of the most important ways people engage with our brand" is backing a $700M acquisition of TheFork and a $400M prior acquisition of Tock, both within 24 months. If the TheFork deal closes before year-end, Amex will operate the world's largest bookable dining network by venue count. The operational question — whether three distinct platform architectures can be integrated without alienating the restaurant-operator trust each was built on — is what to watch over the next 18 months.

**Brendan Sweeney (CEO, Popmenu)** — His public warning at the NRA Show that AI token costs can exceed the salaries of workers being replaced, and that OpenAI has yet to achieve profitability, is the sharpest corrective against the vendor consensus that AI is immediately cost-saving for restaurants. Worth tracking as a bellwether: if independent restaurant operators start asking "what does this actually cost to run?" before signing AI vendor contracts, Sweeney's framing will be vindicated in the market.

**Anna Scozzafava (Chief Data, AI and Technology Officer, Choice Hotels)** — Her positioning of Choice's early Google AI Mode partnership as a move to "compete and win in the age of agentic commerce" is the clearest articulation of the franchised hotel brand's strategic challenge: be visible in the AI layer or cede discovery to OTAs who are already embedded in it. Whether the direct-booking channel benefit materialises — or whether Google becomes the new OTA intermediary — is the distribution question of the next two years.

**Robert Matsuoka (CTO, Duetto)** — His distinction between "automated execution" and "autonomous decision-making" — where the revenue manager sets guardrails and the system optimizes within them — is the most precise definition of human-AI collaboration in revenue management currently circulating in hotel trade press. That framing is gaining traction with cautious mid-market operators and is worth tracking as the reference architecture against which other RMS vendors will be evaluated.

**Joel Montaniel (Co-Founder, SevenRooms)** — His statement that "delivery and dine-in have typically been siloed data sets — if a customer has ordered six times and is coming into the restaurant for the first time, are they a first-time customer or a seventh-time customer?" defines the strategic rationale for the DoorDash acquisition. The degree to which DoorDash can operationalise that cross-channel guest profile — without triggering the data-trust concerns Boston operators have already started voicing — will determine whether the $1.2 billion acquisition creates durable competitive advantage or becomes a cautionary tale about brand mismatch.
