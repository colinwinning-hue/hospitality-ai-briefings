## AI Visibility & Discovery

Google's agentic hotel booking launch exposes a two-speed market where mega-brands are already inside the funnel, and independent operators are not.

**Google** formally launched hotel booking through AI Mode in Search on September 1. 
English-speaking users in the U.S. can now review and compare hotels, choose a room from integrated partners, and complete the reservation with Google Pay. Google is partnering with 10 travel websites and hotel chains for the initial rollout, including Choice Hotels International, Hilton, and Wyndham Hotels & Resorts.
 According to the Choice Hotels press release, 
travelers "can describe what they're looking for in plain English" and receive a visual list of hotel options along with side-by-side comparisons.
 Anna Scozzafava, Choice's chief data, AI, and technology officer, said: "By working with Google as an early partner for hotel booking through AI Mode in Search, we're helping make it easier for travelers to find the right Choice hotel for their trip while positioning our franchisees to compete and win in the age of agentic commerce." The Sept 1 launch was preceded by Google's August 7 confirmation to Skift of a limited U.S. test: 
Booking Holdings CEO Glenn Fogel said on an earnings call: "I think Google may have come out today, a small percentage of their traffic seeing their agentic booking … we are one of the first with them."
 The underlying infrastructure matters: 
Google's new Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP) could allow bookings to be completed directly inside a chat session — Amadeus is its first B2B foundational partner for lodging.
 Critically, though: 
bookings coming through conversational AI agents currently represent less than 1% of total room nights industry-wide, according to Booking Holdings' own CFO.
 The European dimension adds complexity: 
the European Commission fined Google 890 million euros naming hotels among self-preferenced verticals, with 60 days to comply — a window that closes in the second half of September 2026.


**DoorDash** launched its "Ask DoorDash" conversational chatbot on June 11. 
The chatbot allows users to order food and make reservations with text prompts and photos. Users can tell it they want a "filling dinner for a family of 4," and the app will surface restaurants alongside a personalized blurb explaining why it matches the search. Ask DoorDash for Reservations lets users ask for "a table for two downtown for a date-night dinner around 8 PM."
 This sits on top of DoorDash's own research: 
22% of consumers have already used AI search tools like ChatGPT or Google Gemini to choose a restaurant, and restaurant listing sites like DoorDash account for more than 41% of the sources AI tools cite when recommending restaurants.
 The supply-side gap is stark: 
75% of consumers say they're comfortable using AI for reservations, but only 28% of operators are using AI to manage calls and customer service, with an estimated 40% of reservation calls going unanswered industry-wide.


**OpenAI/ChatGPT** has now integrated DoorDash, OpenTable, Resy (via Amex), and TheFork (via the ChatGPT apps ecosystem), effectively becoming a meta-layer above individual platforms. 
OpenAI announced integrations with apps including DoorDash, Uber Eats, OpenTable, and Instacart, allowing consumers to chat with apps to book a trip, listen to music, or order food.
 **Resy** separately launched Resy Reservations inside ChatGPT and Claude via Anthropic integration, giving Amex Platinum cardholders a conversational reservation path.

For restaurants, new benchmarks from SOCi and BrightEdge have quantified the AI visibility gap: 
AI-recommended local businesses average 4.3 stars on ChatGPT, 4.1 on Perplexity, and 3.9 on Gemini — meaning the rating threshold to be cited at all is structural. SOCi found AI is 3–30x more selective than traditional local search: only ~1.2% of locations are recommended by ChatGPT versus 35.9% appearing in Google's local 3-pack.
 BrightEdge documented restaurants going from a 10% AI Overviews trigger rate in early 2025 to 78% by February 2026 — one of the fastest-growing verticals tracked.

Sources: [Hotel Dive, Sept. 1](https://www.hoteldive.com/news/google-introduces-ai-mode-hotel-booking/829265/) | [Skift, Aug. 7](https://skift.com/2026/08/07/google-confirms-hotel-agentic-booking-is-now-in-testing/) | [DoorDash Trends Report](https://about.doordash.com/en-us/news/doordash-restaurant-industry-trends-report-2026)

So what: For a mid-market hotel or independent restaurant, AI is no longer a future channel — it is an active one being controlled by brands that got into Google's UCP and DoorDash's citation loop first. The immediate action is profile completeness: accurate hours, menu data, photos, and reviews are now ranking signals for LLMs, not just SEO. Hotels not in Google's initial partner set should treat direct UCP/Business Profile optimization as a distribution priority equivalent to OTA parity.

## Vendor Moves

The restaurant reservation market has entered a full consolidation war, while hotel PMS vendors are expanding aggressively into adjacent stacks.

**American Express** announced in February the merger of Tock into Resy, creating a 25,000-venue network. 
The move boosts Resy's venue count to 25,000 and will sunset the Tock brand, formally uniting two onetime startups against reservations market leader OpenTable.
 The commercial case is proven: 
in the three weeks following the announcement of a $400 Platinum card Resy credit, there was a 36% increase in Resy reservations made by Platinum cardholders, and a five times increase in daily average account linking. People using the Resy credit are spending, on average, 25% more.
 The RTN Yelp article also mentions American Express is in the process of acquiring TheFork (the European reservation dominant) for $700 million — a move that would extend Amex's dining ecosystem into France, Spain, and Italy at scale. A Resy-Toast POS integration is separately underway. Pablo Rivero, CEO of Resy and Tock, said: "Resy will be the singular app for the best culinary experiences used by the hospitality world's most ambitious operators."

**SevenRooms** (owned by DoorDash) launched Channel Connect on June 17 — a free, open-license desktop app that aggregates reservations from all booking channels into one system. 
Channel Connect will allow restaurants to manage bookings from different platforms all in one place. The program will pull in bookings from reservation apps, search engines, social media, review sites, the restaurant's website, AI agents, and hotel concierges, injecting them all into the restaurant's preferred system of record.
 
This includes reservations from "major booking platforms," which means SevenRooms' chief rivals, OpenTable and Resy. It is offered free under an open license. Channel Connect was built as a response to OpenTable's April requirement that clients make it their system of record — SevenRooms framing it as "no single platform has to be the gatekeeper to your operations."
 The Boston Globe's on-the-ground reporting from Boston's restaurant community adds texture: 
one Boston restaurateur reported being offered six-figure signing bonuses to switch platforms. The competition is "powerful corporate mergers and big incentives" heating up what operators call "reservation wars."
 One operator said directly: "It's less about the reservations, and it's all about the data."

**Toast** launched Toast Drive-Thru on April 14, a unified enterprise QSR drive-thru platform. 
Toast Drive-Thru replaces fragmented systems with a future-ready platform across hardware, POS-native software, AI voice ordering integrations, and end-to-end support. The product includes AI voice ordering integrations with partners like Incept AI to automate order taking, Speed of Service reporting, and Drive-Thru KDS enhancements.
 Steve Fredette, President of Toast, said: "In the drive-thru lane, every second matters. By seamlessly integrating POS, KDS, outdoor digital menu boards, and AI voice ordering partnerships, Toast Drive-Thru gives operators the tools they need to keep pace with peak demand today." Toast also separately announced an agentic food-ordering integration with Google Maps' Ask Maps feature (August 6), enabling voice or text restaurant queries to flow through to a completed order. On the marketing side: 
Toast's first AI-enabled workflow product, Toast IQ Grow, is poised to become the company's fastest-growing product, with executives expecting it to generate $10 million in annual revenue since its May introduction, per CEO Aman Narang. Over time, he said, Toast will deploy "agentic products" for demand forecasting, labor schedules, income tax help, food costs, and availability.
 Toast also added a record 9,500 new locations in Q2, bringing its total to 180,000, with annualized recurring run-rate rising 25% to $2.4 billion.

**Mews** launched point-of-sale in North America in September 2026 (per Hotel Dive), extending its platform beyond PMS into F&B — a continuation of the acquire-and-expand strategy that saw Mews absorb Flexkeeping and DataChat in late 2025 after its $300M Series D.

**Yelp for Restaurants** expanded its front-of-house suite, presenting at the 2026 NRA Show as an AI-enabled reservation, waitlist, and phone management provider. 
Yelp's 2026 Spring Product Release introduced a broader Yelp Assistant designed to help consumers move from search to answers and actions, plus an enhanced Menu Vision that overlays dish and drink photos on text-based menus through a phone camera.
 
Long known as a consumer discovery platform, Yelp has expanded into a broader front-of-house suite that helps operators manage reservations, waitlists, seating, guest communication, performance visibility, and AI-powered phone interactions.


Sources: [Fast Company, Feb. 24](https://www.fastcompany.com/91496951/amex-resy-tock-restaurant-reservation-wars) | [NRN, June 17](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology/sevenrooms-launches-a-reservations-aggregator-for-restaurants) | [BusinessWire, April 14](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260414359751/en/Toast-Launches-Unified-Drive-Thru-Solution-to-Modernize-Enterprise-Quick-Service-Restaurant-Operations)

So what: The reservation platform is no longer a booking utility — it is the primary data-capture layer for the entire guest relationship. Operators choosing or renewing platform contracts are effectively choosing which payment network and delivery giant owns access to their guest profiles. The free Channel Connect play from SevenRooms is an aggressive open-ecosystem move that tests whether operators will accept DoorDash as the neutral infrastructure behind their reservations.

## Operational AI

Restaurant AI is delivering measurable back-office ROI, but a significant adoption gap persists.

The most consequential data point of this cycle comes from Restaurant365's mid-year study of 420 operators. 
Of operators currently using AI in at least one back-office function (62%), 61% said they have seen reduced food costs since implementation. 62% also saw reduced labor costs, while 88% reported AI was "saving time every week." Nearly a third said AI helped reduce costs by at least 6%.
 Tony Smith, CEO of Restaurant365, said: "The operators who are consistently pulling ahead aren't simply adopting AI, they're using it to make faster, better decisions every day." Barriers remain for non-adopters: 
more than a third cite data privacy and security concerns, while 34% mentioned output accuracy, 29% implementation cost, and 18% uncertainty about where to begin.


**Toast**'s own usage data corroborates: 
in Q1 2026 alone, Toast data shows more than 179,000 users across 125,000+ restaurant locations actively used Toast IQ in just 90 days.
 The most-used prompt — used by 15% of all Toast IQ restaurants — was "Create a short, easy-to-read daily briefing for my restaurant." 
In Q1 2026, 33,000 times restaurant operators said "please" or "thank you" to their AI assistant.


**SevenRooms** published one of the clearest outcome-attributed case studies in the cycle. 
Casper Hospitality put Voice AI on the phones and in one month saw 3,800+ calls handled, 850+ covers created, and $28K in booked revenue. Across SevenRooms customers, AI-powered messaging shows a 27% decrease in response time for guest inquiries.


Sources: [Restaurant365](https://www.restaurant365.com/in-the-news/would-you-like-fries-with-those-prompts-operators-using-ai-are-cutting-food-labor-other-costs/) | [Toast Blog](https://pos.toasttab.com/blog/on-the-line/ai-for-restaurants) | [SevenRooms](https://sevenrooms.com/blog/restaurant-AI/)

So what: The AI ROI story in restaurant back-office is now evidence-based, not theoretical — operators using it across at least one function are seeing 6%+ cost reduction. The barrier to adoption is now trust and change management, not capability. Any mid-market operator not running at least a pilot in scheduling or food-cost analytics is ceding a margin advantage to the 62% already in market.

## Data & Integration

Every major hotel chain's AI roadmap is bottlenecked at the same layer: fragmented guest identity data.


Every major hotel chain has now committed publicly to an agentic AI roadmap — Marriott's $1B+ agentic mesh, Hilton's AI Planner, Hyatt and Accor inside ChatGPT, IHG's AI-enhanced CRM. All of them are bottlenecked at the same layer: the property management system was built when a guest was a stay at a property, not a relationship across the chain.
 The data distribution problem is severe: 
the loyalty platform holds membership history, the CRM holds campaigns and channel preferences, F&B POS holds dining behavior, the spa system holds wellness behavior, the mobile app holds digital-key activity and in-stay messaging, and the channel manager holds OTA-originated bookings — often with masked email addresses that don't match anything else.
 The integration reality: 
hoteliers now expect their PMS to serve as the central nervous system connecting every app, department, and data stream — 44% say CRM and guest-marketing integrations are critical to daily operations, 40% rank business intelligence dashboards as key links. "The PMS is shifting from being a standalone platform to an orchestration layer," per HotelTechReport's 2026 PMS Impact Study.


The replatforming wave is real — 
Marriott to Oracle Opera Cloud, Motel One completing a 100+ property migration to the same platform, Hilton breaking from Opera entirely in favour of HotelKey, PPHE and Rotana standardising on Opera Cloud across their estates
 — but Apptad's analysis is blunt: replatforming alone cannot fix the entity layer. A clean, governed, real-time master data foundation for the guest must sit above all PMS platforms. The duplicate profile rate adds urgency: identity resolution platforms working with hotel chains report typical duplicate-profile rates of 6–16% of total profile count. A chain with 200 million guest profiles and an 8% duplicate rate is reasoning over 16 million ghost guests — and every AI use case runs on that broken foundation.


Cloudbeds' 2026 State of Independent Hotels Report identified "the connectivity imperative" as a defining trend: as margins tighten and competition increases, disconnected systems are no longer just inefficient — they're a risk to performance.


Sources: [Apptad](https://apptad.com/insights/the-hotel-industrys-ai-problem-lives-in-the-pms/) | [HotelTechReport 2026 PMS Study](https://hoteltechreport.com/news/2026-hotel-pms-report) | [Cloudbeds](https://www.cloudbeds.com/articles/pms-integrations/)

So what: For PE-backed hotel SaaS companies, the "agentic AI" pitch that relies on PMS data alone is structurally weaker than one built on an entity layer above the PMS. For hotel operators, the 90-day diagnostic isn't "which AI model do we buy" — it's "can we produce a single, de-duplicated guest profile in real time?" The answer for most mid-market chains is currently no.

## Funding & M&A

Hospitality tech hit a $1B+ funding milestone — but Q2 2026 showed a sharp deceleration, with acquisitions outpacing rounds.


Hospitality technology startups raised more than $1 billion across 40 companies between April 2025 and March 2026, with property management systems and AI-led platforms attracting the largest share of investment, according to Abode Worldwide's Hospitality Tech Investment Index 2026.
 
The surge was most visible in a 90-day burst between December 2025 and February 2026, when Mews ($300 million), Kindred ($125 million), and Limehome (€75 million) raised back-to-back.
 
PMS was the clear funding leader: seven companies raised a combined $408.1 million. AI-led guest experience platforms were another standout, with Duve, Chatlyn, Conduit, and Canary Technologies raising a combined $152.6 million.
 Canary Technologies separately acquired mobile key platform OpenKey in February 2026. Jessica Gillingham, CEO of Abode Worldwide, said: "Unified systems generate more data, better automation and higher switching costs. This is a compounding dynamic that is proving more appealing to investors."

Q2 2026 told a different story: 
disclosed funding rounds were thin, with just three AI-native rounds closing — Smartness at €47 million Series B, Pricepoint at $6.6 million seed for AI revenue management, and Besty AI at $3.75 million seed. Acquisitions ran ahead of rounds, with Lighthouse acquiring Hotelrank.ai, RealTime Reservation acquiring STAY, and Ixigo taking a majority stake in Brevistay.


On the restaurant side, **DoorDash** made two significant M&A moves this week: on September 15, DoorDash invested $125M in Wonder as part of a strategic partnership, and separately acquired Grubhub's campus university delivery division for $300 million. These moves extend DoorDash's footprint into captive-audience food delivery and vertically integrated restaurant concepts — a very different strategic bet from the SevenRooms ($1.2B) acquisition of 2025.

Sources: [Hotel Online / Abode Worldwide](https://www.hotel-online.com/news/hospitality-tech-drew-over-1-billion-in-the-last-year-with-pms-and-ai-led-platforms-capturing-biggest-share) | [Abode Worldwide Q2 2026](https://abodeworldwide.com/hospitality-tech-funding-q2-2026/) | [NRN, Sept. 15](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology/doordash-invests-125m-in-wonder-as-part-of-strategic-partnership)

So what: The funding narrative is bifurcating. Large rounds are going to platforms that sit at the centre of daily operations (PMS, AI guest experience) with compounding data advantages. Point solutions without clear workflow depth are facing higher proof bars. For founders: build toward the operator's system of record, not around it.

## Governance & Security

The White House AI Executive Order has reached the hotel industry's policy agenda; regulators are also turning their attention directly to hotel AI governance.


On June 2, 2026, the White House issued an Executive Order titled "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security." For hospitality, the relevance is not theoretical. The modern hotel has quietly become a dense, interconnected intelligent environment — binding together identity, payments, guest data, building systems, IoT, energy orchestration, and AI-driven workflows.
 Terence Ronson's white paper for Hospitality Net frames the strategic shift clearly: 
AI capability is now treated as national strategic capability. For hospitality, the competitive question is shifting from "How many AI features do we have?" to "Can we govern AI responsibly, secure our connected systems, and turn operational trust into guest experience?"


Regulators are separately turning attention to hotel AI governance. 
Regulators are increasing scrutiny of hotel AI governance as AI becomes embedded in guest personalisation, pricing, and travel discovery. Unlike traditional OTA rankings or SEO, AI-driven discovery lacks transparency in how results are generated, making it harder for hotels to understand or correct visibility outcomes. Hotel operators are now being pushed toward structured AI governance frameworks including clearer documentation of how guest data is collected and used, stronger vendor oversight, and improved data standardisation.



VikingCloud reports 82% of North American hotels suffered cyberattacks last summer, with AI-powered phishing, IoT exploits, and vendor vulnerabilities emerging as top 2026 threats. Hotels are rapidly adopting technology, but cybersecurity maturity isn't keeping up.
 A key finding: 
48% of hotel IT leaders said they lack confidence in their team's ability to detect AI-generated attacks, with 22% saying hackers now outpace their defenses.


On the compliance side, Colorado's 2026 Automated Decision-Making Technology Act takes effect January 1, 2027, and the FTC published final orders in August 2026 concerning deceptive AI-powered active-listening marketing claims with a $930,000 total settlement — a signal that marketing AI governance is now an enforcement priority, not just a policy discussion.

Sources: [Hospitality Net, June 3](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/opinion/4132771/when-national-ai-security-becomes-a-hospitality-imperative) | [Yahoo Finance / Hotel Management Network](https://finance.yahoo.com/sectors/technology/articles/regulators-turn-attention-hotel-ai-062109728.html) | [Nomadix](https://nomadix.com/2026-hotel-cybersecurity-predictions/)

So what: Governance is becoming a procurement criterion. An AI vendor that cannot produce an audit trail, explain data residency, and demonstrate human override capability will increasingly fail enterprise hotel RFPs and cyber insurer reviews. For mid-market operators: the minimum viable governance step is an inventory of every AI system already running in the property — including features that vendors switched on after the original contract was signed.

## People & Skills

Hospitality ranks as the most exposed sector in the AI talent shortage — and the gap between student confidence and actual preparedness is widening.


ManpowerGroup's 2026 Talent Shortage Survey finds 72% of employers globally reporting hiring difficulty. Hospitality (74%) is tied with Public Sector as the second-most talent-scarce sector. AI Model & Application Development (20%) and AI Literacy (19%) now lead the global ranking of skills in demand. "Companies are responding with upskilling and more flexible workforce models, recognizing they will need to hire for potential," said Jonas Prising, ManpowerGroup Chair & CEO.



Hospitality ranks as the most exposed industry for AI readiness, according to a 2026 workforce analysis by Resume Now — followed by healthcare, financial services, and logistics. These sectors rely heavily on frontline roles, where training is harder to scale and daily operations leave little room for upskilling.
 
A common pattern: AI tools are being rolled out faster than workers can be trained to use them effectively, creating friction and increasing the risk of errors. The challenge becomes less about the technology and more about preparing people to work alongside it.


HSMAI's talent research adds a specific pipeline concern: 
hospitality students rated their confidence in applying AI to workplace tasks at 3.24 out of 5, but their academic preparation at only 2.78 out of 5 — a "confidence-preparedness paradox" where they develop AI skills through independent experimentation rather than structured coursework.
 Amanda Voss, HSMAI Foundation chair, said: "Companies that invest early in AI education and operational readiness will be best positioned to develop future commercial talent."


BCG research shows only 5% of companies offer AI training at scale, and just 26% have developed the capabilities to move beyond proofs of concept and generate tangible value. Hotels that skip this foundational work will join the failure statistics. Traditional hotel work remains task-based; agentic work becomes outcome-based.


Sources: [ManpowerGroup 2026 Talent Shortage Survey](https://www.manpowergroup.com/en/news-releases/news/global-talent-shortage-reaches-turning-point-as-ai-skills-claim-top-spot) | [Allwork.Space](https://allwork.space/2026/04/hospitality-ranks-least-prepared-for-ai-as-workforce-gaps-widen/) | [Asian Hospitality / HSMAI](https://www.asianhospitality.com/hospitality-students-ai-training-gap/)

So what: For hotel operators running AI deployment programmes, workforce literacy is now the rate-limiting factor — not technology access. The near-term investment that pays fastest is structured AI onboarding and clear operational playbooks, not another AI vendor contract.
