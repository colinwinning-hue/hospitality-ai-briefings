## Funding & M&A

**The restaurant reservation market has undergone its most concentrated restructuring in a generation**, compressing what was a two-player fight into a three-way platform war with payment rails, delivery flywheels, and tens of thousands of European venues now in play.

**DoorDash** closed its $1.2 billion acquisition of **SevenRooms** in mid-2025 and 
via SevenRooms is now offering restaurants voice AI that can take reservations over the phone and event management capabilities including group bookings and private dining.
 
The reservations feature, which launched in September under the name "Going Out," allows users to book tables and earn dining-related rewards.
 DoorDash CEO framing is explicit: this is not a reservation side-hustle. 
"Delivery and dine-in have typically been siloed data sets," SevenRooms co-founder Joel Montaniel said. "So if a customer has ordered six times, and they're coming into the restaurant for the first time, are they a first-time customer or a seventh-time customer?"


**American Express** escalated in June with a proposed $700 million acquisition of **TheFork** from Tripadvisor. 
Announcing the proposed deal in a Monday (June 15) news release, American Express said it was designed to build on the company's broader dining strategy following its acquisitions of Resy and Tock. Put together, the three platforms expand the Amex dining network to 75,000 venues.
 
"Dining is one of the most important ways people engage with our brand," said Rafa Marquez, President of International Card Services at American Express. "Over time, the proposed acquisition would help us enrich our differentiated Membership Model by offering Card Members more ways to discover, book and access great restaurants."
 Resy and Tock are also merging under the Resy brand this summer. 
The news solidifies American Express as a large and unique player in restaurant tech. The company brings together high-rolling Amex cardholders and a large network of restaurants, while also offering restaurants tools to help serve their customers better.


**Toast** reported Q2 2026 earnings on August 4 that crystallise its own platform ambition. 
"The first half of 2026 reflects the strength we have across the business. In Q2, recurring gross profit streams grew 28%, GAAP Operating Income margins expanded to 26%, and we added a record 9,500 net locations," said Toast CEO Aman Narang.
 
Narang added that Toast IQ Grow, the company's AI-powered marketing product, has become the fastest-growing offering in Toast's history.
 The company illustrated that claim with a case study: 
a Louisiana restaurant, Spirits Food & Friends, consolidated multiple business systems onto Toast before adopting Toast IQ Grow — the restaurant reduced its outside marketing costs by 70% while generating more than $100,000 in marketing-attributed sales in less than two months.
 Separately, 
American Express and Toast announced a strategic partnership, with plans to build tools combining the power of Resy and Tock's guestbook capabilities and Toast's Digital Chits technology, allowing restaurant staff to easily view important customer information directly on Toast Go handhelds and POS terminals during service.


On the hotel side, 
forty global hospitality technology startups raised more than $1 billion in the past year, with property management systems and AI-led platforms attracting the most investment. Seven PMS companies raised a combined $408.1 million. The three biggest raises landed almost back-to-back: Mews ($300 million), Kindred ($125 million), and Limehome (€75 million).
 
Four AI-powered guest experience platforms raised a combined $152.6 million: Duve, Chatlyn, Conduit, and Canary Technologies (the largest at $80 million, which also acquired mobile key platform OpenKey in February 2026).


Sources: [CNBC / Expedite News](https://www.expedite.news/p/doordash-reservations-are-here) · [American Express Newsroom](https://www.americanexpress.com/en-us/newsroom/articles/travel-and-dining/american-express-announces-proposed-acquisition-of-thefork--a-le.html) · [PYMNTS / Toast Q2](https://www.pymnts.com/earnings/2026/toast-adds-record-9500-restaurants-while-betting-big-on-ai/)

So what: The three platforms are no longer just reservation tools — they are front doors into payment ecosystems with distinct data strategies. Operators choosing between them are choosing which data flywheel to feed. Mid-market groups should benchmark their current platform fees against what DoorDash (delivery data integration), Amex (affluent cardholder spend), and Booking Holdings/OpenTable (70M+ diner network) each offer before the next contract renewal.

## AI Visibility & Discovery

**The booking path for both hotels and restaurants is being rewritten by agentic platforms**, with concrete deployments now in production rather than announced.

**IHG Hotels & Resorts** launched the most significant brand-operated hotel AI product of this news cycle. 
On July 28, 2026, IHG announced the beta launch of AI-powered conversational search on IHG.com and the IHG One Rewards mobile app, making it easier and faster than ever for guests to move seamlessly from inspiration and discovery to booking one of more than 7,000 hotels globally.
 
IHG CEO Elie Maalouf said: "It's a milestone that builds on years of strategic investment in our digital foundations, including the unification of our data in the cloud and the continued evolution of our best-in-class technology platforms."
 
A new AI-optimized hotel content platform will also roll out over the next several months, making it easier for hotels to upload rich, detailed information and improve how their properties are discovered, supporting new content such as floor plans, videos and updated imagery.


**Google** is repositioning itself from referral layer to transaction layer across both sectors. 
On May 19, Google named hotel booking as the next vertical for its Universal Commerce Protocol, bringing agentic shopping to travel. The Agent Payments Protocol will enable AI agents to complete purchases on users' behalf, starting with Google's Gemini Spark AI.
 
UCP for Lodging enables any AI interaction to be converted into an instant room reservation executed directly within Google surfaces. Crucially, unlike a booking made through an OTA, the hotel retains Merchant of Record status — the guest remains the hotel's guest.
 On the restaurant side, 
Google launched agentic booking capabilities inside AI Mode in Search for UK users on April 10, 2026, with eight partner platforms: TheFork, SevenRooms, ResDiary, Mozrest, Foodhub, Dojo, DesignMyNight, and OpenTable.
 
According to Google, AI Mode had reached 75 million daily active users by January 2026.


**OpenTable** (Booking Holdings) made its most aggressive discovery play to date. 
OpenTable published its 2026 Top 100 Hotel Restaurants in America — drawn from more than 10 million verified diner reviews — and simultaneously upgraded its generative AI dining assistant, Concierge, to operate directly from the platform's homepage for the first time.
 The technical architecture is substantive: 
OpenTable publicly identified Perplexity as the real-time web grounding layer and OpenAI as the underlying LLM inference engine. Concierge's answer on availability draws from live reservation inventory — the same database OpenTable uses to confirm actual bookings. That real-time availability grounding is the architecturally significant part: the system cannot tell you that a restaurant is open when the reservation data shows it is full.
 OpenTable has also 
built connections to Microsoft Copilot, Amazon Alexa+, and Google's Model Context Protocol.



Google and OpenAI are diverging strategically: while Google is expanding direct transaction capabilities inside its AI ecosystem, OpenAI has reportedly stepped back from enabling direct purchases inside ChatGPT.


Sources: [IHG Press Release](https://www.ihgplc.com/en/news-and-media/news-releases/2026/ihg-hotels-and-resorts-launches-ai-conversational-search-across-its-digital-channels-transforming-the-guest-experience) · [Skift / Google UCP](https://skift.com/2026/05/19/google-names-hotels-as-next-vertical-for-agentic-shopping/) · [TechTimes / OpenTable Concierge](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320769/20260716/best-hotel-restaurants-2026-opentable-list-goes-live-ai-concierge-moves-homepage.htm)

So what: Hotels that still treat discovery as a marketing function rather than an operational one will feel this cycle first. Machine-readable rates, explicit cancellation logic, and structured attribute data are now prerequisites for appearing in agentic recommendation flows — not a roadmap item. IHG's AI content platform rollout, tied to its conversational search launch, is the right sequencing model.

## Vendor Moves

**SevenRooms** made a pointed competitive move on June 17. 
The company unveiled Channel Connect, a desktop app that aggregates reservations from various booking channels into a single system, while also automatically updating a restaurant's inventory across those channels.
 It is offered for free under an open license. 
The move comes amid fierce competition in the reservations market — in April, OpenTable began requiring restaurant clients to make it their system of record and make all of their inventory available on OpenTable.
 SevenRooms CEO Joel Montaniel's framing is pointed: 
"Channel Connect was built so that no single platform has to be the gatekeeper to your operations."


**Oracle Hospitality** launched OPERA Cloud Assistant at HITEC 2026 — 
AI-powered capabilities embedded directly into OPERA Cloud workflows, including AI-assisted room assignments, AI-generated rate descriptions, operational guidance, and translation. When AI becomes embedded in the system of record used by front desk teams, revenue managers and property leaders, it has the potential to shape everyday decisions rather than automate only a narrow task at the edge of the operation.


**Revinate** introduced Ivy, 
an AI decision-intelligence layer built across its platform, including automated call scoring and Revinate Chat. The additions show how guest data platforms are moving from segmentation and campaign execution toward AI-supported decisioning across reservations, marketing, messaging, coaching and service recovery.


**Grevon** debuted at HITEC 2026 with an MCP-based connectivity layer. 
The platform powers Pulse, an AI booking agent for hotel websites; Echo, an AI voice agent for inbound calls; and Ops, a staff intelligence platform. Grevon's launch points to an emerging competitive front in hotel distribution: making official hotel content, rates, availability and booking paths usable by AI agents.


**Toast** launched its drive-thru product in 2026. 
Toast Drive-Thru is a unified solution for enterprise quick-service operations, bringing together hardware, POS-native software, AI voice ordering integrations and services.


Sources: [NRN / SevenRooms Channel Connect](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology/sevenrooms-launches-a-reservations-aggregator-for-restaurants) · [HTN / HITEC Connected Hotel](https://hoteltechnologynews.com/2026/07/recent-hotel-technology-launches-show-ai-moving-from-standalone-tools-into-connected-hotel-operations/) · [Toast / AmEx Partnership](https://pos.toasttab.com/news/american-express-and-toast-announce-strategic-partnership)

So what: OpenTable's system-of-record requirement and SevenRooms' Channel Connect response define the current front line in data ownership. For a PE-backed restaurant group, this is not a product feature question — it's a data rights question. Which platform controls your inventory signals, and on what terms, directly affects your negotiating position with every distribution partner downstream.

## Data & Integration

**The fundamental bottleneck in hotel AI is not models — it is the entity layer underneath them.** 
Every major hotel chain has now committed publicly to an agentic AI roadmap — Marriott's $1B+ agentic mesh, Hilton's AI Planner, Hyatt and Accor inside ChatGPT, IHG's AI-enhanced CRM. All of them are bottlenecked at the same layer: the property management system was built when a guest was a stay at a property, not a relationship across the chain.



The best-funded PMS businesses are using their position to build broader platforms through product development and acquisition rather than relying on third-party integrations. Mews' acquisitions of Flexkeeping and DataChat in late 2025 are one example of this strategy.
 
AI can't compensate for scattered data, disconnected systems or poor processes. If a hotel's underlying systems are fragmented, AI will only make operational problems more visible. AI is delivering where it's closest to daily operational pressures — guest messaging, digital check-in, reading and responding to reviews, revenue management and support workflows.


In restaurants, the equivalent integration problem is surfacing through the Tenzo MCP architecture: 
with Tenzo MCP, operators can ask questions in plain English, in whichever AI tool they already use — ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot — and get a joined-up answer drawn from all their connected data sources at once.


Sources: [Abode Worldwide Hospitality Tech Index](https://abodeworldwide.com/hospitality-tech-investment-index/) · [Hotel Tech Report PMS Study](https://hoteltechreport.com/news/2026-hotel-pms-report) · [Tenzo](https://www.gotenzo.com/resources/insight/what-can-ai-do-for-restaurants-in-2026/)

So what: For a hospitality SaaS company evaluating M&A, the investment thesis this cycle is clear: platforms that own the data layer compound in value as AI improves. Point solutions that don't connect cleanly face commoditisation. Before acquiring or building, map where in the data stack you sit and whether you can become load-bearing infrastructure for AI workflows.

## Operational AI

**The restaurant industry's AI mood at the 2026 National Restaurant Association Show was sceptical enthusiasm**, not the peak hype of prior years.


Almost universally among restaurant tech leaders, the answer to "what are people getting wrong?" was artificial intelligence. Shake Shack is using AI to identify and alert managers to potential inefficiencies. Culver's began rolling out computer vision to measure service execution and vehicle flow in its drive-thrus. In April, Dairy Queen expanded a voice AI drive-thru test to more locations in partnership with Presto.


**Taco Bell** has the most significant QSR voice AI deployment in production. 
Through a partnership with Omilia, which has brought voice AI to nearly 900 U.S. restaurants, the Mexican chain will continue to add the tech across its domestic system.


The candid failure signal comes from Starbucks: 
the coffee giant ended its computer vision inventory counting system — which employees called "unreliable" — in favor of traditional stock-keeping methods.


Toast's usage data from 125,000 restaurants is the most credible read on what operators actually do with AI day-to-day. 
Operators most frequently initiated conversations about sales and revenue (47%), followed by menu and inventory (34%) and guests and marketing (32%). Far fewer used it for forecasting and planning (1%). Fine-dining locations had 29% more threads with Toast IQ than fast casuals.


The Popmenu CEO's NRA floor intervention deserves attention. 
Brendan Sweeney said overadoption of large language models rests on shaky fundamentals — the cost of AI tokens required to operate coding services can exceed the salaries of the workers the technology purports to replace. With OpenAI pursuing an IPO, AI developers could face greater pressure from investors to turn a profit. "Being first is not always the best," Sweeney said. "Being right and doing it the right way is the best."


Sources: [Restaurant Dive / NRA AI Overdependence](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/restaurant-technology-executives-warn-against-ai-overdependence/821024/) · [NRN / Toast IQ Usage](https://www.nrn.com/independent-restaurants/here-s-how-restaurants-are-using-toast-s-ai-chatbot) · [Business Wire / Toast 90-day IQ Report](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260610036506/en/90-Days-with-Toast-IQ-How-Restaurant-Operators-Are-Using-Toast-IQ-to-Find-Time-Protect-Margins-and-Grow)

So what: The Toast usage data is the most useful benchmark available this cycle for justifying or killing an AI project. Sales/revenue analytics at 47% adoption means operators want better P&L visibility more than anything else. If you're selling an operational AI tool that can't connect to transaction data, you're pitching the wrong thing.

## Governance & Security

**Two regulatory triggers landed in the past week and both have operational teeth for hospitality.**


Article 50 of the EU AI Act applies from 2 August 2026. Penalties run to fifteen million euros or 3% of worldwide annual turnover. If a chatbot or voice agent talks to guests, people have to be told they are dealing with AI — that design duty sits with the provider, so make it a procurement question and get the answer in writing. Emotion recognition in the workplace has been prohibited outright since 2 February 2025, with only a narrow medical or safety exception.


The White House also issued an AI Executive Order in June 2026. 
The Order, titled "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security," signals that AI has crossed a threshold from experimental productivity tool to critical infrastructure that governments now treat as a matter of national security. For hospitality, the competitive question is shifting from "How many AI features do we have?" to "Can we govern AI responsibly, secure our connected systems, and turn operational trust into guest experience?"


The operational risk is concrete, not theoretical. 
Hotels are now deploying AI agents rapidly and sometimes handing them the same capabilities that hackers worked to get: plain-text credentials and the autonomy to use them. Between January 2025 and February 2026, at least 20 separate security incidents have occurred across AI-powered applications. Nearly all had preventable causes like mishandled credentials or lax authentication.


The Chekin/Gastrodat breach is the clearest case study: 
an attacker compromised 527 credentials and extracted guest names, booking details, and ID documents for over 5 million guests.
 And the emerging threat vector goes beyond credential theft. 
Using publicly available information from breached databases and social media, autonomous AI agents can impersonate loyalty members, contact hotel staff using synthesized voices and request actions such as points transfers or account changes. Unlike human attackers, AI agents can operate simultaneously across hundreds of properties and thousands of accounts, adapting their responses in real time.


Sources: [Hotel Tech News / AI Moving Faster Than Security](https://hoteltechnologynews.com/2026/05/why-hotel-ai-adoption-is-moving-faster-than-security-controls-and-increasing-risk-exposure/) · [Tableview / EU AI Act Restaurant Guide](https://www.tableview.com/blog/ai-in-restaurants/) · [Hotels Mag / AI Cybersecurity](https://hotelsmag.com/news/hotels-have-a-new-ai-cybersecurity-problem-heres-what-to-know/)

So what: EU AI Act Article 50 is already in force as of August 2. Any hotel or restaurant group with EU customers running AI chatbots without a disclosure mechanism is now in violation. Minimum action today: require your AI chat and voice vendors to confirm in writing how they disclose AI identity to users, and audit whether any workplace emotion recognition tools exist in your HR stack.

Worth tracking whether independent operators actually adopt it or whether it becomes a DoorDash-ecosystem tool in practice.
