## AI Visibility & Discovery

Restaurant and hotel inventory is being reclassified as AI-eligible or AI-invisible, and the gap between those two states is widening by the week.

**DoorDash** published its 2026 Restaurant Industry Trends Report (3,001 consumers, 509 operators) revealing that 
22% of consumers have used an AI tool like ChatGPT or Google Gemini to help choose a restaurant
, and that 
restaurant listing sites like DoorDash account for more than 41% of the sources AI tools cite when recommending restaurants
. On the operator side, the gap is stark: 
three-quarters of consumers say they're comfortable using AI for reservations, but only 28% of operators are using AI to manage calls and customer service, with an estimated 40% of reservation calls going unanswered industry-wide.


**Uberall** published its *Fast Food, Faster Discovery: 2026 GEO Playbook for Multi-Location QSRs*, framing the stakes plainly: 
as consumer restaurant discovery rapidly shifts from traditional search to AI assistants, the majority of QSR locations are effectively absent from AI-generated recommendations — at the exact moment AI is becoming consumers' primary discovery channel.
 The report finds 
AI typically recommends only 3–5 brands per query, and ChatGPT primarily recommends businesses averaging 4.3 stars or higher, Perplexity 4.1+, and Gemini 3.9+
 — rating floors that sit above the average QSR. 
Uberall introduced Location Performance Optimization (LPO) as the strategic framework connecting SEO and GEO into a single operating model.
 **Birdeye's** *State of AI Search 2026* report corroborates: 
80% of brands are cited at least once in AI-generated answers, but only about 15% secure the primary recommendation position.


**Google** is monetising this discovery layer directly. 
The Direct Offers pilot is expanding to include IHG Hotels & Resorts, joining earlier partners Booking Holdings and Expedia, while Google builds toward in-conversation booking through the Universal Commerce Protocol.
 
Google is repositioning itself as a direct transaction layer under what it calls an "execution-first" model — Google no longer merely surfaces results, it acts on behalf of the user.
 Crucially, 
unlike a booking made through an OTA, the hotel retains Merchant of Record status and full ownership of the guest relationship under UCP for Lodging.


Sources: [DoorDash 2026 Restaurant Industry Trends Report](https://about.doordash.com/en-us/news/doordash-restaurant-industry-trends-report-2026), [Uberall GEO Playbook (BusinessWire)](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260507962493/en/83-of-Restaurants-Are-Invisible-in-AI-Search-New-Uberall-Report-Reveals-the-Discovery-Gap-Reshaping-the-Quick-Service-Restaurant-Industry), [Skift / Google Earnings](https://skift.com/2026/07/28/google-earnings-ads-artificial-intelligence-ihg-booking/)

So what: Every restaurant operator who hasn't audited their listing accuracy across DoorDash, OpenTable, Google Business Profile, and Yelp is invisible to the plurality of AI recommendation calls today. For mid-market groups, the 4.3-star floor is the single most actionable metric — it's not the AI's preference, it's the gating criterion.

## Vendor Moves

The restaurant reservation and delivery ecosystems consolidated further in July, with platform boundaries hardening around ecosystem ownership.

**DoorDash** made two decisive AI moves in the past three weeks. 
DoorDash introduced an in-app AI chatbot called Ask DoorDash, allowing customers to order food, build grocery carts, and book restaurant reservations using text prompts, photos, or voice inputs.
 Early data is substantive: 
nearly half of all restaurant orders placed through the tool were from a place the customer had never ordered from before, and carts assembled through the chatbot ran more than 35% higher in value than a standard grocery order, with users completing them five times faster.
 Co-founder Andy Fang told Bloomberg the product draws on models from OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google. Then on July 16, 
Fang announced dd-cli in limited beta — a tool that lets AI agents search stores, find the best deals, and check out without opening the consumer app, with demos showing Anthropic Claude completing real payments from a terminal session.
 
DoorDash also intends to license the Ask DoorDash technology to outside partners — grocers, restaurants, and other retailers — who would deploy it under their own branding.


**American Express** (via **Resy**) made two distinct moves. First, 
Claude users can now explore U.S. Resy restaurants directly in their conversations, with Claude connecting to Resy to help diners browse available tables in real time and secure a table seamlessly.
 Second, 
following American Express's acquisition of Tock in 2024, Resy and Tock announced a merger in February 2026 that will bring the combined network to more than 25,000 venues, with deeper POS connectivity coming through a Toast partnership in 2026.


**Square** (Block) 
launched ChatGPT and Claude integrations on July 1 that automatically enroll Square Food & Beverage sellers with activated Square Online Ordering profiles to route agentic orders directly into existing Square setups — at no additional cost to participating operators, no commissions or additional fees.
 Morgan Kuntze of Block framed it as helping restaurants avoid 
"playing an impossible game of catch-up" in the rapidly shifting agentic ordering space.


**Toast** launched two major products in spring 2026. 
Toast Drive-Thru is an enterprise-grade solution designed to help modern quick-service restaurants increase throughput and order accuracy at scale, targeting just over 140,000 drive-thru locations across the U.S. restaurant industry.
 Separately, 
Toast IQ Grow is a complete marketing solution designed to take on the day-to-day work of marketing a business, from identifying a lunch slowdown to automatically launching a campaign. Bundled at $499/month, it pairs an AI-powered Marketing Agent with a dedicated human Marketing Success Manager. In a pilot program, restaurants working with a Marketing Success Manager saw sales grow more than 8% on average, with the top 25% of performers seeing more than 40% growth in the first month.


**OpenTable** (Booking Holdings) gave top-tier loyalty members access to the most exclusive reservations as of July 9, and 
Booking Holdings is powering agentic reservations on OpenTable as part of its Google cloud and AI-powered transactions partnership.


Sources: [TechCrunch / Ask DoorDash](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/11/doordashs-new-ai-chatbot-lets-you-order-with-prompts-and-photos/), [BusinessWire / Toast Drive-Thru](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260414359751/en/Toast-Launches-Unified-Drive-Thru-Solution-to-Modernize-Enterprise-Quick-Service-Restaurant-Operations), [American Express Newsroom / Resy + Claude](https://www.americanexpress.com/en-us/newsroom/articles/travel-and-dining/american-express-partners-with-anthropic-to-make-u-s--resy-resta.html), [Food On Demand / Square](https://foodondemand.com/07082026/ai-integrations-emerges-as-summer-defining-trend-for-square/)

So what: The reservation and ordering stacks are no longer neutral infrastructure — they are the distribution ecosystems that determine AI discoverability. Operators choosing a reservation platform in 2026 are effectively choosing which AI ecosystem their inventory will be findable in. Resy/Claude, DoorDash/SevenRooms, Toast Local/Resy, and Square/ChatGPT are now distinct distribution lanes.

## Operational AI

AI is delivering measurably in narrow, high-frequency tasks in restaurant operations; at the consumer interface it continues to hit a trust ceiling.

**Toast IQ**, launched October 2025 and now covering 170,000+ U.S. restaurants, published its first usage data on July 14: 
operators at more than 125,000 restaurants started millions of threads in Q1 2026. Their most common questions were about sales and revenue (47%), followed by menu and inventory (34%) and guests and marketing (32%). The single most common prompt was "Create a short, easy-to-read daily briefing for my restaurant."
 Fine-dining operators used it most intensively: 
fine-dining locations had 29% more threads with Toast IQ than fast casuals
, suggesting the tool's value scales with operational complexity. One San Diego operator reported 
$3.4K in attributed email revenue over two months, more than three times the cost of Toast IQ Grow, alongside a 4.5x return on ad spend.


**Taco Bell** 
expanded drive-thru voice AI to nearly 900 U.S. restaurants
 through its partnership with Omilia, making it one of the largest live deployments in QSR. **McDonald's** launched ArchIQ chatbots at drive-throughs as part of its McDonald's NEXT initiative. Yet consumer sentiment data published July 30 shows an unresolved tension: 
data from Metrigy shows customer preference toward AI agent chatbots grew more than four percentage points from Q1 to Q2 2026, but recent surveys show Americans still overwhelmingly prefer ordering from human employees rather than AI-powered drive-through assistants or digital kiosks.


Sources: [Nation's Restaurant News / Toast IQ Usage](https://www.nrn.com/independent-restaurants/here-s-how-restaurants-are-using-toast-s-ai-chatbot), [Fortune / Fast-food AI](https://fortune.com/2026/07/30/fast-food-ai-agent-customers-prefer-humans/), [Pulse 2.0 / Toast IQ Grow](https://pulse2.com/toast-iq-grow/)

So what: The Toast IQ usage data is the best operator-level signal available this cycle on what restaurant AI is actually being used for — and it's decidedly mundane (daily briefings, sales summaries, labour queries). That's a positive: daily operational cadence beats demo-only adoption. The 29% uptick in fine-dining usage signals the tool earns its keep where operational complexity is highest, which maps directly to the operator segments most worth targeting.

## Data & Integration

Every major hotel brand's agentic AI roadmap is bottlenecked at the same architectural layer — and Marriott's $1 billion investment has made that bottleneck undeniable.


In February 2026, Marriott told the market it would invest more than a billion dollars in technology this year, with more than a third going into digital transformation: replatforming the PMS, rebuilding central reservations, modernising the loyalty platform, and constructing what Marriott calls an "agentic mesh" — a shared intelligence layer designed to let AI agents operate consistently across brands, geographies, and channels.
 The Apptad analysis, published May 29, identifies what's missing: 
the PMS is the single largest holder of guest signal, but rarely more than a fifth of the total — the rest sits in the CRS, loyalty platform, CRM, F&B POS, spa systems, mobile app, channel manager, and concierge messaging platforms.
 The practical consequence: 
a conversational concierge that cannot remember what the same guest told a different brand's concierge two trips ago is not a conversational concierge — it is a search box with better grammar.


The integration gap is structural: industry survey synthesis cited by Apptad shows fewer than one in four hotels have fully integrated core systems, roughly half are partially integrated, and 30% are largely siloed. 
Cloudbeds articulated the design trade-off plainly: fragmented data produces fragmented intelligence, whereas unified data produces unified intelligence, and the PMS is only as intelligent as the data it can access.


Sources: [Apptad: The Hotel Industry's AI Problem Lives in the PMS](https://apptad.com/insights/the-hotel-industrys-ai-problem-lives-in-the-pms/), [Hospitality ON / Google I/O hotel distribution](https://hospitality-on.com/en/distribution/google-brings-its-full-firepower-claim-central-role-hotel-distribution)

So what: For PE-backed hotel SaaS companies, this is the clearest signal of where platform value is accumulating. Vendors who can credibly position as the entity-layer above the PMS — not the PMS itself — are building the moat that matters. The Mews acquisitions of Flexkeeping and DataChat are exactly this strategy in motion.

## Governance & Security

HITEC 2026 marked the moment the hotel industry acknowledged that deploying AI agents and governing AI agents are two entirely different capabilities.


At HITEC 2026 in San Antonio, the dominant theme shifted from AI deployment to AI governance. As hotels begin deploying dozens of AI-powered workflows across revenue management, marketing, reservations, maintenance, finance, and guest communications, the challenge is taking stock and reining in all these agents. The most important emerging software category observed at the show is the Agent Management Platform (AMP) — a governance layer that controls permissions, monitors performance, manages security, and tracks costs.
 A further dimension: 
token costs are about to become a very real part of the tech stack opex budget; hotels will discover that thousands of automated decisions, guest interactions, forecasts, and reports create a new operating expense category.


The June 2, 2026 White House Executive Order on AI Innovation and Security is pulling in the same direction. Terence Ronson's Hospitality Net white paper translates the Order's implications: 
the modern hotel has quietly become a dense, interconnected intelligent environment — binding together identity, payments, guest data, building systems, IoT, energy orchestration, and AI-driven workflows. The more intelligent a property becomes, the more strategically exposed it becomes.
 On the cyber threat side, 
ransomware groups are moving away from mere encryption towards disrupting operations — hacking property management systems, door locks, HVAC controls, and booking integrations — and deepfakes will move from being celebrity hoaxes to operational fraud in 2026.
 The cost baseline is real: 
the average cost of a data breach in hospitality was $3.86 million in 2024.


Sources: [Hotel Mogel: Agentic Governance at HITEC 2026](https://hotelmogel.com/agentic-governance-is-the-big-trend-from-hitec-2026/), [Hospitality Net: When National AI Security Becomes a Hospitality Imperative](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/opinion/4132771/when-national-ai-security-becomes-a-hospitality-imperative)

So what: The AMP category is early but real. Hotel operators running more than a handful of AI workflows without a governance layer are accumulating invisible risk — in compliance, in data exposure, and in token spend. Expect the first branded AMP products from the major cloud PMS vendors within 12–18 months. Mid-market operators should begin their own agent inventories now.

## People & Skills

The hospitality industry has the widest AI readiness gap of any sector — and is deploying AI fastest at the frontline.


The industries least prepared for AI are those with large operational workforces and ongoing hiring pressure — and hospitality ranks as the most exposed, followed by healthcare, financial services, and logistics.
 The HSMAI Foundation's AI Talent Pipeline report (May 21) quantified the paradox inside the pipeline: 
hospitality students self-teach AI through experimentation but rate their academic programs' preparation at just 2.78 out of 5. Students rated their confidence in applying AI to work tasks at 3.24 out of 5, but usage remains concentrated around writing and research tools rather than operational or analytical applications.
 HSMAI CEO Brian Hicks framed the employer obligation plainly: 
"Employers must equip future talent with the training, governance, and decision-making frameworks needed to apply AI tools successfully in real-world hotel operations."


The Opus Hospitality Training 360 Report (190 L&D leaders, 30,000+ locations) offers a more optimistic signal: 
AI adoption in training increased 20 percentage points year-over-year, and hands-on training also grew by 11 points — suggesting that the most effective programs combine technology with human coaching rather than replacing it.


Sources: [HSMAI Foundation AI Talent Pipeline Report (Hospitality Net)](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4132558/hsmai-foundation-releases-new-ai-talent-pipeline-report-examining-the-future-of-hospitality-workforce-readiness), [Allwork.Space / Resume Now 2026 workforce analysis](https://allwork.space/2026/04/hospitality-ranks-least-prepared-for-ai-as-workforce-gaps-widen/)

So what: The confidence-preparedness paradox identified by HSMAI is a direct hiring problem for 2026 and 2027 hotel and restaurant groups. Incoming staff are AI-habituated but not AI-governable — they use the tools but haven't been taught how to apply them responsibly in operational settings. Operators who build structured AI onboarding frameworks now will have a materially faster ramp to productive AI-augmented staff.
