## AI Visibility & Discovery

Agentic ordering has moved from experiment to infrastructure, with multiple platforms now routing real consumer transactions through AI channels in both restaurant and hotel sectors.

**Square** executed the week's sharpest restaurant distribution move on July 1. 
Square's integrations with ChatGPT and Claude allow chatbot users to discover participating restaurants, browse menus and place orders through Order by Cash App. Those orders route directly into sellers' Square online ordering solutions, including POS and kitchen display systems.
 The commercial hook is commission economics: 
Square launched free ChatGPT and Claude ordering for US restaurants on July 1, 2026, with no marketplace commission versus the 15–30% delivery apps charge. Eligible Food & Beverage sellers with an active Square Online Ordering profile are opted in automatically — no new APIs, setup, or contract.
 Morgan Kuntze, global partnerships lead at Block, framed the move as a race against incumbent platforms: 
"Our investment into agentic commerce aims to offload that responsibility by giving operators time back, helping connect them with customers in their communities and keeping them at the industry's cutting edge."


**DoorDash** moved in two distinct directions this month. First came the consumer-facing Ask DoorDash chatbot in June, which posted measurable early signal: 
nearly half of all restaurant orders placed through the tool were from a place the customer had never ordered from before, and carts assembled through the chatbot ran more than 35% higher in value than a standard grocery order, with users completing them five times faster than they would through the regular app interface.
 Then on July 16, DoorDash opened a developer layer: 
DoorDash opened a limited beta of dd-cli, a command-line tool that lets developers and AI agents search stores, build carts, and place orders from the terminal, marking another step toward software designed for AI agents instead of just humans.
 A public demonstration showed Anthropic's Claude completing a full end-to-end DoorDash order autonomously.

**American Express / Resy** completed an Anthropic integration in April: 
Claude users can now explore US Resy restaurants directly in their conversations when deciding where to eat, connecting to Resy to help diners browse available tables in real time and secure a table seamlessly.
 AmEx has flagged broader 2026 intent: 
in 2026, for US consumers, they plan to introduce enhanced dining discovery and booking features within the American Express app, enable new AI-powered experiences and discovery capabilities in their Resy and Amex Travel apps, and pilot real-time, geolocation-based capabilities for Amex Offers.


**Google** used its July 28 earnings call to signal the most consequential hotel distribution shift yet. 
The Direct Offers pilot is expanding to include IHG Hotels & Resorts, joining earlier partners Booking Holdings and Expedia, while Google builds toward in-conversation booking through the Universal Commerce Protocol — though key details on checkout, routing, and metrics remain undisclosed.
 At Google I/O, the architecture was laid bare: 
Google is stepping out of its traditional role as a search engine that routes users to third-party partners and repositioning itself as a direct transaction layer under an "execution-first" model. Its hospitality-specific implementation, UCP for Lodging, enables any AI interaction to be converted into an instant room reservation, executed directly within Google surfaces. Crucially, unlike a booking made through an OTA, the hotel retains Merchant of Record status.
 Julie Farago, Google's VP of engineering for travel and local search, told PhocusWire: 
"Our goal is that you'll be able to also book flights and hotels directly in AI Mode."


**DoorDash's own consumer research** puts the restaurant discovery shift in context: 
22% of consumers have used an AI tool like ChatGPT or Google Gemini to help choose a restaurant, making AI a new discovery signal to watch. Restaurant listing sites like DoorDash account for more than 41% of the sources AI tools cite when recommending restaurants.
 The operator response gap is stark: 
three-quarters of consumers say they're comfortable using AI for reservations, but only 28% of operators are using AI to manage calls and customer service, with an estimated 40% of reservation calls going unanswered industry-wide.


Sources: [Food on Demand – Square AI integrations](https://foodondemand.com/07082026/ai-integrations-emerges-as-summer-defining-trend-for-square/), [TechCrunch – DoorDash CLI](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/16/yes-you-can-now-order-doordash-from-the-command-line/), [Skift – Google earnings/IHG](https://skift.com/2026/07/28/google-earnings-ads-artificial-intelligence-ihg-booking/), [Hospitality ON – Google I/O hotel distribution](https://hospitality-on.com/en/distribution/google-brings-its-full-firepower-claim-central-role-hotel-distribution)

So what: Restaurant groups not yet optimising listing data — complete menus, updated photos, structured dietary tags — are already invisible to a quarter of consumers who use AI to choose where to eat. For hotels, the UCP for Lodging model is the most direct path to AI-native direct bookings seen since Google Hotel Ads; but Google has not disclosed monetisation terms, so rushing in before those are clear carries margin risk.

## Operational AI

The industry's sharpest operational AI data this cycle came from two independent datasets that converge on the same conclusion: AI is creating a measurable performance gap between adopters and laggards in restaurants, while hotels are still reconciling governance with deployment speed.

**Restaurant365** released its 2026 mid-year industry report on July 16, drawing on 420+ operators across nearly 10,000 US locations. 
The research suggests AI is beginning to create meaningful separation in restaurant performance.
 The headline numbers are concrete: among operators actively using AI, 61% report reduced food costs, 62% report reduced labor costs, 88% report saving time every week, and nearly one-third report cost reductions of 6% or more. 
62% of operators have implemented or plan to implement AI in at least one back-office function — more than double the level reported at the beginning of the year, with reporting and analytics leading adoption, followed by scheduling and inventory forecasting.
 Tony Smith, CEO of Restaurant365, framed the stakes: 
"The operators who are consistently pulling ahead aren't simply adopting AI, they're using it to make faster, better decisions every day. That's translating into lower costs, improved efficiency and stronger financial outcomes."


**Toast IQ** published Q1 usage data on July 14, the first operator-behaviour dataset from a POS AI at real scale. 
Toast IQ had operators at more than 125,000 restaurants starting millions of threads in Q1. Their most common questions were about sales and revenue (47%), followed by menu and inventory (34%) and guests and marketing (32%).
 The single most common prompt was "Create a short, easy-to-read daily briefing for my restaurant." 
Fine-dining locations had 29% more threads with Toast IQ than fast casuals.
 One operator cited in Toast's earlier IQ Grow launch: 
"That sparked an idea for a mid-week pizza party promotion. I let Toast generate the campaign, made a few tweaks, and sent it out. The open rates were the best I've ever seen and the mid-week sales were incredible, jumping 44%. I generated over $10K from this one campaign."
 (Alex Koons, Owner, Hot Tongue Pizza, Los Angeles.)

**Taco Bell** expanded its voice AI to nearly 900 US restaurants (Nation's Restaurant News, Jul 9), partnering with Omilia on the deployment — confirming QSR voice AI has moved from pilot to scaled rollout at a major chain.

Sources: [Restaurant365 PRNewswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/restaurant365-research-identifies-a-new-restaurant-profitability-gap-operators-using-ai-are-pulling-ahead-302825987.html), [Nation's Restaurant News – Toast IQ](https://www.nrn.com/independent-restaurants/here-s-how-restaurants-are-using-toast-s-ai-chatbot), [Restaurant Dive – Taco Bell voice AI](https://www.restaurantdive.com/topic/technology/)

So what: The Restaurant Profitability Gap finding — from an operator running nearly 10,000 locations — is the most credible AI ROI data point in restaurants this cycle. For mid-market operators still evaluating, this is the benchmark to pressure-test. For SaaS investors, the data signals that back-office AI with measurable cost outcomes is outpacing front-of-house novelty plays.

## Vendor Moves

The restaurant reservation market just crystallised into three distinct ecosystems, each controlled by a different platform company.

**American Express** is folding Tock into Resy this summer, roughly doubling Resy's venue library to over 25,000 restaurants and bringing Tock's prepaid booking model directly into the Resy platform. 
American Express now owns both Resy and Tock, having bought them for $400 million in 2024, and DoorDash acquired SevenRooms for $1.2 billion in 2025. OpenTable, long the default, saw its US market share slide from 51% to 46% between 2022 and 2024, though it still leads in 13 of the 15 top US cities.
 Separately, 
AmEx announced a multi-year partnership with Toast to create more personalized hospitality experiences across the Resy, Tock, and Toast restaurant networks.


**DoorDash / SevenRooms** integration is deepening: Food on Demand reported (Jul 28) that SevenRooms is now pulling DoorDash and Deliveroo data directly into its reservations and guest-profile system, the first visible operational fruit of the $1.2 billion acquisition.

**Toast** launched two significant enterprise products this cycle. Toast Drive-Thru (launched April 2026, BusinessWire) brings hardware, POS-native software, and AI voice ordering integrations from partners including Incept AI into a unified enterprise QSR platform. 
"In the drive-thru lane, every second matters. By seamlessly integrating POS, KDS, outdoor digital menu boards, and AI voice ordering partnerships, Toast Drive-Thru gives operators the tools they need to keep pace with peak demand today,"
 said Steve Fredette, President and Co-Founder of Toast. Toast IQ Grow (May 2026) layered a marketing agent on top: 
bundled at $499 per month, Toast IQ Grow pairs the AI-powered Marketing Agent with a dedicated human Marketing Success Manager who helps guide campaign direction, with pilot restaurants seeing sales grow more than 8% on average compared to similar Toast restaurants, with the top 25% seeing more than 40% growth in the first month.


**Mews** (not yet covered in earlier clusters) laid off 15% of staff in what the company described as a pivot to an "AI-native" future — the strongest signal yet from a major PMS vendor that AI is being used to justify structural workforce reduction, not just feature expansion.

**Booking Holdings** reported Q1 2026 results showing AI is delivering across its portfolio: 
OpenTable's AI Concierge answered 80% of diners' questions at launch, reducing the need for phone calls to restaurants. Agoda found that its AI-assisted automation drove a double-digit year-over-year reduction in customer service costs per booking.
 CEO Glenn Fogel is positioning Booking's loyalty data as a moat: 
"Google knows everything about you. They don't know what I can offer to you because you are a Genius Level 3."


Sources: [PRNewswire – Hostie Series A](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/hostie-raises-12m-series-a-to-power-the-future-of-restaurant-hospitality-302820521.html), [BusinessWire – Toast Drive-Thru](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260414359751/en/Toast-Launches-Unified-Drive-Thru-Solution-to-Modernize-Enterprise-Quick-Service-Restaurant-Operations), [Pulse 2.0 – Toast IQ Grow](https://pulse2.com/toast-iq-grow/), [PhocusWire – Booking Holdings Q1](https://www.phocuswire.com/news/online/booking-holdings-earnings-q1-2026)

So what: The three-platform consolidation in restaurant reservations (AmEx/Resy/Tock, DoorDash/SevenRooms, Booking Holdings/OpenTable) means choosing a reservation system is now choosing an ecosystem with a parent company's data and distribution priorities embedded. Independent restaurants need to understand what data they are sharing, with whom, and at what cost.

## Funding & M&A

Capital continues to concentrate in AI-native hospitality platforms with demonstrable workflow integration.

**Hostie** closed a $12 million Series A on July 8, led by Obvious Ventures with participation from Gradient. 
Since launching, Hostie has become a trusted virtual concierge for hundreds of restaurants and hospitality groups nationwide, handling calls, texts, reservations, takeout inquiries, private events, and guest questions.
 The traction metrics are specific: the company managed more than 2 million guest conversations, 24 million messages, booked over 400,000 covers, and supported more than 50,000 private event inquiries. 
Gradient Partner Andrew Brackin discovered Hostie when calling Flour + Water to say he was running late for a reservation: "The experience was so seamless I immediately wanted to know what was powering it."


**Canary Technologies** completed an $80 million Series D (June 2025, confirmed in this cycle's Abode Worldwide report context): 
the round brings Canary's valuation to approximately $600 million. Brighton Park Capital led the round, with existing investors Insight Partners, F-Prime Capital, Thayer Ventures, Y-Combinator and Commerce Ventures also participating.


**The broader hospitality tech investment picture**: 
hospitality technology startups raised more than $1 billion across 40 companies between April 2025 and March 2026, with property management systems and AI-led platforms attracting the largest share of investment.
 The investor thesis per Abode Worldwide CEO Jessica Gillingham: 
"The companies attracting the strongest investor backing share a common trait. They sit close to essential operator workflows and become more valuable over time. Unified systems generate more data, better automation and higher switching costs."


Sources: [PRNewswire – Hostie](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/hostie-raises-12m-series-a-to-power-the-future-of-restaurant-hospitality-302820521.html), [Hotel Dive – Canary $80M](https://www.hoteldive.com/news/canary-raises-80-million-hospitality-ai/750667/), [Hotel Online – Abode Worldwide $1B+ report](https://www.hotel-online.com/news/hospitality-tech-drew-over-1-billion-in-the-last-year-with-pms-and-ai-led-platforms-capturing-biggest-share)

So what: Hostie's specific operating metrics (calls answered, covers booked, private events managed) represent the standard for what Series A restaurant AI companies need to show now — volume, specificity, and operator retention data. Generic "AI-powered concierge" claims without equivalent numbers will struggle in the next fundraising environment.

## Governance & Security

This cycle produced the most concentrated governance coverage hospitality has seen, driven by HITEC 2026 in San Antonio and a June White House executive order.

The defining phrase from HITEC was "agentic governance." 
HITEC 2026 felt like the moment the industry began realizing that AI agents are becoming operational infrastructure, which screams for more transparent oversight. The most important emerging software category observed at the show is what might be called the Agent Management Platform (AMP) — the governance layer for an organization's AI agent ecosystem.
 The cost dimension is underappreciated: 
as hotels adopt AI across departments such as revenue management, marketing, reservations and guest communications, managing dozens or even hundreds of AI agents becomes increasingly complex. Token costs — while currently small — will create a new operating expense category that hotels must actively manage, with the industry likely treating token spending similarly to labor costs or distribution commissions.


On security, the operational data this cycle is alarming. 
In March 2026, CyberNews discovered that an attacker compromised the credentials of accounts on Chekin and Gastrodat — hospitality platforms based out of Spain and Austria — and obtained plain-text copies of usernames, passwords, and tokens, then extracted guest names, booking details, and ID documents. It took 527 stolen credentials to leak the data of over 5 million guests.


The White House June 2026 AI Executive Order has direct hospitality implications. Terence Ronson of Pertlink translated the order for hotel operators: 
the competitive question for hospitality is shifting from 'How many AI features do we have?' to 'Can we govern AI responsibly, secure our connected systems, and turn operational trust into guest experience?' Hospitality is no longer simply about participating in digital transformation; it is becoming part of the intelligent infrastructure economy.



The EU AI Act Article 50 transparency requirements for chatbots apply from 2 August 2026
 — a compliance deadline now days away for any European hotel or restaurant chain deploying AI-powered guest-facing tools.

Sources: [Hotel Mogel – HITEC 2026 governance](https://hotelmogel.com/agentic-governance-is-the-big-trend-from-hitec-2026/), [Hospitality Net – White House EO for hotels](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/opinion/4132771/when-national-ai-security-becomes-a-hospitality-imperative)

So what: The EU AI Act chatbot transparency deadline passed most hospitality operators unnoticed. Any hotel or restaurant running an AI chat interface in Europe needs to audit compliance this week. Longer-term, the Agent Management Platform (AMP) category is worth watching for the same reason CDPs were worth watching in 2019 — early adopters will have governance infrastructure that becomes a procurement differentiator.

## People & Skills

The workforce gap in hospitality AI is the widest of any sector and is widening faster than training programs can close it.


A 2026 workforce analysis by Resume Now shows hospitality ranks as the most exposed sector, followed by healthcare, financial services, and logistics. AI tools are being rolled out faster than workers can be trained to use them effectively.
 
Only 5% of companies offer AI training at scale and BCG research shows just 26% have developed the capabilities to move beyond proofs of concept and generate tangible value.


The consumer backlash against AI drive-through ordering — published July 30 — adds a human-preference dimension to the skills debate. 
Data from Metrigy reports show customer preference toward AI agent chatbots grew more than four percentage points from Q1 2026 to Q2 2026, but recent customer surveys show Americans continue to overwhelmingly prefer ordering from human employees rather than AI-powered drive-through assistants or digital kiosks.
 Wharton Professor Jerry Jacobs: 
"The assumption is there's a task, the computer can do it, therefore the person doesn't. That's one possible way of implementing it. It's not the only way."


Sources: [Fortune – fast food AI consumer backlash](https://fortune.com/2026/07/30/fast-food-ai-agent-customers-prefer-humans/), [Allwork.Space – hospitality AI workforce gaps](https://allwork.space/2026/04/hospitality-ranks-least-prepared-for-ai-as-workforce-gaps-widen/)

So what: The consumer preference data is a signal for operators deploying voice AI at drive-throughs: Metrigy finds companies systematically overestimate consumer comfort with AI agents by roughly double the actual figure. Rolling out voice AI without a genuine human fallback path is a brand risk, not just an operational one.
