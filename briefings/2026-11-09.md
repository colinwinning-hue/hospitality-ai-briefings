# Hospitality AI Briefing — 2026-11-09

## Operational AI

New distribution channels for AI-powered restaurant ordering are entering test phase, with the clearest move of the cycle arriving in a 6 Nov feature from Fast Casual that extends the SoundHound-LG partnership first announced 3 Nov.

**SoundHound AI** (6 Nov) — Fast Casual published a vendor interview with Ben Bellettini, SVP of Restaurants at SoundHound AI, adding new commercial and technical detail to the company's 3 Nov announcement of a smart-TV ordering partnership with LG Electronics. The TV ordering channel is targeted for a December launch across approximately 20 million LG Smart TVs in the U.S. The article confirms that restaurants will pay a standard pickup-and-delivery commission — either directly or through their existing ordering providers — with no subscription option and no new in-store hardware required. Two ordering modalities are planned: a fully voice-driven flow handled by SoundHound's agentic AI (covering menu selection through payment), and a browse-on-screen flow where guests scan a QR code to finalise payment on their phone. SoundHound also confirmed that the same underlying technology is being piloted in vehicles with major automakers. No restaurant partners for the LG channel have been named yet. An anonymous unnamed national coffee chain said in a statement provided by SoundHound: "Our customers are in their cars and in front of their TVs all the time, so being able to take orders there makes a lot of sense for us." SoundHound reports it works with more than 15,000 U.S. restaurant locations, naming Jersey Mike's, Five Guys, and White Castle as existing platform brands, though none are confirmed for the LG channel. "We believe this platform will provide restaurants with a whole new channel that will enable them to be discovered right in people's living rooms," Bellettini said.

Sources: [Fast Casual, 6 Nov 2026](https://www.fastcasual.com/news/why-smart-tvs-could-be-restaurants-next-ordering-channel/)

So what: Smart-TV ordering removes one more friction point from the living-room delivery funnel and, critically, sidesteps third-party marketplace fees for the discovery phase. For mid-market restaurant operators already on SoundHound's platform, the LG channel is effectively a new reach surface with no additional hardware cost — but the channel has zero confirmed restaurant names and no live usage data yet. Wait for the December launch, then assess whether the pickup/delivery commission structure is better or worse than existing aggregator terms before committing to onboarding.
