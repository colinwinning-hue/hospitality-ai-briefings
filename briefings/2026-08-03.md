## Funding & M&A

American Express is assembling the world's largest restaurant-booking network through a sequence of deals with no parallel in the sector's history. 
American Express announced a proposed acquisition of TheFork from Tripadvisor for $700 million in cash, with the transaction expected to close before the end of 2026, subject to regulatory approval.
 
TheFork connects millions of diners with more than 50,000 restaurants across 11 European countries; the proposed acquisition builds on American Express's earlier acquisitions of Resy and Tock and would expand the Amex dining network to 75,000 bookable venues.
 
TheFork turned over $232M in the year to Q1 2026 and made $28M in adjusted earnings, making the $700M price tag roughly three times revenue — a premium Amex is paying for reach and relationships, not just cash flow.


**Resy and Tock** are simultaneously being merged. 
A merger under Resy has been announced for summer 2026, which would roughly double Resy's venue inventory to over 25,000 listings and bring Tock's pre-paid booking and tiered experience features into the Resy platform.
 
Amex also plans, for U.S. consumers, to introduce enhanced dining discovery and booking features within the Amex app and enable new AI-powered experiences in its Resy and Amex Travel apps in 2026.


**DoorDash** closed its Deliveroo integration in early Q1 2026. 
The Deliveroo acquisition, finalized in early Q4 2025, immediately expanded DoorDash's global reach by adding approximately $2.7 billion in GOV — boosting total volume by 10–11% and cementing DoorDash's status as the world's largest food delivery operator, surpassing Uber Eats.
 
DoorDash is also leaning into SevenRooms, pairing the guest-experience CRM's tools with DoorDash's consumer demand to deepen merchant relationships.


**Atoms** (CloudKitchens' parent) 
raised $1.7 billion to bring CloudKitchens, restaurant software and robotics under one roof
, signalling that ghost-kitchen infrastructure is being repositioned as an AI-automation play. **Chowbus** 
closed an $81 million fundraising round led by Prysm Capital and Left Lane Capital, and will expand beyond its POS platform into marketing, accounting, supply ordering and insurance for smaller restaurant brands.
 French restaurant platform **Innovorder** 
raised €20 million to accelerate European growth, roll out Atlas (its AI agent orchestrator for administrative tasks), and pursue strategic acquisitions — growing at 40% annually and profitable since 2024.

## Vendor Moves

The biggest product launches of the cycle are concentrated in two watchlist names: Toast and DoorDash, with additional signals from the HITEC 2026 hotel wave.

**Toast** shipped two material product launches in H1 2026. 
Toast IQ Grow, introduced as part of Toast's Spring 2026 product release, is described as more than 20 updates across marketing, payroll, inventory and operations; at its centre is an AI-powered Marketing Agent designed to take on the day-to-day work of marketing a business.
 
Key highlights include the Marketing Agent — which builds audiences and automates campaigns across channels — and a reimagined Toast Local consumer app featuring integrated Resy reservations, increasing bookable locations to more than 20,000.
 On the QSR side, 
Toast launched Toast Drive-Thru in April 2026, bringing together hardware, POS-native software, AI voice ordering integrations (including with Incept AI), Speed of Service reporting, and Drive-Thru KDS enhancements.
 
There are just over 140,000 total drive-thru locations across the entire U.S. restaurant industry.
 Toast confirmed the platform will roll out first to enterprise brands with 15 or more locations.

**DoorDash** launched "Ask DoorDash" on June 11. 
The chatbot lets users search the app in their own words, share a recipe link to find grocery items, or describe the reservation they're looking for; it is rolling out on iOS in select regions for restaurant search and grocery shopping and within DoorDash Reservations.
 
Early testing showed that nearly half of all restaurant orders placed through the tool were from a place the customer had never ordered from before; carts assembled through the chatbot ran more than 35% higher in value than a standard grocery order, with users completing them five times faster than through the regular interface.
 Co-founder Andy Fang confirmed the product draws on models from OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google.

**American Express + Anthropic**: 
On April 24, 2026, American Express announced a collaboration with Anthropic, enabling users of the Claude AI model to make restaurant reservations via Amex's Resy platform; Claude provides dining recommendations and directs users to Resy for table availability.


On the hotel side, 
a series of recent hotel technology announcements surfaced around HITEC 2026 showing AI moving from standalone applications into core operating systems; vendors are less focused on isolated tools and more focused on intelligence layers that connect the PMS, guest communications, network infrastructure, revenue management, and procurement workflows.
 Specific launches included: **Oracle OPERA Cloud Assistant** (AI-assisted room assignments, AI-generated rate descriptions, operational guidance embedded in the PMS), **Revinate Ivy** (AI decision-intelligence layer with automated call scoring and guest inbox automation), **Innspire Guest Flows** (zero-download AI concierge to digital-key guest journey), and **Grevon** — 
an MCP-based connectivity layer launching Pulse (an AI booking agent for hotel websites), Echo (an AI voice agent for inbound calls), and Ops (a staff intelligence platform), pointing to an emerging competitive front in hotel distribution around making official hotel content usable by AI agents.


A breaking development not yet in trade press: 
IHG Hotels & Resorts launched conversational AI search across its website and mobile app
 in August 2026, among the first major chains to make natural-language search a standard consumer experience rather than a pilot.

## AI Visibility & Discovery

The most consequential structural shift in the cycle: AI is reshaping how both restaurants and hotels are found, with Google making its largest agentic commerce move to date and hard data arriving on what the bias looks like in practice.


With the announcements at Google I/O 2026, Google has pivoted decisively into agentic hotel booking. The announcements signal a complete overhaul of Search and its deep integration with Gemini, deployed across two layers — Gemini Flash and Gemini Spark — repositioning Google as a direct transaction layer under an "execution-first" model.
 
The hospitality-specific implementation, UCP for Lodging, enables any AI interaction to be converted into an instant room reservation executed directly within Google surfaces; crucially, unlike an OTA booking, the hotel retains Merchant of Record status and full ownership of the guest relationship.
 
As of June 2026, agentic flight and hotel booking in AI Mode has been announced but not launched; the partner list and booking flow are still being finalized.
 Named hotel launch partners include Booking.com, Choice Hotels International, Expedia, IHG Hotels & Resorts, Marriott International and Wyndham Hotels & Resorts.

**OpenAI** moved in the opposite direction. 
OpenAI shifted its shopping strategy to focus on discovery and research, leaving checkout to third-party app developers; an OpenAI spokesperson confirmed: "We're prioritizing making ChatGPT search and product discovery great, with the Agentic Commerce Protocol serving as the infrastructure that connects users to merchants." The change alleviated competitive pressure on Booking Holdings and Expedia, whose shares rose following the announcement.
 
The retreat revealed that only around a dozen Shopify merchants had gone live with native ChatGPT checkout — out of millions — before OpenAI moved the feature into third-party apps.


**Lighthouse** published the first large-scale empirical study of AI hotel visibility. 
At Luminate 2026, Lighthouse Director of Hospitality Research Blake Reiter built a study on over 4,500 ChatGPT prompts across nine global destinations and five traveler personas to answer: what hotels is AI recommending, why those and not others, and where does it come from?
 Key findings: 
ChatGPT now has over 900 million users; across all 4,545 prompts, ChatGPT mentioned hotels by name 49,707 times, but from only 2,721 unique properties — a small group being recommended repeatedly while the vast majority never appear. Four and five-star properties dominated even on generic prompts; three-star hotels were nearly absent from that discovery pool.


On the restaurant side, 
DoorDash's 2026 Restaurant Industry Trends Report (based on 3,000+ U.S. consumers and 500+ operators) found that 22% of consumers have used AI search and recommendation platforms to choose a restaurant; restaurant listing sites like DoorDash account for more than 41% of the sources AI tools cite when recommending restaurants.
 
Three-quarters of consumers say they're comfortable using AI for reservations, but only 28% of operators are using AI to manage calls and customer service; an estimated 40% of reservation calls go unanswered industry-wide.


Sources: [Hospitality ON – Google I/O 2026](https://hospitality-on.com/en/distribution/google-brings-its-full-firepower-claim-central-role-hotel-distribution) | [Skift – OpenAI Checkout Walkback](https://skift.com/2026/03/05/openai-chatgpt-checkout-walkback/) | [Lighthouse – AI Visibility](https://www.mylighthouse.com/resources/blog/ai-recommendations-as-new-battleground-for-hotel-distribution) | [DoorDash Trends Report](https://about.doordash.com/en-us/news/doordash-restaurant-industry-trends-report-2026)

So what: AI discovery is now a real distribution surface, not a future projection. The Lighthouse data gives operators something they lacked: a specific, measurable visibility gap to close. For a mid-market hotel, the practical implication is immediate: your OTA listings, editorial mentions, and structured web content are what LLMs are reading to decide if you exist. For a restaurant group, 40% of reservation calls going unanswered while 75% of consumers want AI to handle them is a direct revenue leak with a current solution.

## Operational AI

Industry practitioners are calibrating against the hype — and the counter-signal from operators is substantial.


At the National Restaurant Association Show 2026, technology leaders almost universally identified AI as the thing people were getting wrong. Brendan Sweeney, CEO and co-founder of Popmenu, warned that overadoption of large language models rests on shaky fundamentals: the cost of AI tokens required to operate coding services can exceed the salaries of the workers the technology purports to replace, and with OpenAI pursuing an IPO, AI developers could face greater pressure from investors to turn a profit.



Many chains are still working to integrate new technology. Shake Shack is using AI to identify and alert managers to potential inefficiencies; Culver's began rolling out computer vision to measure service execution and vehicle flow in its drive-thrus; Dairy Queen expanded a voice AI drive-thru test to more locations in partnership with Presto.


On the hotel side, 
HITEC 2026 in San Antonio showed the question is no longer about chatbots, virtual concierge and one-off tools, but how hotels manage, govern, orchestrate and extract value from dozens and eventually hundreds of AI-powered workflows operating simultaneously across the enterprise.
 
Today, many organizations treat AI costs as negligible. In three years, finance teams will be scrutinizing token consumption the same way they currently analyze labor costs or OTA commissions. Hotels will increasingly arbitrage tasks amongst different models based on performance, speed and cost.


Two new software categories crystallised at HITEC. The first is the **Agent Management Platform (AMP)** — 
a governance layer that determines which agent does what, what permissions it has, and how token-efficient it operates; organizations that can govern permissions, monitor costs, maintain audit trails and control data access across dozens or hundreds of AI agents will gain a significant operational advantage.
 The second is the **Guest Success Management System (GSMS)**: 
for luxury resorts, boutique hotels and independent properties, this category is invaluable for making a great impression in a sea of new luxury competitors; while large chains compete on scale, independents can compete on relevance.


Sources: [Restaurant Dive – NRA Show](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/restaurant-technology-executives-warn-against-ai-overdependence/821024/) | [Hospitality Net – HITEC 2026](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/opinion/4133415/hitec-2026-was-the-show-where-agentic-governance-reached-centerstage)

So what: The AMP category is the clearest near-term buying opportunity in hotel tech — and the most underserved. Any PE-backed hospitality SaaS company that can offer credible agent orchestration, audit trails, and token-cost management will have a commercial argument that resonates with CFOs, not just CTOs. For operators: token cost is a real budget line by 2027 — model now.

## Governance & Security


The White House issued an Executive Order titled "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security" on June 2, 2026. While the Order is about federal systems and national cyber defense, it signals that AI has crossed a threshold — from an experimental productivity tool to a critical layer of operational infrastructure that governments now treat as a matter of national security. For hospitality, the relevance is not theoretical.



A modern hotel is no longer merely a physical property — it is a digitally orchestrated environment and an operational-intelligence platform. The same intelligence that makes a property efficient also makes it exposed. The competitive question is shifting from "How many AI features do we have?" to "Can we govern AI responsibly, secure our connected systems, and turn operational trust into guest experience?"



Regulators are increasing scrutiny of hotel AI governance as AI becomes embedded in guest personalisation, pricing, and travel discovery. AI-driven discovery lacks transparency in how results are generated, making it harder for hotels to understand or correct visibility outcomes; hotel operators are being pushed towards more structured AI governance frameworks including clearer documentation of how guest data is collected and used, stronger vendor oversight, and improved data standardisation.


Sources: [Hospitality Net – White House EO](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/opinion/4132771/when-national-ai-security-becomes-a-hospitality-imperative) | [Hotel Management Network – Governance](https://www.hotelmanagement-network.com/news/regulators-turn-attention-to-hotel-ai-governance/)

So what: The governance gap between "AI as feature marketing" and "AI with auditable decision trails" is closing under regulatory pressure. Hotel brands and SaaS vendors that have not mapped where AI lives in their stacks — in procurement, revenue management, guest messaging — face incoming procurement and insurance scrutiny they are not prepared for. Build the AI inventory now; the requirement to produce it is coming.

## Data & Integration

The data foundation problem is the consistent blocker across every deployment category. 
The 2026 Hotel Operations Index found that only 11% of hotel operators have a fully integrated technology stack; layer AI on top of fragmented systems and disconnected data, and it simply makes bad decisions faster.
 
The thing limiting hospitality AI in 2026 is not model quality — it is the property management system underneath it. Legacy PMS platforms have limited APIs, inconsistent schemas, and maintenance-heavy direct integrations; bolt enough AI tools onto that foundation and you get duplicate guest records, broken workflows, and what the industry has started calling integration debt.


For restaurants, the integration problem mirrors the hotel dynamic. 
Technology fragmentation is fading as restaurants increasingly adopt unified systems; industry analysts predict a wave of consolidation among restaurant technology providers in 2025–2026 precisely because operators are tired of stitching together systems that were never designed to talk to each other.


Sources: [Hapi – Hotel Operations Index](https://www.stayhapi.com/resources/ai-in-hospitality-2026-why-data-has-to-come-before-the-algorithm) | [AlphaCorp – Hospitality AI](https://alphacorp.ai/blog/ai-for-hospitality-2026-what-actually-works-now)

So what: Data integration is the single highest-leverage investment for operators who want AI to deliver ROI. Vendors who can demonstrate a clean, governed entity layer sitting above the PMS — rather than yet another point-solution bolt-on — are selling the right thing for 2026. For a PE-backed portfolio, the data estate quality of an acquisition target is now a material valuation input.
