## AI Visibility & Discovery

The defining fight of this news cycle is who controls the moment a diner or traveller decides where to eat or sleep — and the map is redrawing itself across Google, DoorDash, and OpenTable simultaneously.


**Google** is redefining search itself, introducing "agentic capabilities" within AI Mode beginning with restaurant reservations in the UK. The move signals a shift from discovery to execution — if Google's AI can manage the full booking workflow within search, it could reduce the need for users to engage directly with platforms such as OpenTable, Resy, and TheFork.
 On the hotel side, 
Google's Direct Offers pilot is expanding to IHG Hotels & Resorts, joining earlier partners Booking Holdings and Expedia, while Google builds toward in-conversation booking through the Universal Commerce Protocol. Travel companies are hedging by distributing inventory across multiple AI platforms simultaneously — IHG is also distributing inventory via ChatGPT.



**Booking Holdings** CEO Glenn Fogel disclosed on the company's Q2 earnings call (August 4) that the company saw pressure on organic search results from Google's AI Overviews. "I do believe that some of the changes that were made in the display at Google definitely put some pressure on SEO," Fogel told analysts. Google's AI Overviews are squeezing Booking's organic search, though SEO is a small part of the business relative to billions spent on paid search, and direct traffic in the mid-60% range remains stable. Booking is accelerating its AI strategy while raising its transformation-program savings target to $650 million in annual run-rate savings by 2027.



**Hospitality.today** editor Markus Busch delivered the sharpest structural read of the cycle: on April 28, Booking Holdings CEO Glenn Fogel told analysts the company was working with OpenAI and Google explicitly to ensure Booking's supply is discoverable wherever travellers begin their journey. Busch's diagnosis: OTAs score at the top of every signal hierarchy that drives AI recommendation frequency — deep editorial coverage, enormous YouTube footprint — advantage transferred without OTAs rebuilding anything. "The hotel paid for the top of the funnel. The OTA collected at the bottom. Same as before. Different pipe."



**OpenTable** made its most aggressive positioning move yet: publishing its 2026 Top 100 Hotel Restaurants in America — drawn from more than 10 million verified diner reviews — while simultaneously upgrading its generative AI dining assistant, Concierge, to operate from the platform's homepage for the first time. When OpenTable launched Concierge in July 2025, the tool lived inside individual restaurant profiles. The July 2026 upgrade changes the architecture entirely: Concierge now responds to open-ended, intent-level queries — "spot locals love in Nashville," "best date-night in a hotel near downtown Chicago" — across the full network of 65,000-plus restaurants, filtered by real-time availability.
 
OpenTable's 2026 Diner Trends report finds that 44% of Americans plan to use AI more for restaurant discovery and reservations in 2026.


Sources: [Restaurant Technology News — Google](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/04/google-introduces-ai-powered-restaurant-booking-as-search-evolves-into-action/) · [Skift — Booking Q2](https://skift.com/2026/08/04/booking-holdings-saw-pressure-from-googles-ai-overviews/) · [Hospitality Net — OTAs harvesting](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/opinion/4133170/hotels-are-building-the-ai-discovery-layer-otas-are-harvesting-it) · [TechTimes — OpenTable Concierge](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320769/20260716/best-hotel-restaurants-2026-opentable-list-goes-live-ai-concierge-moves-homepage.htm)

**So what:** Your hotel or restaurant's structured-data and MCP investment gets you into the AI consideration set, but completion still routes through whoever controls the conversion layer. Building AI eligibility without a direct-booking strategy is running someone else's funnel. In restaurants, the cost-benefit arithmetic is shifting fast: 
restaurant listing sites like DoorDash account for more than 41% of the sources AI tools cite when recommending restaurants
 — managing your listing on those platforms is now de facto AI optimisation.

## Vendor Moves

The biggest watchlist development of the cycle is DoorDash's accelerating attempt to become the single app for discovery, ordering, and reservations — backed by the AI stack of three frontier model providers.


**DoorDash** launched a new AI chatbot called "Ask DoorDash" (June 11) that lets users order food and groceries with text prompts and photos. "Traditional search works best when you know the exact restaurant or table you're looking for," DoorDash wrote. "Ask DoorDash is designed for the moments when you don't." Uber Eats launched an AI-powered "Cart Assistant" in February; Instacart has its own AI shopping assistant — the delivery app arms race for conversational ordering is live.
 
The chatbot rollout arrives as DoorDash works to unify the technology underpinning its portfolio — SevenRooms ($1.2 billion) and Deliveroo ($4 billion) — into a single tech stack. Early testing showed nearly half of all restaurant orders placed through the tool came from restaurants the customer had never ordered from before; grocery carts assembled through the chatbot ran more than 35% higher in value and were completed five times faster. DoorDash's co-founder Andy Fang confirmed the product draws on AI models built by OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google, supplemented by open-source options.



**Toast** launched Toast IQ Grow (May 2026) as part of a Spring 2026 product release containing more than 20 updates. At the centre is an AI-powered Marketing Agent that builds audiences and automates campaigns across channels. Toast also launched Toast Drive-Thru in 2026 — a unified enterprise QSR solution combining hardware, POS-native software, AI voice ordering integrations, vehicle ID and lane tagging, and Speed of Service reporting.
 
Pilot customers using Toast IQ Grow recorded an average 8% increase in sales compared with similar Toast restaurants. Engineering coding velocity increased more than 60% year over year, helping the company launch its marketing agent three months earlier than planned. About 40% of customer-support interactions are now resolved by AI.



**American Express** is folding Tock into Resy this summer, roughly doubling the platform's venue library to over 25,000 restaurants. The merger brings Tock's prepaid booking model and tiered experience features directly into Resy, creating a significantly larger single destination for premium restaurant bookings.


Sources: [TechCrunch — Ask DoorDash](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/11/doordashs-new-ai-chatbot-lets-you-order-with-prompts-and-photos/) · [Restaurant Technology News — Toast](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/06/toast-advances-restaurant-operations-with-connected-pos-ai-and-guest-engagement-technology/) · [Yahoo Finance — Toast AI](https://sg.finance.yahoo.com/news/toast-ai-strategy-agents-drive-164600798.html)

**So what:** The reservation market has consolidated into three ecosystems: DoorDash/SevenRooms, Amex/Resy/Tock, Booking Holdings/OpenTable. Choosing a reservation platform is now partly choosing whose data flywheel you feed and which consumer base reaches you. Mid-market operators without strong direct-booking capability are most exposed to the DoorDash platform's increasing scope.

## Operational AI

The clearest signal from the 2026 NRA Show is that the industry has moved past the "should we do AI" debate into a more useful one: "which AI actually makes operations better versus which adds another dashboard nobody checks."


At the National Restaurant Association Show, Restaurant Dive's end-of-interview question — "what's one thing people are getting wrong about restaurants right now?" — produced a near-universal answer from tech leaders: "artificial intelligence." The skepticism didn't correlate with a lack of enthusiasm on the show floor. Shake Shack is using AI to identify and alert managers to inefficiencies; Culver's began rolling out computer vision in its drive-thrus; Dairy Queen expanded a voice AI drive-thru test with Presto. Popmenu CEO Brendan Sweeney warned that overadoption rests on shaky fundamentals — AI token costs can exceed the salaries of the workers the technology purports to replace.



Sweeney's read: "Being first is not always the best. Being right and doing it the right way is the best." Toast CMO Kelly Esten's framing: "It can't be another thing to do. It has to actually do some of the work for you."



**SevenRooms** Voice AI case study provides one of the cleaner outcome data points in the cycle: Casper Hospitality put Voice AI on the phones and in one month saw 3,800+ calls handled, 850+ covers created, and $28K in booked revenue. SevenRooms found that 79% of diners globally are comfortable with AI handling reservations — yet only 32% of operators are using AI for call management, despite 40% of calls going unanswered.



The broader adoption picture: the National Restaurant Association's 2026 State of the Industry report puts AI adoption at 26% of operators (those using AI-related tools). Marketing is the top use case (19% of full-service operators), followed by administrative tasks (10%). Only 6% use it for customer orders. EU AI Act Article 50 transparency obligations apply from August 2, 2026 — any chatbot or voice agent talking to guests must disclose it is AI; emotion recognition in the workplace has been prohibited since February 2025.


Sources: [Restaurant Dive — NRA Show](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/restaurant-technology-executives-warn-against-ai-overdependence/821024/) · [SevenRooms research](https://sevenrooms.com/research/restaurant-trends/)

**So what:** The gap between diner comfort with AI (75% comfortable using it for reservations per DoorDash survey) and operator deployment (28% using AI for calls and customer service) is the clearest near-term commercial opportunity. Voice AI for phone reservations — with a case study showing $28K in monthly booked revenue from a single implementation — is the most accessible entry point for mid-market operators.

## Governance & Security


At HITEC 2026 in San Antonio, the dominant theme shifted from AI deployment to AI governance. Hotel Mogel Consulting identified **Agent Management Platforms (AMPs)** as the critical new software category to emerge from the conference. As hotels begin deploying dozens of AI-powered workflows across revenue management, marketing, reservations, maintenance, finance, and guest communications, the challenge is taking stock and governing all these agents. Key governance questions for any portfolio: Which models are being used? What guest data can they access? What actions are they authorised to take? Is there an audit trail? Can PII be scrubbed before processing?



The AMP becomes the control center that governs permissions, monitoring, security, performance, and cost. Token costs are about to become a very real part of the tech stack opex budget — hotels will eventually scrutinise token consumption the same way they analyse labor costs and OTA commissions.



On the security front: the average cost of a data breach in hospitality was $3.86 million in 2024. AI-driven phishing has moved beyond "urgent invoice" emails — today's AI tools craft contextually relevant messages that closely mimic internal communication styles. Ransomware groups are moving away from encryption toward operational disruption: for hotels, that means targeting property management systems, door locks, HVAC controls, and booking integrations.


Sources: [Hotel Mogel — HITEC 2026](https://hotelmogel.com/agentic-governance-is-the-big-trend-from-hitec-2026/) · [Nomadix — cybersecurity predictions](https://nomadix.com/2026-hotel-cybersecurity-predictions/)

**So what:** Any hotel group that has deployed AI across multiple departments without a governance layer is accumulating operational and reputational risk that compounds as agent count grows. The AMP category is nascent — no established vendor owns it yet — which represents a build-vs-buy decision that hotel CIOs should make now rather than after an incident.
