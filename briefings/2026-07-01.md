## FUNDING & M&A

The biggest capital story of the cycle is the rapid consolidation of restaurant reservation infrastructure into the balance sheets of financial services companies and delivery platforms.

**American Express** announced a proposed $700 million cash acquisition of TheFork from Tripadvisor on 15 June 2026. 
The proposed transaction would add TheFork's network of more than 50,000 restaurants across 11 European countries to American Express' growing dining ecosystem. Combined with existing platforms Resy and Tock, the acquisition would expand American Express' network to approximately 75,000 bookable venues worldwide, creating one of the largest restaurant reservation and dining engagement platforms in the industry.
 
American Express first entered the reservation technology market through its acquisition of Resy in 2019. The acquisition of Tock in 2024 significantly broadened American Express' capabilities in reservations, ticketed events, prepaid dining experiences and hospitality operations.
 Simultaneously, 
American Express is folding Tock into Resy, creating a single, 25,000 venue-strong giant to drive consumer spending and credit card loyalty. The move boosts Resy's venue count to 25,000, but will sunset the Tock brand, formally uniting two onetime startups against reservations market leader OpenTable.
 
In the three weeks following the Resy Credit announcement, there was a 36% increase in Resy reservations made by users with a US Platinum card linked to their account — and a five times increase in the daily average number of accounts being linked. People using the Resy credit are spending, on average, 25% more.


**DoorDash** closed a $1.2 billion acquisition of SevenRooms — the data context behind its new consumer features. 
"Delivery and dine-in have typically been siloed data sets," SevenRooms co-founder Joel Montaniel said. "So if a customer has ordered six times, and they're coming into the restaurant for the first time, are they a first-time customer or a seventh-time customer?"
 
The stakes are high for DoorDash, which is in the middle of a massive investment cycle that involves the creation of a unified tech platform to house all its brands following a string of big acquisitions. Purchases include the $1.2 billion deal for SevenRooms and the nearly $4 billion acquisition of Deliveroo.


On the hotel side, 
hospitality technology startups raised more than $1 billion across 40 companies between April 2025 and March 2026, with property management systems and AI-led platforms attracting the largest share. The burst was most visible in a 90-day period between December 2025 and February 2026, when Mews ($300 million), Kindred ($125 million across two rounds) and Limehome (€75 million) closed almost back-to-back.
 
AI-led guest experience platforms were a standout investment, with Duve, Chatlyn, Conduit and Canary Technologies raising a combined $152.6 million, targeting the pressure to deliver fast, personalised service with leaner teams. Canary notably acquired OpenKey in February to expand its access to door lock providers.
 
Abode Worldwide CEO Jessica Gillingham said: "The companies attracting the strongest investor backing share a common trait. They sit close to essential operator workflows and become more valuable over time. Unified systems generate more data, better automation and higher switching costs."


On the M&A climate for hotel assets, 
deal activity in the US hospitality and leisure sector has slowed in 2026, with overall deal volume down by approximately 2.5% over the past six months. However, the deals that are occurring are concentrated in premium segments such as luxury hotels, wellness resorts, and gaming platforms, commanding higher premiums due to their pricing power, repeat customer engagement, and readiness for AI integration.
 
The concentration of deals in upscale, upper-upscale, and luxury hotels has reached 73% of all hotel transactions — the highest in two years.


Sources: [Restaurant Technology News – TheFork acquisition](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/06/american-express-expands-restaurant-technology-ambitions-with-700-million-thefork-acquisition/), [Hotel Dive – $1B funding report](https://www.hoteldive.com/news/hospitality-tech-attracts-1b-in-funding-pms-ai-leaders/817439/), [Hotel News Resource – PwC M&A midyear](https://www.hotelnewsresource.com/article141836.html)

So what: AmEx now controls the dining relationship from card issuance through discovery, reservation, payment, and data — a vertically integrated position no pure-play restaurant software company can match. For hotel-side PE investors, AI readiness has become an explicit factor in valuation; properties without first-party data infrastructure are being bid down.

## AI VISIBILITY & DISCOVERY

The agentic booking race moved from prototype to consumer reality this quarter, with two distinct dynamics playing out across restaurant and hotel verticals.

**On the restaurant side**, Google's April 2026 expansion of agentic restaurant booking to eight international markets marked the clearest milestone. 
Google rolled out AI Mode's restaurant booking feature to eight countries, letting users in the UK, Australia, Canada, and five other markets reserve tables directly through Search for the first time. The announcement pairs the geographic expansion with a redesigned mobile interface that adds a Gemini 3 model switcher and a new bottom sheet prompt box. By January 2026, AI Mode had reached 75 million daily active users.
 
Eight reservation platform partners power the feature: TheFork, SevenRooms, ResDiary, Mozrest, Foodhub, Dojo, DesignMyNight, and OpenTable. UK searches for "when to book a table" surged 140% in 2026.


**Resy** has responded offensively by building integrations with AI platforms. 
Instead of searching for a list of venues, diners are asking AI tools to provide them with a single, curated answer, and increasingly diners expect to complete the booking experience in the same environment. Resy is meeting diners where they are searching by building integrations with AI platforms like Claude to help restaurants reach diners where decisions are increasingly being made.


The DoorDash data provides a useful demand-side baseline: 
22% of consumers have used an AI tool like ChatGPT or Google Gemini to help choose a restaurant. According to Yext research, restaurant listing sites like DoorDash account for more than 41% of the sources AI tools cite when recommending restaurants.
 
Three-quarters of consumers say they're comfortable using AI for reservations, but only 28% of operators are using AI to manage calls and customer service. With an estimated 40% of reservation calls going unanswered industry-wide, AI gives restaurants another way to capture demand.


**On the hotel side**, Google confirmed in November 2025 that hotel and flight agentic booking is in development. 
As of June 2026, agentic flight and hotel booking has been announced but not launched; there is no public release date. Agentic booking is already live in the US for restaurants, event tickets and appointments, but finishing a hotel booking inside AI Mode is still in development. Named launch partners include Booking.com, Choice Hotels International, Expedia, IHG Hotels & Resorts, Marriott International and Wyndham Hotels & Resorts.


The MCP (Model Context Protocol) is emerging as foundational infrastructure for hotel AI discoverability. 
MCP lets AI tools safely connect to a hotel's PMS, booking engine, and other systems, enabling tools like ChatGPT, Claude, and Perplexity to give real, accurate answers rather than generic ones. Hotels that have adopted an MCP solution will be ready to benefit from agentic bookings when AI tools go a step further and complete actions on behalf of users.


**OpenAI** pulled back from direct transactions in March 2026. 
OpenAI has shifted its focus for ChatGPT from completing transactions to product discovery, moving checkout processes to third-party apps. An OpenAI spokesperson confirmed: "We're prioritising making ChatGPT search and product discovery great, with the Agentic Commerce Protocol serving as the infrastructure that connects users to merchants across the full shopping journey."
 
Booking Holdings shares jumped over 8.5% on the day the news broke. OpenAI isn't surrendering the transaction layer; the AI platforms are declining to compete for it — so Booking.com isn't feeding inventory to platforms that will ultimately replace it; it's establishing itself as the destination those platforms send travelers to when they're ready to book.


Sources: [Restaurant Technology News – Google booking](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/04/google-introduces-ai-powered-restaurant-booking-as-search-evolves-into-action/), [Skift – OpenAI checkout walkback](https://skift.com/2026/03/05/openai-chatgpt-checkout-walkback/), [PhocusWire – Booking Holdings Q1 2026](https://www.phocuswire.com/news/online/booking-holdings-earnings-q1-2026)

So what: For restaurant operators not yet listed on OpenTable, SevenRooms, or another Reserve with Google partner, this is an urgent structural gap: Google's agentic booking layer only surfaces restaurants through its existing partner rails. Visibility in AI search is no longer a marketing metric — it is a capacity-fill mechanism.

## VENDOR MOVES

A cluster of significant product launches in the last 72 hours, concentrated in the restaurant sector.

**DoorDash** launched "Ask DoorDash" on June 11, 2026. 
The chatbot allows users to search the app for what they're looking for in their own words instead of having to scroll through restaurants and stores to build a cart. You can tell the chatbot what you're in the mood for, share a recipe link to find the items, or describe the reservation you're looking for.
 
Nearly half of all restaurant orders placed through the tool were from a place the customer had never ordered from before. Carts assembled through the chatbot ran more than 35% higher in value than a standard grocery order, with users completing them five times faster than through the regular app interface. The system draws on AI models built by OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google, supplemented by open-source options.
 
DoorDash intends to license the technology to outside partners — grocers, restaurants, and other retailers — who would deploy it under their own branding, a move that sharpens its rivalry with Instacart.


**Toast** closed June with three concurrent moves. 
The company launched Toast IQ Grow, a marketing-focused solution introduced as part of its Spring 2026 product release. Toast described the release as more than 20 updates across marketing, payroll, inventory and operations. At the centre of the launch is an AI-powered Marketing Agent that can build audiences and automate campaigns across channels, supported by a reimagined Toast Local app.
 On the QSR side, 
Toast Drive-Thru integrates POS, KDS, outdoor digital menu boards, and AI voice ordering partnerships. "By seamlessly integrating POS, KDS, outdoor digital menu boards, and AI voice ordering partnerships, Toast Drive-Thru gives operators the tools they need to keep pace with peak demand today," said Steve Fredette, President and Co-Founder of Toast.
 And on June 16, 
Toast launched Toast Lab, an incubator that will provide funding and tech for a new restaurant in Boston that it will help operate, allowing Toast to pressure-test new technology. The winning operator will get strategic capital, mentorship from Toast's executive team, access to Toast's full product suite, and real-time customer support.


**PAR Technology and Square** unveiled competing AI agent products in April 2026. 
PAR's agents are tied to its other products like POS and marketing, providing operators with "one source of truth." Taco Bell franchisee Charter Foods asked PAR's agent to identify stores that could benefit by staying open later; late-night sales increased 20% as a result.
 
Square's Managerbot is proactive: it surfaces insights and metrics the restaurant needs to know, like that it's running low on milk, or that several menu items have duplicate names in the POS. It can also perform tasks such as drafting employee schedules, creating and running email campaigns, and generating purchase orders for supplies.


**Yelp for Restaurants** released its 2026 Spring Product Release at the National Restaurant Association Show on June 30. 
Yelp's 2026 Spring Product Release introduced a broader version of Yelp Assistant designed to help consumers move from search to answers and actions, along with an enhanced version of Menu Vision that overlays dish, drink and dessert photos on text-based menus through a phone camera.
 
As of December 31, 2025, Yelp reported 330 million cumulative reviews, along with 485,000 paying advertising locations on a monthly average basis for Q1 2026.


**SevenRooms** launched Channel Connect, described in RTN's recent ticker as bringing "hotel-style distribution management to restaurants" — the first major product initiative under DoorDash ownership, positioning it to compete more directly with OpenTable's network breadth.

Operators deploying all four will face serious data fragmentation; the value of any one platform's AI depends entirely on whether it can see the full guest. Choosing an integration strategy before vendor lock-in arrives is the operational priority.

## OPERATIONAL AI

The focus has shifted from AI as a dashboard add-on to AI as a system that completes tasks autonomously — and the first real-world usage data is confirming where the ROI lands.


Toast launched Toast IQ in October 2025 to help operators run their businesses faster and smarter. Analysis of 125,000 US restaurant locations that used Toast IQ in Q1 2026 showed fine dining locations used Toast IQ 29% more than fast-casual restaurants. Operators most frequently initiated conversations about sales and revenue (47%), menu and inventory (34%), and guest and marketing (32%).
 
Toast IQ acts as an "intelligent right hand" for operators, moving beyond simple data reporting to provide proactive, actionable insights — identifying top-performing menu items, flagging labour cost variances, or anticipating local market trends. Operators can now execute critical tasks — such as 86ing menu items across all channels, adjusting employee shift clock-outs, or launching AI-driven marketing campaigns — with simple commands.


The agentic hotel operations story follows a similar arc. 
Canary Technologies co-founder SJ Sawhney notes that AI Guest Messaging, AI Voice and AI Webchat are already live in thousands of hotels, powering millions of guest touchpoints. "AI is evolving to be fully agentic, handling tasks end-to-end. Instead of just answering a guest question, AI will manage the request, update systems and trigger the next step automatically."


For the hotel revenue management stack, 
AI scheduling tools have produced a 30% reduction in time spent building rosters and a 25% drop in staff turnover, mostly by matching shifts to demand patterns and employee preferences. Demand forecasting is now the same model feeding both pricing and labour, so commercial and operational decisions stop fighting each other.


Sources: [BusinessWire – Toast Drive-Thru launch](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260414359751/en/Toast-Launches-Unified-Drive-Thru-Solution-to-Modernize-Enterprise-Quick-Service-Restaurant-Operations), [Nation's Restaurant News – AI agents](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology/the-hot-new-restaurant-tech-trend-ai-agents)

So what: The most credible signals this cycle come from operators, not vendors — Charter Foods' 20% late-night uplift from PAR's agent and DoorDash's 35% higher cart values are the type of operational attribution that justifies a budget conversation. Stack this against the Toast IQ usage data: if 47% of AI conversations at 125,000+ locations are about sales and revenue, that is evidence of genuine workflow adoption, not experimentation.

## DATA & INTEGRATION

The hospitality tech industry is converging on a consensus that AI capability is downstream of data architecture — and the investment community has noticed.


The thing limiting hospitality AI in 2026 isn't model quality — it's the property management system underneath it. Apptad's May 2026 analysis frames it directly: legacy PMS platforms have limited APIs, inconsistent schemas, and maintenance-heavy direct integrations. Bolt enough AI tools onto that foundation and you get duplicate guest records, broken workflows, and what the industry has started calling "integration debt."



Mews has positioned itself as hospitality's "operating system" rather than just a database. With 15,000 customers across 85 countries and a $2.5 billion valuation, Mews raised $300 million in January 2026 to develop advanced automation capabilities. In February 2026, Mews was named AAHOA's official PMS, representing roughly 60% of US hotels. Mews's technical architecture is built around a semantic layer — a unified data model that gives connected systems the contextual understanding needed to make accurate decisions.



The "should we move to the cloud?" debate is over. The question replacing it: who owns the transaction layer around your hotel? The leading cloud-native PMS vendors are not building toward better front desks — they are building toward payment orchestration, embedded financing, and transaction economics.



Bold prediction from a HITEC contributor: the PMS becomes the heart of the hotel, connecting and coordinating most AI tools, agents and decisions running across the property. "We are already moving toward a world where a guest's AI assistant and a hotel's system can talk to each other — booking, personalising, transacting — with potentially no OTA or brand booking engine involved. That makes data ownership critical."


Sources: [AlphaCorp – Hospitality AI June 2026](https://alphacorp.ai/blog/ai-for-hospitality-2026-what-actually-works-now), [Hotel Dive – $1B funding report](https://www.hoteldive.com/news/hospitality-tech-attracts-1b-in-funding-pms-ai-leaders/817439/)

So what: For PE-backed hospitality SaaS: the most durable moat is not model quality but data architecture. Mews' semantic layer and its acquisitions of Flexkeeping and DataChat are the playbook — consolidate operational data into a governed layer, then AI runs on top. For operators, the practical test: can you pull a clean guest record across booking, stay, loyalty, and messaging in under ten seconds? If not, fix the plumbing before buying another AI tool.

## GOVERNANCE & SECURITY


Hospitality has become what one analyst calls a "cyber front desk": the more third-party tools you connect, the larger your attack surface gets. Every vendor integration is a potential breach vector. Every voice agent and service robot collects behavioural data that has to be governed. Consent is shifting from compliance overhead to brand asset — guests will share data with operators they trust and want a clear answer to "what is this for."



The regulatory surface area around hospitality is expanding. EU Regulation 2024/1028 on short-term rentals applies from May 2026. PCI DSS v4.0.1 has new requirements active since March 2025. Compliance isn't overhead anymore — it's a barrier to entry. Vendors that handle it natively have a structural advantage that no amount of AI marketing can replicate.
