## Vendor Moves

The biggest structural development in restaurant reservations this cycle is consolidation into three corporate ecosystems, each backed by a payments giant or delivery platform. 
**American Express** announced a proposed $700 million acquisition of **TheFork** from Tripadvisor — adding more than 50,000 restaurants across 11 European countries to its dining portfolio. Combined with Resy and Tock, Amex would expand its dining network to 75,000 bookable venues, now the largest in the world by venue count.
 
By folding Tock's inventory into Resy this summer, creating a 25,000-venue fortress, Amex has made the card credit a powerful switch incentive: in the three weeks following the Platinum card credit announcement, there was a 36% increase in Resy reservations from linked Platinum card accounts, and a five-times increase in daily account linkages. People using the Resy credit are spending, on average, 25% more.


**DoorDash/SevenRooms** occupies the second ecosystem. 
DoorDash acquired SevenRooms for $1.2 billion in 2025, giving the delivery giant a direct path into in-restaurant guest data. The reservation platform market now sees three systems functioning less as booking tools and more as front doors into three distinct corporate ecosystems — and picking one is partly picking whose world a restaurant wants to live in.
 
Meanwhile OpenTable is pushing back: its Visa and Chase partnership creates cardholder incentives, and its spring user agreement update required subscribers to agree that OpenTable is their primary reservations platform.
 The Boston Globe reported 
that one Boston restaurateur was offered a six-figure signing bonus to switch platforms
, and an operator quoted by the Globe summarised the stakes plainly: 
"It's less about the reservations, and it's all about the data."


**Toast** continued its platform expansion with two notable launches. 
Toast Drive-Thru launched in 2026 as a unified solution for enterprise quick-service operations, combining hardware, POS-native software, and AI voice ordering integrations. The accompanying Spring 2026 release included Toast IQ Grow, with an AI-powered Marketing Agent designed to build audiences and automate campaigns across channels.
 
At the Goldman Sachs Communacopia + Technology Conference on September 9, 2026, Toast outlined a business that has grown well beyond its POS roots, now commanding an $18.98 billion market cap on $6.8 billion in trailing twelve-month revenue — 23% growth year-over-year — with its share of US restaurant locations rising from approximately 4–5% at IPO to more than 20% now.


**PAR Technology** launched Guest360 on September 1. 
Built alongside PAR Punchh, Guest360 uses Profile Stitching to consolidate a guest's activity across POS, kiosk, drive-thru, and mobile app into a single lasting profile rather than fragmented records, with the unified view then powering PAR Intelligence for audience, campaign, and next-step recommendations.
 CEO Savneet Singh stated: "Most restaurants know their loyalty members well, but that's only a fraction of their guests."

**Mews** completed its most significant product announcement at Unfold 2026, 
launching five new products completing an AI-native operating system that unifies PMS, RMS, messaging, automations, and accounts receivable on a single data model, citing a 476% three-year ROI per IDC research. Mews RMS embeds dynamic pricing inside the platform, running 150 million pricing calculations a day, lifting total revenue per square meter up 13.7% on average against hotels without an RMS.
 Founder Richard Valtr declared: "PMS, RMS, CRS, CRM, POS — these aren't real things. They're acronyms invented by software companies to sell more software. Inside Mews, those categories don't exist as separate systems."

Sources: [BusinessWire — Amex/TheFork](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260614939103/en/American-Express-Announces-Proposed-Acquisition-of-TheFork-a-Leading-European-Restaurant-Booking-Platform) · [Fast Company — Resy/Tock merger](https://www.fastcompany.com/91496951/amex-resy-tock-restaurant-reservation-wars) · [Restaurant Technology News — Toast](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/06/toast-advances-restaurant-operations-with-connected-pos-ai-and-guest-engagement-technology/) · [Hospitality Net — Mews Unfold](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4132634/mews-unveils-the-operating-system-for-hospitality)

So what: The reservation platform is no longer a booking tool — it is the primary CRM and data layer for the guest relationship. Mid-market operators who have not yet audited what data leaves their venue when a booking comes from a network platform (and where it goes under the new ownership structures) are now operating at a material disadvantage. The platform your restaurant or hotel signs with in 2026 is a multi-year data partnership, not just a booking widget.

## AI Visibility & Discovery

The front door of hotel and restaurant discovery is being rebuilt at speed, with Google's AI Mode now live and AI recommendation engines structurally filtering out most operators. 
On September 1, 2026, Google officially launched hotel booking through AI Mode in Search, allowing travelers to discover and book stays "in one seamless conversation." English-speaking users in the US can review and compare hotels, choose a room from integrated partners including Choice Hotels International, Hilton, Wyndham, Marriott, and IHG, and complete the reservation with Google Pay.
 
Google's Universal Commerce Protocol and Agent Payments Protocol power the feature. Crucially, Booking Holdings CFO Ewout Steenbergen confirmed that conversational AI agent bookings currently represent less than 1% of total room nights — meaning this is an early-stage structural shift, not yet a volume story.


**Google (restaurant)**: 
Separately, Google introduced agentic restaurant reservation capabilities in AI Mode, beginning in the UK — marking a shift from passive search toward AI systems that navigate booking systems, select time slots, and complete transactions without requiring the user to intervene at each step.


For restaurants, the AI visibility gap is now quantified and severe. 
A May 2026 report from Uberall found that 83% of restaurant locations are entirely invisible in AI-generated recommendations. The gap is not explained by being offline — 86% of those same restaurants maintain a Google presence. They have done the traditional local-SEO work and still vanish when the question is routed through an AI model.
 
SOCi's 2026 Local Visibility Index shows the average restaurant brand appears in Google's local 3-Pack 24.3% of the time but is recommended by ChatGPT only 5.3% of the time. Culver's — a standout in the LVI — achieves 57.7% Google 3-Pack visibility and a 30% ChatGPT recommendation rate and 45.8% on Gemini. Papa John's, with no locations in the 3-Pack and 67.4% profile accuracy, is recommended by none of the major AI platforms.


**OpenAI**: 
The Foursquare/ChatGPT data partnership reportedly powers 60–70% of ChatGPT's local answers, and the Yelp/OpenAI data licensing deal changed what ChatGPT can surface for restaurants — previously, ChatGPT cited Yelp not at all for restaurants in late 2024.


Sources: [Hotel Dive — Google AI Mode hotel booking](https://www.hoteldive.com/news/google-introduces-ai-mode-hotel-booking/829265/) · [Hospitality AI Intelligence](https://briefings.inverisla.com/) · [SOCi 2026 LVI](https://www.soci.ai/blog/restaurant-local-visibility-benchmarks/)

So what: Hotel and restaurant operators need a GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) strategy alongside their SEO strategy — these are now distinct disciplines. For hotels, ensuring your property data is machine-readable and present in Google's Hotel Center with a direct price feed is no longer optional after Google's September 30 cutoff for third-party rate sourcing. For restaurants, ratings thresholds are now structural: below 4.3 stars on ChatGPT, 4.1 on Perplexity, and 3.9 on Gemini, you are effectively invisible regardless of other optimisation.

## Data & Integration

Every major hotel chain has committed to an agentic AI roadmap, and every one of them is bottlenecked at the same layer. 
Marriott's $1B+ agentic mesh, Hilton's AI Planner, Hyatt and Accor inside ChatGPT, IHG's AI-enhanced CRM — all are constrained by the same architectural problem: the PMS was built when a guest was a stay at a property, not a relationship across the chain.
 
The PMS is the single largest holder of guest signal, but rarely more than 20% of the total signal across a chain. Full-service luxury brands have a smaller PMS share because spa, F&B, and concierge systems carry more of the signal.
 Fewer than one in four hotels have core systems — PMS, CRS, RMS, loyalty, and CRM — fully integrated in a way that supports a unified guest profile, per industry survey synthesis cited by Apptad.


MCP became the de-facto integration standard in H1 2026, with agentic AI crossing into production unevenly, the data-foundation conversation entering the boardroom (SAP+Reltio, Marriott's agentic mesh), and conversational interfaces beginning to replace dashboards.


For restaurants, the equivalent bottleneck is the guest data layer. 
PAR's Guest360 launch directly addresses this: most restaurant transactions happen outside the loyalty program — at POS, kiosk, drive-thru, pay-at-table, and delivery — leaving brands with guest activity that is anonymous, duplicated, or disconnected. Guest360 closes that gap through Profile Stitching.
 The pattern is identical to what hotels face in their entity-layer problem.

Sources: [Apptad — Hotel Industry AI Problem](https://apptad.com/insights/the-hotel-industrys-ai-problem-lives-in-the-pms/) · [PAR Technology Guest360 launch](https://sg.finance.yahoo.com/news/par-technology-launches-guest360-bringing-113000900.html)

So what: "Build AI on top of a broken data layer and the AI reveals the problems rather than solving them," as Abode Worldwide's report noted. CIOs at hotel chains should run a 90-day entity audit before committing to agentic AI vendor contracts — specifically mapping which system is authoritative for the guest entity across the franchise and management estate, and what data-rights clauses actually say. Restaurant multi-unit operators should audit how many guest transactions happen outside their loyalty programme before assuming their CDP can drive personalisation.

## Funding & M&A

The hospitality tech funding picture crossing the $1 billion mark is the headline, but the distribution is the signal. 
Between April 2025 and March 2026, 40 hospitality technology companies raised more than $1 billion, with PMS and AI-led platforms attracting the largest share, according to Abode Worldwide's Hospitality Tech Investment Index 2026.
 
Seven PMS companies raised a combined $408.1 million — the largest category total. The best-funded PMS businesses are using this position to build broader platforms through acquisition rather than third-party integrations: Mews' acquisitions of Flexkeeping and DataChat in late 2025 exemplify the strategy.



Four AI-powered guest experience platforms — Duve, Chatlyn, Conduit, and Canary Technologies — raised a combined $152.6 million. Canary, the largest at $80 million, also acquired mobile key platform OpenKey in February 2026 to expand access to door lock providers.
 
According to a 2026 study by Canary Technologies, 71% of hospitality professionals say AI is already having a significant or transformative impact on their industry.



The weaker claims are around AI as a catch-all fix. As Abode CEO Jessica Gillingham noted: "AI can't compensate for scattered data, disconnected systems or poor processes. If a hotel's underlying systems are fragmented, AI will only make operational problems more visible."


On the restaurant reservation side, the M&A story is being driven by payments-and-delivery companies acquiring data assets. 
American Express, which acquired Resy in 2019, now also owns Tock after a $400 million acquisition in 2024, giving a single card network control over two of the four major reservation platforms. DoorDash entered the category with its $1.2 billion SevenRooms acquisition in 2025, signalling that delivery giants now see in-restaurant guest data as core territory.


Sources: [Hotel Dive — $1B funding report](https://www.hoteldive.com/news/hospitality-tech-attracts-1b-in-funding-pms-ai-leaders/817439/) · [Abode Worldwide Investment Index](https://abodeworldwide.com/hospitality-tech-investment-index/) · [BusinessWire — Amex/TheFork](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260614939103/en/American-Express-Announces-Proposed-Acquisition-of-TheFork-a-Leading-European-Restaurant-Booking-Platform)

So what: For PE-backed hospitality SaaS, the investment thesis is consolidating clearly: unified platform businesses with compounding data advantages are attracting capital, standalone point-solution tools are facing a harder sell. The $1B milestone also confirms hospitality tech is no longer a niche category for specialist investors — the Abode report notes more than half of the 40 funded companies were founded after 2020, and 2023 alone accounts for ten, making this a genuinely early-stage market with leadership positions still up for grabs.

## Operational AI

The restaurant industry's senior technology leaders are now publicly cautioning against the hype they helped create. 
At the 2026 National Restaurant Association Show, Square's James Schonzeit said "AI is the biggest thing of this entire show," yet almost universally restaurant tech leaders answered "artificial intelligence" when asked what the sector is getting wrong. Popmenu CEO Brendan Sweeney warned that overadoption of LLMs rests on shaky fundamentals — the cost of AI tokens required to operate coding services can exceed the salaries of the workers the technology purports to replace, and with OpenAI pursuing an IPO, AI developers could face greater pressure to turn a profit they have not yet achieved.



Toast CMO Kelly Esten set the frame that is gaining the most traction among practical operators: "There's some parts of running a restaurant that are all about reliability. AI can't replace the core aspects of restaurant operations or restaurant technology. Instead, AI can augment robust underlying programs — improving workflows and speed of service." 


For hotels, the picture is similar. 
Mews' May 2026 survey of hoteliers found that 98% had used AI across operations in the prior six months, yet 59% said the front-desk welcome and check-in should remain human-led. Mews' Wouter Geerts noted: "The data tells a consistent story: hoteliers are optimistic about AI and willing to use it broadly." BCG's March 2026 analysis found fewer than 10% of hospitality companies qualified as "future built," while 25% are in an AI-scaling phase achieving ROI.


On the restaurant side, specific deployments with measurable outcomes are becoming the standard for evaluating genuine signal. 
Shake Shack is using AI to identify and alert managers to potential inefficiencies. Culver's began rolling out computer vision to measure service execution and vehicle flow in its drive-thrus. Dairy Queen expanded a voice AI drive-thru test to more locations in partnership with Presto.
 
Chipotle's Autocado system handles avocado prep — one of the most repetitive kitchen tasks — while keeping the rest of the process in human hands. Starbucks' Deep Brew platform supports order flow, staffing, and personalization, with the goal of managing constant overlap of mobile orders, delivery, and in-store traffic without visible robotics.


Sources: [Restaurant Dive — NRA Show AI overdependence](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/restaurant-technology-executives-warn-against-ai-overdependence/821024/) · [KitchenHub — Restaurant AI 2026](https://www.trykitchenhub.com/post/restaurant-ai-in-2026-what-worked-and-what-didnt)

So what: The practical operator consensus is now: narrow, specific AI deployments tied to real operational pressure points survive; attempts to redesign the entire experience rarely make it past early pilots. For mid-market restaurant groups, the question to ask vendors is not "do you use AI?" but "what specific operational outcome does this produce, and what is the attribution methodology?" For hotel operators, the 59% preference for human-led check-in is not a barrier to AI adoption — it is a guide to where to deploy it.

## People & Skills

The AI skills gap in hospitality is structural and is now documented by the industry's own research bodies. 
The HSMAI Foundation's 2026 AI Talent Pipeline Report found a "Confidence-Preparedness Paradox": students rated their confidence in applying AI to work tasks at 3.24 out of 5, while rating their academic program's preparation at just 2.78 out of 5 — implying students are self-teaching AI through experimentation more than structured curriculum. Only 16% report using AI to support their own job search efforts, despite expecting employers to use AI in recruiting.



Brian Hicks, President and CEO of HSMAI, stated: "Students are entering the workforce with established AI habits and confidence, but employers must equip future talent with the training, governance, and decision-making frameworks needed to apply AI tools successfully in real-world hotel operations."



BCG research shows only 5% of companies offer AI training at scale, and just 26% have developed the capabilities to move beyond proofs of concept and generate tangible value.


Sources: [Hospitality Net — HSMAI Foundation AI Talent Pipeline Report](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4132558/hsmai-foundation-releases-new-ai-talent-pipeline-report-examining-the-future-of-hospitality-workforce-readiness)

So what: The workforce AI gap is a two-sided problem: incoming talent has habits but lacks governance frameworks, and existing operators have AI tools but lack the structured onboarding to use them well. Hospitality operators who build structured AI onboarding — covering validation practices, escalation protocols, and decision-making frameworks — before they deploy AI in guest-facing roles will have a compounding advantage as adoption becomes universal.

## Governance & Security


A white paper translating the White House's June 2, 2026 AI Executive Order — titled "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security" — into operational priorities for hotels argues the Order signals that AI has crossed from an experimental productivity tool to a critical layer of operational infrastructure that governments now treat as a matter of national security. For hospitality, the relevance is not theoretical: the modern hotel has quietly become a dense, interconnected intelligent environment binding identity, payments, guest data, building systems, IoT, and energy orchestration.



Figure 1 of the white paper maps the Order's provisions to hospitality consequences, noting that AI capability is now treated as national strategic capability, and the competitive question is shifting from "How many AI features do we have?" to "Can we govern AI responsibly, secure our connected systems, and turn operational trust into guest experience?"


On the regulatory calendar: 
The Colorado 2026 Automated Decision-Making Technology Act takes effect January 1, 2027. The FTC's August 2026 final orders concerning deceptive AI-powered active-listening marketing claims resulted in a $930,000 total settlement. EU Regulation 2024/1028 on short-term rentals is active from May 2026. PCI DSS v4.0.1 has new requirements active since March 2025.



For compliance teams, the priority is a live inventory of AI systems and use cases — including embedded AI features inside the PMS, RMS, CRM, call-center platform, surveillance stack, and workforce software. The most important risks often enter through software updates rather than a stand-alone AI procurement, meaning governance needs to cover features vendors switch on after the original contract was signed.


Sources: [Hospitality Net — When National AI Security Becomes a Hospitality Imperative](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/opinion/4132771/when-national-ai-security-becomes-a-hospitality-imperative)

So what: The governance gap is not academic. With 41% of hoteliers reporting no formal AI policy (Mews 2026 survey), and the regulatory surface area expanding on multiple fronts simultaneously, the practical first step is an AI system inventory — mapping every place AI is already operating inside the property before a board-level AI strategy is even drafted. For PE-backed hospitality SaaS vendors, buyer procurement teams are beginning to ask AI governance questions that previously belonged only to enterprise healthcare and finance.

## WHO TO WATCH

**Rafa Marquez (President, International Card Services, American Express)** — His statement that "Dining is one of the most important ways people engage with our brand" frames what is effectively a global restaurant network acquisition strategy. The TheFork deal, if it clears regulatory approval before end-2026, would give Amex the largest bookable dining inventory in the world. The question now is whether Amex can operationally integrate three distinct platform architectures (Resy, Tock, TheFork) while maintaining the restaurant-operator trust each platform was built on.

**Pablo Rivero (CEO, Resy and Tock; SVP and Head of American Express Global Dining)** — His comment that "Resy will be the singular app for the best culinary experiences" defines the strategic ambition, and the Resy-Tock integration this summer is the first test of whether Amex can execute on it. Worth tracking whether the Tock ticketing model survives inside the Resy brand, and whether the fine-dining operators who chose Tock specifically for its architecture follow the platform or defect.

**Anna Scozzafava (Chief Data, AI and Technology Officer, Choice Hotels)** — Her statement that Choice is helping make it easier for travelers to find the right hotel through Google AI Mode "while positioning our franchisees to compete and win in the age of agentic commerce" is the sharpest articulation of the hotel-chain positioning challenge. Choice's early partnership with Google's AI Mode is worth tracking for distribution economics: does the channel cannibalize OTA bookings or capture genuinely new demand?

**Brendan Sweeney (CEO, Popmenu)** — His public skepticism about LLM economics at the NRA Show — specifically that AI token costs can exceed the salaries of the workers being replaced, and that OpenAI has not achieved profitability — is a corrective against the vendor consensus that AI is immediately cost-saving. Worth tracking as a bellwether: if Popmenu shows better operator ROI from narrower, data-grounded AI than LLM-first competitors, the argument has legs.

**Richard Valtr (Founder, Mews)** — His "hospitality's iPhone moment" framing at Unfold 2026 is either the sharpest competitive positioning of the cycle or the tallest claim still to be proven. The IDC-validated 476% ROI and 13.7% revenue-per-sqm lift are specific enough to take seriously. The test is whether Mews can sustain those outcomes as it expands from 15,000 properties to the next order of magnitude, and whether the five-product unified platform holds its integration promises at scale.
