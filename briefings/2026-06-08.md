**Coverage window:** ~June 3–6, 2026. **Coverage note:** This cycle is heavily skewed toward hotels, driven by the Skift Data + AI Summit in New York on June 3. Restaurant news exists but is thinner on genuine developments; this is noted per cluster.

## AI VISIBILITY & DISCOVERY

The hotel industry's third distribution war — SEO → OTA → AI — is now visibly underway, with a cluster of product launches and strategic moves aimed at owning AI-mediated hotel discovery before OTAs lock in their position.


**IHG Hotels & Resorts** launched a ChatGPT app on June 3 that lets users search and compare its more than 7,000 hotels in over 100 countries, surfacing real-time availability, pricing, interactive maps and amenities, and guiding guests to IHG's direct booking channels to complete their reservation.
 
Conversational search is also coming soon to IHG.com and the IHG One Rewards app. CPO Jolie Fleming said: "With more than one million guests staying with us every night around the world, we're using AI to make the travel experience more seamless from planning and booking through to their stay."


**Lighthouse** moved earlier but deserves re-flagging in this context. 
Launched in March, Lighthouse's Connect AI — which powers The Hotels Network app in ChatGPT — is built on Model Context Protocol and serves as a "live data bridge" between hotels and AI platforms. Rodriguez said The Hotels Network app gives properties control over discovery and allows hotels to surface their own rates within AI conversations. "This isn't an incremental improvement," Rodriguez said. "It's a new distribution channel."
 
Lighthouse does not take a booking commission, and hotels serve as the merchant of record.


**Agentic Hospitality** is pursuing the same distribution thesis with a different architecture. 
Its ChatGPT app connects directly to hotel PMS systems, eliminating intermediaries and enabling hotels to own AI booking conversations from start to finish. The TravelOS MCP Server connects directly to a hotel's CRS and PMS to ensure that all availability, pricing, and inventory originate from the system of record.
 
Brad Brewer, founder and Chief AI Officer, argues: "No traveler is going to download a random aggregator they've never heard of. But they will engage with a hotel brand they recognize."


**On the restaurant side**, the AI visibility problem is more acute and less addressed. 
Uberall released the industry's first benchmark report measuring how AI assistants recommend restaurants, finding that as consumer restaurant discovery rapidly shifts from traditional search to AI assistants, the majority of QSR locations are effectively absent from AI-generated recommendations.
 
The top three brands per category capture 53.4% of total AI Share of Voice; in burger chains, the leader alone captures 10x the Share of Voice of the average brand.
 
ChatGPT primarily recommends businesses averaging 4.3 stars or higher — a restaurant with a 4.0 average can still rank on Google but fall below the threshold AI platforms use.
 No major restaurant chain has yet launched a dedicated ChatGPT app with live inventory; IHG's hotel move highlights how far behind QSRs are on this front.

Sources: [IHG Press Release](https://www.ihgplc.com/en/news-and-media/news-releases/2026/ihg-hotels-and-resorts-advances-conversational-ai-search-capabilities) · [PhocusWire – Lighthouse](https://www.phocuswire.com/news/technology/lighthouse-chatgpt-app-hotels-network-mcp-ai) · [Uberall Report](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260507962493/en/83-of-Restaurants-Are-Invisible-in-AI-Search-New-Uberall-Report-Reveals-the-Discovery-Gap-Reshaping-the-Quick-Service-Restaurant-Industry)

**So what:** OTAs currently feed most of the ARI data AI platforms use to recommend hotels — the same asymmetry as 2008 when OTAs outmobilized hotel websites. Hotels and restaurant groups that build direct MCP-based data feeds to AI platforms now are making the same early-mover bet that mobile-optimized direct websites captured in 2012. The window is open but not wide.

## DATA & INTEGRATION

The sharpest argument from the Skift Summit this week was architectural: AI can't generate revenue it can't see, and most hotel software stacks were designed to silo data by department rather than connect it.


**Mews founder Richard Valtr** argued at the Skift Data and AI Summit that hotels are leaving revenue on the table. Technology could free front desk staff to sell experiences and loyalty instead of dealing with admin — but hotel technology was built around software categories like property management, revenue management, and customer data that rarely talk to each other.



**Colin Coleman**, Marriott's SVP of Enterprise Data, Analytics & AI, presented a different problem: he's running AI build-out across 9,900 properties and more than 30 brands, and his central argument is architectural rather than aspirational. The advantage, Coleman argues, is not the volume of data — 283 million Bonvoy members, decades of first-party signals — but the connected intelligence layer Marriott is constructing on top of it, so that every new AI application deployed across the portfolio starts smarter than the last one rather than from scratch.



The structural constraint holding back mid-market operators is well-documented: legacy PMS platforms have limited APIs, inconsistent schemas, and maintenance-heavy direct integrations. The architectural response taking shape has two parts — a governed master data layer sitting above the PMS, and the Model Context Protocol (MCP), which solves the N×M integration problem so each AI agent doesn't need a custom connector to every other system.



On the product side, **Shiji** partnered with **Omniboost** this week to connect its Daylight PMS with POS and accounting platforms via Omniboost's data integration layer. The stated goal is reducing manual reconciliation and simplifying what is typically a complex, fragmented integration process across hotel tech stacks.


Sources: [Skift – Hotel AI Costs](https://skift.com/2026/06/04/why-hotel-ai-keeps-cutting-costs-instead-of-making-money/) · [PhocusWire June 5 briefs](https://www.phocuswire.com/travel-tech-news-briefs/2026/june-5)

**So what:** Marriott's data layer argument is uncomfortable for PE-backed mid-market operators and independent groups: you can buy the same AI tools as Marriott, but if the underlying data fabric is fragmented, you're running those tools on noise. The question to ask vendors before signing: can your system produce a clean guest record — booking, stay, loyalty, messaging — in under ten seconds across all properties?

## OPERATIONAL AI

The dominant theme from the Skift Summit was a challenge to the entire framing of hospitality AI: operators who are winning have stopped running pilots and have committed to production.


**Evolve**, the vacation rental company, decided to forego traditional pilot structure and commit to scaling from the start. In roughly 120 days, the company went from resolving 30% of conversations without human intervention to 60%. CPTO Arun Nagarajan said there were mistakes made and "a few embarrassing moments," but the company quickly scaled to a successful product. Response times dropped and the platform addressed inquiries in users' native language.
 
Nagarajan said: "By calling something a pilot, sometimes you reserve a little bit of gumption… you sort of say, well, that's just a pilot, let's not hold it to a higher standard."



At the **Mews Unfold** conference in Amsterdam, **Julie Linn Teigland** (EY Global Vice Chair) reframed the AI threat narrative. "The reality on hotel floors today is that it's the lack of AI, rather than the presence of it, that's making guest interactions feel transactional. Staff are buried in administrative work — from invoice matching to responding to messages across a dozen channels — forcing staff themselves to become robotic." She cited data showing that hospitality firms deploying AI for hyper-personalization generate over 23% in additional revenues, while AI-driven forecasting has improved cancellation rate prediction accuracy by 40%.


On the **restaurant side**, voice AI is the clearest operational story. 
Christian Wiens, CEO of Loman AI, said voice AI crosses a critical threshold in 2026, moving from experimental technology to essential infrastructure. Pizza and high-volume takeout categories that deployed first are now 12–18 months ahead, seeing 26%-plus phone revenue increases.


**Wonder** is pursuing a more radical operational move. 
The AI tool Wonder plans to roll out by end of year will build a restaurant based on a customer's description — creating the name, branding, description, pictures, pricing, health information and recipes — and then go live at Wonder locations.
 
The company has 120 locations today and aims to have 400 next year and 1,000 by 2035.
 This is the most genuinely novel restaurant AI claim of the cycle, though it is a roadmap announcement, not a live deployment.

Sources: [Skift – Pilot Phase](https://skift.com/2026/06/04/from-pilot-to-production-travel-ai-tech/) · [Fortune – Mews Unfold](https://fortune.com/2026/06/01/ai-fears-hotels-hospitality-increased-guest-interactions/) · [PYMNTS – Wonder](https://www.pymnts.com/restaurant-technology/2026/wonder-develops-ai-and-infrastructure-to-automate-restaurant-launches/)

**So what:** Evolve's 120-day production trajectory is the most credible operator case study in this cycle. The lesson is infrastructure-first: fast APIs and clean data matter more than the AI model itself. For hospitality operators, the implication is that staging AI by use case is fine, but calling it a "pilot" gives teams permission to cut corners on integration that make the tool useless at scale.

## PEOPLE & SKILLS

The labor disruption conversation moved from abstract to quantified at the executive level this week, while a separate data point from BCG highlights that the skills gap is structural, not temporary.


**Accor CEO Sébastien Bazin**, speaking at NYU IHIF, warned corporate workers that "probably a third, if not 40% of all my jobs under corporate, will be replaced by AI technology" within 18 to 24 months. Hyatt CEO Mark Hoplamazian said he anticipates AI will have a lower labor impact than Bazin suggested, and that Hyatt aims to utilize AI to optimize administrative tasks so workers can interact more thoughtfully with guests.



BCG's AI-First Hotels report found that with AI driving measurable gains in costs, revenue, productivity, talent management, and competitiveness, AI-first hotels are already leaner and deliver stronger guest and employee experiences.
 The same report surfaces a structural gap: only 2.9% of full-time employees in travel and tourism possess AI skills, compared with 21% in the tech and media sectors — yet AI-skilled workers in hospitality are growing nearly 5% year over year.


Amadeus' "Travel Dreams 2026" report found hoteliers are viewing AI as a "core strategic investment." Out of the 500 hoteliers interviewed, 499 said they intended to invest in AI capabilities. On average, they anticipated spending $319,000 in 2026, with one in five planning to spend more than $500,000 per property.


Sources: [Hotel Dive – NYU IHIF](https://www.hoteldive.com/news/hotel-ceo-panel-nyu-ihif-2026/821857/) · [BCG – AI-First Hotels](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/ai-first-hotels-leaner-faster-smarter) · [PhocusWire – Amadeus](https://www.phocuswire.com/news/technology/amadeus-ai-investment-challenges-hotels-travel-dreams-2026)

**So what:** Bazin's 40% figure is the sharpest public statement on hotel AI displacement from a major CEO this cycle, and it will ripple into labour negotiations and HR strategy. The more important signal for PE-backed operators is the BCG skills gap data: you can buy AI tools and budget $319K, but if 97% of your hospitality workforce lacks AI literacy, you are buying tools that will gather dust or be ignored by the team supposed to act on their output.

## VENDOR MOVES

A busy week for product and partnership announcements, with the MCP/agentic booking category drawing the most activity.


**OwlTing Group** announced the upcoming launch of OwlPay Booking Engine for Agent Checkout, a hospitality booking engine designed to support AI agent-initiated bookings and payments, with rollout expected in June 2026. The service integrates booking confirmation, AI agent-authorized payment, and cross-border supplier settlement into a single pathway. OwlTing plans to onboard its existing 2,800-plus hotel clients through its OwlNest PMS at launch.



**Stayntouch** is showcasing at HITEC 2026 (June 15-18) a Guest Messaging tool embedded directly into its cloud PMS that the company claims automates 95% of guest requests, saves hotels 375 manual hours per month, and drives an average of $50 additional RevPAR per month through built-in upsell opportunities. Following its designation as "Hotel PMS of the Year" by the 2026 TravelTech Breakthrough Awards, Stayntouch has surpassed 1,400 Best-in-Class technology integrations.


**Mews** continues its vertical integration push. 
With 15,000 customers across 85 countries and a $2.5 billion valuation, Mews raised $300 million in January 2026. Its acquisitions of Flexkeeping and DataChat in late 2025
 reflect the platform-through-acquisition strategy also noted by Abode Worldwide's investment report.

On the restaurant side, 
**Chowbus**, the AI-powered restaurant platform designed for Asian restaurants, closed an $81 million fundraising round led by Prysm Capital and Left Lane Capital, and will expand beyond its integrated POS platform into more operational services for smaller restaurant brands, including marketing, accounting, supply ordering, and insurance.


Sources: [PhocusWire June 5 briefs](https://www.phocuswire.com/travel-tech-news-briefs/2026/june-5) · [Hospitality Net – Stayntouch](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4132456/stayntouch-to-feature-award-winning-cloud-pms-ai-powered-guest-messaging-at-hitec-2026.html) · [NRN – Chowbus](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology/tech-tracker-ai-chatbots-are-the-next-frontier-in-restaurant-technology)

**So what:** The OwlTing/AI-agent booking engine story is notable because it adds payments and cross-border settlement into a single pipeline — the missing step that has kept MCP hotel bookings in demo mode. If it works at scale across 2,800+ properties, it becomes a reference architecture for the industry.

## FUNDING & M&A


Hospitality technology startups raised more than $1 billion across 40 companies between April 2025 and March 2026, with PMS and AI-led platforms attracting the largest share of investment, according to Abode Worldwide's Hospitality Tech Investment Index 2026. The investment was concentrated in a 90-day burst between December and February, with Mews ($300 million), Kindred ($125 million), and Limehome (€75 million) leading the pack.
 
AI-led guest experience platforms were another standout category, with Duve, Chatlyn, Conduit, and Canary Technologies raising a combined $152.6 million. Abode CEO Jessica Gillingham noted: "Investors are concentrating capital in the platforms that hospitality businesses increasingly depend on every day — especially PMS and AI-led systems that unify operations, improve automation and strengthen the data layer underneath the business."


Sources: [Hospitality Net – $1B](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/editorial/4131882/hospitality-tech-investment-topped-1-billion-pms-platforms-led-the-way-ai-forces-hotels-to-rethink-what-service-means.html) · [Hotel Technology News](https://hoteltechnologynews.com/2026/04/research-hospitality-technology-tops-1-billion-across-40-startups-with-pms-and-ai-platforms-capturing-the-largest-share/)

**So what:** The funding concentration in PMS platforms — seven companies capturing $408M — signals that investors believe the PMS will become the control layer for all hotel AI, not just operations software. For PE-backed hospitality groups evaluating tech stack consolidation, this is the category where switching costs are rising fastest and vendor bargaining power is compounding.

## WHO TO WATCH

**Richard Valtr (Mews founder)** — His argument at the Skift Summit — that hotel AI is trapped on the cost side of the ledger because fragmented software prevents AI from seeing sellable inventory — is the sharpest commercial framing of the week. Worth tracking whether Mews' own product roadmap can demonstrate this thesis with real RevPAR data from connected properties.

**Colin Coleman (Marriott SVP Enterprise Data, Analytics & AI)** — His data-layer compounding argument is the most consequential strategic concept from the Skift Summit: every new AI tool Marriott deploys starts with 283M Bonvoy members' data already underneath it. Operators evaluating AI vendors should use Marriott's architecture as the benchmark question: does my stack compound, or does every new tool start from zero?

**Adam Harris (Cloudbeds CEO)** — 
Harris argued at Skift that most hotel AI platforms being adopted today are built on probabilistic architectures that will be outdated by 2029, comparing them to fax machines. His thesis: the AI that actually runs a hotel takes action with certainty — different architecture, different data layer, different vendor profile.
 If this argument holds, it is the most procurement-severe idea in the industry right now.

**Brad Brewer (Agentic Hospitality Chief AI Officer)** — 
His framing — "No traveler is going to download a random aggregator they've never heard of, but they will engage with a hotel brand they recognize" — is a direct counter to the aggregator-in-AI model that OTAs and some emerging players are building.
 The architecture is coherent; execution at scale remains unproven. Track whether named hotel brands attach themselves publicly to the TravelOS MCP.

**Juanjo Rodriguez (Lighthouse head of direct booking)** — 
"AI is the biggest shift in how travelers plan their trips and discover hotels since Google," Rodriguez said, adding that OTAs have already made the leap to AI discovery.
 Lighthouse's flat-fee, no-commission model for the ChatGPT app is the clearest commercial signal that the MCP distribution layer will not simply recreate OTA economics. Track whether competitor vendors match the model or defend commissions.

**Arun Nagarajan (Evolve CPTO)** — The only operator executive in this cycle who cited a specific before/after outcome (30% to 60% automated resolution in 120 days) rather than a strategic vision. His "no-pilot" deployment philosophy and emphasis on fast APIs as the real constraint are the most actionable operator insights of the week.
