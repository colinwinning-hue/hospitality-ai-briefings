## AI Visibility & Discovery

The platform that controls discovery now controls the transaction. Google completed that move this week.


**Google** launched agentic hotel booking in AI Mode on August 27, 2026, letting travelers find, select, and reserve rooms using natural language without leaving the chat.
 
Launch partners include Booking.com, Choice Hotels, Expedia, Hilton, Hotels.com, IHG, Marriott, Priceline, Trip.com, and Wyndham. Consumers can find and compare rooms, make a selection, review cancellation terms, and pay with Google Pay — all in one place. The hotel or OTA still charges the card, sends the confirmation, and offers support.
 Critically, per Skift's analysis, Google shares only the minimum data needed to complete a booking — name, payment, occupancy, property — 
withholding broader chat context, inspiration, and decision signals even in aggregate, reducing partners' visibility into the path to conversion.


Three weeks earlier, on August 6, 
Google announced that Google Maps' "Ask Maps" feature was gaining agentic capabilities, including the ability to order food, book hotels, and find event tickets. With the food-ordering capabilities, users can ask "Where can I order vegan avocado toast and an oat milk latte near home?" — the tool surfaces nearby restaurants, and after selecting one, users can place an order through supported platforms such as Square, Toast, or Uber Eats. Ask Maps will add items to a cart for checkout on the supported platform.
 
The tech giant is also bringing Personal Intelligence to Ask Maps, allowing the AI tool to personalize its responses by drawing on information from a user's Gmail and Google Calendar.



On August 7, 2026, a Google spokesperson confirmed that a limited test of AI agent hotel booking was underway in the United States, with Amadeus as technical partner. The feature, called Ask Maps, now returns real prices and availability rather than ranges or estimates. It's designed to handle complex queries — for example finding a hotel with a particular atmosphere, within walking distance of a gym and restaurants, for a conference in a given city center. The same day, during its quarterly earnings presentation, Booking Holdings CEO Glenn Fogel confirmed that a portion of his group's traffic already runs through this agentic booking, and that Booking Holdings counts among Google's first partners.



Booking Holdings CEO Glenn Fogel said Tuesday that the company saw pressure on organic search results from Google's AI Overviews in Q2. "I do believe that some of the changes that were made in the display at Google definitely put some pressure on SEO," Fogel told analysts. Booking is accelerating its AI strategy across customer service, AI discovery, and LLM visibility, while raising its transformation-program savings target to $650 million in annual run-rate savings by 2027.
 The hard number: 
Booking Holdings CFO Ewout Steenbergen said in Q2 2026 that bookings coming from conversational agents, free or paid, account for less than 1% of the group's room-nights, with no significant recent change in that share. The distinction is worth stating clearly: the loss of traffic is real and documented by several independent studies, while the shift in bookings remains, for now, a hypothesis that the available figures do not yet confirm.


On the restaurant side, DoorDash's 2026 Restaurant Industry Trends Report (3,001 consumers, 509 operators) maps the discovery gap: 
22% of consumers have used an AI tool like ChatGPT or Google Gemini to help choose a restaurant. According to Yext research, restaurant listing sites like DoorDash account for more than 41% of the sources AI tools cite when recommending restaurants. Three-quarters of consumers say they're comfortable using AI for reservations, but only 28% of operators are using AI to manage calls and customer service. With an estimated 40% of reservation calls going unanswered industry-wide, AI gives restaurants another way to capture demand.


Sources: [Skift, Aug 27](https://skift.com/2026/08/27/googles-agentic-hotel-booking-tool-comes-to-ai-mode/) | [TechCrunch, Aug 6](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/06/google-maps-adds-agentic-features-including-food-ordering-and-hotel-bookings/) | [Skift, Aug 4](https://skift.com/2026/08/04/booking-holdings-saw-pressure-from-googles-ai-overviews/) | [DoorDash Trends Report](https://about.doordash.com/en-us/news/doordash-restaurant-industry-trends-report-2026)

**So what:** Google now sits between the hotel and the guest at every stage of the journey — search, compare, book, pay — while deliberately starving partners of the discovery data that explains *why* a guest chose them. For any hotel without a loyalty programme strong enough to drive direct search by name, the Google AI Mode booking flow is not a partnership; it is a new dependency with worse data terms than the old one. For restaurant operators, the 40% unanswered-calls figure is the single most actionable commercial gap in this briefing.

## Vendor Moves

The restaurant reservation market reorganized into three distinct data ecosystems this cycle, as DoorDash activated its SevenRooms acquisition and American Express completed the Tock-into-Resy merger.


**DoorDash** launched a new AI chatbot called "Ask DoorDash," allowing users to order food and groceries with text prompts and photos. You can tell the chatbot what you're in the mood for, share a recipe link to find the items, or describe the reservation you're looking for. Once you select a place, you can ask DoorDash to build a cart with suggestions based on your dietary preferences, budget, group size, or past orders. With Ask DoorDash for Reservations, users can ask the chatbot to find "a table for two downtown for a date-night dinner around 8 PM."
 DoorDash published its engineering architecture in a three-part deep dive: 
the system was built using large language models, specialized AI agents, Model Context Protocol (MCP)-based tooling, persistent consumer memory, and automated evaluation infrastructure. DoorDash reported early production results showing that computed consumer memory improved grocery checkout conversion by approximately 24%, increased basket sizes by 17%, and reduced conversational turns by 7% during a seven-day evaluation. For restaurant discovery, the assistant achieved a 15% higher conversion rate on open-ended queries.
 
The stakes are high for DoorDash, which is in the middle of a massive investment cycle following its $1.2 billion deal for SevenRooms and the nearly $4 billion acquisition of Deliveroo. It's been a rough year on Wall Street for DoorDash, with its stock down 33%.


Boston restaurants are experiencing the platform war on the ground. 
Booking platforms — such as OpenTable, Resy, Tock, and now-major player SevenRooms — have rolled out new features and partnerships. Powerful corporate mergers and big incentives are heating up these reservation wars. One Boston restaurateur reported they've been offered six-figure signing bonuses to switch platforms. The race is on to become the dominant platform — not just a booker, but an all-in-one digital concierge, marketing agency, and loyalty-reward tracker.
 
After purchasing Resy in 2019, American Express in 2024 paid $400 million for Tock, which will roll into the Resy platform this summer. A Resy partnership with point-of-sale provider Toast is underway. Last year, DoorDash bought SevenRooms for $1.2 billion.
 As one operator told the Globe: "Who does the guest relationship actually belong to? I think with some platforms, it belongs to them, and the restaurant is seen more as a fulfillment mechanism for them in the marketplace." 
One of those features is OpenTable's AI-powered dining room configuration tool that helps restaurants shuffle bookings to open up additional seats.



**Toast** launched Toast Drive-Thru in 2026 as a unified solution for enterprise quick-service operations, bringing together hardware, POS-native software, AI voice ordering integrations, and services. Restaurant operators are managing more complexity than ever across the guest journey — orders now come from dining rooms, drive-thru lanes, mobile apps, websites, kiosks, catering channels, delivery platforms, and servers carrying handheld devices. Toast IQ and Toast IQ Grow show how the company is trying to move from reporting toward action, with AI helping operators interpret signals and execute tasks such as marketing campaigns.
 
Amex's Alex Drummond said: "Our Card Members spent over $87 billion on dining in the U.S. alone in 2024. This partnership is a natural evolution of our investment in this growing category."
 BWH Hotels named Toast an endorsed POS provider as of August 5, 2026 — a meaningful hotel F&B channel opening.


**PAR Technology** and **Square** unveiled AI products that can assist operators and complete tasks on their behalf. PAR's new PAR Intelligence system features several agents: the Insights Agent, which can surface sales and other business data and make recommendations; the Offers Agent, which can create and deploy marketing campaigns; and the Developer Assist Agent. Because they are directly tied to PAR's other products, like POS and marketing, PAR says the agents provide operators with "one source of truth." Taco Bell franchisee Charter Foods began testing one of PAR's agents and asked it to identify stores that could benefit by staying open later. Late-night sales increased 20% as a result of its recommendations.
 Square's **Managerbot** goes a step further: it proactively surfaces insights and can draft employee schedules, create and run email campaigns, and generate purchase orders — acting before being asked.

**Owner.com** raised $240 million to expand its AI-powered technology and marketing platform for restaurants (Restaurant Technology News, August 2026). 
**Olo** has been adding AI across its restaurant platform, including personalization, operational workflows, and AI-powered tools, with its Summer 2026 product release explicitly positioned around "AI that works for restaurant operators." **Deliverect** has expanded from digital-order management into AI agents designed specifically for restaurant operations, covering menu optimization, promotions, order issues, restaurant data, and operational workflows.


Sources: [TechCrunch, June 11](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/11/doordashs-new-ai-chatbot-lets-you-order-with-prompts-and-photos/) | [InfoQ, July 13](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/07/doordash-ai-ask-assistant/) | [Boston Globe, Aug 17](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/08/17/lifestyle/reservation-platforms-sevenrooms-opentable-resy/) | [NRN, April 10](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology/the-hot-new-restaurant-tech-trend-ai-agents) | [RTN, June 29](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/06/toast-advances-restaurant-operations-with-connected-pos-ai-and-guest-engagement-technology/)

**So what:** The reservation market has three ecosystems — Amex/Resy/Tock/Toast, DoorDash/SevenRooms, and Booking/OpenTable — and operators are being actively courted, with cash, to declare loyalty. The decision of which platform to use is now a data-strategy choice, not a software selection. For a PE-backed restaurant group, the data that flows through the reservation layer is increasingly what determines personalisation capability and repeat-visit economics.

## Funding & M&A


Forty global hospitality technology startups raised more than $1 billion in the past year, with property management systems and artificial intelligence-led platforms attracting the most investment, according to Abode Worldwide's Hospitality Tech Investment Index 2026. "That matters because PMS is increasingly becoming the control layer of the hospitality tech stack. As hotel and lodging operators push to simplify fragmented systems, the PMS is taking on more responsibility — connecting teams, revenue, guest journeys and data in one place," the report states. AI-led guest experience platforms were also a "standout" investment, with Duve, Chatlyn, Conduit and Canary Technologies raising a combined $152.6 million. Canary notably acquired OpenKey in February to expand its access to door lock providers.



The shift was most visible in a 90-day burst between December 2025 and February 2026, when the sector's three biggest raises landed almost back-to-back: Mews ($300 million), Kindred ($125 million across two simultaneous rounds) and Limehome (€75 million).
 
Mews said the round followed a year in which it served 15,000 customers across 85 countries and processed $19.7 billion in platform transaction volume.


In Q2 2026, 
disclosed funding rounds were thin, with three rounds closing in the quarter, all AI-native by description. Smartness closed the largest, a €47 million Series B for its agentic AI platform for hotels and short-term rentals. Pricepoint raised a $6.6 million seed for AI revenue management. Besty AI raised a $3.75 million seed for its AI automation tool on the STR side. Acquisitions ran ahead: Lighthouse acquired Hotelrank.ai, an AI visibility measurement platform. Ixigo took a majority stake in hotel booking platform Brevistay. RealTime Reservation, an ancillary booking platform, acquired guest experience platform STAY.



Smartness started in 2020 as Smartpricing, a dynamic pricing tool for hotels, and has since expanded into a broad platform spanning pricing, guest communications, CRM, payments, and property management. Its €47 million Series B, far larger than the two seeds, is set partly toward acquisitions across Europe.
 On the restaurant side, Owner.com raised $240 million (August 2026), signalling that the AI marketing layer for independent restaurants is attracting significant capital.

Sources: [Hotel Dive, April 14](https://www.hoteldive.com/news/hospitality-tech-attracts-1b-in-funding-pms-ai-leaders/817439/) | [Abode Worldwide Q2 2026](https://abodeworldwide.com/hospitality-tech-funding-q2-2026/)

**So what:** The consolidation playbook is clear: well-funded PMS platforms acquire specialist capabilities (Mews/Flexkeeping/DataChat; Smartness expanding outward); AI visibility tools are now considered core infrastructure (Lighthouse/Hotelrank.ai). Any hospitality SaaS company pitching standalone point-solution AI needs a clear answer to why it should not be absorbed into the PMS layer.

## Operational AI


In a 2026 product update, Google Cloud reported that Wendy's FreshAI was expanding across 24 US states and handling about 50,000 orders per day, with a reported 95% success rate.
 This is the strongest production voice AI case study in QSR by volume, and the benchmark every other chain drive-thru programme is now measured against.


**Nory**, a provider of agentic AI and predictive analytics for restaurants, demonstrated at the 2026 National Restaurant Association Show how its agentic AI consolidates labor, inventory and sales data in one place and schedules staff, manages ordering and identifies waste areas. Agentic AI also enhanced conversational voice ordering, as demonstrated by Maple, which interacts in real time with customers on restaurant orders. Conversational voice AI is becoming a competitive advantage for early adopters.



Deloitte's restaurant AI survey found notable regional differences — Asian restaurants exhibit higher adoption rates in almost all AI capabilities. Among regions, US restaurants are relative laggards in intelligent automation, but are ahead in conversational voice AI.
 
Identifying the right use cases and managing risks are top challenges. Other obstacles include a lack of technical talent and skills, concerns about compliance with regulations, and a lack of governance. Most respondents say their organizations lack readiness when it comes to strategy, operations, technology infrastructure, and other AI enablers.


Sources: [Google Cloud / allainews.net](https://allainews.net/ai-for-restaurants/) | [Kiosk Industry, NRA Show 2026](https://kioskindustry.org/national-restaurant-show-2026-tech-review/) | [Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/industry/retail-distribution/ai-in-restaurants.html)

**So what:** Wendy's FreshAI at 50,000 orders per day is proof of scale, not proof of concept. The governance readiness gap (only ~20% of restaurant executives have adequate AI risk frameworks) is now the primary operational risk — particularly as agentic systems begin making autonomous decisions about pricing, staffing, and inventory without human-in-the-loop review.

## Governance & Security


**American Express** launched an agentic commerce developer kit as well as purchase protection for erroneous purchases made by registered AI agents. "To date there have probably been as many press releases [on agentic commerce] as transactions, but no doubt it will happen," said Luke Gebb, AmEx EVP and head of global innovation. American Express is pledging to protect users from erroneous transactions made by agents that are registered with Amex. Gebb argued the company is in a good position to do so, given that it already has processes in place for handling disputed transactions from customers. When asked about the cost to American Express of protecting against AI errors, Gebb said that agents will improve over time, and if consumers have the confidence to transact using agents on the Amex network, that will ultimately bring more transaction volume to the company.
 With Mastercard, Visa, and now Amex all having agentic commerce infrastructure live, the payment rails question is settled — consumer trust and messy merchant data are the remaining blockers.


Modern Restaurant Management notes that agentic commerce introduces new fraud vectors that require new detection domains, but no public data currently exists on how often autonomous agents submit fraudulent orders to restaurants.
 This is the governance gap operators should be raising with vendors before committing to agentic ordering flows.

Sources: [Fortune, April 14](https://fortune.com/2026/04/14/american-express-ai-payments-developers-purchase-protection/) | [Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/industry/retail-distribution/ai-in-restaurants.html)

**So what:** AmEx's purchase-protection play is the first serious attempt to solve the trust problem in agentic commerce. For hospitality operators building AI-enabled booking or ordering flows, the question of liability when an agent makes an erroneous reservation or order is no longer theoretical — and operators should be demanding explicit answers from vendors before deployment.

## WHO TO WATCH

**Glenn Fogel (Booking Holdings CEO)** — His Q2 earnings call on August 4 revealed two simultaneous truths in the same breath: Google's AI Overviews are already squeezing Booking's organic traffic, while Booking is simultaneously participating as a launch partner in Google's agentic hotel booking. His candid admission that "no travel business has found the silver bullet yet" for LLM visibility is the most honest executive statement in this cycle. The gap he described — real traffic impact, negligible booking impact — is where the most consequential hospitality AI story of the next 12 months will resolve.

**Andy Fang (DoorDash Co-Founder)** — His claim that Ask DoorDash can build a grocery cart "~5x faster than doing it manually" is backed by the most detailed production engineering disclosure in hospitality-adjacent AI this cycle. DoorDash's MCP-based architecture — domain agents, shared tooling, three-layer persistent memory, 2,000+ automated daily evaluations — is the production blueprint that restaurant tech vendors are working toward. Whether the 33% stock decline reflects investor impatience with investment timing or a structural business concern is worth tracking closely.

**Pablo Rivero (CEO, Resy and Tock)** — His quote that connecting Resy's guestbook to Toast ensures guest insights reach the people who shape the experience "in real time, creating surround sound on the dining room floor" is the sharpest articulation of what the Amex–Toast partnership is actually building. He now controls the guest data layer for 25,000+ restaurants and is mid-integration with the most-used restaurant POS in the U.S. If the data flywheel closes, this becomes the dominant relationship infrastructure in premium dining.

**Jessica Gillingham (Abode Worldwide CEO and Founder)** — Her framing that "the companies attracting the strongest investor backing share a common trait" — proximity to essential operator workflows — correctly identifies why the funding concentration is in PMS and AI guest experience rather than point solutions. Her Q2 2026 "End of the Frankenstack" webinar thesis, that well-funded platforms absorb capabilities by acquisition while specialists are funded to consolidate outward, is playing out exactly as described in the Smartness and Lighthouse deals.
