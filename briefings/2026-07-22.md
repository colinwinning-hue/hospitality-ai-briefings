## Funding & M&A

The restaurant reservation market has undergone its most disruptive structural reshaping in a decade, with three compounding deals converting reservations from a product category into a distribution infrastructure war.


**American Express** is acquiring restaurant reservation management platform **TheFork** from Tripadvisor for $700 million, announced June 15. The deal is designed to build on Amex's broader dining strategy following its acquisitions of Resy and Tock. Combined, the three platforms expand the Amex dining network to an estimated 75,000 bookable venues.
 
TheFork generated $232 million in revenue for the year ended March 31, a 25% jump from a year earlier, connecting millions of diners with more than 50,000 restaurants across 11 European countries.
 
TheFork's adjusted EBITDA advanced from $5 million in 2024 to $21 million in 2025, while the adjusted EBITDA margin improved from 2.9% to 9.2%.
 Amex President of International Card Services Rafa Marquez: 
"Dining is one of the most important ways people engage with our brand."



**Amex is also merging Tock's restaurant inventory with Resy's this summer,** adding roughly 8,000 bookable venues to Resy's app and website—including 1,200 wineries. The move is designed to bolster Amex's position amid intensifying competition from DoorDash, which spent $1.2 billion to acquire reservations platform SevenRooms, and Chase Sapphire, which linked up with OpenTable as part of a Visa partnership.



**American Express and Toast** announced a multi-year strategic partnership to build tools combining the power of Resy and Tock's guestbook capabilities and Toast's Digital Chits technology, which allows restaurant staff to view important guest information directly on Toast Go handhelds and POS terminals during service. The partnership will also make Resy and Tock listings available on the Local by Toast app.
 Resy CEO Pablo Rivero: 
"Restaurant Academy is an opportunity to support the operators behind those experiences, helping them build resilient teams and continue delivering standout dining for their guests."


**DoorDash** completed its $1.2 billion SevenRooms acquisition and has now integrated three significant follow-on moves. 
**SevenRooms launched Channel Connect**, a free, open-license desktop app that aggregates reservations from booking platforms—including OpenTable and Resy—into a single system while syncing inventory across all channels. The launch comes after OpenTable in April 2026 began requiring restaurant clients to make it their system of record. Channel Connect's stated positioning: "no single platform has to be the gatekeeper to your operations."



**DoorDash also introduced Ask DoorDash**, an in-app AI chatbot allowing customers to order food, build grocery carts, and book restaurant reservations using text prompts, photos, or voice inputs, built on AI models from OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google.
 
Early testing showed nearly half of all restaurant orders placed through the tool were from venues the customer had never ordered from before. DoorDash also said carts assembled through the chatbot ran more than 35% higher in value than a standard grocery order, with users completing them five times faster than through the regular app interface.



**Toast**'s Spring 2026 release launched Toast IQ Grow, a marketing-focused solution with an AI-powered Marketing Agent that builds audiences and automates campaigns across channels. Toast also launched Toast Drive-Thru as a unified solution for enterprise quick-service operations, bringing together hardware, POS-native software, AI voice ordering integrations and services.
 
Toast Lab—an initiative to co-design a new restaurant with one operator in Greater Boston, with funding and the full tech stack provided by Toast—had applications open through July 14, 2026, with ~171,000 merchant locations currently on the platform.



Other smaller deals: GoTab acquired marketing platform **Fishbowl**; Parts Town acquired **86 Repairs** to combine parts supply with equipment-maintenance software. Danny Meyer's investment fund led a **$17 million round in Blink**, a super-app for frontline workers, which also announced Shake Shack as a customer—Blink added more than 700,000 users in the hospitality industry last year, including a global deal with McDonald's.


Sources: [PYMNTS – Amex/TheFork](https://www.pymnts.com/acquisitions/2026/amex-looks-to-spend-700-million-to-buy-thefork-from-tripadvisor/), [NRN Tech Tracker](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology/tech-tracker-a-burst-of-m-a-to-start-the-summer), [NRN – SevenRooms Channel Connect](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology/sevenrooms-launches-a-reservations-aggregator-for-restaurants), [Toast – Amex Partnership](https://pos.toasttab.com/news/american-express-and-toast-announce-strategic-partnership)

**So what:** Every mid-market restaurant now faces three well-resourced ecosystems (DoorDash/SevenRooms/Deliveroo, Amex/Resy/Tock/TheFork, OpenTable/Booking Holdings/Uber) competing for table inventory. The operator-favorable short term—DoorDash's no-cover-fee model, Amex's card-member incentives—will narrow once data moats consolidate. The Toast–Amex Digital Chits integration is the most operationally immediate development for full-service operators: real-time guest history flowing to the tableside handheld is a genuine workflow upgrade, not just a distribution story. PE buyers evaluating restaurant platforms should price in which ecosystem the acquisition belongs to.

## AI Visibility & Discovery

The strategic question across both sectors has shifted from whether to pursue AI discoverability to who captures the conversion after AI generates intent.


**Google** named hotel booking as the next vertical for its Universal Commerce Protocol at Google I/O, publicly committing to extending its agentic commerce infrastructure to travel. The Agent Payments Protocol will enable AI agents to complete purchases on users' behalf, starting with Google's Gemini Spark AI, while travel packages remain ineligible for UCP checkout.
 
UCP for Lodging will let hotels turn AI interactions into instant bookings for room reservations via Google's AI Mode in Search, with the hotel retaining Merchant of Record status and full ownership of customer relationships and post-booking experience.
 Hotel agentic booking is announced but not yet live; 
Google's agentic restaurant booking is live in the UK as of April 10, 2026, with eight partner platforms—TheFork, SevenRooms, ResDiary, Mozrest, Foodhub, Dojo, DesignMyNight, and OpenTable.
 
Google Trends data cited in the launch shows searches for "when to book a table" increased 140% in 2026 to date.


**OpenAI is diverging from Google strategically.** 
While Google expands direct transaction capabilities inside its AI ecosystem, OpenAI has reportedly stepped back from enabling direct purchases inside ChatGPT—routing users to partners like Expedia and Booking.com for completion instead.


The most important strategic framing of the cycle comes from two OTA earnings calls. 
Booking Holdings CEO Glenn Fogel stated the company is working closely with OpenAI and Google to ensure its supply is discoverable wherever travelers begin their journey. Expedia CEO Ariane Gorin commented that the AI pullback from completing bookings reinforces Expedia's view that actual booking and servicing is best handled by a trusted, scaled provider—and added that if AI evolves toward paid advertising at the discovery layer, "that's a space Expedia knows well." OTAs score at the top of every AI recommendation signal (YouTube footprint, domain authority, earned editorial coverage)—an advantage built for metasearch that transferred into AI eligibility without OTAs having to rebuild anything.


Hospitality.today Editor Markus Busch draws the unvarnished conclusion: 
what the eligibility infrastructure hotels are building produces is a more legible hotel in the discovery layer of a system whose conversion layer OTAs already own. A traveler who finds an independent property through ChatGPT, verifies through Google, lands on an OTA listing, and books there—that traveler was discovered through infrastructure the hotel invested in and converted through infrastructure the OTA owns. The hotel paid for the top of the funnel. The OTA collected at the bottom. Same as before. Different pipe.



**Grevon** launched at HITEC 2026 with Kore, an MCP-based connectivity layer for hospitality, powering Pulse (an AI booking agent for hotel websites), Echo (an AI voice agent for inbound calls), and Ops (a staff intelligence platform)—pointing to an emerging competitive front in hotel distribution: making official hotel content, rates, availability, and booking paths usable by AI agents.
 
**The Houstonian Hotel** (July 10, 2026) launched a redesigned website and ChatGPT application powered by Agentic Hospitality, designed for the next generation of AI-powered travel discovery.
 Agentic Hospitality founder Brad Brewer: 
"Hotels have invested heavily in the systems that run their operations. Our approach is simple. We extend those systems into AI platforms rather than replacing them. Hotels keep their pricing control, inventory authority and direct relationship with the guest."


Sources: [Skift – Google UCP Hotels](https://skift.com/2026/05/19/google-names-hotels-as-next-vertical-for-agentic-shopping/), [Hospitality.today – OTAs harvesting](https://www.hospitality.today/article/hotels-are-building-the-ai-discovery-layer-otas-are-harvesting-it), [HTN – HITEC launches](https://hoteltechnologynews.com/2026/07/recent-hotel-technology-launches-show-ai-moving-from-standalone-tools-into-connected-hotel-operations/)

**So what:** The Fogel/Gorin earnings framing is the most important strategic signal of this cycle. Both OTA CEOs described the same architecture from the same side—AI generates demand, OTAs capture conversion. For a PE-backed hospitality SaaS company, the commercial opportunity is not MCP eligibility tools (commoditizing fast) but conversion-layer products: booking engines that retain Merchant of Record status, loyalty-aware rate personalization, and direct-booking pathways that compete with OTA listings inside AI-referred journeys.

## Operational AI

The sharpest operational AI signal of the cycle comes from the labor cost crisis, not guest-facing innovation.


**Labor accounts for 51.7% of hotel operating expenses and 32.4% of total revenue at U.S. hotels**, according to CBRE Group's analysis of 2,456 properties. Hotels paid 11.9% more in total labor costs while operating with 5.9% fewer employees than in 2019. In North America, 65% of hotels reported staffing shortages in 2025 (BCG/AHLA).
 
AI platforms are replacing the coordination layer between departments by connecting occupancy forecasts, IoT sensor data, and guest activity into a single operational system that adjusts continuously. The Ritz-Carlton San Francisco implemented an AI system that synchronizes room-cleaning schedules with checkout patterns, guest preferences, and staff availability, cutting room preparation time by 20% (BCG). IHG has deployed predictive housekeeping models that anticipate peak cleaning times and allocate resources accordingly.
 
Predictive maintenance programs can reduce maintenance costs by 25-30% and cut equipment downtime by 35-45%, according to the U.S. Department of Energy. Hilton's LightStay platform has delivered more than $1 billion in cumulative savings since 2009.


HITEC 2026 embedded AI directly into core operating systems rather than presenting it as an add-on layer. 
**Oracle** launched the OPERA Cloud Assistant—AI-assisted room assignments, AI-generated rate descriptions, operational guidance, and translation, embedded directly into OPERA Cloud workflows. When AI becomes embedded in the system of record used by front desk teams and revenue managers, it has the potential to shape everyday decisions rather than automate only a narrow task at the edge of the operation. **Revinate** introduced Ivy, an AI decision-intelligence layer designed to understand a hotel's guests, property, and brand voice, including automated call scoring and Revinate Chat for the reservations call floor and guest inbox.



The latest wave of product launches points to a market less focused on isolated tools and more focused on intelligence layers that connect the PMS, guest communications, network infrastructure, revenue management, procurement, access control, building operations, and commercial sales workflows. Many announcements surfaced around HITEC 2026, where AI appeared across core hotel workflows embedded into platforms that already support front desk operations, guest engagement, pricing, connectivity, sales, and back-office management.


On the restaurant side, 
**Toast** is building a broad operating platform combining POS, payments, online ordering, delivery, kiosks, drive-thru technology, kitchen display systems, AI, guest engagement, marketing, payroll, scheduling, inventory, cost analytics, finance tools, and commercial-grade hardware.
 
**Taco Bell's** drive-thru voice AI has expanded to nearly 900 restaurants (NRN, July 8).


Sources: [PYMNTS – Hotel AI Manager](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/ai-is-becoming-the-hotel-industrys-new-manager/), [HTN – HITEC launches](https://hoteltechnologynews.com/2026/07/recent-hotel-technology-launches-show-ai-moving-from-standalone-tools-into-connected-hotel-operations/), [RTN – Toast](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/06/toast-advances-restaurant-operations-with-connected-pos-ai-and-guest-engagement-technology/)

**So what:** Oracle OPERA Cloud's AI Assistant is the most structurally significant hotel technology move of the cycle because it acts inside the system every front-desk team already uses. For mid-market operators, PMS-embedded AI is lower friction than standalone tools because the data is already there. For restaurant operators, the Toast/Taco Bell voice AI deployments represent production-scale evidence—not pilots—that AI voice ordering in QSR has crossed the operational threshold.

## People & Skills


**American Express and Resy**, in partnership with the National Restaurant Association Educational Foundation, launched **Restaurant Academy** on July 1, 2026—a four-month national leadership development program for 30 restaurant leaders running September 2026 through March 2027. The program covers hiring with intention, onboarding frameworks, retention-focused AI tools, P&L literacy, food and labor costs, and guest experience skills, with sessions led by James Beard Award winners Mashama Bailey, Gregory Gourdet, and Nok Suntaranon.



The underlying problem is structural: the NRA's 2026 State of the Restaurant Industry report found 80% of operators were short at least one position in 2025, with the average operator carrying five open roles (up from 3.8) and an average training cost per hire of $3,037. Annual QSR turnover regularly exceeds 130%.


The most important governance-adjacent finding: 
PYMNTS Intelligence found that 37% of Labor Economy workers, including hospitality, said their employer had introduced new AI tools in the previous 12 months—yet nearly 60% of workers affected by those tools received no training, suggesting deployment is moving faster than workforce readiness.


Sources: [PRNewswire – Restaurant Academy](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/american-express-and-resy-with-national-restaurant-association-educational-foundation-launch-new-restaurant-academy-to-develop-the-next-generation-of-restaurant-leaders-302815742.html), [PYMNTS – Hotel AI Manager](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/ai-is-becoming-the-hotel-industrys-new-manager/)

**So what:** The 60% no-training finding is the most underreported operational risk in the sector. PE-backed operators rolling out AI tools across multi-unit portfolios should treat workforce AI literacy as an integration cost line, not a byproduct of deployment.

## Governance & Security


The White House issued an Executive Order on June 2, 2026 titled "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security." For hospitality, the relevance is not theoretical: the modern hotel has quietly become a dense, interconnected intelligent environment—binding together identity, payments, guest data, building systems, IoT, energy orchestration, and AI-driven workflows. The more intelligent a property becomes, the more strategically exposed it becomes.



The Order directs federal bodies to prioritize cyber defense of government information systems, creates a classified benchmarking process for "covered frontier models," directs Treasury to form an AI cybersecurity clearinghouse, and tasks the Attorney General to prioritize criminal enforcement against those using AI to unlawfully access or damage computer systems. Notably, it does not impose any mandatory licensing, preclearance, or permitting requirements for developing or releasing AI models.


Two hard regulatory deadlines are immediately relevant: 
EU AI Act Article 50 transparency requirements for chatbots apply from August 2, 2026. High-risk AI obligations under the Act also apply from August 2026 to Annex III systems. California's Automated Decisionmaking Technology access and opt-out rights take effect January 1, 2027.



On data security: hospitality AI deployments frequently aggregate bulk categories of sensitive data—identifiers, geolocation, biometrics, and financial data—triggering DOJ Bulk Data Security Program compliance obligations. Many organizations assume de-identified or tokenized datasets fall outside the restrictions; that assumption is incorrect under the DSP rules.



The near-term threat most named by cyber experts is AI-enabled deepfakes: "Imagine a voice message that sounds convincingly like your GM asking to approve a wire transfer." With free AI tools proliferating in 2026, targeted impersonation of senior management for financial fraud is the sharpest operational threat.


Sources: [Hospitality Net – White House EO](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/opinion/4132771/when-national-ai-security-becomes-a-hospitality-imperative)

**So what:** August 2 is a hard EU AI Act compliance date, not a planning horizon. Any operator deploying AI chatbots with European guests must confirm chatbot disclosure language is in place now, or risk regulatory exposure. The DOJ Bulk Data rule is the underappreciated risk: hotel AI vendors with offshore development teams or cloud-hosted model pipelines may be creating covered data transaction obligations operators don't know they've inherited.
