## Vendor Moves

The restaurant reservation market is being redrawn by three financial-services and delivery giants in parallel, and the moves are happening faster than most operators have registered.

**American Express** is assembling the largest dining-technology portfolio in the industry's history. Its proposed [$700 million acquisition of TheFork from Tripadvisor](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/06/american-express-expands-restaurant-technology-ambitions-with-700-million-thefork-acquisition/) — announced June 15 — would add more than 50,000 restaurants across 11 European countries to a portfolio that already includes Resy and Tock. 
Combined with existing platforms Resy and Tock, the acquisition would expand American Express' network to approximately 75,000 bookable venues worldwide, creating one of the largest restaurant reservation and dining engagement platforms in the industry.
 The deal closes before year-end pending regulatory approval.

**American Express / Resy + Tock** are simultaneously executing the consumer-facing merger. 
The move boosts Resy's venue count to 25,000, but will sunset the Tock brand, formally uniting two onetime startups against reservations market leader OpenTable, the incumbent provider they each hoped to disrupt.
 The financial mechanism is working: 
in the three weeks following the Platinum card credit announcement, there was a 36% increase in Resy reservations made by users with a U.S. Platinum card linked to their account — and a five times increase in the daily average number of accounts being linked. People using the Resy credit are spending, on average, 25% more.
 
In 2026, Resy plans to release upgraded APIs for third-party partners, including generative AI platforms, allowing reservation inventory to appear across more digital channels.
 Resy and Toast are integrating their platforms so servers can view Resy guestbook data — wine preferences, birthdays — directly within Toast handheld devices. 
Resy CEO Pablo Rivero describes a "powerful tech stack — one that links AI-powered discovery and booking, table and guestbook management, content and curation, ticketing and transactions, check-level point of sale data, and American Express membership."


**DoorDash / SevenRooms** is executing a two-front product strategy. 
SevenRooms, which is owned by DoorDash, launched Channel Connect — a free, open-license desktop app that aggregates reservations from various booking channels into a single system, while also automatically updating a restaurant's inventory across those channels.
 CEO Joel Montaniel said: 
"More tables for diners, one book for restaurants."
 
The app will pull in bookings from "major booking platforms," including SevenRooms' chief rivals. A SevenRooms blog post explicitly states "Channel Connect was built so that no single platform has to be the gatekeeper to your operations" — a direct counter to OpenTable's April move requiring restaurants to make it their system of record.
 Separately, 
DoorDash launched an in-app AI chatbot called Ask DoorDash, allowing customers to order food and groceries and make reservations using text prompts, photos, or voice inputs. The system draws on AI models built by OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google, supplemented by open-source options.
 DoorDash also unveiled **dd-cli**, a command-line interface: 
the tool allows AI agents to autonomously search restaurants, compare deals, and complete checkout on DoorDash without any manual input from a human user — processing real payments rather than simulated transactions.


**Toast** published its most concrete usage data yet on July 14. 
Toast, the POS supplier to more than 170,000 U.S. restaurants, published data about how operators used its AI assistant, Toast IQ, in the first quarter of the year. Operators at more than 125,000 restaurants started millions of threads with Toast IQ.
 
Their most common questions were about sales and revenue (47%), followed by menu and inventory (34%) and guests and marketing (32%). Far fewer used it to ask about forecasting and planning (1%) or catering and reservations (1%).
 
Fine-dining locations had 29% more threads with Toast IQ than fast casuals.
 Earlier in Q2, 
Toast introduced Toast IQ Grow, a complete marketing solution designed to take on the day-to-day work of marketing a business, from identifying a lunch slowdown to automatically launching a campaign to address it. Bundled at $499 per month, Toast IQ Grow pairs an AI-powered Marketing Agent with a dedicated human Marketing Success Manager. In a pilot program, restaurants working with a Marketing Success Manager saw sales grow more than 8% on average compared to similar Toast restaurants, with the top 25% of performers seeing more than 40% growth in the first month.
 One early adopter, Matoi in San Diego, 
generated $3.4K in attributed email revenue over just two months — more than three times the cost of Toast IQ Grow — alongside a 4.5x return on ad spend.


**OpenTable** made its most aggressive AI discovery move on July 16. 
The platform published its 2026 Top 100 Hotel Restaurants in America — drawn from more than 10 million verified diner reviews — and simultaneously upgraded its generative AI dining assistant, Concierge, to operate directly from the platform's homepage for the first time.
 The Concierge upgrade is architecturally significant: 
the integration landscape around Concierge has expanded to include Microsoft Copilot, Amazon Alexa+, Google's Model Context Protocol (which enabled the Gemini Spark integration launched in May 2026), Salesforce Agentforce, and voice AI platforms Slang AI and PolyAI.
 Survey data supporting the Top 100 release: 
60% of respondents planning summer travel say they booked a hotel because of its on-site restaurant, and hotel restaurant dining by travelers rose 13% year-over-year.


**Uber** agreed to acquire Germany-based Delivery Hero for $14.8 billion (reported July 16 by Nation's Restaurant News). [PAYWALLED/BLOCKED — sourced from NRN snippet only.] This deal, combined with DoorDash's $3.9B Deliveroo acquisition earlier in 2026, is rapidly consolidating the global delivery market into two dominant players.

Sources: [RTN — AmEx TheFork](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/06/american-express-expands-restaurant-technology-ambitions-with-700-million-thefork-acquisition/) · [Fast Company — Resy/Tock](https://www.fastcompany.com/91496951/amex-resy-tock-restaurant-reservation-wars) · [NRN — SevenRooms Channel Connect](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology/sevenrooms-launches-a-reservations-aggregator-for-restaurants) · [NRN — Toast IQ usage](https://www.nrn.com/independent-restaurants/here-s-how-restaurants-are-using-toast-s-ai-chatbot)

So what: The reservation market is no longer a software category — it is a battle for the guest data layer between three different parent ecosystems (American Express payments, DoorDash delivery, Booking Holdings/OTA). Operators picking a reservation platform are now implicitly picking whose data infrastructure and distribution network they want to be part of. The platform-neutral play (SevenRooms Channel Connect, offered free) is the most interesting structural move: it could shift the category toward a hotel-style CRS model where the operator owns the relationship and distributes to multiple surfaces.

## AI Visibility & Discovery

The discovery layer is shifting from blue-link search to AI-generated recommendations — and most hospitality businesses are structurally absent from it.


AI search visibility now decides who gets recommended. A May 2026 report from Uberall found that 83% of restaurant locations are entirely invisible in AI-generated recommendations. 86% of those same restaurants maintain some presence on Google — they have done the traditional local-SEO work — and still vanish the moment the question is routed through an AI model.


**Google** is the most consequential platform move in this cycle. 
Google rolled out AI Mode's restaurant booking feature to eight new countries — Australia, Canada, Hong Kong, India, New Zealand, Singapore, South Africa, and the UK — with no Labs opt-in required. Eight reservation platform partners power the feature: TheFork, SevenRooms, ResDiary, Mozrest, Foodhub, Dojo, DesignMyNight, and OpenTable.
 
AI Mode had reached 75 million daily active users by January 2026.
 At Google I/O in May, 
Google named hotel booking as the next vertical for its Universal Commerce Protocol, publicly committing to extending its agentic commerce infrastructure to travel.
 The named hotel partners are Booking.com, Choice Hotels, Expedia, IHG, Marriott, and Wyndham. 
With the announcements at Google I/O 2026, the tech giant has pivoted decisively into agentic hotel booking. Google is stepping out of its traditional role as a search engine and repositioning itself as a direct transaction layer under what it calls an "execution-first" model.


**Booking Holdings** CEO Glenn Fogel is explicitly treating AI platforms as a new distribution threat and opportunity simultaneously. 
The company has partnered with AI incumbents externally — OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and Amazon named. "We continue to believe AI-enabled productivity and efficiencies are an area of notable opportunity," Fogel said.
 The concern is structural: 
OTA CEOs at Booking Holdings and Expedia have publicly positioned their companies to own the conversion layer of AI-driven travel discovery, while hotels invest in eligibility infrastructure that feeds demand into OTA-controlled funnels. AI-referred travelers who arrived with high intent converted 28% less than other visitors — they were completing their journey through a conversion layer that routes to whoever holds the strongest position in it.


**Agentic Hospitality** launched its TravelOS Model Context Protocol (MCP) Server in March 2026 as the hotel infrastructure counter to this threat. 
The TravelOS MCP Server is designed to function as an infrastructure layer that organizes structured hotel data for AI consumption. "Our approach is simple. We extend those systems into AI platforms rather than replacing them. Hotels keep their pricing control, inventory authority and direct relationship with the guest," said Brad Brewer, chief AI officer.


For the hotel sector, the AHLA/HTNG Strategic Brief (Version 2, June 2026) spells out the urgency: 
the AI-Native Hospitality Commerce Ecosystem Diagram identifies a direct path, in which hotels expose their own MCP endpoints to AI agents, versus an indirect path, mediated by third-party intermediaries.
 
Quick-win priorities: open crawler access, refresh and structure hotel content, and begin MCP integration conversations with CRS/PMS/booking-engine partners. The closing message: AI isn't the future — it's here. Hotels that move quickly will help shape the protocol rails before less favorable monetization models become entrenched.


Sources: [TechTimes — OpenTable Concierge homepage](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320769/20260716/best-hotel-restaurants-2026-opentable-list-goes-live-ai-concierge-moves-homepage.htm) · [WinBuzzer — Google AI Mode 8 countries](https://winbuzzer.com/2026/04/13/google-ai-mode-rolls-out-restaurant-booking-to-8-countries-xcxwbn/) · [Skift — Google UCP hotels](https://skift.com/2026/05/19/google-names-hotels-as-next-vertical-for-agentic-shopping/) · [Hotel Management — Agentic Hospitality MCP](https://www.hotelmanagement.net/tech/agentic-hospitality-launches-infrastructure-connect-hotels-ai-booking-platforms)

So what: For mid-market restaurants, this is the same SEO reckoning hotels had with OTAs in 2012. The operators who build structured, AI-readable profiles now — consistent NAP data, rich schema markup, booking links that feed active inventory into API-connected platforms — will be the ones AI assistants recommend. For hotels, the MCP window is short: whoever controls the first-party structured data endpoint controls the guest relationship in the agentic era.

## Data & Integration

The PMS is eating the hotel tech stack; the POS is trying to eat the restaurant tech stack — and in both cases, the battle is over who controls the data layer.


Forty global hospitality technology startups raised more than $1 billion in the past year, with property management systems and artificial intelligence-led platforms attracting the most investment, according to Abode Worldwide's Hospitality Tech Investment Index 2026. The three companies with the largest raises included Mews at $300 million, Kindred at $125 million, and Limehome at €75 million.
 
Seven PMS companies raised a combined $408.1 million. PMS is increasingly becoming the control layer of the hospitality tech stack.
 
PMS businesses are building broader platforms through development and acquisition — Mews' acquisitions of Flexkeeping and DataChat in late 2025 are cited as examples.
 
AI-led guest experience platforms were also a standout investment, with Duve, Chatlyn, Conduit and Canary Technologies raising a combined $152.6 million.


On the restaurant side, **Lavu's** 2026 Restaurant POS AI Capabilities Report (March 2026) is the sharpest data-layer analysis in this cycle. 
The report evaluated platforms across six areas: cross-platform data connections, automatic profit leak detection, labor compliance tracking, real-time problem alerts, AI chat assistance, and overall intelligence depth. It found a market split into three tiers. Tier 1: Lavu's Marty AI connects POS, payroll, and scheduling data simultaneously, running analysis overnight and delivering a Morning Deposit briefing to each store manager by 6 AM. Tier 2: several major POS providers offer AI chat on their own data but cannot pull in payroll or scheduling.
 The operational evidence from a live 169-store deployment: 
annual productivity losses from staffing gaps: $1.86 million. 9,558 unscheduled shifts identified in a single quarter. The best store generated $93 in revenue per labor hour; the worst generated $37 — a 2.5x gap invisible to any single-platform AI.
 CEO Saleem S. Khatri: 
"The problems that cost multi-unit operators the most money — unscheduled overtime, productivity gaps between stores, clock padding — are cross-system problems. You will not find them inside any single platform."


At **HITEC 2026** (San Antonio, June), 
Oracle Hospitality introduced new AI capabilities in OPERA Cloud, including the new OPERA Cloud Assistant bringing embedded intelligence into core hotel operations: AI-assisted room assignments, staff guidance, rate-description generation and multilingual translation. The significance is where the AI sits — inside the PMS environment, close to workflows where front desk, reservations and operations teams already spend their day.
 
Stayfull won the Judges' Choice Award with an AI-native PMS concept; Abra Hospitality won the People's Choice Award with a guest data and personalization platform.


Sources: [Hotel Dive — $1B funding](https://www.hoteldive.com/news/hospitality-tech-attracts-1b-in-funding-pms-ai-leaders/817439/) · [RTN — Lavu POS report](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/05/lavu-publishes-restaurant-pos-ai-capabilities-report-finding-most-platforms-lack-cross-system-intelligence/) · [HTN — HITEC 2026](https://hoteltechnologynews.com/2026/06/what-hitec-2026-revealed-about-the-future-of-hotel-technology/)

So what: The data gap between Tier 1 and Tier 2 POS AI is the most actionable finding for PE-backed multi-unit restaurant operators. A cross-system $1.86M productivity leak in a 169-store portfolio is not a technology problem — it is an integration architecture problem, and it only becomes visible when POS, payroll, and scheduling are in the same data environment simultaneously. For hotel tech investors, PMS consolidation is the structural trade: the top PMS players are becoming broader operating platforms through acquisition, and the sub-scale point solutions need a clear integration story or a buyer.

## Operational AI

The clearest signal from this cycle: operators who get past the demo stage find AI most useful in the narrowest, most repetitive parts of the workflow — not the theatrical ones.


Agentic AI is expanding in restaurant and self-service applications. Nory, a provider of agentic AI and predictive analytics for restaurants, consolidates labor, inventory and sales data in one place and schedules staff, manages ordering and identifies waste areas. SoundHound AI uses agentic AI to automate guest support services and proactively manage IT operations, including ticket creation and resolution.


The Toast IQ usage data (see Vendor Moves) is the most honest operational readout in the briefing: operators are using AI primarily as a performance review tool, not a decision-execution agent. 
The single most common prompt was "Create a short, easy-to-read daily briefing for my restaurant."
 That is a reporting task, not an agentic one. The gap between what operators want (quick data summaries) and what vendors are building (autonomous agents) is real and worth tracking.

On the hotel side, 
the strongest demonstrations at HITEC 2026 were specific: how a front desk agent could make a better room assignment, how a reservations manager could review call quality faster, how a revenue leader could ask a better question, how a housekeeper could receive a clearer task.
 
The agentic operations trend is clearest in the data: software that used to draft a reply or flag an anomaly now completes the job end to end — works the lead, sends the answer, books the slot, moves the rate inside set limits. A person steps in for the exceptions.


**Cendyn** launched Wayfinder at HITEC — described in the HITEC review as an LLM monitoring and generative engine optimization platform designed to help hotels understand how their properties appear across AI-powered search environments. This is the first hospitality-specific GEO product from a major CRM vendor — a new category forming inside hotel marketing. [Coverage sourced from HITEC overview; direct article blocked.]

Sources: [HTN — HITEC 2026](https://hoteltechnologynews.com/2026/06/what-hitec-2026-revealed-about-the-future-of-hotel-technology/) · [Hotel Tech Report Q2](https://hoteltechreport.com/news/q2-2026-hotel-ai-trends-report) · [RTN — Lavu POS report](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/05/lavu-publishes-restaurant-pos-ai-capabilities-report-finding-most-platforms-lack-cross-system-intelligence/)

So what: The operators extracting real ROI from AI are the ones who resist the urge to automate everything and instead ask: what is the single most repetitive task my team does that costs the most in time or errors? That scoped approach is the pattern behind Chipotle's Autocado, Toast IQ's daily briefing adoption, and Oracle's room assignment assistant. The theatrical deployments (full robotic restaurants, AI-that-runs-the-hotel) are not where the money is in 2026.

## Funding & M&A

The structural story of this news cycle is consolidation: three deals are reshaping the entire restaurant booking landscape simultaneously.


DoorDash's $1.2 billion acquisition of SevenRooms puts the delivery giant in direct competition with OpenTable. American Express escalated the fight by buying Tock for $400 million, creating a three-way war. OpenTable controls 60,000 restaurants, while Resy (owned by AmEx) will reach 25,000 venues after integrating Tock.
 American Express's proposed $700M TheFork deal pushes its combined venue count to 75,000. Uber's reported $14.8B acquisition of Delivery Hero (July 16) — if confirmed — adds a fourth dimension, concentrating European delivery.

On the hotel side, 
deal activity in the US hospitality and leisure sector has slowed in 2026, with overall deal volume down approximately 2.5%. However, the concentration of deals in upscale, upper-upscale, and luxury hotels has reached 73% of all hotel transactions — the highest in two years. Luxury segments are projected to see a 5.4% rise in RevPAR in 2026.


In restaurant foodservice delivery, 
Uber agreed to buy Germany-based Delivery Hero for $14.8 billion (reported July 16, 2026, Nation's Restaurant News).


Sources: [RTN — AmEx TheFork](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/06/american-express-expands-restaurant-technology-ambitions-with-700-million-thefork-acquisition/) · [Entrepreneur — DoorDash/SevenRooms](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/doordash-buys-12b-reservation-platform-to-battle-opentable/502986) · [Hotel News Resource — PwC 2026](https://www.hotelnewsresource.com/article141836.html)

So what: For PE-backed hospitality SaaS companies, the buyer universe is narrowing at the large end (fewer, larger acquirers with platform ambitions) and broadening at the mid-market (payment companies, card networks, delivery giants all want restaurant software revenue). Any point solution that touches guest data, reservations, or loyalty is now a potential acquisition candidate for one of these ecosystems. The time to position is before the ecosystem battle is decided.

## WHO TO WATCH

**Pablo Rivero (CEO, Resy and Tock; SVP, American Express Global Dining)** — Rivero is executing the most ambitious dining-tech integration in this cycle: folding Tock's fine-dining inventory into Resy, building a Toast POS data bridge, opening APIs to generative AI platforms, and extending the Amex card credit to Tock venues. His framing that this is about "meaningful connection" rather than platform conquest is either genuine hospitality conviction or very good positioning — either way, the financial results (20%+ Amex spend lift at Resy restaurants in Q4 2025) are making the case for him.

**Joel Montaniel (Co-founder and CEO, SevenRooms)** — The Channel Connect launch is the most interesting competitive move in the reservation wars: offering a free, open-license aggregator to all restaurants, including those on rival platforms, is a direct attack on OpenTable's "system of record" terms and a signal that DoorDash is willing to prioritize merchant relationships over short-term platform lock-in. Whether the open-license framing survives as SevenRooms scales the product will be the tell.

**Saleem S. Khatri (CEO, Lavu)** — Published the most operationally credible restaurant AI benchmark this cycle, with specific dollar figures and a live deployment methodology. The claim that cross-system AI is a distinct category from single-platform chat AI is the most practically important product positioning in QSR/multi-unit tech right now. **Matt Davis (Head of North America Hotels, OpenTable)** — His quote that "restaurants aren't just an itinerary line item anymore, they're the anchor" captures the strategic thesis behind OpenTable's aggressive move to put Concierge on the homepage.
