## Vendor Moves

The restaurant reservation and discovery market saw its most consequential structural moves of the cycle, with two watchlist names dominating and a dense cluster of hotel-side product launches out of HITEC 2026.

**DoorDash** launched Ask DoorDash on June 11 — 
a new AI chatbot that lets users order food and groceries with text prompts and photos in its latest AI push.
 
The chatbot allows customers to order food, build grocery carts, and book restaurant reservations using text prompts, photos, or voice inputs, accessible via a dedicated "Ask" button in the app's search bar. The system pulls from each user's ordering history and outside sources such as blog posts and social media reviews.
 
Early testing showed nearly half of all restaurant orders placed through the tool were from a place the customer had never ordered from before; carts assembled through the chatbot ran more than 35% higher in value than a standard grocery order. DoorDash co-founder Andy Fang said: "We've spent over a decade building an app that puts everything in your city at your fingertips, but more options shouldn't mean more work." The product draws on AI models built by OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google, supplemented by open-source options.
 
DoorDash is in the middle of a massive investment cycle that involves revamping its tech platform following a string of acquisitions including the $1.2 billion deal for SevenRooms and the nearly $4 billion acquisition of Deliveroo. Its stock is down 33% YTD. Finance chief Ravi Inukonda said DoorDash is making progress on the tech stack overhaul and plans to carry out most of the spending this year.
 Separately, 
DoorDash wound down its AI-powered standalone discovery app Zesty less than a year after launch, integrating its conversational search and recommendation capabilities into the core DoorDash app. The shutdown is less about the failure of an idea and more about the maturation of a strategy — DoorDash is bringing discovery closer to the transaction layer.


**American Express** announced a proposed 
$700 million acquisition of TheFork from Tripadvisor, which would add TheFork's network of more than 50,000 restaurants across 11 European countries to American Express' growing dining ecosystem. Combined with existing platforms Resy and Tock, the acquisition would expand American Express' network to approximately 75,000 bookable venues worldwide, creating one of the largest restaurant reservation and dining engagement platforms in the industry.
 
American Express first entered the reservation technology market through its acquisition of Resy in 2019; the acquisition of Tock in 2024 significantly broadened its capabilities; and the proposed addition of TheFork would extend that strategy internationally.
 Separately, 
Claude users can now explore U.S. Resy restaurants directly in their conversations when deciding where to eat — Claude connects to Resy to help diners browse available tables in real time and secure a table seamlessly.
 And 
a Resy–Tock merger completing this summer will roughly double Resy's venue inventory to over 25,000 listings.


**Toast** continued its platform expansion with two material launches. 
Toast IQ Grow (May 5) is a complete marketing solution designed to take on the day-to-day work of marketing a business, from identifying a lunch slowdown to automatically launching a campaign to address it. The Marketing Agent builds audiences and automates campaigns across email, SMS, social media, and paid advertising, bundled at $499/month and paired with a dedicated human Marketing Success Manager. In a pilot, restaurants working with a Marketing Success Manager saw sales grow more than 8% on average compared to similar Toast restaurants.
 
Toast also expanded Toast IQ with a conversational AI assistant drawing on real-time and historical data from approximately 148,000 customer locations. The assistant goes beyond answering questions by proactively surfacing insights and executing tasks directly within Toast.
 One early adopter, Romain Bernus of Le Petit Four Bakery, said: 
"Pulling reports was just too slow when we're so busy — it always meant late-night research scrolling through tabs and filters."


**Radisson Hotel Group** launched AI-powered real-time price matching on July 6. 
The new AI-powered feature automatically detects and matches lower third-party hotel rates in real time — eliminating manual claims, screenshots
 and approval processes entirely. Gianni Di Fede, Global Chief Commercial Officer, said: "Price matching is our commitment to ensuring that guests who choose to book directly with us never have to wonder whether they could have found a better deal elsewhere."

## AI Visibility & Discovery

The structural shift from keyword search to conversational AI recommendations is producing measurable commercial consequences.


Google expanded agentic booking for restaurant reservations in AI Mode to U.S. users without Labs opt-in. Two weeks later, Google confirmed it is developing similar capabilities for hotel and flight reservations, working with partners including Booking.com, Expedia, Marriott International, IHG Hotels & Resorts, Choice Hotels International and Wyndham Hotels & Resorts. As of June 2026, hotel and flight booking in AI Mode has not yet launched.
 Julie Farago, Google's VP of engineering for travel and local search, said: 
"We're not going to rush this out the door because we want to make sure that it's a seamless experience and that people have all the control that they need and expect."
 
After Google's announcement, shares of Booking Holdings and Expedia fell, reflecting investor fear that Google could siphon away the customers those companies pay billions to reach.


**OpenTable** (Booking Holdings) has 
deepened its OpenAI integration with official ChatGPT booking, and added table inventory across Perplexity and Microsoft Copilot. It integrates with 11 voice AI providers, including Slang, PolyAI, and Hostie. In the last year, adoption scaled sixfold and diners have booked more than 1 million voice reservations to date.


**Resy** built its Claude integration around a striking consumer trend: 
20% of diners already use AI to look for information about venues, and that number jumps to 40% among Gen Z. Anthropic's Economic Index report found that "directive" interactions — where users delegate tasks rather than just asking questions — rose from 27% to 39% over an eight-month period.


**DoorDash**'s 2026 Restaurant Industry Trends Report found 
22% of consumers have used an AI tool like ChatGPT or Google Gemini to help choose a restaurant. Restaurant listing sites like DoorDash account for more than 41% of the sources AI tools cite when recommending restaurants. Operators are responding by updating menu information (39%), managing reviews (34%), and improving photo quality (32%).
 Against that, 
Uberall's 2026 GEO benchmark found AI assistants typically name only 3–5 restaurants per query and stop — and 83% of restaurant locations never appear in AI-generated recommendations at all.


Sources: [PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/google-agentic-travel-booking-ai) · [DoorDash Newsroom](https://about.doordash.com/en-us/news/doordash-restaurant-industry-trends-report-2026) · [Resy](https://resy.com/join/resources/ai-restaurant-search/)

**So what:** AI search is now a live revenue lever. The gap between operators who have structured data, rich review profiles, and real-time inventory APIs accessible to AI agents, and those who do not, is becoming a measurable cover-count difference. For restaurant groups, the priority action is an AI discovery audit: test whether ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity recommend your venues when guests ask the kinds of questions your target diners actually ask.

## Data & Integration

HITEC 2026 (San Antonio, June) produced a concentrated wave of hotel technology announcements sharing a common theme: AI moving from standalone applications into core operating systems.


A series of recent hotel technology announcements shows how quickly AI is moving from standalone applications into the core operating systems hotels use every day. Many of the announcements surfaced around HITEC 2026, where AI was not presented simply as a new product category but appeared across core hotel workflows embedded into platforms that already support front desk operations, guest engagement, pricing, connectivity, sales, and back-office management.


**Oracle Hospitality** announced Oracle OPERA Cloud Assistant, embedding AI-powered room assignments, AI-generated rate descriptions, operational guidance, and translation directly into OPERA Cloud workflows. Because OPERA Cloud is one of the hotel industry's most widely deployed PMSs, embedding AI at the system-of-record level carries disproportionate reach.

**Grevon** debuted Grevon Kore, 
an MCP-based connectivity layer for hospitality powering Pulse (an AI booking agent for hotel websites), Echo (an AI voice agent for inbound calls), and Ops (a staff intelligence platform). Grevon's launch points to an emerging competitive front in hotel distribution: making official hotel content, rates, availability and booking paths usable by AI agents.


**Revinate** introduced Ivy, an AI decision-intelligence layer across its platform including automated call scoring and AI-assisted guest inbox capabilities. **RoomPriceGenie** added plain-language Price Explanations to its rate recommendations — addressing a real adoption barrier among independent hoteliers who are cautious about algorithmic pricing decisions they cannot understand.

The cycle's most structurally important critique came from Darko Bosancic (Sirma Group): 
most AI agents in hospitality live entirely inside the vendor's own ecosystem — reading that vendor's data, automating that vendor's workflows, speaking exclusively to that vendor's modules. The moment a hotel needs that agent to interact with a different system, the intelligence stops.
 He identified back-office AI — housekeeping optimisation, predictive maintenance, procurement intelligence, energy management, labor scheduling — as the highest-value and most neglected application area. 
As hotels move guest data through AI pipelines at an accelerating rate, questions of GDPR compliance, PCI DSS obligations, and data governance are becoming urgent. Most independent properties and mid-scale operators are not asking them — often because their technology vendors are not raising the issue.


Sources: [Hotel Technology News — HITEC](https://hoteltechnologynews.com/2026/07/recent-hotel-technology-launches-show-ai-moving-from-standalone-tools-into-connected-hotel-operations/) · [Hospitality Net — AI Agent Trap](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/opinion/4131969/the-ai-agent-trap-why-hospitalitys-next-technology-crisis-is-already-in-motion)

**So what:** The vendor that wins the next phase of hotel tech will provide cross-system orchestration, not just standalone AI features. Operators evaluating technology now should test a simple question: can this AI agent act on data from your PMS, RMS, F&B system, and housekeeping system simultaneously? If not, the ROI is theoretical. For PE-backed hospitality SaaS companies, this is the moment to build or acquire orchestration capability before the market prices it in.

A notable restaurant-sector data point from the RTN ticker: Liberty Mutual backed inKind with $320 million in financing to help restaurants access capital and drive guest demand — a sign that restaurant fintech is attracting institutional capital alongside pure-play tech.

## Governance & Security


On June 2, 2026, the White House issued an Executive Order titled "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security." The Order treats AI as a national strategic capability and a cybersecurity priority. For hospitality, the competitive question is shifting from "How many AI features do we have?" to "Can we govern AI responsibly, secure our connected systems, and turn operational trust into guest experience?"


Terence Ronson (Pertlink Limited) identified 
one of the most overlooked shifts of the decade: the convergence of hospitality infrastructure with national infrastructure. Large hotels, integrated resorts, convention centers, gaming properties, cruise terminals, and airport hotels increasingly intersect with transportation, healthcare support, financial networks, public utilities, and emergency response. The same intelligence that makes a property efficient also makes it exposed.



The DOJ's Data Security Program restricts hospitality AI deployments that give countries of concern access to Americans' bulk sensitive personal data. AI deployments typically require massive volumes of customer and device data and often rely on third-party vendors, offshore development teams, and cloud-hosted model pipelines. Hospitality companies routinely touch at least four of the DSP's sensitive categories: identifiers, geolocation, biometrics, and financial data.
 Many organizations assume that de-identified or tokenized data falls outside the restrictions — 
that assumption is incorrect.


Sources: [Hospitality Net — AI Security EO](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/opinion/4132771/when-national-ai-security-becomes-a-hospitality-imperative) · [Hospitality Technology — DOJ DSP](https://hospitalitytech.com/beyond-hipaa-navigating-dojs-new-bulk-data-thresholds-hospitality-ai)

**So what:** Hotel and restaurant tech buyers need a vendor AI governance questionnaire in procurement — now, not at renewal. Minimum questions: where is guest data hosted; how are prompts logged; is data used for model training; what human override exists over agentic actions; which jurisdictions govern data residency. Vendors that cannot answer clearly represent regulatory liability, not just feature gaps.
