## Vendor Moves

The restaurant reservations market has fractured into open warfare, with three distinct platform alliances now competing for the guest relationship across delivery, dine-in, and discovery.

**DoorDash** completed its $1.2 billion acquisition of SevenRooms in June 2025, and the integration is now producing competitive product. 
Via SevenRooms, DoorDash is offering restaurants voice AI that can take reservations over the phone and event management capabilities including group bookings and private dining.
 The more consequential product news arrived on June 17, when 
SevenRooms unveiled Channel Connect, a desktop app that aggregates reservations from various booking channels into a single system while automatically updating a restaurant's inventory across those channels. The program pulls in bookings from reservation apps, search engines, social media, review sites, the restaurant's website, AI agents, and hotel concierges — including from "major booking platforms" such as OpenTable and Resy.
 Critically, 
Channel Connect is being offered for free under an open license, meaning it will be available to any restaurant, even those that don't use SevenRooms
 — a direct counter to 
OpenTable's April move requiring restaurant clients to make it their system of record and make all inventory available on OpenTable.


**American Express** is building the competing empire. 
American Express is proposing to acquire TheFork from Tripadvisor for $700 million in cash. TheFork connects millions of diners with more than 50,000 restaurants across 11 European countries. Combined with Resy and Tock, the deal would expand American Express' dining network to 75,000 bookable venues. The proposed transaction is expected to close before the end of 2026, subject to regulatory approvals.
 Meanwhile, 
American Express is combining its two reservation platforms — Resy and Tock — into one under Resy this summer, approximately doubling Resy's library to more than 25,000 venues and activating a major partnership with POS provider Toast that will connect Resy guestbook data with Toast handheld devices, allowing servers to view customer information such as wine preferences and birthdays.
 On AI discovery, 
Claude users can now explore U.S. Resy restaurants directly in their conversations when deciding where to eat; Claude connects to Resy to help diners browse available tables in real time. For restaurants, the partnership reflects American Express' focus on creating AI-powered experiences and embedding Resy into leading AI platforms.


**Booking Holdings** continues to press its AI advantage. 
Priceline's AI travel agent Penny is getting more interactive and more personalized; in early testing "we are seeing a noticeable uplift from users who engage with Penny compared to non-Penny users." Booking.com's agentic service flows for complaints and cancellations have reduced customer service contacts. OpenTable launched an AI Concierge that initially answered diner questions and is now evolving into a broader discovery tool.
 Separately, 
Agoda found that its AI-assisted automation drove a double-digit year-over-year reduction in customer service costs per booking.


Sources: [DoorDash/SevenRooms](https://about.doordash.com/en-us/news/doordash-completes-acquisition-of-sevenrooms) | [NRN – Channel Connect](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology/sevenrooms-launches-a-reservations-aggregator-for-restaurants) | [American Express / TheFork](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/06/american-express-expands-restaurant-technology-ambitions-with-700-million-thefork-acquisition/) | [AmEx / Resy / Anthropic](https://www.americanexpress.com/en-us/newsroom/articles/travel-and-dining/american-express-partners-with-anthropic-to-make-u-s--resy-resta.html)

**So what:** The reservation market has consolidated into three alliance structures — DoorDash/SevenRooms, AmEx (Resy+Tock+TheFork+Toast+Claude), and OpenTable/Uber — each trying to own first-party guest data across delivery and dine-in. For independent restaurants, Channel Connect's free open-license model is the most immediately actionable item: it removes the inventory lock-in that OpenTable's April policy change introduced. For PE-backed restaurant SaaS, the question is which stack your portfolio companies are plugged into, because the data leverage will compound in favour of whichever platform reaches ~25,000+ venues first.

## AI Visibility & Discovery

The distribution layer for hospitality bookings is being structurally rewritten by AI discovery, with Google already live and OpenAI retreating from direct transaction completion.

**Google** has already deployed agentic restaurant booking in AI Mode across the United States without a Labs opt-in requirement. 
Two weeks after launching agentic bookings in AI Mode to reserve restaurants, buy event tickets, and make beauty and wellness appointments, Google confirmed it is developing similar capabilities for hotel and flight reservations, working with Booking.com, Expedia, Marriott International, IHG Hotels & Resorts, Choice Hotels International, and Wyndham Hotels & Resorts. Google said specifics regarding booking flow, payments, and other details are still being worked out.
 Google's VP of engineering for travel and local search was explicit: 
"We're not going to rush this out the door because we want to make sure that it's a seamless experience and that people have all the control that they need and expect."
 Importantly, 
after the hotel/flight booking announcement, shares of Booking Holdings and Expedia Group fell, reflecting investor fear that Google could siphon away the customers those companies pay billions to reach.


**OpenAI** moved in the opposite direction. 
OpenAI shifted its focus for ChatGPT from completing transactions to product discovery, with Instant Checkout moving to apps. OpenAI abandoned plans for enabling direct purchases within ChatGPT, prioritizing the platform's role in search and product discovery and leaving transaction processing to external apps. This strategic pivot has relieved concerns for online travel agencies like Expedia and Booking Holdings.


The guest discovery reality from DoorDash and SevenRooms' joint research is striking: 
22% of consumers now use AI to discover restaurants. 41% of AI recommendations come from listing platforms like DoorDash, followed by restaurant websites. 64% of diners still call to book reservations, yet 40% of those calls go unanswered — while 74% of diners are open to AI handling reservations, creating a clear opportunity to capture more demand.
 On the hotel side, 
BCG found that 37% of travelers already use AI large language models to plan and book trips.


Sources: [PhocusWire – Google agentic booking](https://www.phocuswire.com/google-agentic-travel-booking-ai) | [Skift – OpenAI checkout walkback](https://skift.com/2026/03/05/openai-chatgpt-checkout-walkback/) | [DoorDash/SevenRooms Restaurant Trends 2026](https://sevenrooms.com/research/restaurant-trends/)

**So what:** For hotel operators, Google's move is not yet live for lodging but the direction is confirmed. Properties need structured, machine-readable content now — not when the product launches. For restaurants, the 40% unanswered call rate alongside 74% diner openness to AI reservations is the clearest ROI case in this cycle for voice AI reservation investment. OpenAI's retreat from checkout is good news for OTAs but not a permanent reprieve.

## Funding & M&A

The hospitality tech investment market crossed a structural milestone, with the PMS emerging as the category investors now treat as essential infrastructure.


Forty global hospitality technology startups raised more than $1 billion in the past year, with property management systems and AI-led platforms attracting the most investment, according to U.K.-based Abode Worldwide's Hospitality Tech Investment Index 2026. The three companies with the largest raises included Mews at $300 million; Kindred at $125 million across two funding rounds; and Limehome at €75 million.
 
The hospitality startups attracting capital "sit close to essential operator workflows" and become more valuable over time, Abode Worldwide founder and CEO Jessica Gillingham said. "Investors are concentrating capital in the platforms that hospitality businesses increasingly depend on every day, especially PMS and AI-led systems that unify operations, improve automation and strengthen the data layer underneath the business," she said.



PMS was the clear funding leader. Seven PMS companies raised a combined $408.1 million — more than any other category. The timing suggests investors are backing hospitality tech as a category, not just isolated subsegments.
 
PMS businesses are building broader platforms through development and acquisition, with Mews' acquisitions of Flexkeeping and DataChat in late 2025 cited as examples. AI-led guest experience platforms — Duve, Chatlyn, Conduit, and Canary Technologies — raised a combined $152.6 million. Canary notably acquired OpenKey in February to expand its access to door lock providers.


On the hotel real estate side, 
PwC's US Deals 2026 midyear outlook notes that deal volume is down approximately 2.5% over the past six months, but deals are concentrated in premium segments such as luxury hotels and wellness resorts. Properties that are ready for AI integration are commanding higher premiums
, and 
companies lacking in data and AI-enabled operations are seeing lower bids or withdrawn offers, while those with robust data and AI-enabled operations are attracting higher valuations.


Sources: [Hotel Dive – $1B funding report](https://www.hoteldive.com/news/hospitality-tech-attracts-1b-in-funding-pms-ai-leaders/817439/) | [Hospitalitynet – Abode Worldwide Index](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/report/4131877/hospitality-tech-drew-over-1-billion-in-the-last-year-with-pms-and-ai-led-platforms-capturing-biggest-share) | [Hotel News Resource – PwC deals midyear](https://www.hotelnewsresource.com/article141836.html)

**So what:** For PE-backed hospitality SaaS, the Abode data confirms that investors now treat the PMS as the control layer — meaning platforms that integrate into or replace PMS functions are higher-value acquisition targets. For operators, the PwC signal is direct: AI readiness is now a valuation input in asset transactions, not just an operational aspiration.

## Operational AI

Three developments this cycle show genuine operational AI moving past pilot, with specific attribution evidence.

**Radisson Hotel Group** launched the freshest move of this cycle on July 6. 
Radisson launched AI-powered real-time hotel price matching that automatically detects lower publicly available rates for its properties on third-party booking platforms and instantly matches them on RadissonHotels.com — removing the need for guests to submit claims, screenshots, or wait for manual approval. The launch replaces a legacy, reactive Best Online Rate Guarantee process with a proactive and fully automated solution; the feature is live across all Radisson Hotel Group properties worldwide.
 CCO Gianni Di Fede stated: 
"Today's travelers expect speed, simplicity and confidence when they book. Price matching is our commitment to ensuring that guests who choose to book directly with us never have to wonder whether they could have found a better deal elsewhere."
 The system 
currently covers rates from leading OTAs including Booking.com, Expedia, Hotels.com, Agoda, Priceline, Trip.com, Makemytrip, and several others.


**PAR Technology and Square** launched restaurant AI agents in April 2026. 
PAR Technology and Square unveiled AI products that can assist operators with their business and complete tasks on their behalf, joining Toast and Thanx in the growing field of agentic AI.
 The PAR case is the strongest documented: 
Taco Bell franchisee Charter Foods began testing one of PAR's agents last year and asked it to identify stores that could benefit by staying open later. Late-night sales increased 20% as a result of its recommendations. "In this industry, speed of analyzing data is real money," Charter Senior Director John Rankin told Restaurant Business.
 Square's Managerbot goes further: 
while the old assistant was reactive, Managerbot is proactive — it surfaces insights and metrics the restaurant needs to know, like running low on milk or menu items with duplicate names in the POS, and can perform tasks such as drafting employee schedules, creating and running email campaigns, and generating purchase orders.


At scale, **McDonald's and Google** have the most-cited drive-thru outcome in the cycle: 
McDonald's is rolling Google AI conversational ordering to 14,300 US drive-thrus, with 27 seconds shaved off service time and roughly $65,000 of additional annual revenue per store.
 BCG's AI-First Hotels report adds a hotels-side data point: 
Ritz-Carlton San Francisco implemented an AI system that synchronizes room-cleaning schedules with check-out patterns, guest preferences, and staff availability — speeding up room preparation by 20%.


Sources: [eHotelier – Radisson price matching](https://insights.ehotelier.com/global-news/2026/07/06/radisson-hotel-group-launches-hospitality-industry-leading-ai-powered-real-time-price-matching-technology/) | [NRN – PAR and Square AI agents](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology/the-hot-new-restaurant-tech-trend-ai-agents) | [GlacierGrid – Agentic AI hype vs. savings](https://www.glaciergrid.com/resources/research-and-impact/agentic-ai-restaurants-hype-vs-savings) | [BCG – AI-First Hotels](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/ai-first-hotels-leaner-faster-smarter)

**So what:** The Radisson price-matching launch is worth watching closely: it automates a guest-facing function that previously required staff time and guest friction. If it drives measurable direct booking uplift, expect other major hotel groups to accelerate similar deployments before Q4. For multi-unit restaurant operators, the PAR Charter Foods case (20% late-night sales uplift) is the clearest attributable ROI from an AI agent this cycle — match it against your own benchmarks before committing to competing platforms.

## Governance & Security

Two distinct governance signals arrived this cycle — one regulatory, one workforce — and both point to the same underlying problem: AI is being deployed faster than oversight structures can follow.

**The White House AI Executive Order** (June 2, 2026) is landing directly in hospitality. 
On June 2, 2026, the White House issued an Executive Order titled "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security." On its face, the Order is about federal systems, frontier-model security, and national cyber defense. Read carefully, it signals that artificial intelligence has crossed a threshold — from an experimental productivity tool to a critical layer of operational infrastructure that governments now treat as a matter of national security.
 The hotel-specific implication, per Hospitality Net's analysis: 
the modern hotel has quietly become a dense, interconnected intelligent environment — binding together identity, payments, guest data, building systems, IoT, energy orchestration, and AI-driven workflows. The more intelligent a property becomes, the more strategically exposed it becomes.
 The EO's clearinghouse model for vulnerability sharing is expected to cascade into vendor procurement requirements and cyber insurance terms.

**HSMAI Foundation's AI Talent Pipeline Report** (May 2026) quantifies the workforce readiness gap. 
Students are entering the workforce with established AI habits and confidence, but employers must equip future talent with the training, governance, and decision-making frameworks needed to apply AI tools successfully in real-world hotel operations, said HSMAI President Brian Hicks. Students rated their confidence in applying AI to work tasks at 3.24 out of 5, while rating their program's preparation at just 2.78 out of 5 — implying students are self-teaching AI through experimentation more than through structured curriculum.
 
Students identified data analysis as highly important for future hospitality careers, yet current AI use remains heavily weighted toward academic productivity tasks. Only 16% report using AI to support their own job search efforts.


BCG's structural data makes the gap concrete: 
only 2.9% of full-time employees in travel and tourism possess AI skills, compared with 21% in the tech and media sectors.


Sources: [Hospitalitynet – White House EO / hospitality](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/opinion/4132771/when-national-ai-security-becomes-a-hospitality-imperative) | [Hospitalitynet – HSMAI AI Talent Pipeline Report](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4132558/hsmai-foundation-releases-new-ai-talent-pipeline-report-examining-the-future-of-hospitality-workforce-readiness)

**So what:** For hotel operators and SaaS vendors, the EO signals that "AI governance" is moving from a voluntary best practice to a procurement and insurance requirement. Begin auditing what AI tools are already running in your stack, what data they touch, and whether vendors can demonstrate governance. For PE-backed operators building out commercial teams, the HSMAI talent gap means new hires arrive with habitual AI use but no operational frameworks — onboarding programs need to fill that gap explicitly.
