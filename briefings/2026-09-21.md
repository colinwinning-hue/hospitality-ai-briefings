## AI Visibility & Discovery

The booking funnel for both hotels and restaurants is being rebuilt around AI-mediated discovery, with two structural shifts now confirmed rather than forecast.

**Google** moved from observer to participant in travel booking on August 27, 2026. 
Google rolled out agentic hotel booking in AI Mode on August 27, 2026, initially for U.S. users in English. Ten partners are live at launch: Booking.com, Choice Hotels International, Expedia, Hilton, Hotels.com, IHG Hotels & Resorts, Marriott International, Priceline, Trip.com, and Wyndham Hotels & Resorts. Google is not the merchant of record — the hotel or booking platform handles that side of the transaction.
 The mechanism matters: 
Google shares only the minimum data needed to complete a booking (name, payment, occupancy, property), withholding richer chat context and inspiration signals from partners, which reduces their visibility into the path to conversion.
 On the infrastructure side, 
Amadeus has two product announcements — AI Commerce, which makes hotels bookable through AI assistant channels, and the official launch of Amadeus Max, which lets hotel staff query data in plain English. But the bigger story is infrastructure. Amadeus is Google's only B2B hospitality partner on the Universal Commerce Protocol, the framework being built to govern how AI agents find, book, and transact hotels.


**Booking Holdings** confirmed the shift but urged proportionality. 
Booking Holdings CFO Ewout Steenbergen said in the second quarter of 2026 that bookings coming from conversational agents, free or paid, account for less than 1% of the group's room-nights, with no significant recent change in that share. Phocuswright projects a stable split in the United States through 2029 between bookings via online travel agencies and direct bookings, at 52% and 48% respectively.
 Yet the traffic disruption is documented: 
Booking Holdings separately disclosed AI Overviews are squeezing organic search, while raising its transformation-program savings target to $650 million in annual run-rate savings by 2027. CEO Glenn Fogel told analysts that Google's AI Overviews "definitely put some pressure on SEO," though Booking's direct traffic in the mid-60% range "hasn't gone down at all."


On the restaurant side, the AI discovery gap has been quantified and is severe. 
A May 2026 report from Uberall found that 83% of restaurant locations are entirely invisible in AI-generated recommendations. When a consumer asks an AI assistant for a nearby option, only 17% of restaurants ever appear in the response. Uberall found that 86% of those same restaurants maintain some presence on Google — they have done the traditional local SEO work and still vanish the moment the question is routed through an AI model.
 The adoption curve on the consumer side is steepening fast: 
BrightLocal's 2026 consumer survey found 45% of consumers now use AI tools for local business recommendations — up from 6% one year earlier. DoorDash's 2026 Restaurant Industry Trends Report (3,000 U.S. consumers) found 22% have used AI to choose a restaurant.
 And search surface coverage is shifting: 
BrightEdge found restaurants went from a 10% AI Overview trigger rate in early 2025 to 78% by February 2026 — one of the fastest-growing verticals BrightEdge tracks.


Sources: [Skift](https://skift.com/2026/08/27/googles-agentic-hotel-booking-tool-comes-to-ai-mode/), [Hotel Dive](https://www.hoteldive.com/news/google-introduces-ai-mode-hotel-booking/829265/), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/27/googles-ai-mode-can-now-track-flight-prices-help-book-hotels-and-more/), [Skift Newsletter](https://newsletters.skift.com/p/ai-tools-target-hotel-booking), [Hospitality On](https://hospitality-on.com/en/distribution/google-launches-ai-agent-hotel-booking-test-united-states)

So what: Every hotel and restaurant that has not audited its machine-readable content — schema markup, Google Business Profile completeness, live pricing accuracy — is already losing positioning in the distribution layer that will define the next five years. For hotels, the first question is whether your CRS can connect to Amadeus UCP. For restaurants, it is whether your menu data, reviews, and location pages are structured in a way AI engines can parse and cite.

## Vendor Moves

The reservation market has undergone its largest structural shake-up in a generation, with ownership now determining which ecosystem a restaurant or hotel diner inhabits.

**DoorDash** has moved the SevenRooms acquisition into live product. 
DoorDash has rolled reservations into its app. The feature, which launched in September, is called "Going Out," and allows users to book tables and earn dining-related rewards.
 The data play is explicit: 
The integration with SevenRooms gives DoorDash and its restaurants access to more data about diners. "Delivery and dine-in have typically been siloed data sets," SevenRooms co-founder Joel Montaniel said. "So if a customer has ordered six times, and they're coming into the restaurant for the first time, are they a first-time customer or a seventh-time customer?"
 To counter platform lock-in from rivals, **SevenRooms** has introduced Channel Connect: 
Channel Connect by SevenRooms, a desktop app that aggregates reservations from various booking channels into a single system, while also automatically updating a restaurant's inventory across those channels. SevenRooms, which is owned by DoorDash, said Channel Connect will free operators from having to use multiple tablets to manually reconcile double bookings.
 The competitive framing is direct: 
The company references OpenTable's new terms and says "Channel Connect was built so that no single platform has to be the gatekeeper to your operations."


**American Express** completed its consolidation of the Resy-Tock ecosystem. 
As of September 15, 2026, hundreds of restaurants that use Tock have become eligible for the Amex Resy credit.
 Meanwhile, Tripadvisor is selling TheFork to American Express for $700 million to focus on the experiences market through Viator. 
Tripadvisor is selling TheFork to focus more on delivering experiences for consumers, and formed a partnership with Airbnb to make tours, activities, and attractions available to book on Airbnb.
 That gives AmEx a European dining reservation footprint alongside Resy and Tock in the U.S.

**Toast** launched Toast Drive-Thru in 2026, 
as a unified solution for enterprise quick-service operations, bringing together hardware, POS-native software, AI voice ordering integrations and services.
 On marketing, 
Toast IQ Grow was launched as part of Toast's Spring 2026 product release, described as more than 20 updates across marketing, payroll, inventory and operations, with Toast IQ Grow designed to take on the day-to-day work of marketing a business. At the center is an AI-powered Marketing Agent that can build audiences and automate campaigns across channels.


**Mews** launched its POS in North America in September 2026 (noted in Hotel Dive), extending its platform beyond property management into point-of-sale — continuing the acquisition-and-expand strategy that saw Mews acquire Flexkeeping and DataChat in late 2025.

**Expedia** acquired Layla in 2026, 
a planning app built on conversational AI, with the aim of capturing travelers as early as the research phase rather than only at the point of final booking.


Sources: [Expedite.news](https://www.expedite.news/p/doordash-reservations-are-here), [NRN/SevenRooms](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology/sevenrooms-launches-a-reservations-aggregator-for-restaurants), [Upgraded Points](https://upgradedpoints.com/news/amex-resy-credit-tock-restaurants/), [Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/08/17/lifestyle/reservation-platforms-sevenrooms-opentable-resy/), [Restaurant Technology News/Toast](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/06/toast-advances-restaurant-operations-with-connected-pos-ai-and-guest-engagement-technology/)

So what: The reservation market has consolidated into three ecosystem bets — American Express (Resy + Tock + now TheFork, Amex cardholder base), DoorDash (SevenRooms, delivery data, conversational ordering, 42M active users), and OpenTable/Booking Holdings (Visa/Chase partnerships, discovery reach). A restaurant choosing a reservation platform is now choosing whose wallet and data ecosystem it wants to live in, not just a booking tool. For mid-market groups, Channel Connect's open-license aggregation layer is the most operator-friendly development of the cycle — it lets restaurants maintain optionality without committing exclusively.

## Operational AI

The restaurant sector is past peak hype and entering a performance accountability phase, with a sharp divide between implementations that have survived contact with real operations and those that haven't.

Restaurant technology executives publicly corrected overclaiming at the 2026 NRA Show. 
At the National Restaurant Association Show, Restaurant Dive made a point of closing interviews with the question: "What's one thing people are getting wrong?" Almost universally among restaurant tech leaders, the answer was "artificial intelligence." Shake Shack is using AI to identify and alert managers to potential inefficiencies. Culver's began rolling out computer vision to measure service execution and vehicle flow in its drive-thrus.
 Popmenu's CEO was the most pointed: 
Part of the problem, Sweeney said, is that overadoption of large language models rests on shaky fundamentals. The cost of AI tokens required to operate coding services can exceed the salaries of the workers the technology purports to replace. "Being first is not always the best. Being right and doing it the right way is the best."
 Toast's CMO drew a clear implementation bar: 
"It can't be another thing to do. It has to actually do some of the work for you."


The FS/TEC 2026 conference starting this week in Grapevine, Texas is the next major signal event. 
If you're interested in learning more about the growing trend of agentic AI, there will be two sessions focused on that topic Thursday morning, hosted by Oracle and Momos, respectively.
 The scheduling theme is prominent: 
In "AI Scheduling That Learns," Harri will talk to an operator about the ins and outs of using AI to automate scheduling, a "candid conversation" looking at how AI is helping with one of the hardest parts of running a restaurant.


On the hotel side, AI revenue management has crossed from experimentation to standard practice. 
Hotels using AI-driven revenue management tools report an estimated 17% increase in total revenue compared to those still relying on traditional methods. More than 86% of hoteliers say they now depend on AI for forecasting and demand analytics.
 Marriott confirmed organizational changes to its Customer Engagement Centers in September 2026, framed around "better reflecting how our guests interact with us across channels" — widely read as accelerated automation of contact-center functions.

At the Citi conference on September 8, **Booking Holdings** CEO Glenn Fogel articulated the most operator-useful framing of AI's role in hospitality: 
Fogel described AI as a tool for predicting travel problems before customers feel them, not just for answering questions. Examples include weather-based activity changes, itinerary adjustments and proactive rebooking. The company is combining real-time data such as weather, flight status and hotel availability with customer history.


Sources: [Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/restaurant-technology-executives-warn-against-ai-overdependence/821024/), [NRN/FS-TEC](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology/what-to-watch-for-at-fs-tec-2026), [Investing.com/Booking](https://in.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/booking-holdings-at-citis-2026-global-tmt-conference-ai-loyalty-lead-93CH-5585313), [Hotel Tech News (51-2)](https://hoteltechnologynews.com/2025/11/how-ai-will-rewrite-hotel-revenue-management-systems-in-2026/)

So what: The NRA Show skepticism and the FS/TEC agenda tell the same story: operators have survived one cycle of AI hype and are now demanding point-specific ROI before committing. For SaaS vendors, this means generic "AI-powered" messaging is a liability; for operators, the frame is now "does this save labor hours in a quantifiable way, and how fast?" Scheduling and revenue management have the clearest payback periods. Drive-thru voice AI remains contested territory.

## Data & Integration

A cross-sector consensus is hardening: fragmented data is the most costly AI failure mode in hospitality.

The restaurant POS sector is splitting on this axis. 
Lavu's 2026 Restaurant POS AI Capabilities Report compared seven major platforms and found most restaurant AI on the market today is built on a single data source — leaving multi-unit operators with serious blind spots. The report found a market split into three tiers, with a wide gap between the top performer and the rest. "Everyone is building AI chat on top of their own POS data, and that is useful. But the problems that cost multi-unit operators the most money — the ones worth seven figures a year — are cross-system problems," said CEO Saleem S. Khatri.


On the hotel side, the warning is structural: 
"AI can't compensate for scattered data, disconnected systems or poor processes. If a hotel's underlying systems are fragmented, AI will only make operational problems more visible." AI is delivering where it's closest to daily operational pressures — guest messaging, digital check-in, revenue management, and support workflows — because AI removes repetitive work and helps stretched teams move faster.


Agentic Hospitality, a Louisville-based startup, launched the TravelOS MCP Server to address the hotel-side version of this problem: 
The TravelOS MCP Server is designed to function as an infrastructure layer that organizes structured hotel data for AI consumption, connecting directly to a hotel's central reservation system and PMS. The company also introduced the Agentic Booking Engine, an interface that enables conversational booking flows across websites and AI environments while routing transactions through the hotel's existing CRS. "Hotels have invested heavily in the systems that run their operations. Our approach is simple. We extend those systems into AI platforms rather than replacing them," said Brad Brewer, chief AI officer.


Sources: [GlobeNewswire/Lavu](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/03/03/3248661/0/en/lavu-publishes-2026-restaurant-pos-ai-capabilities-report-revealing-a-market-wide-gap-in-operational-intelligence-for-multi-unit-operators.html), [WiT/Abode Worldwide](https://www.webintravel.com/hospitality-tech-hit-a-billion-dollar-milestone-heres-where-the-smart-money-is-going/), [Hotel Management](https://www.hotelmanagement.net/tech/agentic-hospitality-launches-infrastructure-connect-hotels-ai-booking-platforms)

So what: The single most valuable infrastructure decision a multi-unit restaurant operator or mid-scale hotel group can make right now is a data-layer audit before buying another AI tool. Systems that unify POS, payroll, and scheduling data for restaurants, or CRS and PMS for hotels, are the prerequisite for any AI that claims to improve decisions. Vendors selling "AI-powered" insight on top of a single disconnected data source should be challenged to demonstrate cross-system payback.

## Funding & M&A

Investor conviction in hospitality tech as a category — not just individual companies — is now documented.


Forty global hospitality technology startups raised more than $1 billion between April 2025 and March 2026, with property management systems and AI-led platforms attracting the most investment, according to Abode Worldwide's Hospitality Tech Investment Index 2026. Three companies had the largest raises: Mews at $300 million; apartment operator Limehouse at €75 million; and home-swapping platform Kindred at $125 million. The activity illustrates "growing conviction among investors" regarding hospitality tech as a whole. PMS businesses are building broader platforms through development and acquisition, citing Mews' acquisitions of Flexkeeping and DataChat in late 2025.



The three biggest raises landed almost back-to-back in a 90-day burst between December 2025 and February 2026. PMS was the clear funding leader: seven PMS companies raised a combined $408.1 million.
 
AI-led guest experience platforms were another standout category, with Duve, Chatlyn, Conduit and Canary Technologies raising a combined $152.6 million. These platforms are targeting one of the industry's biggest pressures: the need to deliver fast, personalised service with leaner teams.



According to Canary Technologies' 2026 study, 71% of hospitality professionals say AI is having a significant or transformative impact on their industry, and 85% expect to allocate at least 5% of their IT budget to AI tools this year.
 
Mews achieved a $2.5 billion valuation in its January 2026 Series D, which at roughly 10x revenue represents the premium multiple that high-growth SaaS companies with strong retention metrics can command. Mews said the round followed a year in which it served 15,000 customers across 85 countries and processed $19.7 billion in platform transaction volume.


Sources: [Hotel Dive](https://www.hoteldive.com/news/hospitality-tech-attracts-1b-in-funding-pms-ai-leaders/817439/), [Abode Worldwide](https://abodeworldwide.com/hospitality-tech-investment-index/), [Levera Partners](https://www.leverapartners.com/blog/hospitality-software-ma/)

So what: PMS has become the fulcrum of the hospitality tech stack. Investors are backing the thesis that whoever owns the PMS layer controls data flow, integration relationships, and increasingly AI capabilities. For PE-backed hospitality SaaS, the M&A angle is clear: point solutions without a clear path to PMS adjacency will face pressure on valuation multiples as the larger platforms consolidate the stack.
