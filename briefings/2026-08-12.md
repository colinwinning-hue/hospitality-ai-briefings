## AI Visibility & Discovery

The most consequential shift in distribution since OTA dominance is compressing from promise to live product in the span of days.


**Google** began a limited U.S. test this week for agentic hotel booking, confirming the move to Skift. Booking Holdings CEO Glenn Fogel said on an earnings call: "I think Google may have come out today, a small percentage of their traffic seeing their agentic booking … we are one of the first with them."
 
Google's newly introduced Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP) could enable bookings to be completed directly inside a chat session. Major travel players — Booking, Expedia, Marriott, Wyndham, IHG — plus infrastructure providers Amadeus and Sabre are collaborating. While Google insists it has no ambition to become an OTA and is leaving servicing to partners, the infrastructure it is assembling positions it to power full end-to-end AI travel agents embedded in its flagship search engine.



On **August 10**, the gap between AI restaurant recommendation and AI restaurant booking closed. Yelp announced that ChatGPT users can now access Yelp Reservations and Waitlist to book a table or join a queue at thousands of restaurants across the US and Canada without leaving the chat. Resy, owned by American Express, launched Resy Reservations in ChatGPT for US restaurants the same week. OpenTable powers recommendations globally.
 
This is the first mass-market instance of an assistant completing a real-world transaction inside the conversation — the concrete thing the agentic commerce stack has been building toward.



**IHG Hotels & Resorts** launched a beta version of AI-powered conversational search across IHG.com and the IHG One Rewards mobile app, providing travelers a way to search using natural language. The feature allows users to describe destinations, trip purposes, preferred amenities and other preferences to receive tailored hotel recommendations, with real-time availability, pricing and options to book using cash, points or a combination of both.
 
The conversational search launch follows the recent introduction of an IHG app in ChatGPT and is part of the company's broader AI strategy. Future enhancements are expected to include agentic booking capabilities.
 Separately, 
**G6 Hospitality** introduced a Motel 6 plugin in ChatGPT, allowing travelers to discover Motel 6 properties through conversational prompts rather than traditional keyword searches.


Despite the speed of these moves, the actual booking volumes remain small. 
Booking Holdings CFO Ewout Steenbergen told analysts that LLM referral traffic represents less than 1% of total room nights. "That hasn't moved so much recently, so no material change over the last few months or quarters," he said. "At this moment, it's still very minimal and is not really moving so much."
 
Separately, CEO Glenn Fogel acknowledged that Google's AI Overviews are creating "pressure" on Booking Holdings' organic search results.
 Meanwhile, 
SparkToro's analysis of clickstream data found that 68% of U.S. Google searches now end without a click to any website — up from 60% just two years earlier. AI Overviews are driving most of that shift, and their presence alone cuts click-through rate by roughly 60%.


The hotel-side tension: 
Curacity and Cornell research shows 94% of hotels are invisible in AI search results. According to research from Hotel Technology News, over 86% of hoteliers now say they rely on AI for forecasting and demand analytics.
 The gap between those two numbers defines the commercial urgency.

Sources: [Skift — Google Agentic Hotel Booking](https://skift.com/2026/08/07/google-confirms-hotel-agentic-booking-is-now-in-testing/) · [PhocusWire — Booking Holdings Q2](https://www.phocuswire.com/news/finance/booking-holdings-q2-2026-earnings) · [Skift — AI Hotel Discovery Budget](https://skift.com/2026/07/31/ai-driven-travel-discovery-hotel-visibility/)

So what: Any hotel not appearing in AI-generated recommendation sets is effectively invisible at the moment of decision. The immediate action is not buying new tools — it is auditing structured data, reviews, and schema markup to ensure AI systems can confidently describe and recommend the property. The Google UCP test makes the timeline concrete: this is no longer a 2027 planning exercise.

## Vendor Moves

The biggest product week for restaurant tech in months, with two major moves breaking on the same day.


**Toast's** first AI-enabled workflow product — a digital marketing tool called Toast IQ Grow — is poised to become the company's fastest-growing product, with executives expecting it to generate $10 million in annual revenue since its May introduction, co-founder and CEO Aman Narang said on an August quarterly earnings call with analysts.
 
Over time, Narang said, Toast will deploy "agentic products" atop its software for various services a restaurant needs to operate, including demand forecasting, labor schedules, income tax help, food costs and availability. "That's an incredibly exciting future, especially in an industry known for slim margins and long hours," Narang said.
 
For the second quarter, Boston-based Toast reported net income of $154 million, nearly double the $80 million from the same period of 2025. Revenue rose 23% to $1.91 billion from the year-ago quarter.
 Toast also announced deals with BWH Hotels (Best Western parent) and Kung Fu Tea, and separately, 
an expanded integration with Google that brings agentic food ordering to Ask Maps — when a diner asks by voice or text for a restaurant recommendation, Ask Maps can now put a Toast restaurant's menu directly in front of them and carry that request through to a completed order.



**Toast Drive-Thru**, launched in April, is an enterprise-grade solution designed to help QSRs increase throughput and order accuracy at scale. Toast Drive-Thru also includes AI voice ordering integrations with partners like Incept AI to automate order taking, Speed of Service reporting to help identify lane bottlenecks, and Drive-Thru KDS enhancements designed to optimize kitchen assembly and load balancing.



**SevenRooms** now allows restaurants to tie DoorDash and Deliveroo customer profiles to in-person bookings, even if that person has never visited the restaurant before — a feature called Global Guest Search. InKind received $320 million in strategic financing from Liberty Mutual Investments, the insurance company's investment arm. This follows a $450 million funding round for InKind in February.



**TouchBistro** was sold to Harris Computer for a reportedly $100 million — a steep fall for a company once valued at $650 million. TouchBistro is used by more than 16,000 restaurants in more than 100 countries.



**American Express** is folding Tock into Resy this summer, doubling its venue library to over 25,000 restaurants. The merger brings Tock's prepaid booking model and tiered experience features directly into the Resy platform, creating a significantly larger single destination for diners seeking premium restaurant bookings.



**DoorDash** launched a new chatbot called Ask DoorDash in June, allowing customers to order food and make reservations with photos and prompts. DoorDash is in the middle of a massive investment cycle that includes revamping its tech platform after a string of acquisitions.
 
Early testing showed tangible results: nearly half of all restaurant orders placed through the tool were from a place the customer had never ordered from before. The company also said carts assembled through the chatbot ran more than 35% higher in value than a standard grocery order, with users completing them five times faster than through the regular app interface.


Sources: [Restaurant Dive — Toast AI into Dining](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/toast-aims-to-drive-ai-into-dining/827503/) · [NRN — Tech Tracker July](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology/tech-tracker-inkind-lands-another-big-investment) · [BusinessWire — Toast Drive-Thru](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260414359751/en/Toast-Launches-Unified-Drive-Thru-Solution-to-Modernize-Enterprise-Quick-Service-Restaurant-Operations)

So what: Toast's move from POS to "agentic operating system" is the clearest competitive repositioning in restaurant tech this cycle. The Toast/Google Maps integration means a restaurant's digital presence is now directly wired to the fastest-growing discovery surface. For operators still evaluating POS replacements, the platform that wins won't be the one with the best check-printing features — it'll be the one with the deepest AI agent layer.

## Data & Integration

The hotel industry's AI ambition is colliding with a forty-year-old architectural problem.


Every major hotel chain has now committed publicly to an agentic AI roadmap — Marriott's $1B+ agentic mesh, Hilton's AI Planner, Hyatt and Accor inside ChatGPT, IHG's AI-enhanced CRM. All of them are bottlenecked at the same layer: the property management system was built when a guest was a stay at a property, not a relationship across the chain.
 
The PMS is the single largest holder of guest signal, but rarely more than a fifth of the total. The composition varies by chain; full-service luxury and boutique brands typically have a smaller PMS share because spa, F&B, and concierge systems carry more of the signal.


Apptad's synthesis of industry surveys finds that 
fewer than one in four hotels report that their core systems — PMS, CRS, RMS, loyalty, CRM — are fully integrated in a way that supports a unified guest profile, with roughly half partially integrated and three in ten largely siloed.
 The consequence is concrete: 
identity-resolution platforms that work with hotel chains report a typical duplicate-profile rate of around 6%, with some estates running as high as 16%. Most of the duplication originates at check-in — placeholder email addresses, masked OTA-origin addresses, transliteration variants in international markets.
 Every AI use case sits on top of this problem.

On the restaurant side, 
the ChatGPT restaurant booking integrations via Resy, Yelp, and OpenTable rely on the Model Context Protocol. A reservation platform shipping the same capability into two different assistants (ChatGPT and Claude) in the same week is what a protocol looks like from the outside — not a bespoke one-off integration.
 The practical implication for operators: you inherit the ChatGPT booking channel by being on Resy, Yelp Guest Manager, or OpenTable. There is no separate ChatGPT listing to manage.

Sources: [Apptad — Hotel AI Problem in the PMS](https://apptad.com/insights/the-hotel-industrys-ai-problem-lives-in-the-pms/) · [explainx.ai — ChatGPT Restaurant Reservations](https://explainx.ai/blog/chatgpt-restaurant-reservations-opentable-resy-yelp-august-2026)

So what: For hotel groups mid-way through a PMS migration who are simultaneously commissioning AI concierge or personalisation tools — pause. The AI use case will fail at the data layer, not the model layer. Invest in identity resolution and a master guest entity before buying another AI vendor. For restaurants: being on the right reservation platform now controls your AI booking surface. Review which platforms are live in ChatGPT and ensure your listings are accurate and complete.

## Governance & Security

The EU AI Act became enforceable this week for hospitality operators.


Article 50 of the EU AI Act applies from 2 August 2026, with Commission guidelines adopted on 20 July 2026. Penalties run to fifteen million euros or 3% of worldwide annual turnover. Four things matter in practice: if a chatbot or voice agent talks to guests, people have to be told they are dealing with AI — that design duty sits with the provider (your vendor), so make it a procurement question and get the answer in writing. As the deployer, you carry the duty to inform anyone exposed to emotion recognition or biometric categorisation. Emotion recognition in the workplace has been prohibited outright since 2 February 2025, with only a narrow medical or safety exception.



As hospitality businesses increasingly rely on digital tools, automation, biometrics, and AI‑enabled services, their collection and use of personal data has expanded significantly. Hospitality operations frequently rely on franchisor‑provided systems or third‑party vendors for POS, reservation platforms, loyalty solutions, HR tools, and marketing technology. However, the data controller remains responsible for legal compliance — including when vendors or franchisors dictate the technology used.



Hospitality companies are accelerating adoption of AI — from dynamic pricing and marketing personalisation to fraud detection, digital key platforms, call-centre transcription, and biometric check-in. These deployments typically require massive volumes of customer and device data, and often rely on third-party vendors, offshore development teams, and cloud-hosted model pipelines. That combination creates a fast-emerging compliance risk under the DOJ's Data Security Program rules. Hospitality companies routinely touch at least four of the six categories of sensitive personal data — identifiers, geolocation, biometrics, and financial.


Sources: [Fox Rothschild — Hospitality Privacy & AI](https://dataprivacy.foxrothschild.com/2026/03/articles/general-privacy-data-security-news-developments/what-hospitality-hr-operations-and-leadership-need-to-know-about-data-privacy-and-ai/) · [Tableview — EU AI Act Restaurant](https://www.tableview.com/blog/ai-in-restaurants/) · [Hospitality Technology — DOJ Bulk Data](https://hospitalitytech.com/beyond-hipaa-navigating-dojs-new-bulk-data-thresholds-hospitality-ai)

So what: EU AI Act Article 50 is not theoretical — it is in force now. Any hospitality operator with a customer-facing AI chatbot or voice agent in Europe must be able to demonstrate transparent disclosure that guests are speaking to AI. That obligation flows to vendors who deploy the tech, but operators remain legally responsible. Run a quick audit: which of your AI-powered tools talks to guests, and does it clearly identify itself? If not, that is a procurement and contractual question to resolve this week.

## Operational AI

The NRA's headline number — 26% of restaurant operators using AI tools — hides a wide internal variance.


Marketing is the top area where AI tools are being used: 19% of full-service operators and 15% of limited-service operators said they use AI to assist with marketing. Ten percent of operators are using a version of the technology for at least some administrative tasks. While many chains are adopting or testing drive-thru voice automation, only 6% of restaurants are using AI for customer orders.



Fine dining locations used Toast IQ 29% more than fast-casual restaurants across 125,000 U.S.-based restaurant locations that adopted Toast IQ between January 1 and March 31, 2026. Top conversation categories: sales and revenue (47% of restaurants using the product), menu and inventory (34%), and guest and marketing (32%).
 This is the most granular operator-level AI usage data published this cycle, and it runs against the common assumption that AI adoption leads in QSR.


At the National Restaurant Association Show, operators warned against AI overdependence. "What people are getting wrong is that it's either going to be nothing or it's going to be everything," said one executive. Part of the problem is that overadoption of large language models rests on shaky fundamentals — the cost of AI tokens required to operate coding services can exceed the salaries of the workers the technology purports to replace.



Multilingual AI-powered hiring tools reduced Chipotle's application-to-start date from 12 days to 4, nearly doubled applicant flow, and pushed application completion rates from roughly 50% to nearly 85%.
 This is one of the cleanest operator-verified outcomes in restaurant AI this cycle.

Chili's announced investments in 23,000 iPads and 9,000 kitchen screens while publicly taking a selective approach to AI — signalling that hardware-led reliability remains the preference for casual dining. Starbucks pulled its AI inventory counting system, which employees had called "unreliable," reverting to traditional stock-keeping methods.

Sources: [Restaurant Dive — NRA Show](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/restaurant-technology-executives-warn-against-ai-overdependence/821024/) · [Toast — 90 Days with Toast IQ](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260610036506/en/90-Days-with-Toast-IQ-How-Restaurant-Operators-Are-Using-Toast-IQ-to-Find-Time-Protect-Margins-and-Grow) · [PYMNTS — Restaurants Can't Find Workers](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/restaurants-cannot-find-workers-ai-says-it-can/)

So what: The Starbucks AI inventory reversal is a useful signal for operators being sold AI-driven inventory tools: verify accuracy in production before committing. The Toast IQ data, by contrast, shows that fine dining — not QSR — is the segment most actively using AI for daily operational decisions. If you are a full-service or fine dining operator who hasn't explored AI-assisted daily operations reporting, your direct competitors likely have.
