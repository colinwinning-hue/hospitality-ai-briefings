## AI Visibility & Discovery

The biggest structural shift of this cycle landed in a 48-hour window: **Google** moved agentic hotel booking from roadmap to live product, and **OpenAI** closed the last-mile gap on restaurant reservations — in the same week.

**Google** confirmed to Skift (Aug 7) that it has begun a limited U.S. test of agentic hotel booking inside Search's AI Mode. 
Booking Holdings CEO Glenn Fogel referenced the tests on an earnings call, saying "I think Google may have come out today, a small percentage of their traffic seeing their agentic booking … we are one of the first with them."
 
Google has launched a limited U.S. test of agentic hotel booking within Search's AI Mode, with Booking Holdings among the first partners and Expedia, Marriott, Wyndham, and IHG also collaborating. Google's new Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP) could allow bookings to be completed directly inside a chat session — Amadeus is its first B2B foundational partner for lodging, with Sabre advising on the air side. Google maintains it does not intend to become an OTA and plans to leave servicing to industry experts, but its supplier ties and UCP infrastructure keep the door open to full end-to-end AI travel booking embedded in its flagship search engine.


**OpenAI** simultaneously closed the last-mile gap on restaurant reservations. 
OpenAI expanded ChatGPT by integrating direct restaurant reservations and waitlists through partnerships with Yelp, OpenTable, and Resy. Available across the U.S. and Canada, the feature lets users search for dining options and secure tables within their chat, though any post-booking modifications or cancellations must be managed directly on the respective provider's platform.
 
Reservations are the easy case — they move a record, not a payment. Deposits, prepaid tickets, and ticketed tasting menus need actual rails, which is where the Agentic Commerce Protocol (OpenAI and Stripe), Cloudflare Wallets, and Mastercard Agent Pay for Machines enter the picture.


**Booking Holdings** is already feeling the early effects of AI-mediated discovery. 
CEO Glenn Fogel said Tuesday that the company saw pressure on organic search results from Google's AI Overviews in the second quarter, although it is a small part of overall business. Google's AI Overviews are pressuring Booking's organic search (SEO) results, but the impact is limited since SEO is a small part of the business and direct traffic in the mid-60% range remains stable.
 Crucially, the company also put the first precise public number on the planning-to-booking gap: 
Booking Holdings processed 325 million room nights in the second quarter of 2026, a 5% increase from the same period last year. At below 1%, fewer than 3.25 million of those nights arrived via an AI referral.
 
An Expedia Group survey of more than 5,700 adults conducted earlier this year found that while 53% were comfortable letting AI suggest travel options, only 8% said they were comfortable completing a booking through an AI platform.


On the restaurant side, **DoorDash** has put numbers on the AI discovery opportunity. 
22% of consumers have used an AI tool like ChatGPT or Google Gemini to help choose a restaurant, making AI a new discovery signal to watch. According to Yext research, restaurant listing sites like DoorDash account for more than 41% of the sources AI tools cite when recommending restaurants. Operators are already responding by strengthening the basics, like updating menu information (39%), managing reviews (34%), and improving photo quality (32%).
 There is a stark operator-side lag: 
three-quarters of consumers say they're comfortable using AI for reservations, but only 28% of operators are using AI to manage calls and customer service. With an estimated 40% of reservation calls going unanswered industry-wide, AI gives restaurants another way to capture demand.


Sources: [Skift — Google Agentic Booking](https://skift.com/2026/08/07/google-confirms-hotel-agentic-booking-is-now-in-testing/), [Android Headlines — ChatGPT Restaurant Reservations](https://www.androidheadlines.com/2026/08/chatgpt-restaurant-reservations-yelp-opentable-resy.html), [Travelers Today — Booking Holdings AI Chatbot Data](https://www.travelerstoday.com/articles/60754/20260805/hotel-bookings-via-ai-chatbots-stay-below-1-booking-holdings-confirms.htm), [DoorDash 2026 Restaurant Industry Trends Report](https://about.doordash.com/en-us/news/doordash-restaurant-industry-trends-report-2026)

**So what:** The last-mile infrastructure for AI booking is being assembled in real time. Google's UCP is not yet OTA-grade, and ChatGPT reservations cannot modify or cancel — but the direction of travel is clear. Operators without clean, current digital listings will be invisible in the AI discovery layer before they realise it. For hotel brands: direct-booking moat now depends less on SEO and more on loyalty depth and LLM presence.

## Vendor Moves

This cycle's single most consequential structural story is the full reshaping of the restaurant reservation market into three vertically integrated ecosystems — each controlled by a payments or delivery platform.

**DoorDash / SevenRooms** integration has now matured past the acquisition announcement. 
"Delivery and dine-in have typically been siloed data sets," SevenRooms co-founder Joel Montaniel said. "So if a customer has ordered six times, and they're coming into the restaurant for the first time, are they a first-time customer or a seventh-time customer?"
 
DoorDash is looking to capture the range of dining possibilities, whether it's delivery, takeout or table. In the early months of its reservations integration, the platform was offering users DoorDash cash to use on future delivery orders for dining using the reservations feature.


**American Express / Resy / Tock** — Amex is folding Tock's 8,000+ venue inventory into Resy this summer, creating a 25,000-venue platform and sunsetting the Tock brand. 
In the three weeks following the Amex Platinum credit announcement, there was a 36% increase in Resy reservations made by users with a U.S. Platinum card linked to their account — and a five times increase in the daily average number of accounts being linked. People using the Resy credit are spending, on average, 25% more.
 
Amex's position has bolstered amid competition: DoorDash spent $1.2 billion to acquire reservations platform SevenRooms and Chase Sapphire linked up with OpenTable as part of its larger Visa partnership to offer exclusive restaurant bookings.


**Toast** had its busiest week in months. 
Toast's first AI-enabled workflow product – a digital marketing tool called Toast IQ Grow – is poised to become the company's fastest-growing product, with executives expecting it to generate $10 million in annual revenue since its May introduction.
 
Over time, Toast will deploy "agentic products" atop its software for various services a restaurant needs to operate, including demand forecasting, labor schedules, income tax help, food costs and availability. "That's an incredibly exciting future, especially in an industry known for slim margins and long hours," Narang said.
 The company also deferred BWH Hotels endorsement across ~4,300 properties, and co-developed with Google a Universal Commerce Protocol for Food enabling AI-driven ordering through Ask Maps — with Square also partnering on launch and Uber Eats to follow. 
American Express and Toast announced a strategic partnership combining the power of Resy and Tock's guestbook capabilities and Toast's Digital Chits technology, which allows restaurant staff to easily view important customer information directly on Toast Go handhelds and POS terminals during service.


**DoorDash's "Ask DoorDash"** AI chatbot (launched June 11) is posting early commercial results worth noting. 
Nearly half of all restaurant orders placed through the tool were from a place the customer had never ordered from before. The company also said carts assembled through the chatbot ran more than 35% higher in value than a standard grocery order, with users completing them five times faster than they would through the regular app interface. DoorDash co-founder Andy Fang said the product draws on AI models built by OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google, supplemented by open-source options.


**Google Maps' "Ask Maps"** is another new front. 
Google Maps will be able to order food, buy event tickets, book hotels, and give even more personalized recommendations. The food ordering feature will rely on partnerships with food industry payment processing platforms Square and Toast at first launch, and will later include Uber Eats.


Sources: [Fast Company — Amex Resy Tock](https://www.fastcompany.com/91496951/amex-resy-tock-restaurant-reservation-wars), [Restaurant Dive — Toast AI](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/toast-aims-to-drive-ai-into-dining/827503/), [PYMNTS — Toast Q2](https://www.pymnts.com/earnings/2026/toast-adds-record-9500-restaurants-while-betting-big-on-ai/)

**So what:** The restaurant reservation market has become a credit card and delivery platform war. The underlying AI capability is now secondary to which ecosystem owns the guest data and the wallet. For independent operators, the strategic question is no longer which reservation system is best — it is which data relationship they are willing to hand over, and to whom.

## Operational AI

Agentic AI has crossed from marketing language into measurable restaurant P&L. The Toast IQ Grow case is the clearest signal this cycle.


Toast IQ Grow, the company's AI-powered marketing product, has become the fastest-growing offering in Toast's history. The company illustrated that strategy with an example of a Louisiana restaurant, Spirits Food & Friends, that consolidated multiple business systems onto Toast before adopting Toast IQ Grow. According to management, the restaurant reduced its outside marketing costs by 70% while generating more than $100,000 in marketing-attributed sales in less than two months.


**Toast** is explicit that IQ Grow is the on-ramp to a broader agentic stack: 
at the center of the launch is an AI-powered Marketing Agent that can build audiences and automate campaigns across channels. For operators, the appeal is practical. Marketing often falls to owners and managers who are already responsible for staffing, service, inventory, vendor relationships and guest satisfaction. Campaign planning, audience building, offer creation and follow-up can be difficult to execute consistently, especially for independent restaurants and emerging groups.



Fine dining locations used Toast IQ 29% more than fast-casual restaurants. Top categories: Operators most frequently initiated conversations about sales and revenue (47% of restaurants using the product), menu and inventory (34%), and guest and marketing (32%). The top prompt: "Create a short, easy-to-read daily briefing for my restaurant."


On the hotel side, **Booking Holdings** internal AI deployment is further ahead than its public-facing agentic product. 
As of Q2 2026, Booking Holdings said it had extended voice AI across the majority of eligible inbound traveler calls, with customer-service cost per booking declining at a double-digit rate while satisfaction scores held steady. The company is also building AI-powered tools that help accommodation providers respond to guests, manage property content, and present inventory more effectively.


Sources: [Restaurant Dive — Toast AI (Aug 10)](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/toast-aims-to-drive-ai-into-dining/827503/), [BusinessWire — Toast IQ 90 Days](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260610036506/en/)

**So what:** The $100K-in-two-months attribution figure from Spirits Food & Friends is the kind of outcome data that moves mid-market operators. Watch whether Toast can replicate it at scale and publish aggregate data — that's the difference between a compelling case study and a genuinely measurable product.

## Governance & Security

HITEC 2026 in San Antonio marked the moment the hotel industry shifted from "deploy more AI" to "govern what you've already deployed." Two new software categories emerged from the show floor with enough traction to name.

**The Agent Management Platform (AMP)** is now the most discussed new category in hotel tech. 
The question is no more about chatbots, virtual concierge and one-off tools, but how hotels manage, govern, orchestrate and extract value from dozens and eventually hundreds of AI-powered workflows operating simultaneously across the enterprise. In short, AI agents are infrastructure, and fast movers can reap big rewards.
 
Today, a hotel may have one workflow using a frontier model, another powered by a small language model (SLM) and a third embedded within a vendor platform. Each agent accesses different data, operates under different permissions and incurs different costs. The AMP becomes the control center that governs permissions, monitoring, security, performance and cost. Token costs are about to become a very real part of the tech stack opex budget.



Today, many organizations treat AI costs as negligible. In three years, finance teams will be scrutinizing token consumption the same way they currently analyze labor costs or OTA commissions. Hotels will increasingly arbitrage tasks amongst different models based on performance, speed and cost. Simple guest requests may use inexpensive SLMs while high-value commercial decisions are escalated to more sophisticated systems.


**The Guest Success Management System (GSMS)** is a second emerging category: 
looking back on the week, the biggest takeaway is that hospitality technology is entering its second phase of AI adoption centered around accessible orchestration tools that can manage an entire ecosystem of agents, harmonized data and workflows. The winners will be those with the best governance, the cleanest data foundations and the clearest understanding of where human judgment still creates value. As hotels deploy AI across revenue management, marketing, guest communications and operations, the challenge is no longer whether to use AI but how to manage it responsibly and efficiently. Organizations that can govern permissions, monitor costs, maintain audit trails and control data access across dozens or hundreds of AI agents will gain a significant operational advantage over those that simply deploy the most tools.


The EU AI Act is adding urgency to governance timelines. 
EU AI Act transparency obligations are set out in Article 50; those chatbot transparency requirements apply from 2 August 2026. High-risk AI obligations under the Act apply from August 2026 to Annex III systems. California's Automated Decisionmaking Technology access and opt-out rights take effect January 1, 2027.


Sources: [HospitalityNet — HITEC 2026 Agentic Governance (Aug 3)](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/opinion/4133415/hitec-2026-was-the-show-where-agentic-governance-reached-centerstage), [Hotel Mogel — HITEC Floor Report](https://hotelmogel.com/agentic-governance-is-the-big-trend-from-hitec-2026/)

**So what:** Any hotel group running more than a handful of AI-powered workflows without a governance layer is accumulating liability: regulatory exposure under the EU AI Act, financial opacity on token costs, and security gaps where agent permissions have never been reviewed. The AMP category is nascent but the business case for it is not.

## Data & Integration

The PMS-as-data-foundation thesis is now the organising logic for hotel AI investment — and the funding market is confirming it.


The thing limiting hospitality AI in 2026 isn't model quality. It's the property management system underneath it. Legacy PMS platforms have limited APIs, inconsistent schemas, and maintenance-heavy direct integrations. Bolt enough AI tools onto that foundation and you get duplicate guest records, broken workflows, and what the industry has started calling integration debt.



Every major hotel chain has now committed publicly to an agentic AI roadmap — Marriott's $1B+ agentic mesh, Hilton's AI Planner, Hyatt and Accor inside ChatGPT, IHG's AI-enhanced CRM. All of them are bottlenecked at the same layer: the property management system was built when a guest was a stay at a property, not a relationship across the chain.


On the restaurant side, the cross-system data gap is equally acute. 
Lavu Inc. published its 2026 Restaurant POS AI Capabilities Report, a feature-by-feature comparison of artificial intelligence capabilities across seven of the most widely deployed point-of-sale platforms. The report found that most restaurant AI on the market today is built on a single data source — leaving multi-unit operators with serious blind spots. "Everyone is building AI chat on top of their own POS data, and that is useful. But the problems that cost multi-unit operators the most money — the ones worth seven figures a year — are cross-system problems. You will not find them inside any single platform," said Saleem S. Khatri, CEO, Lavu Inc.


Sources: [Apptad — Hotel AI Problem Lives in PMS](https://apptad.com/insights/the-hotel-industrys-ai-problem-lives-in-the-pms/), [Lavu 2026 POS AI Capabilities Report](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/05/lavu-publishes-restaurant-pos-ai-capabilities-report-finding-most-platforms-lack-cross-system-intelligence/)

**So what:** AI quality is a data layer problem before it is a model problem. For operators evaluating PMS migration or POS consolidation, the right question is not "does this platform have AI?" but "can my AI agents query across all the data this system touches, in real time?"

## Funding & M&A

The prior funding cycle set the conditions for this one. 
Forty global hospitality technology startups raised more than $1 billion in the past year, with property management systems and artificial intelligence-led platforms attracting the most investment, according to Abode Worldwide's Hospitality Tech Investment Index 2026. The three companies that had the largest raises included property management software Mews at $300 million; apartment operator Limehouse at €75 million; and home-swapping platform Kindred at $125 million across two funding rounds.



AI-led guest experience platforms were also a "standout" investment, with Duve, Chatlyn, Conduit and Canary Technologies raising a combined $152.6 million. Canary notably acquired OpenKey in February to expand its access to door lock providers. All four of these companies worked to solve the challenge of providing "personalized, responsive service" to an industry facing labor shortages.


Q2 2026 on the M&A front was quieter on volume but significant on direction. 
Only two Series B rounds stand out for the quarter from April to the end of June: Hotel technology company Smartness landed €47 million, and The Hosteller, an India-based hostel company, announced $16 million.
 
Mergers & acquisitions in travel have created more of a buzz in Q2 than most funding stories. Expedia Group's acquisition of CarTrawler is a stand out example. Meanwhile, Lighthouse acquired Hotelrank.ai in the quarter as part of its effort to build out its hotel operating system.


Sources: [Hotel Dive — $1B Hospitality Tech Funding](https://www.hoteldive.com/news/hospitality-tech-attracts-1b-in-funding-pms-ai-leaders/817439/), [PhocusWire — Q2 2026 Startup Funding](https://www.phocuswire.com/news/startups/startup-funding-mergers-acquisition-q2-2026)

**So what:** PMS and AI guest experience are where capital is concentrating — not voice robots or robot delivery. Lighthouse's Hotelrank.ai acquisition is notable: it signals that commercial intelligence platforms are moving toward AI search visibility as a core product capability, not an add-on.
