## AI Visibility & Discovery

The agentic booking transaction — not just discovery — is now live across hotels and restaurants in the United States simultaneously, closing a gap that has been theorised for two years.


**Google** fully launched its agentic hotel booking tool inside AI Mode on August 27, 2026. Consumers in the U.S. can now find, compare, select, and pay for hotels without leaving the chat interface, using Google Pay for payment while the hotel or OTA remains the merchant of record handling the charge, confirmation, changes, and support.
 
Launch partners include Booking.com, Choice Hotels, Expedia, Hilton, Hotels.com, IHG, Marriott, Priceline, Trip.com, and Wyndham.
 Critically, 
Google shares only the minimum data needed to complete a booking — name, payment, occupancy, property — withholding broader chat context, inspiration, and decision signals even in aggregate.
 The hotel or OTA gets the transaction; Google keeps the intent data. This is the structural risk operators should not miss.


The feature, called Ask Maps, now returns real prices and availability rather than ranges or estimates, and is designed to handle complex natural-language queries — for example, finding a hotel with a particular atmosphere, within walking distance of a gym and restaurants, for a conference in a given city centre. Amadeus is named as the technical partner for the hotel sector, alongside Sabre on the airline side.



The traffic loss is real and documented, while the shift in bookings remains, for now, a hypothesis the available figures do not yet confirm. Booking Holdings CFO Ewout Steenbergen said in Q2 2026 that bookings coming from conversational agents account for less than 1% of the group's room-nights, with no significant recent change in that share.
 
Booking Holdings CEO Glenn Fogel acknowledged that Google's AI Overviews put pressure on organic search (SEO) results in Q2, though direct traffic in the mid-60% range has not gone down. Fogel said he doesn't like "losing SEO" and that "no travel business has found the silver bullet yet in how to show up prominently on a consistent basis within the major LLMs."


**On the restaurant side**, 
August 10 saw OpenTable, Resy (owned by American Express), and Yelp simultaneously launch in-chat restaurant reservations and waitlists inside ChatGPT — completing the booking inside the conversation rather than handing off to a browser. Resy's version surfaces real-time reservation availability directly beneath each venue in the recommendation list, with in-conversation refinement by time, party size, cuisine, or price.
 
For Yelp, the strategic logic is blunt: Yelp depends on Google for more than 70% of its US web traffic. Every booking originating inside ChatGPT is a booking that did not require a Google SERP.



**Toast** separately announced on August 6 an expanded integration with Google that brings agentic food ordering to Ask Maps. When a diner asks by voice or text for a restaurant recommendation, Ask Maps can now surface a Toast restaurant's menu and carry the request all the way through to a completed order.
 
This experience is powered by an ongoing partnership between Toast and Google. Toast is a co-developer of the Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP) for Food, an open standard defining how AI agents discover, order from, and check out with restaurants across surfaces like Search, Maps, and Gemini.


**Sources:** [Skift — Google's Agentic Hotel Booking Tool Comes to AI Mode](https://skift.com/2026/08/27/googles-agentic-hotel-booking-tool-comes-to-ai-mode/) · [Hospitality ON — Google Launches AI Agent Hotel Booking Test](https://hospitality-on.com/en/distribution/google-launches-ai-agent-hotel-booking-test-united-states) · [ExplainX — ChatGPT Books Restaurant Tables](https://explainx.ai/blog/chatgpt-restaurant-reservations-opentable-resy-yelp-august-2026) · [Skift — Booking Holdings CEO: AI Overviews Are Squeezing SEO](https://skift.com/2026/08/04/booking-holdings-saw-pressure-from-googles-ai-overviews/)

**So what:** Every restaurant and hotel that is not listed on a bookable platform with live inventory is now structurally invisible at the transaction layer, regardless of website quality. The question is no longer "can AI help guests find us?" — it is "who owns the guest relationship when the agent completes the booking?" Google answered that it intends to keep the intent data and leave servicing to partners. That is not neutral for distribution strategy.

## Vendor Moves

This was an unusually dense cycle for watchlist names making concrete product or platform moves.


**DoorDash** launched "Ask DoorDash," an AI chatbot that allows users to search the app in their own words instead of scrolling to build a cart. Users can describe what they want ("filling dinner for a family of 4"), share a recipe link, or describe a reservation need, and the chatbot surfaces personalized options. Once a restaurant is selected, Ask DoorDash can build a cart based on dietary preferences, budget, group size, or past orders.
 
DoorDash is in the middle of a massive investment cycle, overhauling its unified tech platform following its $1.2 billion acquisition of SevenRooms and the nearly $4 billion acquisition of Deliveroo. Finance chief Ravi Inukonda told investors that DoorDash plans to carry out most of its tech stack spending this year.



**SevenRooms/DoorDash vs. OpenTable vs. Resy/Tock** reached an active competitive peak on the ground. One Boston restaurateur reported being offered six-figure signing bonuses to switch platforms. Officially, DoorDash's new product is called "DoorDash Reservations Powered by SevenRooms," though local restaurant booking pages display SevenRooms branding. American Express in 2024 paid $400 million for Tock, which is rolling into the Resy platform this summer, doubling Resy's venue library to over 25,000 restaurants.
 
A Resy partnership with point-of-sale provider Toast is underway, and OpenTable's spring user agreement update required that subscribers agree it is their primary platform.
 A Boston operator summed up the stakes: "It's less about the reservations, and it's all about the data."


**Mews** launched five new products at Unfold 2026, completing what it describes as an AI-native operating system that unifies PMS, RMS, messaging, automations, and accounts receivable on a single data model. In a 2026 Mews survey of 500 hoteliers across five countries, the most-cited outcome hoteliers want from new AI investment is not more AI — it's their existing systems working better together.
 
Mews founder Richard Valtr stated: "The hotel industry has been running on a category map drawn forty years ago. PMS, RMS, CRS, CRM, POS — these aren't real things. They're acronyms invented by software companies to sell more software. Inside Mews, those categories don't exist as separate systems."


**Sources:** [TechCrunch — DoorDash's New AI Chatbot](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/11/doordashs-new-ai-chatbot-lets-you-order-with-prompts-and-photos/) · [Boston Globe — Reservation Platforms Vying for Restaurants](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/08/17/lifestyle/reservation-platforms-sevenrooms-opentable-resy/) · [Hospitality Net — Mews Unveils the Operating System for Hospitality](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4132634/mews-unveils-the-operating-system-for-hospitality)

**So what:** The reservation platform wars are fundamentally a data-ownership dispute. Platform consolidation (Amex owning Resy+Tock, DoorDash owning SevenRooms+Deliveroo, Booking Holdings owning OpenTable) means every booking now feeds proprietary AI training and loyalty ecosystems. An independent restaurant choosing a platform today is making a 3–5 year commitment about whose guest relationship intelligence their customers belong to.

## Operational AI

The clearest signal this cycle is that AI is moving from experimentation to quantified operational use — at scale and with operator-side data attached.


**Toast** is working to capture more business from time-strapped customers by using AI tools to assume more of a restaurateur's workflows. Toast IQ Grow — a digital marketing tool launched in May — is poised to become the company's fastest-growing product, with executives expecting it to generate $10 million in annual revenue.
 
Over time, CEO Aman Narang said, Toast will deploy "agentic products" atop its software for demand forecasting, labor schedules, income tax help, food costs, and availability. "That's an incredibly exciting future, especially in an industry known for slim margins and long hours," Narang said.
 The Q2 report was concrete: 
Toast reported net income of $154 million, nearly double the $80 million from the same period of 2025, with revenue rising 23% to $1.91 billion. Toast added a record 9,500 new locations in Q2.


Underlying real usage data: 
Toast data from Q1 2026 shows that among 125,000 US restaurant locations using Toast IQ, 47% asked about sales and revenue, 34% about menu and inventory, 32% about guests and marketing, and 29% about operations and reporting. The most-used prompt was a request for a short, easy-to-read daily briefing.
 That is signal, not positioning — operators are using AI to ask about their actual businesses.


**Crunchtime** announced four new AI capabilities across its restaurant operations management suite: Voice-Based Inventory (allowing managers to count inventory 3–4x faster with AI-powered speech-to-text), Photo Intelligence (automatically verifying store execution at scale with AI-powered photo review), AI Actions (identifying non-compliant execution by analysing thousands of form submissions), and AI Analyst (turning complex data into plain-English answers through an LLM-powered natural language interface). Brian Herr, Sr. VP of Product, stated: "Restaurant operators aren't struggling with a lack of data, they are struggling with what to do with it."


Also noted from the RTN sidebar: **Palona AI** expanded beyond voice ordering with a new restaurant operations platform and $20 million in funding — a watch item, not yet fully confirmed with attribution.


**RoboOp365** (a subsidiary of Nightfood Holdings, OTCQB: NGTF) held a dealer demo on August 27 in Irwindale, California, for its Robby One automated cooking robot targeting the cookline — described as the last manual station in the commercial kitchen, where warewashing, holding, refrigeration, and prep have already seen meaningful automation. The company operates under a Robot-as-a-Service model rather than capital purchase.
 [NOTE: This is a micro-cap OTC company in early commercialisation — treat as market signal rather than enterprise-grade deployment news.]

**Sources:** [Restaurant Dive — Toast Aims to Drive AI into Dining](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/toast-aims-to-drive-ai-into-dining/827503/) · [RTN — Crunchtime Expands AI Capabilities](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/04/crunchtime-expands-ai-capabilities-across-its-restaurant-operations-management-suite/) · [GlobeNewswire — RoboOp365 Demo Robby One](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/08/26/3351338/)

**So what:** Toast's real usage data from 125,000 locations is the most credible pulse reading available on what restaurant AI is actually doing in 2026. Operators are using it primarily for business intelligence queries, not agentic automation. The Crunchtime product suite — voice inventory, photo compliance verification — represents the more modest, higher-ROI archetype that is actually being adopted at scale vs. the robotic kitchen narrative.

## Funding & M&A


Forty global hospitality technology startups raised more than $1 billion in the past year, with PMS and AI-led platforms attracting the most investment, according to Abode Worldwide's Hospitality Tech Investment Index 2026. The three largest raises were Mews at $300 million, Kindred at $125 million, and Limehome at €75 million. AI-led guest experience platforms Duve, Chatlyn, Conduit, and Canary Technologies raised a combined $152.6 million. Canary notably acquired OpenKey in February to expand its access to door lock providers.



Seven PMS companies raised a combined $408.1 million — more than any other category. The best-funded PMS businesses are using this capital to build broader platforms through product development and acquisition rather than relying on third-party integrations. Jessica Gillingham, CEO of Abode Worldwide, said: "The companies attracting the strongest investor backing share a common trait — they sit close to essential operator workflows and become more valuable over time. Unified systems generate more data, better automation, and higher switching costs."


**Sources:** [Hotel Dive — Hospitality Tech Attracts $1B in Funding](https://www.hoteldive.com/news/hospitality-tech-attracts-1b-in-funding-pms-ai-leaders/817439/)

**So what:** Capital is concentrating in the PMS layer because investors understand that whoever owns the data model underneath AI owns the durable competitive position. For PE-backed hospitality SaaS, the investment thesis has shifted from feature differentiation to data network effects — which is why Mews is acquiring analytics tools and Canary is acquiring door locks.

## Governance & Security


Following the White House's June 2, 2026 Executive Order titled "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security," hospitality consultant Terence Ronson (Pertlink Limited) translated its federal-facing language into hotel operational priorities. The Order recognises AI as a national strategic capability and extends AI-enabled cybersecurity services to operators of critical infrastructure — operators not dissimilar in profile from a large connected resort: locally significant, economically load-bearing, and historically under-resourced on security.



On the regulatory pricing front, the UK CMA opened an investigation in February 2026 against three hotel chains and a shared pricing analytics tool suspected of exchanging competitively sensitive information through the same algorithm — a so-called "hub-and-spoke" pattern. Separately, the EU AI Act's high-risk system obligations are taking effect in August 2026, which strictly regulates algorithmic discrimination in pricing.



Hospitality's specific cyber threats in 2026 include AI-crafted phishing that closely mimics internal communication styles, deepfake executive fraud ("imagine a voice message that sounds convincingly like your GM asking to approve a wire transfer"), and ransomware targeting property management systems, door locks, HVAC controls, and bookings integrations.


**Sources:** [Hospitality Net — When National AI Security Becomes a Hospitality Imperative](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/opinion/4132771/when-national-ai-security-becomes-a-hospitality-imperative) · [Rield — AI Revenue Management Technical Guide](https://rield-rm.com/en/ai-machine-learning-revenue-management/)

**So what:** The UK CMA pricing collusion investigation is the most underreported story in hotel tech right now. Any hotel group using a shared third-party RMS alongside competitors in the same geographic market should have legal counsel review the hub-and-spoke exposure immediately. The EU AI Act's August 2026 high-risk system obligations apply to dynamic pricing engines — US hoteliers with European operations need to audit their pricing stack now.

## WHO TO WATCH

**Aman Narang (Toast CEO)** — His Q2 earnings framing — that AI allows Toast to take on more of a restaurant's work (marketing, payroll, bookkeeping) and that agentic products will cover "demand forecasting, labor schedules, income tax help, food costs" — is the most ambitious commercial AI roadmap stated by a publicly listed restaurant tech company this cycle. The Google/UCP co-development position makes him central to whether agentic ordering becomes an industry standard or a Toast-proprietary channel.

**Glenn Fogel (Booking Holdings CEO)** — His public admission that Google's AI Overviews are squeezing SEO, combined with his confirmation that Booking is a launch partner for Google's agentic hotel booking, captures the central tension of 2026 distribution: OTAs are simultaneously threatened by and dependent on the platforms that are taking their organic traffic. His "no travel business has found the silver bullet" comment is the most candid exec statement of the cycle.

**Richard Valtr (Mews Founder)** — His "hospitality's iPhone moment" framing for the Unfold 2026 product suite launch is either the cycle's sharpest positioning statement or its most aggressive overreach, depending on whether the IDC-validated ROI numbers survive contact with multi-property operators at scale. Worth watching whether Mews can maintain data quality claims as it scales beyond 15,000 properties.

**Pablo Rivero (CEO, Resy and Tock)** — Now running both Resy and Tock under American Express, Rivero is managing the most structurally interesting portfolio in restaurant reservations: a flat-rate marketplace with Chase/Visa incentives, a prepaid fine-dining model, a Toast POS integration, and now ChatGPT in-chat booking. How he prices the combined platform post-Tock merger will set the competitive anchor point for the entire sector.

**Terence Ronson (Pertlink Limited)** — His white paper translating the White House's June 2026 AI Executive Order into hotel operational priorities is the clearest framework available for hospitality leaders on AI governance and security exposure. His observation that "the more intelligent hotels become, the more strategically exposed they also become" is the most practically urgent framing in an industry that has largely treated cybersecurity as a compliance footnote rather than a brand-risk issue.
