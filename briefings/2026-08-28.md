## AI VISIBILITY & DISCOVERY

The conversational interface has become the new booking channel — for restaurants first, hotels second, with Google closing the loop this week.

**Google** delivered the most significant distribution event of this news cycle in two moves. First, on 6 August, 
Google Maps' Ask Maps feature gained agentic capabilities including the ability to order food, book hotels, and find event tickets. For food ordering, users can ask for a specific dish and the tool surfaces nearby restaurants; after selecting one, they tap "Order online" to place an order through Square, Toast, or Uber Eats.
 Then, on 27 August, 
AI Mode can now track flight prices, book hotels, and quote fares in airline miles or hotel points. The feature moves AI Mode from a place where you research a trip to a place where you arrange one, with booking happening inside the conversation, the transaction handed to a partner and paid through Google Pay.
 
Launch partners include Booking.com, Choice Hotels, Expedia, Hilton, Hotels.com, IHG, Marriott, Priceline, Trip.com, and Wyndham.
 
Consumers can chat to find and compare rooms, make a selection, review cancellation terms, and then pay with Google Pay all in one place. The hotel or OTA still charges the card, sends the confirmation, and offers support.
 
AI Mode passed one billion monthly users within a year of launch and Google says queries have more than doubled every quarter since.


**OpenAI** shipped restaurant reservations into ChatGPT on 10 August. 
Between 10 and 14 August, OpenAI shipped a reservation search that checks OpenTable, Resy and Yelp for an open table. The feature turns a natural-language request into a live availability search across three booking platforms and hands the user into the partner's flow to confirm.
 
OpenAI added restaurant reservation booking to ChatGPT through integrations with OpenTable, Resy, and Yelp, rolling out across all plans on mobile, web, and desktop as of August 10.
 
The system leverages real-time availability from restaurants connected to Yelp Guest Manager, OpenTable, and Resy to secure spots instantly. According to Morning Consult research cited by Yelp, nearly two-thirds of respondents (65%) say being able to take direct action — such as making a reservation — is essential when using AI tools for local discovery.
 Crucially, 
changes and cancellations happen back on Yelp, OpenTable, or Resy — the ChatGPT interface is not managing the reservation post-booking.


On the restaurant GEO pressure building underneath these integrations: 
BrightEdge found restaurants went from a 10% AI Overview trigger rate in early 2025 to 78% by February 2026 — one of the fastest-growing verticals they track.
 
The average restaurant brand appears in Google's local 3-Pack 24.3% of the time but is recommended by ChatGPT only 5.3% of the time.
 
SOCi's 2026 LVI reports that AI-recommended local businesses average 4.3 stars on ChatGPT, 4.1 on Perplexity, and 3.9 on Gemini — meaning the rating threshold to be cited at all is structural, not optional. SOCi also found AI is 3–30x more selective than traditional local search: only ~1.2% of locations are recommended by ChatGPT vs. 35.9% appearing in Google's local 3-pack.


Sources: [Skift — Google AI Mode Hotel Booking](https://skift.com/2026/08/27/googles-agentic-hotel-booking-tool-comes-to-ai-mode/) | [TechCrunch — Google Maps Agentic](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/06/google-maps-adds-agentic-features-including-food-ordering-and-hotel-bookings/) | [Progressive Robot — ChatGPT Reservations](https://www.progressiverobot.com/2026/08/15/chatgpt-update-quiz-reservations-google-drive/)

**So what:** The restaurant discovery battle has already shifted to AI surfaces; hotel booking is following in real time. Any operator without complete, structured, machine-readable data (clean GBP, schema markup, current rates) is invisible to the largest intent pools in consumer software. The priority action is data hygiene, not AI feature adoption.

## VENDOR MOVES

The reservation and delivery stack is consolidating rapidly around a small number of platform owners, each racing to own the conversational interface between diner and table.

**DoorDash** had the most active news cycle of any watchlist name. 
DoorDash reported Q2 revenue of $4.45 billion, up 36% year over year, while Marketplace Gross Order Value climbed 36% to $33.1 billion and total orders increased 27% to 970 million. Adjusted EBITDA rose 40% to $914 million, exceeding expectations.
 
The company highlighted the rollout of its global technology platform and new AI-powered features aimed at improving personalization, merchant onboarding and delivery routing. It also launched "Ask," an AI assistant designed to help consumers discover restaurants and build grocery baskets more efficiently.
 Separately, in July, 
DoorDash CTO Andy Fang unveiled dd-cli, a command-line interface tool that allows AI agents to autonomously search restaurants, compare deals, and complete checkout on DoorDash without any manual input — processing real payments rather than simulated transactions. A public demonstration showed Anthropic's Claude completing a full end-to-end DoorDash order autonomously.
 
Earlier in 2026, CEO Tony Xu outlined plans to consolidate DoorDash, Deliveroo, and Wolt onto a single unified technology platform. dd-cli extends that logic outward, positioning DoorDash's ordering network as a service layer that third-party developers and AI systems can plug into.


**American Express** announced a proposed acquisition of **TheFork** from Tripadvisor for $700 million. 
Founded in 2007, TheFork provides restaurants with reservation management, guest engagement and operational tools. The proposed transaction is expected to close before the end of 2026, subject to labor consultation and customary conditions, including regulatory approvals.
 
For American Express, the acquisition will significantly expand its dining footprint, increasing its network to around 75,000 bookable restaurants. It follows American Express' earlier acquisitions of dining platforms Resy and Tock.
 
It would give Amex a reservations trifecta with Resy and Tock, which are set to merge this summer. Together, the booking services would encompass 75,000 venues.


**Toast** reported a strong Q2. 
Toast's first AI-enabled workflow product — Toast IQ Grow — is poised to become the company's fastest-growing product, with executives expecting it to generate $10 million in annual revenue since its May introduction, according to CEO Aman Narang.
 
Narang added that initial success strengthens management's confidence that AI can increase the amount customers spend on the platform. The company illustrated that strategy with a Louisiana restaurant, Spirits Food & Friends, that reduced its outside marketing costs by 70% while generating more than $100,000 in marketing-attributed sales in less than two months.
 
Toast also expanded its partnership with Google to power agentic food ordering through Ask Maps. The integration connects AI agents directly to restaurant ordering systems that run on Toast. Toast is a co-developer of the Universal Commerce Protocol for Food, an open standard designed to govern how AI agents interact with restaurant commerce online.
 A separate Amex-Toast partnership was also formalised. 
The companies plan to build tools for restaurants combining the power of Resy and Tock's guestbook capabilities and Toast's Digital Chits technology, which allows restaurant staff to view important customer information directly on Toast handhelds during service.


**Radisson Hotel Group** launched a ChatGPT app built with Accenture. 
Available as @RadissonHotels in ChatGPT, the app enables travelers to search for Radisson Hotels properties across over 100 countries through natural conversation. Travelers can ask for a family-friendly weekend stay in Amsterdam, or a hotel in Paris with a gym and spa near the Eiffel Tower. The app responds with relevant properties with live inventory and rates.
 
According to Accenture's Consumer Pulse Research, 87% of travelers are open to collaborating with an AI-powered travel agent to find the best option, and 71% of travelers say that at least half of their spend for hotels or airlines will be influenced by AI over the next 12 months.


The **reservation wars** on the restaurant side are producing unusual commercial behaviour. 
One Boston restaurateur reported they've been offered six-figure signing bonuses to switch platforms. After purchasing Resy in 2019, American Express in 2024 paid $400 million for Tock, which will roll into the Resy platform this summer. Last year, DoorDash bought SevenRooms for $1.2 billion.
 The Boston Globe's reporting surfaces the real prize: 
"It's less about the reservations, and it's all about the data," said Pajo Bruich, president of Umami Riot Experience. "When I look at the DoorDash move, that's what I see. I see them trying to get access to more data."


Sources: [Yahoo Finance — DoorDash Q2](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/doordash-revenue-jumps-36-q2-205651199.html) | [Restaurant Industry — AmEx/TheFork](https://restaurantindustry.co.uk/2026/08/14/american-express-announces-proposed-acquisition-of-thefork-a-leading-european-restaurant-booking-platform/) | [Restaurant Dive — Toast AI](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/toast-aims-to-drive-ai-into-dining/827503/) | [Boston Globe — Reservation Wars](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/08/17/lifestyle/reservation-platforms-sevenrooms-opentable-resy/)

**So what:** Payment networks (Amex) and logistics platforms (DoorDash) are buying their way into restaurant guest data. For mid-market restaurant groups, the platform you sign with is now a data-sharing decision as much as a technology one. Operators should audit what guest data leaves their premises through each reservation channel before signing any switching incentive.

## FUNDING & M&A


Hospitality technology startups raised more than $1 billion across 40 companies between April 2025 and March 2026, with property management systems and AI-led platforms attracting the largest share of investment. That shift was most visible in a 90-day burst between December 2025 and February 2026, when the sector's three biggest raises landed almost back-to-back: Mews ($300 million), Kindred ($125 million across two simultaneous rounds) and Limehome (€75 million).


**Mews** led all raises at $300M (Series D). 
PMS businesses are building broader platforms through development and acquisition. Mews' acquisitions of Flexkeeping and DataChat in late 2025 are examples of this strategy.
 The firm's founder framed the direction: "Instead of relying on a 'Frankenstack' of disconnected tools that need constant manual input, properties are moving toward AI agents that can proactively coordinate revenue, operations, and guest service in real time" (Richard Valtr, Mews Founder).


Four AI-powered guest experience platforms raised a combined $152.6 million: Duve, Chatlyn, Conduit, and Canary Technologies (the largest at $80 million, which also acquired mobile key platform OpenKey in February 2026). What connects all four is the problem they're solving: hospitality teams are expected to deliver responsive, personalized service with fewer staff than ever before.


In Q2 2026 specifically, 
three disclosed rounds closed, all of them AI-native by description. Smartness closed the largest, a €47 million Series B for its agentic AI platform for hotels and short-term rentals. Pricepoint raised a $6.6 million seed for AI revenue management. Besty AI raised a $3.75 million seed for its AI automation tool on the STR side.
 On the acquisition side, 
Smartness started in 2020 as Smartpricing, a dynamic pricing tool for hotels, and has since expanded into a broad platform spanning pricing, guest communications, CRM, payments, and property management. Its €47 million Series B is set partly toward acquisitions across Europe.
 
Lighthouse, a hospitality intelligence platform, acquired Hotelrank, a tool that measures where hotels show up across AI platforms, from a startup founded in 2025.


Sources: [Abode Worldwide — Investment Index](https://abodeworldwide.com/hospitality-tech-investment-index/) | [Abode Worldwide — Q2 2026 Roundup](https://abodeworldwide.com/hospitality-tech-funding-q2-2026/) | [Hotel Dive — $1B Funding](https://www.hoteldive.com/news/hospitality-tech-attracts-1b-in-funding-pms-ai-leaders/817439/)

**So what:** The PMS is becoming the control layer of the entire hospitality tech stack — not just booking. PE-backed SaaS operators should map current integrations against PMS consolidation risk: as Mews and peers absorb point solutions, standalone tools will face harder renewal conversations. Lighthouse acquiring AI-visibility measurement is a signal that tracking LLM citations is becoming table stakes for hotel intelligence platforms.

## OPERATIONAL AI

Restaurant AI adoption is broader but shallower than vendor marketing implies — and the data is now precise enough to act on.


According to the National Restaurant Association's 2026 State of the Restaurant Industry report, 26% of restaurant operators now use AI-related tools in their operations. While marketing gets the headlines, the fastest payback is in the back of house.
 
Marketing is where operators feel it most: 19% of full-service and 15% of limited-service operators use it in their marketing work. Only 6% use it for customer orders.
 The adoption gap between back-of-house and front-of-house is the clearest signal in the data: automation of invoicing, payroll and scheduling is proving out; customer-facing AI is still a narrow bet.


Taco Bell, through its partnership with voice-AI company Omilia, now runs drive-thru voice AI at more than 890 U.S. locations across 38 states. Wendy's has expanded its Google Cloud-built FreshAI from a two-state pilot toward a systemwide rollout.
 
Domino's Pizza, working with Microsoft, reported a 72% improvement in demand planning accuracy after implementing AI-driven forecasting.
 Toast's Q2 case study for IQ Grow — 
a restaurant that reduced outside marketing costs by 70% while generating more than $100,000 in marketing-attributed sales in less than two months
 — is the strongest operator-attributable outcome in this news cycle.

On the hotel side, 
in 2026, more than half (58%) of hoteliers will devote upwards of 10% of their IT budget to AI. The top priorities driving increased hotel tech investment are improving the guest experience (52%), increasing efficiencies (52%), increasing revenue (51%) and reducing costs (45%).
 
49% of guests say their hotel experience is improved when AI provides personalized recommendations, and AI-driven dynamic pricing can lead to a 10% increase in RevPAR.


Sources: [Restaurantware — Kitchen Ops Guide](https://www.restaurantware.com/blogs/kitchen-and-cooking-tips/how_ai_is_transforming_commercial_kitchen_operations) | [Restaurant Dive — Toast AI](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/toast-aims-to-drive-ai-into-dining/827503/)

**So what:** The gap between 26% of operators using AI and vendors claiming 80%+ lies in survey framing — show operators a list including invoice automation and the number jumps. Operators evaluating AI should separate: (1) back-office tools with established ROI (invoicing, scheduling, forecasting) from (2) customer-facing tools with thin evidence (AI phone, voice ordering). Start with category 1 and measure before expanding.

## GOVERNANCE & SECURITY


On June 2, 2026, the White House issued an Executive Order titled "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security." Read carefully, it is one of the clearest signals yet that artificial intelligence has crossed a threshold — from an experimental productivity tool to a critical layer of operational infrastructure that governments now treat as a matter of national security. For hospitality, the relevance is not theoretical. The modern hotel has quietly become a dense, interconnected intelligent environment — binding together identity, payments, guest data, building systems, IoT, energy orchestration, and AI-driven workflows.



The Order tasks the Treasury with forming an AI cybersecurity clearinghouse — a voluntary collaboration with the AI industry and infrastructure operators to coordinate vulnerability scanning, validation, and patch distribution. It also extends AI-enabled cybersecurity tooling to operators of critical infrastructure.
 Terence Ronson (Pertlink) translates the EO for hotel operators: 
"The smartest move available to any hotel, vendor or integrator today is unglamorous: know where your AI already lives, govern it deliberately, and make trust a feature you can prove."


On EU compliance, 
the EU AI Act Article 50 transparency requirements for chatbots apply from 2 August 2026. Hospitality operators face simultaneous pressures: proving AI control to regulators and boards, preventing brand and legal exposure, and defending a technical attack surface that traditional security tools weren't built to defend against.



The average cost of a data breach in hospitality was $3.86 million in 2024. Malicious actors are increasingly using artificial intelligence to craft sophisticated, contextually relevant messages that closely mimic internal communication styles — unlike the "urgent invoice" emails, today's AI tools can make these messages extremely targeted with appropriate tone.


Sources: [Hospitality Net — White House EO Analysis](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/opinion/4132771/when-national-ai-security-becomes-a-hospitality-imperative) | [Hotel Business — Cybersecurity Predictions](https://hotelbusiness.com/the-year-ahead-2026-cybersecurity-predictions-for-the-hotel-industry/)

**So what:** EU chatbot transparency obligations went live August 2. Any hotel or restaurant deploying an AI chat interface that doesn't disclose it is an AI — including third-party tools embedded via vendor contracts — is now in violation. Run a vendor audit before the next procurement cycle; the controller (the operator) bears responsibility regardless of who supplied the tool.
