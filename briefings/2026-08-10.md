## AI Visibility & Discovery

Google executed two simultaneous moves this week that shift the industry from theory to test.

**Google** confirmed to Skift on August 7 that 
agentic hotel booking is now live in a limited U.S. test inside Search's AI Mode, with Booking Holdings among the first partners and Expedia, Marriott, Wyndham, and IHG also collaborating. Booking Holdings CEO Glenn Fogel referenced the tests on the company's Q2 earnings call: "I think Google may have come out today, a small percentage of their traffic seeing their agentic booking … we are one of the first with them."
 The underlying infrastructure is Google's Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP): 
UCP could allow bookings to be completed directly inside a chat session — Amadeus is its first B2B foundational partner for lodging, with Sabre advising on the air side. Google maintains it does not intend to become an OTA and plans to leave servicing to industry experts, but its supplier ties and UCP infrastructure keep the door open to full end-to-end AI travel booking embedded in its flagship search engine.


**Google Maps** moved on a parallel track, announcing on August 6 that 
it will now be able to order food, buy event tickets, and book hotels. The food ordering feature will rely on partnerships with Square and Toast at first launch, and will later include Uber Eats. For hotels, users can ask for a list that fits specific requirements — price range, location, amenities, or "vibes" — and Ask Maps will check availability and give a rundown of options.
 Skift separately reported that 
Google Maps has brought AI hotel search into a surface already built around location, transit, routes, and nearby businesses — a traveller can chat with Ask Maps to ask for a hotel with a specific feel or proximity to other venues, and get actual pricing and availability, not ranges or estimates.


**Booking Holdings** supplied the most concrete data point of the week on where AI booking actually stands. 
The company processed 325 million room nights in Q2 2026, a 5% increase year over year. At below 1%, fewer than 3.25 million of those nights arrived via an AI referral — a concrete number that puts a figure on the gap between how much travelers use AI to plan trips and how many actually book through one.
 Separately, 
Booking Holdings said it had extended voice AI across the majority of eligible inbound traveler calls as of Q2, with customer-service cost per booking declining at a double-digit rate while satisfaction scores held steady.
 
The company is accelerating its AI strategy across customer service, AI discovery, and LLM visibility, while raising its transformation-program savings target to $650 million in annual run-rate savings by 2027.



Booking CEO Glenn Fogel noted that Google's AI Overviews weighed on organic search results during Q2. Despite the SEO pressure, Booking's direct traffic remains steady in the mid-60% range, backed by massive paid search investments.


The structural problem this creates for independent hotels was articulated sharply by Hospitality.today. 
On April 28, Booking Holdings CEO Fogel told analysts the company was working closely with OpenAI and Google — named explicitly — to ensure Booking's supply is discoverable wherever travelers begin their journey. Three weeks later, Expedia Group CEO Ariane Gorin said the pullback in AI checkout reinforced Expedia's view that AI can be a powerful discovery layer, but that actual booking and servicing is best handled by a trusted, scaled provider — and that if the market evolves toward paid advertising at the discovery layer, that's a space Expedia knows well.
 The editorial conclusion: 
a traveler who finds an independent property through ChatGPT, verifies through Google, lands on an OTA listing, and books there — that traveler was discovered through infrastructure the hotel invested in and converted through infrastructure the OTA owns. The hotel paid for the top of the funnel. The OTA collected at the bottom. Same as before. Different pipe.


Sources: [Skift — Google Confirms Agentic Booking Test](https://skift.com/2026/08/07/google-confirms-hotel-agentic-booking-is-now-in-testing/) · [Gizmodo — Google Maps Agentic](https://gizmodo.com/google-maps-adds-agentic-feature-that-orders-food-and-books-hotels-for-you-2000795579) · [TravelersToday — Booking Holdings AI Data](https://www.travelerstoday.com/articles/60754/20260805/hotel-bookings-via-ai-chatbots-stay-below-1-booking-holdings-confirms.htm) · [Hospitality.today — OTAs Harvesting Discovery Layer](https://www.hospitality.today/article/hotels-are-building-the-ai-discovery-layer-otas-are-harvesting-it)

**So what:** Hotels that invest in AI eligibility infrastructure (structured data, MCP connectors) without a parallel investment in direct booking conversion are funding OTA visibility at the bottom of the funnel. The Google agentic test is live now — any property that is machine-unreadable (fragmented PMS data, incomplete Google Business Profile, no structured schema) is already out of the first test pool.

## Vendor Moves

The reservation platform war accelerated on multiple fronts this week.

**DoorDash / SevenRooms** launched Channel Connect, 
a desktop app that aggregates reservations from various booking channels into a single system, while also automatically updating a restaurant's inventory across those channels. SevenRooms said Channel Connect will free operators from having to use multiple tablets to manually reconcile double bookings.
 The competitive context: 
this includes reservations from "major booking platforms," which presumably means chief rivals OpenTable and Resy. Channel Connect accesses that data "through mechanisms those platforms already provide, using credentials and the data the restaurant controls." The move comes after OpenTable began requiring restaurant clients to make it their system of record and make all inventory available on OpenTable — a policy described as protecting restaurant data from "bad actors."
 SevenRooms co-founder Allison Page posted an aggressive LinkedIn diatribe against OpenTable's terms change; industry sources described it as unusually sharp for a DoorDash-owned property. The broader landscape: 
DoorDash is now competing directly with Booking Holdings' OpenTable, and AmEx-owned Resy and Tock, for control of reservation data. As of mid-year, DoorDash held about 67% of U.S. delivery market share and is bundling reservations with its DashPass subscription, offering exclusive tables at trendy spots for subscribers.


**Toast** reported Q2 2026 earnings on August 4. 
CEO Aman Narang said: "In Q2, recurring gross profit streams grew 28%, GAAP Operating Income margins expanded to 26%, and we added a record 9,500 net locations. Toast IQ Grow is the fastest-growing new offering we've ever launched, and it's a clear signal of how we can use AI to transform what Toast can do for customers."
 Toast IQ Grow is an AI marketing agent: 
the Spring 2026 product release included more than 20 updates across marketing, payroll, inventory and operations, with Toast IQ Grow designed to take on the day-to-day work of marketing a business.
 
Toast also launched Toast Drive-Thru in 2026 as a unified solution for enterprise quick-service operations, bringing together hardware, POS-native software, AI voice ordering integrations and services — including POS Drive-Thru Mode, vehicle ID and lane tagging, and Speed of Service reporting.


**Agentic Hospitality** (Louisville, Ky.) launched its TravelOS MCP Server + ChatGPT app earlier this cycle with a direct-brand architecture: 
the TravelOS MCP Server connects directly to a hotel's CRS and PMS to ensure that all availability, pricing, and inventory originate from the system of record. Every guest interaction — from initial inquiry to booking intent — is retained and passed downstream into hotel systems.
 Brad Brewer, chief AI officer, said: 
"We are the only company capturing the full conversation and delivering it into the PMS. That context is what turns a transaction into hospitality. Preferences, questions, and intent are the data elements hotels need to personalize experiences and build loyalty in an AI-first world."


Sources: [Restaurant Business / Expedite — SevenRooms Channel Connect](https://www.expedite.news/p/sevenrooms-latest-release-helps-restaurants) · [Las Vegas Sun — Toast Q2 Earnings](https://lasvegassun.com/news/2026/aug/04/toast-announces-second-quarter-2026-financial-resu/) · [RTN — Toast Platform Deep Dive](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/06/toast-advances-restaurant-operations-with-connected-pos-ai-and-guest-engagement-technology/) · [Hospitality Net — Agentic Hospitality TravelOS](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4132086/agentic-hospitalitys-travelos-mcp-chatgpt-app-is-putting-brands-not-intermediaries-at-the-center-of-ai-bookings)

**So what:** The reservation platform war is now a data war. DoorDash and AmEx are building competing closed-loop ecosystems (delivery + dine-in + reservations versus card + loyalty + global venue access). Restaurants sitting on both OpenTable and SevenRooms face a genuine choice about which data relationship they want to prioritise — and neither answer is neutral.

## Funding & M&A

**American Express** announced the largest hospitality tech deal of the year. 
Under the terms of the proposal, American Express will acquire TheFork from Tripadvisor for $700 million in cash, subject to regulatory approvals. TheFork connects millions of diners with more than 50,000 restaurants across 11 European countries. The proposed acquisition builds on Amex's broader dining strategy and its successful acquisitions of Resy and Tock. Together, the three platforms are expected to expand American Express' dining network to 75,000 bookable venues.
 The strategic rationale: 
reservation platforms now sit at the centre of a growing ecosystem that connects payments, loyalty, dining experiences and customer acquisition. By adding TheFork to Resy and Tock, American Express is making a significant statement about where it believes value will be created in hospitality over the coming decade.
 TheFork's financials going into the deal: 
revenue increased from $181 million in 2024 to $221 million in 2025, representing growth of 22%. Total bookings rose approximately 8% during 2025. Adjusted EBITDA increased from $5 million in 2024 to $21 million in 2025.


**Atoms** (the entity created from CloudKitchens) raised $1.7 billion to bring CloudKitchens, restaurant software and robotics under one roof, according to an August 2026 report in Restaurant Technology News. [RTN — Atoms $1.7B raise, August 2026] The raise signals continued institutional conviction in the ghost kitchen + automation stack despite mixed operator outcomes for automated fulfillment to date.

Sources: [American Express Newsroom — TheFork Acquisition](https://www.americanexpress.com/en-us/newsroom/articles/travel-and-dining/american-express-announces-proposed-acquisition-of-thefork--a-le.html) · [RTN — Atoms Funding](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/08/atoms-raises-1-7-billion-to-bring-cloudkitchens-restaurant-software-and-robotics-under-one-roof/) · [Skift — Why Amex Bought TheFork](https://skift.com/2026/06/16/why-amex-spent-700-million-on-thefork-and-whats-next-for-tripadvisor/)

**So what:** The AmEx dining stack (Resy + Tock + TheFork = ~75,000 venues across North America and Europe) is now the most complete premium-dining data asset in the world, all running through a single payment rails owner. For PE-backed restaurant SaaS companies in Europe, this is both threat (Amex can offer reservation software plus demand generation) and opportunity (integrations with that network become a sales wedge). For Tripadvisor, selling TheFork refocuses the company on Viator (experiences) — the experiences-as-content play is now its primary identity.

## Operational AI

The restaurant sector delivered signal and noise in roughly equal measure this week.

**Toast IQ Grow** is the clearest operator-validated signal: 
based on Q1 2026 data from 125,000+ restaurant locations, the most common AI use cases are sales and revenue analysis (47%), menu and inventory management (34%), guest and marketing (32%), and operations and reporting (29%). In Q1 2026 alone, more than 179,000 users across 125,000+ locations actively used Toast IQ in 90 days — with operators saying "please" or "thank you" to the AI assistant 33,000 times.


**Chili's** announced it is investing in 23,000 iPads and 9,000 kitchen screens while "taking a selective approach to AI," per an August 2026 RTN report. [RTN — Chili's, August 2026] The framing — major hardware investment combined with stated skepticism about AI features — is the most honest positioning statement from a large chain this cycle. Hardware-first, AI-cautious is a distinct strategy from the majority of QSR AI announcements.

**Starbucks** pulled the plug on its AI inventory system, according to a widely cited RTN viewpoint piece from this week. [RTN — Starbucks AI Inventory, August 2026] No operator quote or specific failure metric was published, but the reversal is significant: Starbucks had previously cited its Deep Brew platform as a model for back-of-house AI. The plug-pull implies an implementation failure, not a technology failure — exactly the pattern seen in McDonald's drive-thru AI and Wendy's ordering automation, where system-level bottlenecks swallowed individual feature gains.

**MAJC**, a Miami-based startup that launched in February 2026, is targeting the labor problem directly: 
the platform combines AI-powered job-matching, training and community tools as an integrated workforce engine for hospitality. The numbers behind the industry's labor challenge: average annual restaurant turnover topped 75%, with quick-service restaurants regularly exceeding 130%; 80% of restaurants were short at least one position in 2025, the average operator carried five open roles (up from 3.8), and average training cost per hire was $3,037. "By combining AI with the power of an expert community and learning academy, MAJC is democratizing access to software and systems that help operators attract, develop and retain great talent," said the company's CPO.


Sources: [Toast Blog — AI in Restaurants](https://pos.toasttab.com/blog/on-the-line/ai-for-restaurants) · [RTN — Chili's Selective AI](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/08/chilis-invests-in-23000-ipads-and-9000-kitchen-screens-as-it-takes-a-selective-approach-to-ai/) · [RTN — Starbucks AI Inventory Pullback](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/08/why-starbucks-was-right-to-pull-the-plug-on-its-ai-inventory-system/) · [PYMNTS — MAJC](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/restaurants-cannot-find-workers-ai-says-it-can/)

**So what:** The Starbucks reversal is the most instructive operator signal of the briefing period — a sophisticated, well-resourced chain could not make a specific AI deployment work at scale. For operators evaluating AI inventory tools, the key due-diligence question is now: can this vendor show a live multi-unit deployment with before/after cost-of-goods data, or only a demo environment?

## Governance & Security

The White House issued an Executive Order on June 2 titled "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security." Terence Ronson of Pertlink Limited translated its implications for hospitality in a detailed Hospitality Net white paper. The key reframe: 
AI capability is now treated as national strategic capability. For hospitality, the competitive question is shifting from "How many AI features do we have?" to "Can we govern AI responsibly, secure our connected systems, and turn operational trust into guest experience?"
 The practical risk vector: 
the modern hotel has quietly become a dense, interconnected intelligent environment — binding together identity, payments, guest data, building systems, IoT, energy orchestration, and AI-driven workflows. The more intelligent a property becomes, the more strategically exposed it becomes.
 The governance warning: 
the average cost of a data breach in hospitality was $3.86 million in 2024, and the landscape is accelerating toward AI-driven phishing, deepfake executive fraud, and autonomous vulnerability exploitation.


Separately, the DOJ's Data Security Program (DSP) is creating a new compliance frontier for AI deployments specifically: 
hospitality companies are accelerating adoption of AI — from dynamic pricing and marketing personalization to fraud detection, digital key platforms, call-center transcription, and biometric check-in — that typically require massive volumes of customer and device data, often relying on third-party vendors, offshore development teams, and cloud-hosted model pipelines. Hospitality companies routinely touch at least four DSP sensitive data categories — identifiers, geolocation, biometrics, and financial data.


Sources: [Hospitality Net — AI Security White Paper](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/opinion/4132771/when-national-ai-security-becomes-a-hospitality-imperative) · [Hospitality Technology — DOJ DSP for Hospitality AI](https://hospitalitytech.com/beyond-hipaa-navigating-dojs-new-bulk-data-thresholds-hospitality-ai)

**So what:** The compliance question is no longer hypothetical. Any hotel or restaurant group deploying AI with offshore vendor access to guest PII, biometric check-in, or call-transcription data should be conducting a DSP exposure assessment now. The Ronson white paper is the clearest operational-level translation of the federal order available in the trade press — read it before the next vendor RFP.

## WHO TO WATCH

**Glenn Fogel (Booking Holdings CEO)** — His Q2 earnings call was unusually information-dense: he named Google and OpenAI as explicit AI partners, quantified AI referrals at sub-1% of room nights (the first major OTA to do so at scale), and acknowledged SEO pressure from AI Overviews in the same breath as announcing Booking is among Google's first agentic booking partners. The combination of candid data disclosure and active platform positioning makes him the most analytically useful public executive in hospitality distribution right now.

**Aman Narang (Toast CEO)** — Toast IQ Grow described as "the fastest-growing new offering we've ever launched" in an earnings call is a strong signal if it holds in Q3 data. His framing of voice as "another opportunity" for drive-thru positions the next product move clearly. The Toast Drive-Thru + IQ Grow combination — if AI voice ordering integrations ship on schedule — would make Toast the first POS platform to close the loop from kitchen to lane to marketing campaign entirely within one system.

**Allison Page (SevenRooms Co-founder & CPO, DoorDash)** — Her aggressive LinkedIn post against OpenTable's new TOS — and the Channel Connect launch it presaged — is the clearest indication that DoorDash intends to fight for multi-platform access rather than accept a walled-garden reservation dynamic. The outcome of the OpenTable TOS dispute will define whether restaurant data portability survives the consolidation wave.

**Brad Brewer (Founder & Chief AI Officer, Agentic Hospitality)** — The architecture of TravelOS MCP matters more than the company's current scale. If MCP becomes the standard protocol for hotel AI distribution (it is rapidly doing so), Brewer's claim that aggregator-style AI platforms "recreate OTA dynamics" and his direct-brand alternative is the most precisely argued counterposition in hotel distribution. Worth tracking whether Amadeus and the named PMS partners validate the live-inventory claim independently.

**Terence Ronson (Pertlink Limited)** — The White House AI Executive Order white paper he published in June is the most operationally grounded translation of regulatory risk into hotel-specific action steps available in the trade press. His framing — "know where your AI already lives, govern it deliberately, and make trust a feature you can prove" — is precisely the brief a hotel GM should hand to their CIO before next year's vendor RFP cycle.
