## AI Visibility & Discovery

The restaurant discovery stack is fracturing along a fault line that most operators haven't noticed yet: traditional local SEO and AI-mediated search are now separate games with different rules, and the majority of the industry is losing the second one.


**Uberall** released *Fast Food, Faster Discovery: The 2026 GEO Playbook for Multi-Location QSRs* — the industry's first benchmark report measuring how ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Copilot, and Google AI Overviews recommend restaurants. Its central finding: as consumer restaurant discovery rapidly shifts from traditional search to AI assistants, the majority of QSR locations are effectively absent from AI-generated recommendations. The report introduces Location Performance Optimization (LPO) as the strategic framework multi-location brands need to stay visible across both traditional and AI-mediated search, connecting SEO and Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) into a single operating model.



The findings are stark. AI restaurant discovery is research-heavy, not transactional — informational and comparative prompts like "what's the healthiest breakfast I can grab on the go" or "which coffee chain has the best mobile rewards program" drive nearly 79% of AI-generated restaurant responses. Brands must win preference before the moment of decision, not at the point of sale. AI platforms have also raised the bar on reviews: ChatGPT primarily recommends businesses averaging 4.3 stars or higher, Perplexity 4.1+, and Gemini 3.9+.



The gap between Google presence and AI presence is not explained by brands being offline. Uberall found that 86% of those same invisible restaurants maintain some presence on Google — they have done the traditional local-SEO work (claimed listings, hours, photos) and still vanish the moment the question is routed through an AI model.



**DoorDash**'s 2026 Restaurant Industry Trends Report (3,000+ U.S. consumers, 500+ operators) echoes the discovery shift: 22% of consumers have now used an AI tool like ChatGPT or Google Gemini to choose a restaurant. According to Yext research, restaurant listing sites like DoorDash account for more than 41% of the sources AI tools cite when recommending restaurants. Three-quarters of consumers say they're comfortable using AI for reservations, but only 28% of operators are using AI to manage calls and customer service — with an estimated 40% of reservation calls going unanswered industry-wide.



**Resy** has responded to this discovery shift with a live integration with Anthropic's Claude, launched April 23, 2026. Claude users can explore U.S. Resy restaurants directly in conversations when deciding where to eat, check real-time availability, and complete a booking — all while staying within the Claude environment. According to Reputation.com data cited by Resy, 20% of diners already use AI to look for venue information, a number that jumps to 40% among Gen Z.


Sources: [Uberall / BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260507962493/en/83-of-Restaurants-Are-Invisible-in-AI-Search-New-Uberall-Report-Reveals-the-Discovery-Gap-Reshaping-the-Quick-Service-Restaurant-Industry) · [DoorDash / QSR Magazine](https://www.qsrmagazine.com/news/ai-is-reshaping-how-diners-discover-restaurants-doordash-study-finds/) · [Resy Newsroom](https://blog.resy.com/newsroom/resy-restaurants-claude-anthropic/)

So what: If 83% of your locations are invisible in AI search despite active Google listings, your marketing budget is protecting a channel that's declining while leaving the growing one undefended. Audit AI citation share before Q4, not after it.

## Vendor Moves

The restaurant reservations market is undergoing the deepest structural reorganization in its history, with ecosystem ownership — not product features — now the primary competitive variable.


**SevenRooms** (DoorDash-owned) launched **Channel Connect** on June 17, 2026 — a free, open-license desktop app that aggregates reservations from all booking channels (OpenTable, Resy, AI agents, hotel concierges, social media, search engines) into a single system, while automatically syncing inventory across all those channels. The company is offering it under an open license available to any restaurant, even those that don't use SevenRooms, positioning it explicitly as an industry utility rather than a growth tool. The launch came directly after OpenTable in April began requiring restaurant clients to make it their system of record and make all inventory available on OpenTable — Channel Connect's blog response states plainly: "Channel Connect was built so that no single platform has to be the gatekeeper to your operations."
 CEO Joel Montaniel framed it as bringing restaurants up to speed with hotels and airlines, which have managed multi-channel booking inventory this way for years.


**American Express** is merging Tock into Resy this summer, creating a 25,000-venue platform in a direct challenge to OpenTable's 60,000-venue network. The move follows an aggressive card-credit strategy: in the three weeks after Amex raised its Platinum card fee and added a $400 Resy dining credit, Resy reservations by Platinum card users rose 36%, and in Q4 2025, spend by Amex card members at Resy restaurants was up more than 20% while overall restaurant spend was up 9%.



The ownership map now looks like this: American Express owns both Resy and Tock (acquired for $400 million in 2024); DoorDash acquired SevenRooms for $1.2 billion in 2025; OpenTable remains part of Booking Holdings and independent of both. OpenTable's U.S. market share slipped from 51% to 46% between 2022 and 2024, though it still leads in 13 of the 15 top U.S. cities.



American Express is also folding Tock into Resy this summer, doubling its venue library to over 25,000 restaurants. That merger brings Tock's prepaid booking model and tiered experience features directly into the Resy platform, with Tock's prepaid model offering operators a meaningful tool to reduce no-shows through upfront payment.


OpenTable on July 9 gave top-tier loyalty members exclusive access to its hardest-to-get reservations, a move that deepens its diner-network advantage even as it faces structural pressure from both Resy-Amex and SevenRooms-DoorDash.

Sources: [Nation's Restaurant News](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology/sevenrooms-launches-a-reservations-aggregator-for-restaurants) · [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91496951/amex-resy-tock-restaurant-reservation-wars)

So what: Choosing a reservation platform is now choosing an ecosystem. DoorDash offers delivery integration + agentic discovery data; Amex offers high-spend cardholder flow + cultural events; Booking Holdings offers OTA-level scale. Negotiate with all three — switching costs have become the commodity incumbents trade on.

## Operational AI

AI is moving from analytics layer to execution layer across restaurant and hotel operations, with the most credible launches this cycle tied to concrete platform integrations rather than standalone tools.


**Toast** launched **Toast Drive-Thru** on April 14, 2026, an enterprise-grade unified solution covering hardware, POS-native software, AI voice ordering integrations, and end-to-end support. There are just over 140,000 total drive-thru locations across the U.S. restaurant industry, and the growth of AI voice ordering solutions has left many brands struggling to integrate new technologies into old, fractured systems burdened by disconnected vendors for POS, menu boards, and audio systems. Toast Drive-Thru replaces those fragmented systems with integrations with AI voice ordering partners including Incept AI, with additional partners to follow in 2026.



Toast also launched **Toast IQ Grow** as part of its Spring 2026 product release, a marketing-focused AI solution described as taking on "the day-to-day work of marketing a business." At the center of the launch is an AI-powered Marketing Agent that can build audiences and automate campaigns across channels, supported by a reimagined Toast Local app. Toast described the release as covering more than 20 updates across marketing, payroll, inventory, and operations.



The broader Toast IQ platform draws on real-time and historical data from approximately 148,000 customer locations and goes beyond answering questions — proactively surfacing insights and executing tasks directly within Toast. Steve Fredette, President and Co-Founder of Toast, has described the problem it addresses: "Operators have access to a mountain of data that can help them make" better decisions, but often don't have the time or training to interpret and act on it.


On the hotel side: 
**Booking Holdings** Q1 2026 earnings revealed concrete AI returns across its portfolio. Agoda found that its AI-assisted automation drove a double-digit year-over-year reduction in customer service costs per booking. OpenTable's AI Concierge, launched July 2025, answered 80% of diners' questions, reducing the need for phone calls to restaurants. Priceline's AI travel agent Penny showed "a noticeable uplift from users who engage with Penny compared to non-Penny users" in early testing. CEO Glenn Fogel confirmed partnerships with OpenAI, Google, Anthropic, and Amazon to position Booking "wherever that journey begins."



**Mews** at its Unfold 2026 event launched five new products completing what it describes as the first AI-native operating system for hospitality on a single data model. The embedded Mews RMS runs 150 million pricing calculations a day and executes rate changes against live demand without manual intervention, lifting total revenue per square meter by 13.7% on average against hotels without an RMS, according to the company.
 [IDC research cited by Mews at launch claims a 476% three-year ROI for properties on the full platform — treat as vendor-sourced and cross-reference before presenting to board.]

Sources: [BusinessWire / Toast](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260414359751/en/Toast-Launches-Unified-Drive-Thru-Solution-to-Modernize-Enterprise-Quick-Service-Restaurant-Operations) · [Restaurant Technology News](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/06/toast-advances-restaurant-operations-with-connected-pos-ai-and-guest-engagement-technology/) · [PYMNTS / Booking Holdings](https://www.pymnts.com/earnings/2026/booking-holdings-ai-assistants-slash-costs-and-boost-bookings/)

So what: Toast's drive-thru play is its clearest signal yet that it is moving up-market from indie restaurants to enterprise QSR. If you're a multi-unit QSR brand in contract renewal discussions with legacy drive-thru vendors, Toast Drive-Thru is now a genuine competitive alternative — but the AI voice integrations are still partner-dependent, not native.

## Data & Integration

The architectural debate in hotel technology has sharpened: is the right model a unified PMS suite that expands across all functions, or a best-of-breed stack connected by open APIs and MCP servers — and is the PMS itself becoming redundant as commerce moves outside the property?


Markus Busch (hospitality.today) published a pointed analysis arguing that the PMS era is ending because the focus has moved away from property-level operations and toward external commerce. PMS vendors are acquiring revenue management systems, channel managers, payment processors, and booking engines, pitching consolidation as the answer — but the problem is speed: the new protocols ship monthly, and a unified PMS suite ships on the annual release cycle of its largest module. Notably: Sabre, written off for years as legacy infrastructure, shipped first-to-market agentic APIs and an MCP server in September 2025, then published the industry's first Agentic Blueprint three months later.



In the past few months, Stripe, Visa, Mastercard, AWS, OpenAI, and Google have all launched payment infrastructure specifically for AI agents. Amazon's announcement named hotel bookings as a primary use case. The rails of the agentic economy are being laid right now, by the largest payment and technology companies on earth — and PMS-bundled payment products were built for a different world, human guests handing over cards.



**Google** confirmed it is developing agentic hotel and flight booking capabilities in AI Mode, two weeks after launching agentic restaurant reservation booking. Google is working with Booking.com, Expedia, Marriott International, IHG Hotels & Resorts, Choice Hotels International, and Wyndham Hotels & Resorts. Google VP Julie Farago told PhocusWire: "We're not going to rush this out the door because we want to make sure that it's a seamless experience and that people have all the control that they need and expect." Google has confirmed it will not serve as merchant of record — partner companies will manage transactions.



When Google announced its agentic travel booking ambitions, shares in Booking Holdings and Expedia fell between 4 and 7 percent almost immediately. The market reaction reflected genuine concern that Google was building the infrastructure to bypass OTAs entirely. Research shows 79% of hotel links in AI Mode already go to Google Business Profiles, with OTAs receiving just 3.6% of clicks despite dominating source citations.



The investment picture reinforces the integration thesis: hospitality technology startups raised more than $1 billion across 40 companies between April 2025 and March 2026, with PMS and AI-led platforms attracting the largest share. Mews' acquisitions of Flexkeeping and DataChat in late 2025 exemplify the strategy: best-funded PMS businesses are building broader platforms through product development and acquisition. AI-led guest experience platforms — including Duve, Chatlyn, Conduit, and Canary Technologies — raised a combined $152.6 million, targeting the industry's core pressure: delivering fast, personalized service with leaner teams.


Sources: [Hospitality Net / Busch](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/opinion/4132382/the-pms-era-is-ending) · [PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/google-agentic-travel-booking-ai) · [Hospitality Technology](https://hospitalitytech.com/news-briefs/2026-04-13)

So what: Google's "not the merchant of record" position is the tell: they want the demand channel, not the liability. Hotels that have invested in Google Business Profile optimization are in a stronger position for this wave than OTA-dependent properties — the data shows 79% of AI Mode hotel links already flow through GBP.

## Governance & Security

The governance conversation in hospitality has moved from GDPR compliance boilerplate to a national-security-level framing, catalyzed by a White House executive order that operators need to read carefully.


On June 2, 2026, the White House issued an Executive Order titled "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security." Terence Ronson of Pertlink Limited argues that for hospitality, the relevance is not theoretical: the modern hotel has quietly become a dense, interconnected intelligent environment — binding together identity, payments, guest data, building systems, IoT, energy orchestration, and AI-driven workflows. The Order tasks the Treasury with forming an AI cybersecurity clearinghouse — a voluntary collaboration with the AI industry and infrastructure operators to coordinate vulnerability scanning, validation, and patch distribution — and extends AI-enabled cybersecurity tooling to operators of critical infrastructure.



The Order's significance lies in what it collectively recognizes: that AI is now a national strategic capability, a cybersecurity priority, an economic competitiveness engine, and a critical infrastructure dependency all at once. Its repeated theme is collaboration between government and private industry to modernize systems, protect intellectual property, accelerate adoption, and harden defenses. The key competitive question for hospitality is shifting from "how many AI features do we have?" to "can we govern AI responsibly, secure our connected systems, and turn operational trust into guest experience?"



Separately, the DOJ's Data Security Program (effective April 2025) creates direct enforcement exposure for hospitality AI deployments. DOJ's bulk data thresholds are low enough that many hospitality organizations will meet them quickly: precise geolocation data triggers at just 1,000 U.S. devices; biometric identifiers at 1,000 U.S. persons; personal financial data at 10,000 U.S. persons. AI magnifies this exposure by aggregating and reusing data for training and inference across systems. An AI governance program with defined screening processes and contract controls is key to compliance.



A CISO benchmark study of 200+ security leaders in retail and hospitality found 71% identified AI as a primary concern, ahead of ransomware and phishing. IBM found that organizations using extensive AI security tools incur breach costs averaging $3.62M, compared with $5.52M without them — and shadow AI alone adds a $670K cost premium per breach according to IBM's 2025 Cost of a Data Breach Report.


Sources: [Hospitality Net / Ronson](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/opinion/4132771/when-national-ai-security-becomes-a-hospitality-imperative) · [Hospitality Technology / DOJ DSP](https://hospitalitytech.com/beyond-hipaa-navigating-dojs-new-bulk-data-thresholds-hospitality-ai)

So what: The DOJ DSP thresholds are the most immediate operational risk. If your property runs facial recognition check-in, geofencing, or biometric timekeeping, you are almost certainly above the bulk-data threshold. Vendor contracts signed before April 2025 almost certainly lack the required controls.
