Vendor Moves — The Restaurant Reservation Stack Is Being Redrawn by Platform Money

The reservation layer — historically a fragmented cottage industry — is consolidating fast under four capital-heavy platforms, each controlled by a different strategic buyer, each now wiring into POS systems to own the guest relationship end-to-end.

DoorDash completed its $1.2 billion acquisition of SevenRooms, integrating its CRM, reservations, and guest-experience tools into the DoorDash Commerce Platform. SevenRooms leadership remains in place. The acquisition is already producing data products: SevenRooms announced on July 21 the Global Guest capability, which lets partner restaurants search hundreds of millions of DoorDash and Deliveroo customer records to recognise guests at check-in — even first-time visitors.

"Hospitality has always been about knowing your guests, but as restaurants grow, delivering that level of personalization consistently becomes more challenging," said Allison Page, founder and chief product officer at SevenRooms.

SevenRooms also launched Channel Connect in June — a free, open-licence desktop aggregator that pulls reservations from all booking platforms (including OpenTable and Resy) into a single system, automatically syncing inventory. The company said the move was designed to prevent "any single platform" from being a "gatekeeper" to restaurant operations — a pointed reference to OpenTable's April 2026 policy requiring clients to make OpenTable their system of record.

A Seattle business leader asked the Washington State AG to assess whether OpenTable's updated terms "unfairly restrict restaurant choice and competition." [PAYWALLED/BLOCKED on full Channel Connect article — using search snippet above.]

American Express announced plans to acquire European platform TheFork for $700 million in cash. When completed, the deal will bring Amex's total bookable venues across Resy, Tock, and TheFork to 75,000 — more than OpenTable's global roster of 65,000 restaurants.

TheFork, launched in France in 2007 as LaFourchette and acquired by TripAdvisor in 2014, earned $232 million in revenue in the 12 months ending March 31.

The Resy/Tock merger has also rolled out this summer, with Tock's 8,000 venues now absorbed into Resy, activating a major partnership with Toast that connects Resy guestbook data — preferences, birthdays — directly to Toast handheld devices.

Square deepened its OpenTable integration (Aug 19), allowing operators to see reservation history and customer preferences alongside Square transaction data in a single view across Square's software and handheld ordering devices. Five days earlier, Toast had rolled out its equivalent: Digital Chits, which surfaces Resy data on Toast POS screens and handhelds. The moves solidify the Toast/Resy and Square/OpenTable alliances as competing data ecosystems for the same pool of SMB restaurants.

Sources: Food On Demand – SevenRooms Global Guest, Expedite – Amex/TheFork, NRN – Square/Toast

So what: Any operator choosing a reservation platform today is choosing a data ecosystem for the next 3–5 years, not just a booking engine. DoorDash's off-premise→on-premise data bridge is new and real; operators using both SevenRooms and DoorDash should evaluate the privacy and data-ownership implications of Global Guest before turning it on.

AI Visibility & Discovery — Google's Agentic Booking Test Is Live; 83% of Restaurants Are Invisible to AI

The shift from ranked search results to AI-generated answers is no longer theoretical. Two major data points this cycle define the scale of the visibility problem and the distribution infrastructure being built around it.

Google has launched a limited U.S. test of agentic hotel booking within Search's AI Mode, with Booking Holdings among the first partners. Booking Holdings CEO Glenn Fogel referenced the tests on an August earnings call: "I think Google may have come out today, a small percentage of their traffic seeing their agentic booking … we are one of the first with them."

Amadeus confirmed it is the first infrastructure partner Google has wired into the new Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP) — the protocol built to let an AI agent complete a hotel booking. Sabre's Kurt Ekert was blunter: Google "does shop very well. It doesn't do book or service" — and Sabre could hand it that capability "en masse tomorrow."

IHG confirmed participation in the pilot during its H1 2026 earnings report; bookings are processed as direct bookings within Google's AI Mode. IHG CEO Elie Maalouf also said the company's AI-enhanced revenue management system had "unlocked revenue uplift and market share gains for our owners."

The hard data on actual transaction impact arrived on the same day the Google test launched. Booking Holdings processed 325 million room nights in Q2 2026. Traffic from large language models — paid and unpaid — accounts for less than 1% of those nights. An Expedia Group survey of more than 5,700 adults found that while 53% were comfortable letting AI suggest travel options, only 8% said they were comfortable completing a booking through an AI platform. The message: discovery is shifting; transactions are not — yet.

On the restaurant side, Uberall released the industry's first benchmark measuring how AI assistants recommend restaurants. The central finding: 83% of restaurant locations are entirely invisible in AI-generated recommendations — despite 86% maintaining some presence on Google.

A small leading cohort dominates AI attention. Across the QSR benchmark, the top three brands per category capture 53.4% of total Share of Voice. In burger chains, the leader alone captures 10x the Share of Voice of the average brand.

Uberall introduced Location Performance Optimization (LPO) as the strategic framework connecting SEO and Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) into a single operating model built on four pillars: Visibility, Reputation, Engagement, and Conversion.

Toast is also building infrastructure here: Toast expanded its partnership with Google to power agentic food ordering through Ask Maps, a conversational feature in Google Maps. The integration connects AI agents directly to restaurant ordering systems that run on Toast. Toast is co-developer of the Universal Commerce Protocol for Food, an open standard designed to govern how AI agents interact with restaurant commerce online.

Sources: Skift – Google agentic hotel booking, PhocusWire – IHG, Hospitality.today – agentic booking analysis, BusinessWire – Uberall

So what: The Google agentic booking test is live but thin — under 1% of bookings are transacting through AI channels. Operators should treat this as a 12–18 month preparation window, not a crisis. For hotels, the key question is which pipe carries the booking when the test scales: GDS, OTA, or direct. For restaurants, the Uberall data suggests Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) is now a real marketing discipline, not a buzzword — start with review density and structured data before spending on tooling.

Operational AI — Toast Bets the Roadmap on Agentic; Palona Raises $20M for the Restaurant Operating Layer

Restaurant AI is bifurcating: established platforms are embedding AI into existing workflows; new entrants are building a layer above the POS that spans multiple functions.

Toast is working to capture more business from time-strapped operators by using AI tools to assume more of a restaurateur's workflows. Toast's first AI-enabled workflow product — Toast IQ Grow, a digital marketing tool — is poised to become the company's fastest-growing product, with executives expecting it to generate $10 million in annual revenue since its May introduction, CEO Aman Narang said on an August quarterly earnings call.

The company illustrated the strategy with a Louisiana restaurant, Spirits Food & Friends, which consolidated onto Toast before adopting Toast IQ Grow. According to management, the restaurant reduced its outside marketing costs by 70% while generating more than $100,000 in marketing-attributed sales in less than two months.

Over time, Narang said, Toast will deploy "agentic products" for demand forecasting, labor schedules, income tax help, food costs and availability. "That's an incredibly exciting future, especially in an industry known for slim margins and long hours."

Palona AI has launched what it calls a multimodal AI operating layer for physical businesses, beginning with restaurants and positioning the technology as something broader than another voice-ordering system.

The company's current product suite covers Revenue Expansion, Revenue Intelligence, and Operations Excellence. Palona says the platform can capture demand from calls, catering requests, private events and large-order inquiries, identify intent and urgency, and convert operational signals into workflows acted on by managers or AI agents. The round: the launch comes as Palona closes its Series A, bringing total funding to $20 million.

Slang AI, Palona's closest competitor in higher-value guest interactions, raised $36 million in Series B financing in February 2026, bringing its total to $68 million, and has positioned its technology as an AI "Superhost."

Delivery Hero unveiled a new agentic AI assistant for neighbourhood shops and restaurants that acts on behalf of business owners — not just advising — the moment they approve, directly inside WhatsApp. "Small business owners have a lot to juggle and our new AI assistant helps them grow faster without adding to their workload," said Johannes Bruder, CPO at Delivery Hero.

The assistant currently supports over 40,000 restaurants across Delivery Hero's global brands, an early slice of approximately 1.5 million platform partners.

Consumer sentiment is also moving in operators' favour. In 2025, 41% of surveyed consumers said they'd choose a restaurant that avoided AI altogether. One year later, that figure has fallen to 27% — meaning nearly 3 in 4 diners are now open to AI playing some role in their experience.

"The operators who move now to build that trust will own the guest relationship. The ones who wait will be handing it to someone else," said Savneet Singh, CEO of PAR Technology.

Sources: Restaurant Dive – Toast AI, RTN – Palona AI, RTIH – Delivery Hero, QSR Magazine – PAR report

So what: Toast's closed-loop claim — connecting marketing campaigns directly to completed orders via its POS — is the most commercially credible AI narrative in restaurant tech right now. Independent operators evaluating AI marketing tools should ask every vendor this specific question: can you show me campaign spend tied to actual transaction data, not just attributed clicks?

Funding & M&A — Hospitality Tech Hits $1B; Q2 Acquisitions Signal a Consolidating Stack

The headline figure is now twelve months old but the structural trend it points to is accelerating in the current quarter.

Forty global hospitality technology startups raised more than $1 billion in the twelve months from April 2025 to March 2026, with property management systems and AI-led platforms attracting the most investment, according to Abode Worldwide's Hospitality Tech Investment Index 2026.

PMS companies raised a combined $408.1 million — seven companies led by Mews at $300 million. AI-led guest experience platforms Duve, Chatlyn, Conduit, and Canary Technologies raised a combined $152.6 million.

In Q2 2026, three AI-native rounds closed: Smartness took the largest at €47 million Series B for its agentic AI platform for hotels and short-term rentals; Pricepoint raised $6.6 million for AI revenue management; Besty AI raised $3.75 million for STR automation.

Lighthouse, a hospitality intelligence platform with $370 million in growth equity raised across prior rounds, acquired Hotelrank, a tool that measures where hotels show up across AI platforms — the acquisition of a startup founded in 2025 that became immediately relevant as AI discovery rose on the agenda.

Also in the RTN sidebar this cycle: MarginEdge raised $80 million as competition for the AI-powered restaurant back office intensifies; and Olo was reported acquired by Thoma Bravo in an all-cash deal at approximately $2 billion (search snippet only; full article not fetched).

Sources: Hotel Dive – $1B hospitality tech, Abode Worldwide Q2 2026 roundup

So what: Lighthouse buying Hotelrank is the sharpest signal in this cycle — a commercial intelligence platform specifically acquiring the capability to measure AI visibility. That tells you where sophisticated hotel operators see their next competitive battlefield. For PE-backed hospitality SaaS companies evaluating M&A, AI visibility tooling is now a bolt-on rationale, not just a feature.

Data & Integration — PMS Becomes the Control Layer; Google Plugs Into the GDS Rails

The structural argument for PMS consolidation has moved from vendor positioning to investor conviction.

"PMS is increasingly becoming the control layer of the hospitality tech stack. As hotel and lodging operators push to simplify fragmented systems, the PMS is taking on more responsibility — connecting teams, revenue, guest journeys and data in one place," per the Abode Worldwide Index.

"AI can't compensate for scattered data, disconnected systems or poor processes. If a hotel's underlying systems are fragmented, AI will only make operational problems more visible," the same report concludes.

IHG is in the final stages of its cloud-based PMS rollout, expected to reach 4,000 hotels by the end of 2026. The Google agentic booking test is connecting to this infrastructure through the GDS: Amadeus confirmed it is the first infrastructure partner wired into Google's Universal Commerce Protocol for lodging. Sabre's position is that Google "does shop very well. It doesn't do book or service" — and Sabre can supply that capability at scale. It is the first time Google has tied its agentic hotel booking, on the record, to the global distribution systems. The implication for independent hotels: the independent hotel reaches the Google AI agent only if a pipe carries it there — the OTA it already sells through, or the GDS. Present, but by proxy — never with the direct line the chains built for themselves.

Sources: Hospitality.today – agentic booking analysis, Abode Worldwide Index

So what: Hotels not on a GDS-connected PMS should add "GDS coverage in Google's AI Mode" to their distribution review checklist now. The path into agentic booking for chains is direct; for independents, it runs through the rails they already pay for.

Governance & Security — The White House EO Translates Into Hotel Risk Exposure

A white paper by Terence Ronson of Pertlink Limited translates the White House's June 2, 2026 Executive Order on "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security" into operational priorities for hotels. The modern hotel has quietly become a dense, interconnected intelligent environment — binding together identity, payments, guest data, building systems, IoT, energy orchestration, and AI-driven workflows. The more intelligent a property becomes, the more strategically exposed it becomes.

The Order directs federal bodies to prioritise cyber defence within 30 days; tasks Treasury with forming an AI cybersecurity clearinghouse for coordinated vulnerability scanning and patching; and extends AI-enabled cybersecurity tooling to operators of critical infrastructure. The paper's core risk categories for hotel operators: AI shadow operations (departments deploying AI without governance), autonomous operational drift (agents operating beyond intended boundaries), and data contamination (guest data unintentionally absorbed into external model training).

The talent gap is compounding the governance risk. The HSMAI Foundation's AI Talent Pipeline Report found that students entering hospitality operations have established AI habits but employers must equip future talent with governance and decision-making frameworks. Students identify data analysis as highly important for future careers, yet current AI use remains heavily weighted toward academic productivity tasks.

Sources: Hospitality Net – White House EO, Hospitality Net – HSMAI Foundation report

So what: The EO doesn't impose mandatory licensing on hotels — but it normalises vendor security audits and governance expectations at national scale. Any hotel operator renewing a technology contract in 2026 should add three questions to the RFP: (1) Does this vendor use guest data to train external models? (2) What human override exists for any automated action? (3) What is the incident response plan if an AI agent acts on incorrect data?
