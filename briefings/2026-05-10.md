## CLUSTER 1 — AI Visibility & Discovery
### *Hotels Are Invisible to the New Front Door*

The most concentrated burst of signal this period surrounds a single structural threat: hotel brands are losing the battle for AI citation. 
When an AI agent is queried about a Hyatt hotel, the source it cites most isn't Hyatt — it's NerdWallet at 13.6% of citations, ahead of Hyatt's own website at 10.3%. Research by a16z-backed AI-visibility firm Limy shows that AI models surface content that helps users compare value, not content optimised to sell a room.
 The implication is alarming: 
owning inventory or booking channels no longer ensures visibility, and travel brands risk losing influence over how their products are framed in consumer decision-making.


In response, two vendors made structural moves. 
SiteMinder expanded its platform to help 53,000 hotels appear in AI-powered trip planning and booking tools, including a partnership with DirectBooker that connects live hotel rates directly to platforms like ChatGPT and Claude.
 
Both approaches rely on MCP (Model Context Protocol), which acts as a standard layer connecting AI models to live data and booking systems. According to Phocuswright research, 56% of travel companies have implemented MCP and Agent2Agent (A2A) protocols or are exploring doing so.
 Meanwhile, 
IHG is introducing a natural-language search tool and restructuring its hotel data for AI compatibility, partnering with Google, OpenAI, and Anthropic, while also deepening loyalty via a new Salesforce-powered CRM.


The luxury segment faces its own version of this problem. 
AI agents are increasingly responsible for researching and booking luxury travel, shifting the target audience from humans to machines. Luxury brands must prioritise clear, consistent, and structured online information over traditional visual marketing to remain discoverable by AI.


**So what:** MCP is rapidly becoming the distribution layer mid-market operators cannot ignore. If you're not structured for machine-readable discovery by end-2026, you will not appear in AI booking flows — and unlike SEO, there's no "catch-up" paid channel. Audit your schema, OTA listing quality, and review specificity now. Your next booking may already be coming from an AI agent, not a human.

## CLUSTER 2 — Operational AI
### *Specific Outcomes Are Arriving; The Proof Points Are Getting Harder*


Hotels are seeing RevPAR gains up to 15% after implementing AI-powered pricing systems that adjust prices in real time based on demand signals. In operations, the Ritz-Carlton San Francisco reported a 20% increase in room-cleaning speed via an AI system that optimises housekeeping schedules based on checkout timing, guest preferences, and staffing levels.


The standout new deployment this week is the Hivr + Radisson rooming list agent. 
Hivr has launched an agentic AI tool, co-developed with Radisson Hotel Group, that automates the processing of rooming lists for groups and meetings — described as the "most hated job in hotels" because the manual workflow hasn't meaningfully changed in years. The tool uses an AI agent to ingest guest data in any format and structure it directly into the hotel's PMS. A second agent reconciles changes across versions. Hivr estimates the tool saves roughly 50 minutes of admin time per 100-person booking.
 This is a genuine workflow improvement with a concrete time-saving metric, not a chatbot rebrand.

On the consumer-facing side, 
MSC Cruises has launched an AI-powered concierge built into its MSC for Me app. The launch follows a pilot programme in which more than 170,000 guests exchanged more than 1 million messages with the tool, generating a 93% service satisfaction score. The assistant is available 24/7 in more than 90 languages and can manage bookings including restaurant reservations, shore excursions, and spa treatments.
 The previous iteration (Zoe, a cabin voice device) 
struggled with accuracy, speech recognition, and limited functionality — many guests simply stopped using the devices altogether.
 MSC Concierge is a genuine second-generation rebuild with pilot evidence.

In the restaurant sector, Wonder's Marc Lore unveiled the most provocative concept: 
Wonder Create would let anyone use AI to design and launch their own restaurant brand in under a minute, described as a "Shopify front end with an AI prompt" — the AI generates the name, branding, description, pictures, pricing, health information, and all recipes.
 
Ghost kitchens had a rocky run in the early 2020s; Wonder's added layer of automation may address some pitfalls, but the model is still unproven at scale.


**So what:** Operational AI is bifurcating between genuine automation with measurable outcomes (Hivr/Radisson rooming lists, Ritz-Carlton housekeeping, MSC Concierge) and conceptual plays. For mid-market operators, rooming list automation and housekeeping scheduling are the immediate, ROI-proven entry points. Build your vendor shortlist around operators who can show time-per-task reduction, not feature lists.

## CLUSTER 3 — Funding & M&A
### *European Lodging SaaS Attracts Institutional Capital; PE Eyes Stack Disruption*


Smartness, a Trentino-based vertical SaaS company, has closed a €47 million Series B — the largest round ever for an Italian vertical SaaS company. The round was co-led by United Ventures and CDP Venture Capital, with continued backing from Partech.
 
Net revenue retention above 150% — a metric that resonated strongly with institutional investors — positions Smartness at the frontier of vertical AI adoption in European hospitality.
 
The company plans to triple 2026 revenue through organic growth and strategic acquisitions across Europe.


Also notable: 
Key Data CEO and co-founder Jason Sprenkle will retire at the end of May, with president Dustin Downing set to take over as CEO in a planned transition. Duetto has promoted Michael Schäffner to chief sales officer from SVP of global sales.


On the PE analysis side, 
Vivek Bhogaraju, Executive In Residence at private equity firms, argues that "hotels have been held back for years by fragmented tech stacks, information silos, and subscale solution providers. AI agents are the perfect antidote, stitching together disparate datasets to unlock revenue and boost profitability that were operationally impossible before."


**So what:** Smartness is the template for the next wave of European hospitality SaaS consolidation: start with pricing, expand into full-stack PMS/CRM/payments, then position as the "agentic OS" for independents. For PE-backed SaaS operators, the 150%+ NRR and 10% MoM growth are the benchmarks to beat. Watch for Smartness M&A activity in DACH and UK markets imminently.

## CLUSTER 4 — Data & Integration
### *The MCP Protocol Is Becoming Infrastructure; Data Debt Is the Real Blocker*


Many hotels operate with fragmented technology systems — separate platforms for property management, point-of-sale, CRM, and loyalty. Hotels need to integrate these systems and build a centralised data platform as a single source of truth, according to BCG.
 This is not new advice, but the urgency is new: without clean, consolidated data, MCP connections to AI booking channels will surface garbage.


Minor Hotels is building an AI stack from scratch to improve personalisation, aiming to unify guest data across silos to enable real-time, context-aware service. The initiative leverages partnerships with Google Cloud, Salesforce, and OneTrust, with full deployment planned by end of 2026.



Human-driven web traffic is declining across the hospitality industry. As organic visits fall, automated traffic from AI agents, OTA bots, and scrapers now represents a growing share of what platforms like Google Analytics record. Signal integrity is collapsing — when bot activity is misclassified as genuine interest, the distortion compounds. Properties may overestimate demand, misjudge marketing performance, or make strategic decisions based on patterns that don't reflect reality.


**So what:** Data readiness is the gating factor for everything else in this brief. Before buying more AI tools, mid-market operators should audit: (1) whether their PMS data is clean enough to feed an AI agent, (2) whether their analytics can distinguish bot from human traffic, and (3) whether their schema is structured for MCP ingestion. Readiness advisory services have a clear commercial hook here.

## CLUSTER 5 — People & Skills
### *The Change Management Crisis Is Bigger Than the Technology Gap*


Everyone's talking about agentic AI in hospitality. Fewer people are talking about whether the teams supposed to work with it are actually ready. The anxiety hoteliers express isn't "will AI replace my job?" — it's more subtle: "I don't fully understand what this system is doing, and I'm not sure when I should trust it." That's not a technology problem. That's a change management problem.



The tourism sector lags behind other industries in AI talent: just 2.9% of full-time travel and tourism employees are skilled in AI, compared to 21% in technology and media.



The great AI upskilling of the travel workforce has begun, but even among the industry's largest companies, the gap between who is preparing employees for AI and who is just cutting with it is widening fast. Airbnb has a dedicated workstream called "AI for Non-Developers" inside BizTech, building OneChat agents and task automation tools designed for employees who are not engineers — the listings explicitly reference Claude and MCP. A select group including Airbnb, Amadeus, SNCF, Expedia, and Booking.com have advanced initiatives; many major companies show little to no public evidence of such efforts.



Many hotels will never return to pre-pandemic staffing levels, making it critical to "up-level" existing staff by helping them learn faster, adapt to new roles, and deliver better guest experiences with fewer people.


**So what:** For PE-backed SaaS companies, this is a product gap: the operator who can package AI deployment with change management, role-specific training, and trust-building — not just the software — wins retention and NRR. The technology is table stakes. The organisational readiness layer is the moat.

## CLUSTER 6 — Governance & Security
### *Privacy Is the Top Adoption Blocker; Analytics Integrity Is a New Risk*


Data privacy and security concerns remain the top factors limiting hoteliers from AI adoption. Hoteliers also reported difficulty integrating AI with legacy systems.
 
APAC leads in adoption: 62% of APAC respondents are piloting or have adopted AI and 93% allocate more than 5% of their IT budget to these technologies. Obstacles persist elsewhere — notably data security and privacy (43%), integration challenges (40%), and staff training (38%).


A less-discussed governance risk is emerging from AI-driven analytics: 
when bot activity from AI agents, OTA scrapers, and crawlers is misclassified as genuine human demand, properties may overestimate demand, misjudge marketing performance, or make strategic decisions based on distorted patterns.
 No vendor has yet published a clear solution to this problem.

**So what:** For mid-market operators building their AI business case internally, lead with data governance as a prerequisite — not a blocker. Boards and owners will ask about guest data handling before approving spend. For SaaS companies, analytics integrity (separating AI agent traffic from human intent) is an underserved product opportunity.

## CLUSTER 7 — Vendor Moves
### *IHG Goes Direct to Foundation Models; Mews Founder Calls Out the Industry*


Mews founder Richard Valtr is sharply critical of the hotel industry's adoption of AI, arguing most companies are simply adding conversational AI features without meaningful strategy. "I'm actually very, very disappointed in the imagination of a lot of the entrepreneurs coming into this industry," he told Skift. He praises Hyatt and Accor for willingness to experiment quickly while criticising others for slow, box-ticking approaches.



J.P. Morgan analysts have called an inflection point: 2026 could be the first year AI investments lead to measurable profits. Hyatt said its group sales teams have become roughly 20% more productive since deploying AI tools. Wyndham said its AI-powered call centres have cut labour costs for franchisees.



Wyndham's Chief Commercial Officer Scott Strickland — who oversees IT, cybersecurity, revenue management, and marketing — has led a decade-long technology overhaul including cloud migration and a centralised reservation system. Wyndham's agentic AI deployments include direct room-booking through an agent, plus AI-assisted staff support that surfaces proactive information such as loyalty status or a prior noise complaint to offer quieter rooms.


**So what:** The gap between operators building genuine AI infrastructure (Wyndham, IHG, Hyatt) and those adding AI labels to legacy features is widening into a competitive moat. For PE-backed SaaS vendors, the question is whether your product is in the stack of the companies doing it right — or whether you're being bypassed by in-house foundation model partnerships.
