## Vendor Moves

The restaurant reservation market is being structurally redrawn by financial-services and delivery platforms, and HITEC 2026 crystallised the hotel-side equivalent: AI is being embedded into core operating systems, not bolted on as a feature.

**American Express** announced the proposed acquisition of **TheFork** from Tripadvisor for $700 million in cash on June 15, 2026 — its third dining-platform purchase after Resy (2019) and Tock (2024). 
TheFork generated $232 million in revenue for the year ended March 31, a 25% jump from a year earlier. The deal would expand AmEx's dining network to 75,000 bookable venues and bolster its fastest-growing segment, international.
 Simultaneously, 
AmEx is folding Tock's restaurant inventory into Resy this summer, adding roughly 8,000 bookable venues — including 1,200 wineries — to Resy's app. "Resy will be the singular app for the best culinary experiences used by the hospitality world's most ambitious operators," said Pablo Rivero, CEO of Resy and SVP of AmEx Global Dining.
 The combined Resy+Tock+TheFork empire will sit at roughly 75,000 venues, giving AmEx card-linked dining reach across North America and 11 European markets.

**DoorDash** (via its **SevenRooms** acquisition) fired back on June 17 with the launch of **Channel Connect**, 
a desktop app that aggregates reservations from various booking channels — including "major booking platforms," which presumably means rivals OpenTable and Resy — into a single system, while automatically updating inventory across those channels. Offered for free under an open license, it's available to any restaurant, even those that don't use SevenRooms. SevenRooms CEO Joel Montaniel described it: "More tables for diners, one book for restaurants."
 The move is partly a direct response to OpenTable's April policy requiring restaurants to make it their system of record. 
A SevenRooms blog post stated: "Channel Connect was built so that no single platform has to be the gatekeeper to your operations."
 DoorDash also launched **Ask DoorDash**, an in-app AI chatbot for ordering, reservations, and grocery cart building. 
The feature allows customers to describe a craving, upload a recipe photo, or snap a grocery list; the app builds a cart from results. Early testing showed nearly half of all restaurant orders placed through the tool were from places the customer had never ordered from before, and carts assembled through the chatbot ran more than 35% higher in value than a standard grocery order.


**Toast** has had a prolific release cycle. 
Toast expanded its Toast IQ intelligence ecosystem with a conversational AI assistant drawing on real-time and historical data across approximately 148,000 customer locations. The assistant proactively surfaces insights and executes tasks — like updating menus or editing shifts — and provides a personalised "For you" feed that surfaces prompts such as "A major event in your area this weekend may increase late-night crowds. Want to check how your staffing looks?"
 Early adopter Romain Bernus (Le Petit Four Bakery): 
"Pulling reports was just too slow when we're so busy — it always meant late-night research scrolling through tabs and filters."
 On the QSR side, 
Toast launched Toast Drive-Thru, an enterprise-grade unified platform for quick-service drive-thrus, integrating POS, AI voice ordering (via partners like Incept AI), Speed of Service reporting, and Delphi hardware. There are just over 140,000 total drive-thru locations across the U.S., and additional AI voice ordering partner integrations will follow in 2026.


On the hotel side, 
Oracle Hospitality announced Oracle OPERA Cloud Assistant at HITEC 2026 — AI-powered capabilities embedded directly into OPERA Cloud workflows, designed to support AI-assisted room assignments, AI-generated rate descriptions, operational guidance, and multilingual translation. Innspire also launched Guest Flows, a zero-download guest journey solution supporting mobile check-in, digital key, AI concierge, F&B ordering, upgrades, and automated checkout through a guest's smartphone with no app download required.


**Sources:** [Nation's Restaurant News](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology/sevenrooms-launches-a-reservations-aggregator-for-restaurants) | [PYMNTS/AmEx](https://www.pymnts.com/acquisitions/2026/amex-looks-to-spend-700-million-to-buy-thefork-from-tripadvisor/) | [BusinessWire / Toast](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260414359751/en/Toast-Launches-Unified-Drive-Thru-Solution-to-Modernize-Enterprise-Quick-Service-Restaurant-Operations) | [Hotel Technology News](https://hoteltechnologynews.com/2026/07/recent-hotel-technology-launches-show-ai-moving-from-standalone-tools-into-connected-hotel-operations/)

**So what:** The reservation market is no longer a technology competition — it's an ecosystem war. Picking a reservations platform is now partly picking whose financial, delivery, and loyalty infrastructure you live inside (DoorDash, AmEx, or Booking Holdings/OpenTable). For independent operators, Channel Connect's open-license play is the most tactically significant: it restores inventory control without requiring SevenRooms adoption. For mid-market hotel groups evaluating PMS upgrades, OPERA Cloud Assistant is significant precisely because AI embedded in the system of record shapes everyday decisions across every property — not just edge automations.

## AI Visibility & Discovery

Google is transforming itself from a search engine into a transaction layer for hospitality — the structural implications for hotel distribution are severe and immediate.


At Google I/O and Google Marketing Live 2026, Google announced that hotel booking will be the next vertical for its Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP), bringing agentic shopping to travel. The Agent Payments Protocol (AP2) will enable AI agents to complete purchases on users' behalf, starting with Google's Gemini Spark AI. Google is expanding its agentic commerce infrastructure to handle live hotel data, while travel packages remain ineligible for UCP checkout.
 The mechanism is notable for hotel owners: 
UCP for Lodging enables AI interactions to be converted into instant room reservations executed directly within Google surfaces. Crucially, unlike bookings made through an OTA, the hotel retains Merchant of Record status, keeping full ownership of the guest relationship and all associated data.



Google and OpenAI are diverging strategically: while Google is expanding direct transaction capabilities inside its AI ecosystem, OpenAI has reportedly stepped back from enabling direct purchases inside ChatGPT. Google did not provide a launch timeline for hotel bookings through AI agents, but its infrastructure announcements indicate active development rather than experimentation.


On the restaurant side, Google has already deployed: 
Google AI Mode rolled out restaurant booking to eight new international markets — Australia, Canada, Hong Kong, India, New Zealand, Singapore, South Africa, and the UK — with no Labs opt-in required. AI Mode had reached 75 million daily active users by January 2026. UK searches for "when to book a table" surged 140% in 2026, per Google Trends data.


At the restaurant operator level, 
Olo unveiled the Olo App with an architecture designed for AI agent ordering: every iteration of every menu item will be added to a universal database that AI can search when a customer asks for something. CEO Noah Glass stated: "It sets you up to be the first call that an AI agent makes when you say 'I want to order a turkey sandwich in my area.' It will be the fastest path to connecting to an available restaurant that can fulfill that order on time at a high quality at the lowest price."


Two breaking stories from Hospitality.today (July 9, 2026) [PAYWALLED/BLOCKED — available via sidebar teasers only]: **Hilton** wired its reservation system directly into Navan, routing around the GDS and becoming the first hotel company to give a travel management company direct inventory access. **Mews** cut 15% of staff, calling the roles obsolete — a test of its own AI pitch that it had previously only made to customers.

**Sources:** [Skift / Hospitality.today](https://www.hospitality.today/article/google-moves-hotel-bookings-into-the-agentic-commerce-era) | [Restaurant Business Online](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/technology/how-4-restaurant-chains-are-using-ai-today) | [Google Developers UCP](https://developers.google.com/hotels/ucp)

**So what:** Hotels that are not structuring data as machine-readable and API-accessible will be progressively invisible to the AI agents handling booking decisions. The OTAs, not individual hotels, are currently best positioned to capture UCP traffic — meaning the window for hotels to register directly with Google's booking infrastructure is now, before intermediaries cement their position as the rails. For restaurant groups, Olo's agent-first architecture is the clearest signal yet that menu schema and ordering APIs are a competitive moat.

## Operational AI

The HITEC 2026 theme — and the strongest signal of this news cycle — is that AI is migrating from standalone tools into the operating fabric of the hotel.


What made HITEC 2026 compelling was not that every vendor had an AI story. The more important distinction was between companies that could show where intelligence actually improves the workflow and those that simply added AI language to familiar software. The strongest demonstrations were specific: how a front desk agent could make a better room assignment, how a reservations manager could review call quality faster, how a revenue leader could ask a better question, how an owner could understand a variance.



RoomPriceGenie introduced Price Explanations, giving hoteliers plain-language context behind rate recommendations, including market conditions, demand signals, and property settings that influence pricing decisions. The enhancement addresses one of the biggest barriers to adoption of automated revenue management tools, particularly among smaller hotels and operators cautious about handing pricing decisions to an algorithm.



Inn-Flow used HITEC to showcase expanded AI and automation capabilities across procurement, inventory, accounting, labor, and business intelligence, building on its acquisition of Lilo. The platform connects financial and operational workflows, including AI-assisted inventory tools that tie purchasing more closely to accounting and labor management.


On the restaurant side, real-world voice AI deployment is advancing: 
Taco John's AI bot "Olena" is able to handle between 90% and 93% of orders without an employee having to step in. The system has improved significantly since the chain first began using it about three years ago. Director of restaurant technology Steve Smyth noted: "The first two months are sometimes a little rough as you're getting the guest to accept it, the team to accept it, the community. But then after that, if you can get past that 60 to 90 days, we've seen a lot of success."
 Krispy Kreme is experimenting with AI agents for contract management, with CTO Angela Yochem framing the test: "There's no intrinsic value in AI, right? The value is in the capability set that it delivers to us. And if that capability set costs more than the value it provides, then we're not gonna do it."

**Sources:** [Hotel Technology News HITEC 2026](https://hoteltechnologynews.com/2026/06/what-hitec-2026-revealed-about-the-future-of-hotel-technology/) | [Hotel Technology News July launches](https://hoteltechnologynews.com/2026/07/recent-hotel-technology-launches-show-ai-moving-from-standalone-tools-into-connected-hotel-operations/) | [Restaurant Business Online](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/technology/how-4-restaurant-chains-are-using-ai-today)

**So what:** Explainability is the unlock for revenue management AI adoption at independent and mid-scale hotels. RoomPriceGenie's Price Explanations approach should be a procurement criterion when evaluating any RMS: if the system can't explain a recommendation in plain language, revenue teams won't trust it and will override it — negating the automation benefit. For QSR operators, Taco John's 60–90 day community acceptance curve is the most honest deployment benchmark in the sector.

## Governance & Security


On June 2, 2026, the White House issued an Executive Order titled "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security." On its face, the Order is about federal systems, frontier-model security, and national cyber defense. Read carefully, it signals that artificial intelligence has crossed a threshold — from an experimental productivity tool to a critical layer of operational infrastructure that governments now treat as a matter of national security.



The Order's significance lies less in any single clause than in what it collectively recognises: that AI is now a national strategic capability, a cybersecurity priority, an economic competitiveness engine, and a critical infrastructure dependency all at once. The competitive question for hospitality is shifting from "How many AI features do we have?" to "Can we govern AI responsibly, secure our connected systems, and turn operational trust into guest experience?"


In parallel, the DOJ's Data Security Program (DSP) is creating direct compliance exposure for hospitality AI deployments: 
Hospitality companies are accelerating adoption of AI including dynamic pricing, marketing personalisation, fraud detection, digital key platforms, and call-centre transcription — all requiring massive volumes of customer data, often relying on third-party vendors, offshore development teams, and cloud-hosted model pipelines. That combination creates fast-emerging compliance risk under DOJ DSP rules restricting certain data transactions that could give countries of concern access to Americans' bulk sensitive personal data. Hospitality companies routinely touch at least four DSP-covered categories: identifiers, geolocation, biometrics, and financial.



A 2026 cross-industry security survey found that 63% of organisations cannot enforce purpose limitations on AI agents, 60% cannot terminate misbehaving agents quickly, and 55% cannot isolate AI systems from sensitive networks — a 15–20 point gap between watching and acting.


**Sources:** [Hospitality Net / Terence Ronson](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/opinion/4132771/when-national-ai-security-becomes-a-hospitality-imperative) | [Hospitality Technology / DOJ DSP](https://hospitalitytech.com/beyond-hipaa-navigating-dojs-new-bulk-data-thresholds-hospitality-ai)

**So what:** Hotels deploying AI concierges, dynamic pricing, or biometric check-in that route data through offshore vendors are now in a grey zone under DOJ DSP. Franchised hotel groups are the highest-risk category: the franchisee controls the guest interaction but the franchisor often dictates the technology — yet the data controller remains responsible for compliance regardless of who chose the system. Running an AI governance inventory (where does AI already live, who accesses what data, which vendors have offshore development teams) is now a legal priority, not a strategic nicety.

## People & Skills


The National Restaurant Association's 2026 State of the Restaurant Industry report found that 80% of restaurants said they were short at least one position in 2025. Average annual restaurant turnover topped 75%, with quick-service restaurants regularly exceeding 130%. Miami-based MAJC, which launched in February, combines AI-powered job-matching, training, and community tools to run a single workforce engine built for hospitality professionals. "By combining AI with the power of an expert community and learning academy, MAJC is democratising access to software and systems that help operators attract, develop and retain great talent," said a company spokesperson.


**American Express and Resy** launched **Restaurant Academy** (July 1, 2026) in partnership with the National Restaurant Association Educational Foundation. 
The first cohort of 30 restaurant leaders will participate in facilitated sessions from September 2026 through March 2027. The programme includes AI as a future-ready leadership tool alongside traditional P&L management and team-building skills. Participants will engage with James Beard Award winners Mashama Bailey, Gregory Gourdet, and Nok Suntaranon.


**Sources:** [PRNewswire / AmEx & Resy](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/american-express-and-resy-with-national-restaurant-association-educational-foundation-launch-new-restaurant-academy-to-develop-the-next-generation-of-restaurant-leaders-302815742.html) | [PYMNTS / MAJC](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/restaurants-cannot-find-workers-ai-says-it-can/)

**So what:** AmEx's Restaurant Academy doubles as a loyalty vehicle — operators trained on the Resy ecosystem are more likely to stay in it. For PE-backed multi-unit groups, the 75%+ annual turnover rate means that any AI tool requiring skill continuity to operate is operationally fragile. The highest-ROI workforce AI right now is not customer-facing — it's the scheduling, forecasting, and onboarding automation that reduces dependence on experienced continuity.
