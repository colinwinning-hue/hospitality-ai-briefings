Vendor Moves

The reservation-stack battle escalated sharply across both restaurant and hotel sectors, with three major ecosystems staking out incompatible visions of who owns the guest relationship.

DoorDash launched Ask DoorDash, an in-app AI chatbot that lets consumers order food, build grocery carts, and book restaurant reservations using text prompts, photos, or voice inputs. The chatbot is accessible via a dedicated button labeled "Ask" embedded within the app's search bar. They can describe a craving, upload a cookbook photo or recipe link, or snap a picture of a grocery list, and the app builds a cart from the results.

Early testing showed tangible results: nearly half of all restaurant orders placed through the tool were from a place the customer had never ordered from before. The company also said carts assembled through the chatbot ran more than 35% higher in value than a standard grocery order, with users completing them five times faster than they would through the regular app interface.

DoorDash co-founder Andy Fang told Bloomberg the product draws on AI models built by OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google, supplemented by open-source options. Fang also added that DoorDash intends to license the technology to outside partners — grocers, restaurants, and other retailers — who would deploy it under their own branding, sharpening its rivalry with Instacart. Separately, DoorDash's September 2025 Dash Forward event unveiled Going Out, which brings reservations, in-store rewards, and exclusive offers for DashPass members together in one place. For the first time, diners can book a reservation directly in the DoorDash app with no cover fees, thanks to an integration with SevenRooms. Restaurants can use Going Out to promote events, manage tables, and personalize hospitality with enriched guest profiles.

SevenRooms (owned by DoorDash) launched Channel Connect on June 17, a free, open-license desktop app that aggregates reservations from all booking channels into a single dashboard. SevenRooms said Channel Connect will free operators from having to use multiple tablets to manually reconcile double bookings, and for customers it will mean more access to tables, because restaurants won't have to limit their inventory to certain sites.

The program will pull in bookings from reservation apps, search engines, social media, review sites, the restaurant's website, AI agents, and hotel concierges, and inject them all into the restaurant's preferred system of record.

The move comes amid fierce competition between SevenRooms, OpenTable, and Resy. In April, OpenTable began requiring restaurant clients to make it their system of record and make all of their inventory available on OpenTable. Channel Connect appears to be at least partially a response to that strict policy. SevenRooms CEO Joel Montaniel said: "Channel Connect was built so that no single platform has to be the gatekeeper to your operations."

Toast has been running back-to-back launches. Toast introduced Toast IQ Grow, a complete marketing solution designed to take on the day-to-day work of marketing a business, from identifying a lunch slowdown to automatically launching a campaign to address it. At the center of the launch is the Marketing Agent, which builds audiences and automates campaigns across channels including email, SMS, social media, and paid advertising. Bundled at $499 per month, Toast IQ Grow pairs the AI-powered agent with a dedicated human Marketing Success Manager. In a pilot program, restaurants working with a Marketing Success Manager saw sales grow more than 8% on average compared to similar Toast restaurants, with the top 25% of performers seeing more than 40% growth in the first month. Early operator Keita Okugawa (Matoi, San Diego) reported the platform generated $3.4K in attributed email revenue over just two months, more than three times the cost of Toast IQ Grow over that period, alongside a 4.5x return on ad spend. The earlier Toast IQ conversational assistant (October 2025), drawing on real-time and historical data across approximately 148,000 customer locations, goes beyond answering questions — proactively surfacing insights and executing tasks directly within Toast, empowering operators to run their businesses faster and smarter.

Toast is also collaborating with The Coca-Cola Company to create an exclusive AI-powered feature within Toast IQ designed to help operators improve beverage sales by suggesting recommendations and optimizing menus.

Toast also unveiled a reimagined version of its Toast Local consumer app, now featuring integrated Resy reservations and expanding the number of bookable locations to more than 20,000.

American Express / Resy launched a beta of the Dining Companion, a generative AI restaurant-discovery tool. Dining Companion brings together Amex's proprietary technology, insights from Card Member spend history, an LLM, a personalized recommendation engine, and Resy's API to make restaurant discovery easy. Card Members can submit prompts to explore available U.S. Resy restaurants that meet their culinary preferences, location, and other criteria, and then book directly through Resy. Dining is Amex's largest travel and entertainment spend category globally, with more than $87 billion spent on dining in the U.S. in 2024. Meanwhile, following American Express's acquisition of Tock in 2024, Resy and Tock announced a merger in February 2026 that will bring the combined network to more than 25,000 venues.

Sources: Quartz – Ask DoorDash | DoorDash Dash Forward 2025 | Nation's Restaurant News – Channel Connect | Pulse 2.0 – Toast IQ Grow | American Express Tech – Dining Companion

So what: Mid-market restaurant operators now face three incompatible platform ecosystems: OpenTable (maximize discovery reach, surrender data), SevenRooms/DoorDash (guest-data ownership with delivery integration), and Amex/Resy/Tock (prestige-aligned, prepayment-capable). The Channel Connect move turns DoorDash's SevenRooms acquisition into a direct attack on OpenTable's lock-in policy. For PE-backed SaaS, the data-ownership narrative is the competitive moat — not the feature list.

AI Visibility & Discovery

The structural shift in how travelers and diners discover hospitality is accelerating from aspiration into early commercial reality.

Google has been executing an agentic booking strategy openly since November 2025. Two weeks after launching agentic bookings in AI Mode to reserve restaurants, buy event tickets and make beauty and wellness appointments, Google confirmed it is developing similar capabilities for hotel and flight reservations. "Our goal is that you'll be able to also book flights and hotels directly in AI Mode," Julie Farago, Google's vice president of engineering for travel and local search, told PhocusWire. Google is working on building out the experience with industry partners such as Booking.com, Expedia, Marriott International, IHG Hotels & Resorts, Choice Hotels International and Wyndham Hotels & Resorts.

Google said specifics regarding the booking flow, payments and other details are still being worked out, and it did not provide a timeline for the launch of agentic travel booking. "We're not going to rush this out the door because we want to make sure that it's a seamless experience," Farago said. As of June 2026, Google announced agentic flight and hotel booking for AI Mode in November 2025, but it has not launched and there is no public release date. Restaurant bookings, however, are already live.

The traffic data inside AI Mode is striking. Research analyzing 4,000 Google AI Mode hotel queries found that 79% of hotel links in AI Mode already go to Google Business Profiles, with OTAs receiving just 3.6% of clicks despite dominating source citations. The Local Pack, which appears in only 6.5% of hotel queries, sends 81% of its traffic directly to hotel websites.

When Google announced its agentic travel booking ambitions in November 2025, shares in Booking Holdings and Expedia fell between 4 and 7% almost immediately. Some analysts pushed back: if AI assistants route travelers directly to hotel booking engines through official APIs, OTA commissions of 15 to 25% are structurally vulnerable.

The BCG AI-First Hotels report frames the discoverability shift strategically: 37% of travelers now use AI large language models to plan and book trips. BCG argues that brand equity is shifting from name recognition to algorithmic relevance — hotels whose inventory, pricing, and amenity data is not machine-readable will not appear in agentic recommendations at all.

DoorDash's Zesty app (piloting in San Francisco and New York) represents a parallel restaurant discovery play: DoorDash positions Zesty not as a replacement for review platforms like Yelp or Google, but as a complementary discovery layer focused on intent, mood and context. Users can follow up with conversational questions like "What do most people order?" and receive AI-generated responses that synthesize menu data, customer behavior, and social content. DoorDash co-founder Andy Fang has said Zesty aggregates data from DoorDash, Google Maps, TikTok, and other sources.

Sources: PhocusWire – Google Agentic Booking | SEO Sherpa – Google AI Mode Hotel Links | BCG – AI-First Hotels

So what: Google Business Profile is no longer just a marketing checkbox — it is now the primary click destination in AI Mode hotel queries. Any operator not treating GBP as a live inventory feed and structured data asset is already losing bookings to AI mediation. For hotel SaaS companies, machine-readable API integrations are now a distribution prerequisite.

Funding & M&A

The hospitality tech funding picture has consolidated into a clear thesis: investors are backing control-layer platforms, not features.

Forty global hospitality technology startups raised more than $1 billion in the past year, with property management systems and artificial intelligence-led platforms attracting the most investment, according to U.K.-based Abode Worldwide's Hospitality Tech Investment Index 2026.

PMS companies led with a combined $408.1 million raised across seven players, while AI-driven guest experience platforms (digital check-in, automated messaging, and upsell tools) brought in $152.6 million. Between December 2025 and February 2026, the sector's three biggest raises happened almost simultaneously: Mews at $300 million, Kindred at $125 million, and Limehome at €75 million.

Jessica Gillingham, CEO and Founder of Abode Worldwide, said: "Investors are concentrating capital in the platforms that hospitality businesses increasingly depend on every day, especially PMS and AI-led systems that unify operations, improve automation and strengthen the data layer underneath the business. The companies attracting the strongest investor backing share a common trait — they sit close to essential operator workflows and become more valuable over time. Unified systems generate more data, better automation and higher switching costs."

PMS businesses are also building broader platforms through development and acquisition, citing Mews' acquisitions of Flexkeeping and DataChat in late 2025 as examples of this strategy. AI-led guest experience platforms were a standout, with Duve, Chatlyn, Conduit and Canary Technologies raising a combined $152.6 million. Canary notably acquired OpenKey in February to expand its access to door lock providers.

On the M&A side: in the first half of 2025, the global travel, leisure and hospitality sector saw $10.8 billion in mergers and acquisitions activity, a 17.3% year-over-year increase in deal value. The reservation stack was reshaped by two defining deals: American Express now owns both Resy and Tock, having bought them for $400 million in 2024, and DoorDash acquired SevenRooms for $1.2 billion in 2025. OpenTable, long the default, saw its US market share slide from 51% to 46% between 2022 and 2024, though it still leads in 13 of the 15 top US cities.

The larger PMS players are becoming much more ambitious, moving beyond operations into reservations, inventory, guest communication, data and AI. Acquisitions give them speed and control while capturing more of the value chain. That does create pressure for smaller startups and niche providers. Operators are less willing to buy more tools that mean more complexity.

Sources: Hotel Dive – Hospitality Tech $1B Funding | Hotel Technology News – Abode Worldwide Report

So what: The PMS is eating the hospitality stack from the inside. For PE-backed portfolio companies positioned as point solutions (standalone loyalty, standalone upsells, standalone analytics), the next 18 months require a clear answer to the Mews/Opera Cloud super-platform question: integrate deeply or face displacement. Any acquisition thesis built on hospitality tech should weight "proximity to daily operator workflows" above all other criteria.

Operational AI

The BCG AI-First Hotels report (March 2026) is the most data-rich operator-facing document of this cycle and deserves careful attention.

The hospitality industry is in a labor crisis. In North America alone, 65% of hotels reported staffing shortages in 2025, according to the American Hotel & Lodging Association. At the same time, labor costs have jumped 11.2% year-over-year. Against this backdrop, specific operator outcomes are emerging:

Ritz-Carlton San Francisco implemented an AI system that synchronizes room-cleaning schedules with check-out patterns, guest preferences, and staff availability — speeding up the time it takes to clean and prepare guest rooms by 20%. IHG followed suit with predictive housekeeping models that anticipate peak cleaning times and allocate resources accordingly. Four Seasons Peninsula Papagayo used Winnow's AI waste-tracking tools and cut food waste by roughly 50% within eight months.

Marriott framed its pilot of an AI-driven room-assignment engine as "empowerment, not replacement." Frontline staff co-designed the tool alongside developers, shaping workflows and decision rules while maintaining override authority. Today the system processes more than 1.2 million room assignments across the hotel chain in just seconds.

Nordic chain Scandic is rolling out Quinyx (an AI workforce management tool) to forecast demand and align schedules and labor costs with occupancy, while other hospitality operators are tapping platforms such as Nowsta to screen and onboard temporary workers rapidly.

On the restaurant side, Bojangles' AI-powered drive-thru voice system Bo-Linda, created by tech company Hi Auto, has reduced employee workload by a third, taking payments, upselling, and maintaining a 95% order accuracy. "She has become so integral to the restaurant's operations that workers now think of her as another employee," said Richard Del Valle, Bojangles CIO.

Chili's Grill & Bar improved scheduling efficiency by implementing Fourth IQ as its AI-driven forecasting tool. By analyzing historical sales data and external factors, Chili's achieved a 20% improvement in scheduling accuracy.

Sources: BCG – AI-First Hotels | Getcraver – Restaurant AI Trends | Fourth – AI in Restaurants

So what: The differentiation between AI signal and noise in operations is measurable and simple: does the vendor have a specific outcome (20% faster room cleans, 95% order accuracy, 50% waste reduction with attribution to the tool) or a general efficiency claim? Operators evaluating vendors should demand case studies with control-group comparisons, not aggregate market statistics.

People & Skills

The AI skills gap in hospitality is widening faster than training programs can close it.

HSMAI published its final talent pipeline research in May 2026, identifying a "confidence-preparedness paradox" among hospitality students: students rated their confidence in applying AI to workplace tasks at 3.24 out of 5 and their academic preparation at 2.78 out of 5, suggesting they develop AI skills through independent experimentation rather than structured coursework. "Students are entering the workforce with established AI habits and confidence, but employers must equip future talent with the training, governance and decision-making frameworks needed to apply AI tools successfully in real-world hotel operations," said Brian Hicks, HSMAI president and CEO.

Students mainly use AI for writing, research and conversation, while use for operational and analytical tasks is less common, despite identifying data analysis as an important career skill.

BCG quantifies the skills shortage at the industry level: only 2.9% of full-time employees in travel and tourism possess AI skills, compared with 21% in the tech and media sectors. There are signs of progress: AI-skilled full-time-equivalent workers in hospitality are growing nearly 5% year over year, and the average AI-literate worker now has about four distinct AI skills.

At the structural level, the hospitality industry is expected to face a gap of 8.6 million workers — approximately 18% below required staffing levels — by 2035, according to the World Travel & Tourism Council.

A 2026 workforce analysis by Resume Now shows that hospitality ranks as the most exposed sector for AI skills gaps, followed by healthcare, financial services, and logistics. These sectors rely heavily on frontline roles, where training is harder to scale and daily operations leave little room for upskilling.

Sources: Asian Hospitality – HSMAI Talent Report | BCG – AI-First Hotels | Hotel Dive – WTTC Workforce Shortfall

So what: AI tools are being deployed faster than operators can train staff to use them. For PE-backed hospitality SaaS: onboarding and in-product AI literacy are now product requirements, not support add-ons. The operators most likely to churn are those whose teams never got past using the AI for writing tasks.

Governance & Security

Two governance vectors are converging on hospitality AI deployments: the DOJ's bulk data rules and a rapidly worsening cyberattack surface.

DOJ DSP (28 CFR Part 202) — a national security regulation targeting foreign access to bulk U.S. sensitive personal data — has created unanticipated compliance exposure for hospitality AI. The final rule became effective April 8, 2025. The bulk data thresholds are low enough that many hospitality operators will trigger them quickly: precise geolocation data (≥1,000 U.S. devices), biometric identifiers (≥1,000 U.S. persons), personal financial data (≥10,000 U.S. persons). Geofencing, in-property foot-traffic modeling, facial recognition check-in, and AI fraud scoring all create covered datasets. Hospitality companies should implement a transaction-screening approach similar to export controls and ensure vendor contracts restrict offshore data access and prohibit onward AI training use. [Source: Hospitality Technology — article content PAYWALLED/BLOCKED; search snippet only.]

On cybersecurity: the average cost of a data breach in hospitality rose from $3.62 million in 2023 to $3.86 million in 2024. Recent analysis uncovered 95,040 vulnerabilities across hospitality companies, including 3,884 unique CVEs; of these, 14,318 were classified as critical.

AI isn't perfect in cybersecurity. If a hotel depends too much on AI without proper checks, security gaps or privacy problems can happen. For hospitality providers, AI should be viewed as a supportive tool, not a standalone solution. At the same time, AI also empowers criminal groups. They are already improving phishing emails, creating convincing deepfakes, and cloning voices.

The 2023 MGM Resorts breach (social engineering at the help desk, costing ~$100 million) and a 2024 breach of the Otelier hotel management platform — compromising customer data from hotel brands including Marriott, Hilton, and Hyatt, exposing 437,000 customer email addresses along with travel booking details and partial credit card information — remain the defining incidents that frame operator risk perceptions.

Sources: Help Net Security – Hospitality Cybersecurity | [Hospitality Technology – DOJ Bulk Data (PAYWALLED)]

So what: Any hospitality AI deployment that ingests geolocation, biometrics, or payment history needs a legal review against the DOJ DSP thresholds before it reaches production. The low thresholds (1,000 devices for geolocation) mean even a small pilot hotel with an in-property foot-traffic model is likely covered. This is an active compliance gap that most operators do not yet know they have.
