## AI VISIBILITY & DISCOVERY

Google completes its agentic hospitality stack in a single week, and the reservation platforms fight to stay in the funnel.


**Google** rolled out in-chat hotel booking across AI Mode in the U.S. on August 27, letting travelers find, compare, and reserve rooms via natural language and Google Pay — while the hotel or OTA remains the merchant of record handling the charge, confirmation, and support. Launch partners include Booking.com, Expedia, Hilton, Marriott, IHG, Choice, Wyndham, Priceline, Hotels.com, and Trip.com. The update deepens Google's travel stack with live loyalty-based points-and-miles pricing for flights and hotels; flight booking remains outside AI Mode for now, making hotels the first test of consumer willingness to book through the AI bot.
 Critically, 
Google passes only the minimum data needed to complete a booking — name, payment, occupancy, property — and withholds broader chat context, inspiration, and decision signals even in aggregate, reducing partner visibility into the path to conversion.



**Google Maps** simultaneously added agentic food-ordering and hotel-booking capabilities to its "Ask Maps" feature on August 6, 2026. Users can ask for restaurants by natural language ("Where can I order vegan avocado toast and an oat milk latte near home?"), and after selecting a restaurant can place an order through Square, Toast, or Uber Eats. Ask Maps also draws on Personal Intelligence from users' Gmail and Google Calendar to personalize responses about upcoming flights, dinner reservations, and hotels.



**OpenTable** published its 2026 Top 100 Hotel Restaurants in America — a ranking drawn from more than 10 million verified diner reviews — and simultaneously upgraded its generative AI dining assistant, Concierge, to operate directly from the platform's homepage for the first time. The double announcement marks OpenTable's most aggressive move yet to position itself as the AI discovery layer that intercepts the "where should I eat?" query before travelers reach a search engine or review site.
 
OpenTable has publicly identified Perplexity as the real-time web grounding layer and OpenAI as the underlying LLM inference engine. The grounding in live reservation inventory is the architecturally significant part: the system cannot tell a user that a restaurant is open when the reservation data shows it is full. OpenTable has also built connections to Microsoft Copilot, Amazon Alexa+, and Google's Model Context Protocol.
 Survey data reveals the business case: 
60% of respondents planning summer travel say they booked a hotel because of its on-site restaurant, and hotel restaurant dining by travelers rose 13% year-over-year.



**BrightEdge** data shows restaurants went from a 10% AI Overview trigger rate in early 2025 to 78% by February 2026 — one of the fastest-growing verticals tracked. This means the AI surface is rapidly absorbing restaurant search intent that previously stayed inside the traditional Google local pack.
 
Phocuswright found 56% of US leisure travelers used AI for at least one trip in the past 12 months, up from 43% in late 2025, and 36% of AI-using travelers use AI for restaurant recommendations specifically.


Sources: [Skift](https://skift.com/2026/08/27/googles-agentic-hotel-booking-tool-comes-to-ai-mode/), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/06/google-maps-adds-agentic-features-including-food-ordering-and-hotel-bookings/), [TechTimes/OpenTable](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320769/20260716/best-hotel-restaurants-2026-opentable-list-goes-live-ai-concierge-moves-homepage.htm)

**So what:** Google has quietly inserted itself into every decision moment that matters — restaurant discovery, table booking, hotel search, and now hotel payment — while keeping the revenue from OTA partners. Any operator not actively managing their Google Business Profile, schema markup, and live inventory feeds is already invisible to a growing share of AI-driven bookings. The 79% of hotel links in AI Mode currently routing through Google Business Profiles is the number to act on.

## VENDOR MOVES

The reservation-platform land-grab accelerates, with American Express now controlling the largest bookable dining network in the world.


**American Express** announced the proposed acquisition of TheFork — a leading online restaurant reservation and management platform in Europe — from Tripadvisor for $700 million in cash. TheFork connects millions of diners with more than 50,000 restaurants across 11 European countries. The proposed transaction is expected to close before the end of 2026, subject to labor consultation and regulatory approvals.
 
American Express estimates that TheFork, Resy, and Tock would together create a dining network of approximately 75,000 bookable venues.
 
TheFork generated $232 million in revenue and $28 million in adjusted EBITDA during the 12 months ending with the first quarter of 2026; at $700 million the deal equals approximately three times trailing revenue and 25 times trailing adjusted EBITDA.
 Amex's Rafa Marquez, President of International Card Services, said: "Dining is one of the most important ways people engage with our brand."


**Resy and Tock** are merging under the Resy brand this summer — American Express is combining its two reservation platforms into one, bringing Tock's inventory of fine-dining restaurants and wineries to Resy for the first time and doubling its venue library to over 25,000 restaurants. It also marks an end for Tock as an independent brand.
 
A partnership between American Express and Anthropic enables Claude users to book restaurant reservations directly through the AI model on the Resy platform, generating relevant recommendations and allowing users to view available tables.



**DoorDash** launched "Ask DoorDash," a new AI-powered chatbot allowing customers to order food, build grocery carts, and make reservations using photos and text prompts, initially in select markets. DoorDash is in the middle of a massive investment cycle involving the creation of a unified tech platform to house all its brands following a string of acquisitions; its stock is down 33% compared to the Nasdaq's roughly 8% gain, after announcing plans to spend "several hundred million dollars" on new products and technology in 2026.



**Deliveroo** launched Deliveroo Reservations in London — powered by SevenRooms' reservation and CRM technology — giving Deliveroo users access to tables at hundreds of SevenRooms partner restaurants including Dishoom, Barrafina, and Murano. This follows DoorDash's acquisitions of Deliveroo and SevenRooms in 2025 and is the first major product integration since the businesses came together. The feature includes live availability, "What's Available Tonight," calendar integration, and group bookings for up to eight people.
 Deliveroo VP Suzy McClintock said: "Whether it's a Deliveroo order or a reservation in-store, we want to drive discovery, demand and revenue across every channel."


**SevenRooms** launched new guest intelligence tools on July 21, 2026: Global Guest Search allows restaurants to search eligible DoorDash and Deliveroo consumers so staff can recognize guests at booking; SevenRooms Voice AI, powered by ElevenLabs, has handled more than 400,000 calls across 600+ restaurants, capturing up to 25% more reservations by handling bookings around the clock; and a redesigned first-party booking experience has driven an average 25% lift in premium experience sales.



**Toast** launched Toast Drive-Thru in 2026 as a unified solution for enterprise quick-service operations, bringing together hardware, POS-native software, and AI voice ordering integrations. The Spring 2026 release included more than 20 updates, with Toast IQ Grow — an AI-powered Marketing Agent that builds audiences and automates campaigns — designed to take on the day-to-day work of marketing for restaurant operators.



**OpenAI** stepped back from completing hospitality transactions directly: the company shifted ChatGPT's focus from completing commerce transactions to product discovery, with checkout processes moving to third-party apps. The strategic pivot relieved concerns for online travel agencies like Expedia and Booking Holdings, whose shares rose following the announcement.


Sources: [BusinessWire/AmEx](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260614939103/en/American-Express-Announces-Proposed-Acquisition-of-TheFork-a-Leading-European-Restaurant-Booking-Platform), [Restaurant Business Online](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/technology/reservation-services-resy-tock-are-merging), [Grocery Gazette/Deliveroo](https://www.grocerygazette.co.uk/2026/05/07/deliveroo-moves-beyond-delivery-with-london-restaurant-bookings/), [SevenRooms press](https://sevenrooms.com/press/guest-intelligence-tools-launch/), [RTN/Toast](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/06/toast-advances-restaurant-operations-with-connected-pos-ai-and-guest-engagement-technology/)

**So what:** The reservation platform market is now functionally a three-ecosystem war — American Express (Resy + Tock + TheFork, 75,000 venues), DoorDash (SevenRooms + Deliveroo), and Booking Holdings (OpenTable, still #1 in 13 of 15 top US cities). Mid-market restaurant groups should model now which ecosystem serves their guest profile, because switching costs and data ownership terms are materially different across the three. Whoever controls the reservation data controls the personalization layer.

## OPERATIONAL AI

Industry leaders at the NRA Show delivered the bluntest public AI skepticism of the year, while specific deployments showed genuine outcomes.


At the National Restaurant Association Show, Restaurant Dive asked executives what people are getting wrong about the restaurant sector — "almost universally, among restaurant tech leaders, the answer was artificial intelligence." Despite the skepticism, enthusiasm was high on the show floor: Square's head of food and beverage said "AI is the biggest thing of this entire show this year." Many chains are still working to integrate new technology, including Shake Shack using AI to alert managers to inefficiencies, Culver's rolling out computer vision for drive-thru flow, and Dairy Queen expanding a voice AI drive-thru test with Presto.



Part of the problem, per Popmenu CEO Brendan Sweeney, is that overadoption of large language models rests on shaky fundamentals: the cost of AI tokens required to operate coding services can exceed the salaries of the workers the technology purports to replace. With OpenAI pursuing an IPO, AI developers could face greater pressure from investors to turn a profit — something OpenAI has failed to do.
 Toast CMO Kelly Esten said: "There's some parts of running a restaurant that are all about reliability... AI can't replace the core aspects of restaurant technology. It can augment robust underlying programs."


Starbucks ended its computer vision inventory counting system — which employees called "unreliable" — in favor of traditional stock-keeping methods, per Reuters reporting.



In a 2026 product update, Google Cloud reported that Wendy's FreshAI was expanding across 24 US states and handling about 50,000 orders per day, with a reported 95% success rate.
 
Chipotle's Autocado system handles avocado prep — one of the most physically repetitive tasks in the kitchen — while the rest of the process stays in human hands. Sweetgreen's Infinite Kitchen format continues expanding gradually, with automation focused on portioning and assembly, with staff remaining part of the process.


Sources: [Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/restaurant-technology-executives-warn-against-ai-overdependence/821024/), [KitchenHub Blog](https://www.trykitchenhub.com/post/restaurant-ai-in-2026-what-worked-and-what-didnt/)

**So what:** The gap between AI enthusiasm on the trade show floor and the specific operator outcomes that actually pencil out is widening. The NRA Show consensus — that AI should be treated "like any other innovation" rather than a category-defining cure-all — is the most commercially useful framing in this briefing. Operators should demand a specific metric and a 60-day pilot before committing to any new AI layer.

## FUNDING & M&A

Hospitality tech investment surpassed $1 billion for the year, while broader sector M&A hit a 106.8% year-over-year jump in deal value.


U.S. travel, leisure and hospitality M&A deal value surged to $39.6 billion in H1 2026, a 106.8% increase year over year, according to KPMG — but the number of deals (402) fell 7.6%, signaling buyers pursuing "bigger, higher-conviction" deals that offer "customer ownership, loyalty ecosystems, distribution control, pricing power and scalable operating models." PwC's midyear outlook found investors eyeing a "narrower set of assets," with focus on premium, wellness-anchored and data-rich assets.
 Daniel Fischer of KPMG said: "The true value of these large deals won't be measured on signing day, but by a company's ability to seamlessly transform the business."


Across hospitality tech specifically, 40 companies raised a combined $1 billion between April 2025 and March 2026. PMS companies raised more than any other category — seven companies, a combined $408.1 million — and the three largest raises (Mews $300M, Kindred $125M, Limehome €75M) landed within a 90-day window between December 2025 and February 2026.
 
AI-led guest experience platforms were a "standout" investment category, with Duve, Chatlyn, Conduit, and Canary Technologies raising a combined $152.6 million. Canary notably acquired OpenKey in February to expand its access to door lock providers. All four were targeting the challenge of providing personalized, responsive service with leaner teams.



**Owner**, a tech company aimed at helping independent restaurants grow their online business, raised $240 million in a Series D funding round led by Goldman Sachs Alternatives, bringing its total valuation to $2.3 billion. With AI investment, Owner aims to be the "AI CMO and CTO for local businesses" — restaurants can ask Owner to promote a menu item, and an AI agent creates the promotion, updates the website, and runs the campaign. CEO Adam Guild said: "The world is racing to build AI to replace people's jobs. Owner is building AI to do the opposite."



**Palona AI** launched what it calls a multimodal AI operating layer for physical businesses, beginning with restaurants, as it closed its Series A financing — bringing its total funding to $20 million. The platform is organized around Revenue Expansion, Revenue Intelligence, and Operations Excellence, capturing customer demand from calls, catering requests, and private events and converting operational signals into workflows for managers or AI agents.
 
**Slang AI** raised $36 million in Series B financing in February 2026, bringing its total funding to $68 million; the company says its technology is deployed at more than 2,000 restaurant locations, handling reservations, private dining, catering, and common guest questions.


Sources: [Hotel Dive/KPMG](https://www.hoteldive.com/news/hospitality-mergers-acquisitions-H1-2026-kpmg/827622/), [Hotel Dive/Abode](https://www.hoteldive.com/news/hospitality-tech-attracts-1b-in-funding-pms-ai-leaders/817439/), [Restaurant Business Online/Owner](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/technology/owner-raises-240m-ai-native-restaurant-tech), [RTN/Palona](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/08/palona-ai-expands-beyond-voice-ordering-with-new-restaurant-operations-platform-and-20-million-in-funding/)

**So what:** PE buyers in hospitality are now explicitly pricing data assets alongside physical assets — "customer ownership, loyalty ecosystems" are in the KPMG investment thesis language, not just technology features. For PE-backed hospitality SaaS, this is a valuation signal: platforms with clean, unified guest data that can demonstrate loyalty impact will command materially different multiples than those with fragmented records.

## GOVERNANCE & SECURITY

AI deployment is running well ahead of governance in hospitality — and the liability is beginning to crystallise.


Risk in the modern hotel environment does not live at the perimeter: it lives inside the organization, accumulating through operational decisions made under pressure, vendor relationships managed for convenience, and technologies adopted faster than the governance frameworks designed to oversee them. In practice, a reservations agent's credentials can unlock the personal details of 200,000 guests; a departing revenue manager walks out with a loyalty database export; a channel management vendor has been processing guest data for three years under a contract that predates current data protection requirements; and a facial recognition pilot is live at check-in under a vendor agreement with no mention of biometric data governance — none of these triggers a security alert.



Kiteworks research across 225 enterprise leaders quantifies the gap: 100% of organizations have AI on their 2026 roadmap, yet 63% cannot enforce purpose limitations on AI agents, and 60% cannot quickly terminate a misbehaving one.
 
The EU AI Act's Article 50 transparency obligations for interactive and generative AI systems apply from August 2, 2026 — meaning restaurant operators deploying voice agents, web chat, automated reservation assistants, or synthetic promotional content across EU-facing markets now face clear disclosure requirements.



The average cost of a data breach in hospitality was $3.86 million in 2024.
 
Biometric data is widely used in hospitality — facial recognition for access control, timekeeping, drive-throughs, or kiosks — and many state privacy laws treat biometrics as sensitive data requiring explicit consent, risk assessments, and heightened security. "Personal data" now encompasses far more than traditional personnel or guest records, including digital productivity and workplace surveillance data and information collected through websites, mobile apps, and chatbots.


Sources: [ASIS Security Management](https://www.asisonline.org/security-management-magazine/articles/2026/08/hospitality-data-risk/), [Kiteworks/Cybersecurity Insiders](https://www.cybersecurity-insiders.com/may-2026-is-the-forecast-ai-governance-just-became-data-governance/), [AllAINews/EU AI Act](https://allainews.net/ai-for-restaurants/)

**So what:** Hotels and restaurant groups running AI pilots without a formal legal basis for the underlying data processing are building technical debt that will eventually surface as regulatory exposure or a breach. The ASIS article's five-step sequence — map real data, revoke lapsed access, delete aged records, audit vendor contracts, govern AI at procurement — is the right operational checklist for any property adding a new AI system.

## DATA & INTEGRATION

The hotel PMS is becoming the site of the industry's defining AI bottleneck.


Every major hotel chain has now committed publicly to an agentic AI roadmap — Marriott's $1B+ agentic mesh, Hilton's AI Planner, Hyatt and Accor inside ChatGPT, IHG's AI-enhanced CRM — but all are bottlenecked at the same layer: the property management system was built when a guest was a stay at a property, not a relationship across the chain.
 
The strategic question in hotel technology has shifted: it used to be "Can your PMS talk to system X?" — now it is "Can an AI agent read across all your integrated systems and act on behalf of the front desk?" AI receptionists now answer guest questions by querying PMS, CRM, and POS data simultaneously; conversational AI handles reservation changes that write back to the PMS automatically.



Q2 2026 confirmed that chains are racing to claim AI distribution surfaces while PMS vendors deepen operational automation. Wyndham launched a dedicated ChatGPT app in May 2026, allowing OpenAI users to search Wyndham properties and start booking flows inside the ChatGPT experience — extending its earlier Claude integration and planned Google AI Mode presence.
 
A Mews and Canary Technologies May 2026 survey put hotel AI adoption at roughly 98%, meaning nearly every property surveyed reported using AI somewhere in its operation. However, 59% said the front-desk welcome and check-in should remain human-led.


Sources: [Apptad](https://apptad.com/insights/the-hotel-industrys-ai-problem-lives-in-the-pms/), [Vertize/Q2 2026 roundup](https://www.vertize.io/blog/hotel-pms-vendor-ai-news-q2-2026-wyndhams-chatgpt-app-choice-hotels-x-aws-and-what-it-means-for-your-stack), [HotelSmarters](https://hotelsmarters.com/blog/hotel-pms-integration)

**So what:** For independent hotels and small chains, the actionable gap is the property-level multilingual guest concierge — chains and PMS vendors are both building in other directions and neither is solving this problem. Operators who solve it first with a well-integrated third-party layer have a genuine competitive differentiator against branded competitors relying on the chain's centralized AI.
