## Vendor Moves

The reservation and delivery layers of the restaurant industry are consolidating fast, with three deals reshaping who controls diner data and booking flow.

**DoorDash** launched "Ask DoorDash" on June 11, a conversational AI chatbot inside the app that lets users describe what they want in plain language, upload cookbook photos to build grocery carts, or request a reservation with natural-language prompts. 
"Traditional search works best when you know the exact restaurant or table you're looking for," DoorDash wrote. "Ask DoorDash is designed for the moments when you don't." The company described it as part of a market in which food delivery apps and tech giants are betting that AI can help make shopping more conversational, noting that Uber Eats launched an AI-powered Cart Assistant in February and Instacart has its own AI shopping assistant.
 
The stakes are high for DoorDash, which is in the middle of a massive investment cycle that involves creating a unified tech platform to house all its brands following a string of big acquisitions, including a $1.2 billion deal for restaurant booking platform SevenRooms and the nearly $4 billion acquisition of Deliveroo.


**American Express** agreed on June 15 to acquire **TheFork** from Tripadvisor for $700 million in cash — its third dining platform acquisition after Resy (2019) and Tock (2024). 
TheFork operates in 11 European countries and connects users with more than 50,000 restaurants. The deal structure is an all-cash put-option agreement; Amex grants Tripadvisor the option, an employee works-council consultation runs, and once Tripadvisor exercises it the two sign a definitive purchase agreement. The companies expect it to close before the end of 2026, subject to that consultation and regulatory approvals.
 
Restaurants get diners; Amex gets data and engagement; cardholders get access. TheFork turned over $232m in the year to the first quarter of 2026 and made $28m in adjusted earnings, so $700m is roughly three times revenue — a sign Amex is buying reach and relationships, not just cash flow.
 Separately, Amex is folding **Tock** into **Resy** this summer, sunsetting the Tock brand and doubling Resy's venue inventory to 25,000. 
In the three weeks following the announcement of the Platinum card's $400 Resy credit, there was a 36% increase in Resy reservations made by users with a U.S. Platinum card linked to their account — and a five times increase in the daily average number of accounts being linked. People using the Resy credit are spending, on average, 25% more.


**Amadeus** is set to announce two hotel AI tools at HITEC Chicago (23–26 June): AI Commerce, which makes hotels bookable through AI assistant channels, and Amadeus Max, which lets hotel staff query data in plain English. 
The bigger story is infrastructure. Amadeus is Google's only B2B hospitality partner on the Universal Commerce Protocol, the framework being built to govern how AI agents find, book, and transact hotels. If UCP becomes the standard, Amadeus will have helped write the rules. Hotels that can't connect to agentic booking channels at speed and scale risk not surfacing at all.


Sources: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/11/doordashs-new-ai-chatbot-lets-you-order-with-prompts-and-photos/) | [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/american-express-acquires-thefork-tripadvisor-700-million) | [Restaurant Technology News](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/06/american-express-expands-restaurant-technology-ambitions-with-700-million-thefork-acquisition/) | [Skift](https://newsletters.skift.com/p/ai-tools-target-hotel-booking)

So what: The reservation layer is bifurcating between a payments/loyalty stack (Amex: Resy + Tock + TheFork → 75,000 venues) and a delivery/data stack (DoorDash: SevenRooms + Deliveroo). Operators who are not actively negotiating their data ownership terms with both camps now will find themselves on the losing side of both. For a mid-market restaurant group, this cycle is the moment to audit who owns guest data after each booking, not when the next contract renewal arrives.

## Funding & M&A

The PMS is hardening into the AI control layer, and investors are concentrating capital accordingly.


The hospitality tech sector's biggest raises landed in a 90-day burst between December 2025 and February 2026: Mews ($300 million), Kindred ($125 million across two simultaneous rounds) and Limehome (€75 million). A PMS, a home-swapping platform and a tech-enabled apartment operator in the same fundraising window suggests investors are backing hospitality tech as a category, not just isolated subsegments.
 
AI-led guest experience platforms were another standout category, with Duve, Chatlyn, Conduit and Canary Technologies raising a combined $152.6 million.
 
Canary Technologies was the largest at $80 million and also acquired mobile key platform OpenKey in February 2026.
 
In total, seven PMS companies raised a combined $408.1 million, more than any other category.


Jessica Gillingham, CEO of Abode Worldwide, framed the pattern: 
"Investors are concentrating capital in the platforms that hospitality businesses increasingly depend on every day, especially PMS and AI-led systems that unify operations, improve automation and strengthen the data layer underneath the business. The companies attracting the strongest investor backing share a common trait. They sit close to essential operator workflows and become more valuable over time. Unified systems generate more data, better automation and higher switching costs."


Sources: [Abode Worldwide Index](https://abodeworldwide.com/hospitality-tech-investment-index/) | [Hospitality Net](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/report/4131877/)

So what: The PMS land grab is accelerating. Mews is acquiring capabilities inward (Flexkeeping, DataChat), Canary is acquiring distribution (OpenKey). For a PE-backed portfolio operator, the switching cost curve is already bending upward — the time to lock in multi-property PMS terms is now, before the next consolidation round closes.

## AI Visibility & Discovery

The discovery funnel for both restaurants and hotels has been materially disrupted by AI-mediated search, and most operators are structurally invisible inside it.


Uberall's May 2026 benchmark report — the industry's first measuring how AI assistants recommend restaurants — found that 83% of QSR locations are effectively absent from AI-generated recommendations. AI restaurant discovery is research-heavy, not transactional: informational and comparative prompts drive nearly 79% of AI-generated restaurant responses. Brands must win preference before the moment of decision, not at the point of sale.
 
AI platforms have raised the bar on reviews: ChatGPT primarily recommends businesses averaging 4.3 stars or higher, Perplexity 4.1+, and Gemini 3.9+. AI typically recommends only 3–5 brands per query.
 "AI now decides which restaurants get discovered, and most QSR brands aren't structured for the signals AI relies on," said **Stephanie Genin, CMO at Uberall.**

**DoorDash's** own 2026 Restaurant Industry Trends Report, based on 3,000+ consumer surveys, added a commercial dimension: 
22% of consumers have used an AI tool like ChatGPT or Google Gemini to help choose a restaurant, with restaurant listing sites like DoorDash accounting for more than 41% of the sources AI tools cite when recommending restaurants. Three-quarters of consumers say they're comfortable using AI for reservations, but only 28% of operators are using AI to manage calls and customer service. An estimated 40% of reservation calls go unanswered industry-wide.


**Google AI Mode** for hotel and flight booking was announced in November 2025 but remains unlaunched as of June 2026. 
Named launch partners are Booking.com, Choice Hotels International, Expedia, IHG Hotels & Resorts, Marriott International and Wyndham Hotels & Resorts. Agentic booking is already live in the U.S. for restaurants, event tickets and appointments, but finishing a flight or hotel booking inside AI Mode is still in development.


Sources: [Uberall/BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260507962493/en/) | [DoorDash Industry Trends Report](https://about.doordash.com/en-us/news/doordash-restaurant-industry-trends-report-2026) | [Travelers Today](https://www.travelerstoday.com/articles/60289/20260622/)

So what: A restaurant or hotel with a clean structured-data presence, 4.3+ star rating, and regular review responses is in a fundamentally different competitive position than a neighbour that isn't. GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) is not optional marketing spend — it is now baseline infrastructure for discoverability. Operators should audit their Google Business Profile completeness, review response cadence, and schema markup before any other digital marketing investment.

## Data & Integration

The industry's most consequential infrastructure debate — who controls the data layer and how AI agents connect across systems — is sharpening.


Agentic Hospitality launched its TravelOS MCP Server, which enables hotels to participate directly in an AI environment without duplicating inventory, scraping rates or creating separate booking systems. The platform connects directly to a hotel's central reservation system and property management system, which remain the system of record.
 Brad Brewer, Chief AI Officer of Agentic Hospitality, said: 
"No traveler is going to download a random aggregator they've never heard of. But they will engage with a hotel brand they recognize. That's what we've enabled inside AI."


The siloed-agent problem, however, is escalating. Darko Bosancic (SVP, Sirma Group) argued in a Hospitality Net piece: 
Most current AI agents live entirely inside the vendor's own ecosystem — reading that vendor's data, automating that vendor's workflows. The moment a hotel needs that agent to interact with a different system, the intelligence stops. Furthermore, as hotels move guest data through AI pipelines at an accelerating rate, the questions of GDPR compliance, PCI DSS obligations, and data governance are becoming urgent. Most independent properties and mid-scale operators are not raising these questions — often because their technology vendors are not raising the issue with them.


Mews founder Richard Valtr framed the PMS thesis: 
"Instead of relying on a 'Frankenstack' of disconnected tools that need constant manual input, properties are moving toward AI agents that can proactively coordinate revenue, operations, and guest service in real time."


Sources: [Hospitality Net / Agentic Hospitality](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4131877/) | [Hospitality Net / AI Agent Trap](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/opinion/4131969/) | [Abode Worldwide](https://abodeworldwide.com/hospitality-tech-investment-index/)

So what: The integration question has shifted from "can your systems talk?" to "can an AI agent act across all of them in one conversation?" Mid-market operators need to audit whether their PMS exposes clean API endpoints that could be consumed by an orchestration layer — before they buy any more standalone AI tools.

## Operational AI

Restaurant operators are moving from AI curiosity into daily operational use, with real query-level data now available.


Toast released its 90-day Toast IQ report on June 10. The AI assistant — launched in October 2025 and now connected to Toast's platform serving approximately 171,000 locations — was analyzed across 125,000 locations in Q1 2026. Fine dining locations used Toast IQ 29% more than fast-casual restaurants. Operators most frequently initiated conversations about sales and revenue (47%), menu and inventory (34%), and guest and marketing (32%). The top prompt was "Create a short, easy-to-read daily briefing for my restaurant."


**Booking Holdings** reported Q1 2026 results on April 28 with Priceline's AI travel agent Penny showing early commercial signal. 
CEO Glenn Fogel said: "In very early testing from a small sample set, we are seeing a noticeable uplift from users who engage with Penny compared to non-Penny users." CFO Ewout Steenbergen noted early signs across conversion, faster search, shorter path to booking, lower cancellation rates, positive customer satisfaction and more engagement.
 
Beyond internal efforts, Booking Holdings is also partnering with OpenAI, Google, Anthropic and Amazon to position itself to meet demand for travel and related services wherever they may appear.


Sources: [Toast / BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260610036506/en/) | [PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/news/online/booking-holdings-earnings-q1-2026)

So what: Toast IQ's fine-dining data is the clearest evidence this cycle that AI adoption is not a QSR-only story. The finding that fine dining uses it 29% more than fast-casual upends the assumption that complexity is a barrier. Any operator still treating AI assistants as a future project is watching competitors compound an operational advantage.

## Governance & Security

The White House AI Executive Order (June 2) is the most material policy development for hotel security in this cycle.


On June 2, 2026, the White House issued an Executive Order titled "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security." Terence Ronson (Pertlink Limited) translated the Order's federal-facing language into the practical pressures it will place on hotels: "AI capability is now treated as national strategic capability. For hospitality, the competitive question is shifting from 'How many AI features do we have?' to 'Can we govern AI responsibly, secure our connected systems, and turn operational trust into guest experience?'"


The threat landscape is maturing in parallel. 
The 2026 Retail & Hospitality ISAC Intel Team report highlights that using publicly available information from breached databases and social media, autonomous AI agents can impersonate loyalty members, contact hotel staff using synthesized voices and request actions such as a points transfer or account changes. Unlike human attackers, AI agents can operate simultaneously across hundreds of properties and thousands of accounts, adapting their responses in real time and prioritizing the highest-value targets.



The average cost of a data breach in hospitality was $3.86 million in 2024.


Sources: [Hospitality Net / Terence Ronson](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/opinion/4132771/) | [Hotels Mag](https://hotelsmag.com/news/hotels-have-a-new-ai-cybersecurity-problem-heres-what-to-know/)

So what: The White House Order creates a cascade: federal norms on AI governance will move into insurance underwriting standards, brand-standard requirements, and enterprise procurement reviews. Hotels that cannot demonstrate where their AI already lives, what data it touches, and who supervises it are building a compliance liability. The immediate action: stand up an AI governance inventory before the next cyber insurance renewal.

## WHO TO WATCH

**Andy Fang (Co-founder, DoorDash)** — His statement "We've spent over a decade building an app that puts everything in your city at your fingertips, but more options shouldn't mean more work" positions Ask DoorDash not as a chatbot feature but as a rearchitecting of the discovery layer. With the SevenRooms and Deliveroo integrations still in progress, he is the person to watch for signals on whether the unified platform thesis holds operationally or becomes a sprawling integration debt story.

**Pablo Rivero (CEO of Resy, SVP American Express Global Dining)** — Rivero is executing the most aggressive dining-data consolidation play in the industry, simultaneously sunsetting Tock into Resy and now adding TheFork's 50,000 European restaurants. His framing — "Resy will be the singular app for the best culinary experiences" — will be tested against both European regulatory review and restaurant operator resistance to Amex ecosystem lock-in.

**Glenn Fogel (CEO, Booking Holdings)** — His disciplined framing of AI as a moat against general AI models — "We want to make sure that customers have an experience in our environment that is at least as good as they can get at a generic horizontal agent" — is the sharpest competitive positioning in the OTA space this cycle. The question is whether Penny's early conversion signal survives scale-up, and whether Amadeus's Universal Commerce Protocol ultimately routes around Booking's ecosystem rather than through it.

**Stephanie Genin (CMO, Uberall)** — The 83%-invisible finding is the single most commercially actionable number in restaurant marketing this year, and Genin owns the methodology and the messaging. Worth following for whether a second-wave study emerges with hotel/accommodation data, which would make the same case to the lodging side of the market.

**Terence Ronson (Founder, Pertlink Limited)** — His June 3 white paper translating the White House Executive Order into hotel operations language is the clearest current articulation of why AI governance is now an operational control surface, not a policy discussion. His recommendation — "know where your AI already lives, govern it deliberately, and make trust a feature you can prove" — sets a practical benchmark against which every hotel's current AI inventory should be audited.
