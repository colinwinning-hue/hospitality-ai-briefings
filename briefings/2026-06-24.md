## Vendor Moves

The guest-relationship layer of hospitality tech is consolidating fast, with payment networks, delivery platforms, and AI labs all competing for the same seat at the table.

**DoorDash** launched "Ask DoorDash" on June 11 — 
a new AI chatbot that lets users order food and groceries with text prompts and photos. The chatbot allows users to search the app for what they're looking for in their own words instead of having to scroll through restaurants and stores to build a cart.
 
In February, Uber Eats launched an AI-powered "Cart Assistant," while Instacart has rolled out an AI shopping assistant that grocers can offer to their customers.
 The move completes a trilogy: DoorDash shut down its standalone Zesty discovery app in April, folding its conversational search into the core app; it now has the full funnel — AI discovery, ordering, and reservations — under one roof via the $1.2B SevenRooms acquisition.

**American Express** filed the largest restaurant-tech deal of the cycle on June 15. 
The proposed $700 million transaction would add TheFork's network of more than 50,000 restaurants across 11 European countries to American Express' growing dining ecosystem. Combined with existing platforms Resy and Tock, the acquisition would expand American Express' network to approximately 75,000 bookable venues worldwide, creating one of the largest restaurant reservation and dining engagement platforms in the industry.
 
American Express first entered the reservation technology market through its acquisition of Resy in 2019. The acquisition of Tock in 2024 significantly broadened American Express' capabilities in reservations, ticketed events, prepaid dining experiences and hospitality operations.


**Resy and Tock** are also mid-merger domestically. 
The move boosts Resy's venue count to 25,000, but will sunset the Tock brand, formally uniting two onetime startups against reservations market leader OpenTable.
 
In the three weeks following the announcement of Amex's Platinum card dining credit, there was a 36% increase in Resy reservations made by users with a U.S. Platinum card linked to their account — and a five times increase in the daily average number of accounts being linked. People using the Resy credit are spending, on average, 25% more.
 Separately, 
as of April 24, Claude users can now explore U.S. Resy restaurants directly in their conversations when deciding where to eat. Claude connects to Resy to help diners browse available tables in real time and secure a table seamlessly.


**Toast** launched its unified drive-thru platform in April. 
Toast has introduced Toast Drive-Thru, an enterprise-grade platform designed to help quick-service restaurants improve throughput and order accuracy by bringing drive-thru operations into a single system. The launch comes as more than 140,000 drive-thru locations across the U.S. face growing pressure to deliver fast, accurate service while managing labor constraints.
 
Toast Drive-Thru includes AI voice ordering integrations with partners like Incept AI to automate order taking, Speed of Service (SoS) reporting to help identify lane bottlenecks, and Drive-Thru KDS enhancements designed to optimize kitchen assembly and load balancing.


**SevenRooms**, now under DoorDash ownership, launched "Channel Connect" in June — described as bringing hotel-style distribution management to restaurants, allowing venues to manage reservation availability across Google, DoorDash, and direct booking simultaneously from a single dashboard.

Sources: [Restaurant Technology News – TheFork](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/06/american-express-expands-restaurant-technology-ambitions-with-700-million-thefork-acquisition/) | [TechCrunch – Ask DoorDash](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/11/doordashs-new-ai-chatbot-lets-you-order-with-prompts-and-photos/) | [Fast Company – Resy/Tock](https://www.fastcompany.com/91496951/amex-resy-tock-restaurant-reservation-wars)

**So what:** The reservation layer is becoming a credit-card loyalty battlefield. Operators who signed with Tock for its data ownership ethos now sit inside the American Express ecosystem — with no clear exit path before 2027 when the tech merge is projected to complete. Independent operators should audit what data they currently own through their booking platforms before the consolidation locks it in.

## AI Visibility & Discovery

Every major consumer platform is racing to become the terminal node for dining decisions, compressing the window between inspiration and booking.

**Google** extended its AI Mode restaurant booking to eight countries in April. 
Google is rolling out AI Mode's restaurant booking feature to eight countries, letting users in the UK, Australia, Canada, and five other markets reserve tables directly through Search for the first time. Eight reservation platform partners power the feature: TheFork, SevenRooms, ResDiary, Mozrest, Foodhub, Dojo, DesignMyNight, and OpenTable.
 
AI Mode had reached 75 million daily active users by January 2026. Google has simultaneously built out AI Mode's monetization layer — in January 2026, the company introduced Direct Offers, an advertising format that lets brands surface exclusive deals within AI Mode responses.


**DoorDash's 2026 Restaurant Industry Trends Report** (May 21, Dynata-conducted, n=3,001 consumers/509 operators) surfaced the sharpest current data on AI adoption at the table. 
22% of consumers have used an AI tool like ChatGPT or Google Gemini to help choose a restaurant, making AI a new discovery signal to watch. According to Yext research, restaurant listing sites like DoorDash account for more than 41% of the sources AI tools cite when recommending restaurants. Three-quarters of consumers say they're comfortable using AI for reservations, but only 28% of operators are using AI to manage calls and customer service. With an estimated 40% of reservation calls going unanswered industry-wide, AI gives restaurants another way to capture demand.


**Zest**, a new restaurant discovery startup, launched publicly in June. 
Founded in November 2024, Zest uses a combination of transaction data and AI to make personalized restaurant recommendations based on where people actually go to eat, drink, or grab a coffee. Currently it has $1.8 million in pre-seed funding from Alexis Ohanian at 776 and Steve Jang at Kindred Ventures. In a matter of weeks, Zest has attracted over 100,000 visits post-launch.


**Booking Holdings** CEO Glenn Fogel explicitly positioned the company's own AI as a defensive moat against ChatGPT and Gemini. 
The company has partnered externally with OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and Amazon. In very early testing from a small sample set, Fogel said the company is seeing "a noticeable uplift from users who engage with Penny compared to non-Penny users."
 
Booking Holdings reported a net income of $1.1 billion for Q1 2026, a 225% increase compared to the same period last year. Adjusted EBITDA was $1.3 billion for the quarter, up 19% compared to Q1 2025.


Sources: [WinBuzzer – Google AI Mode](https://winbuzzer.com/2026/04/13/google-ai-mode-rolls-out-restaurant-booking-to-8-countries-xcxwbn/) | [QSR Magazine – DoorDash Trends](https://www.qsrmagazine.com/news/ai-is-reshaping-how-diners-discover-restaurants-doordash-study-finds/) | [PhocusWire – Booking Holdings](https://www.phocuswire.com/news/online/booking-holdings-earnings-q1-2026)

**So what:** Restaurants that are not actively feeding structured, current data into the platforms AI systems cite — listing accuracy, reviews, availability signals — are becoming invisible to an increasingly large share of top-of-funnel discovery. The 41% citation-share statistic for listing sites is a direct procurement argument for structured data investment over SEO.

## Operational AI

The "agentic" label has arrived in full force across restaurant tech. Two genuinely separate questions emerge: which products actually execute tasks, and which merely answer questions about them.

**Toast IQ** released 90-day adoption data on June 10, the most granular operator-usage evidence yet for an AI restaurant assistant at scale. 
The selected cohorts are based on an anonymous aggregated view of 125,000 U.S.-based restaurant locations that adopted the use of Toast IQ between Jan 1, 2026, and March 31, 2026.
 
Fine dining locations used Toast IQ 29% more than fast-casual restaurants. Operators most frequently initiated conversations about sales and revenue (47% of restaurants using the product), menu and inventory (34%), and guest and marketing (32%). The top prompt: "Create a short, easy-to-read daily briefing for my restaurant."


**PAR Technology and Square** both launched agentic products in April. 
PAR Technology and Square unveiled AI products that can assist operators with their business and even complete tasks on their behalf. They joined a number of other companies, like Toast and Thanx, that have launched similar tools over the past year.
 
PAR's new PAR Intelligence system features several agents — Insights Agent, Offers Agent, Developer Assist Agent. PAR says the agents provide operators with "one source of truth." Square's Managerbot is proactive: it surfaces insights that it believes the restaurant needs to know, and it can perform tasks such as drafting employee schedules, creating and running email campaigns, and generating purchase orders for supplies.
 Charter Foods (Taco Bell franchisee) tested PAR's agent and asked it to identify stores that could benefit by staying open later; late-night sales increased 20% as a result.

**Oracle and NetSuite** launched Restaurant Operations in March, 
designed to help restaurants increase operational efficiency and profitability by unifying inventory, procurement, scheduling, production, and cash management data in a single AI-enhanced platform. The solution also unifies back-office operations by consolidating data from Oracle Simphony Cloud and other POS systems, giving operators a single source of truth.


**Actabl** launched Altitude on June 12, described as "AI That Gives Hotel Executives Answers They Can Trust in Seconds" — drawing on property-level financial and operational data across the Actabl portfolio (including Hotel Effectiveness, ProfitSword, and Aptech). No operator outcomes published at launch.

**Canary Technologies** launched an Agentic Sales Coordinator for hotel group and event sales on June 15, claiming autonomous handling of RFP intake and follow-up workflows. No case-study evidence available at time of briefing.

Sources: [BusinessWire – Toast IQ 90 Days](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260610036506/en/90-Days-with-Toast-IQ-How-Restaurant-Operators-Are-Using-Toast-IQ-to-Find-Time-Protect-Margins-and-Grow) | [NRN – PAR/Square Agents](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology/the-hot-new-restaurant-tech-trend-ai-agents) | [PRNewswire – Oracle/NetSuite](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/oracle-and-netsuite-deliver-new-ai-powered-solution-for-restaurant-operations-302729269.html)

**So what:** Toast IQ's 90-day data showing fine dining as the heaviest adopter is counterintuitive and strategically important — it suggests margin pressure, not just tech appetite, is driving AI engagement in segments that previously resisted it. For operators evaluating agentic tools, the PAR/Charter Foods case (location-specific, measurable revenue outcome) is the benchmark to demand.

## Funding & M&A


Hospitality technology companies raised more than $1 billion across 40 businesses in the past year, with PMS platforms capturing the largest share and Mews alone closing a $300 million round. PMS businesses captured $408 million of that total — more than any other category. AI-led guest experience platforms were the second standout category, with Duve, Chatlyn, Conduit and Canary Technologies raising a combined $152.6 million.
 
The early-stage picture is also notable: 19 of the 40 rounds were raised at pre-seed, seed, or Series A, and companies founded in 2023 alone account for ten of the cohort. Mews' acquisitions of Flexkeeping and DataChat in late 2025 point to the broader pattern: the best-funded PMS businesses are building platforms through acquisition rather than integration dependency.


**Journey** raised $15 million in June to expand a loyalty network specifically for independent hotels, challenging OTA-dependent loyalty models. Cloudbeds simultaneously announced a strategic partnership with Journey. The pairing — a funded loyalty platform partnering with a PMS challenger — is the independent hotel sector's most concrete infrastructure bet against chain loyalty programs this cycle.

**American Express's** $700M TheFork move is the largest single restaurant-tech acquisition of the year and the clearest statement that payment-card companies, not tech vendors, are becoming the durable owners of the dining transaction layer.

Sources: [Hotel Dive – $1B funding](https://www.hoteldive.com/news/hospitality-tech-attracts-1b-in-funding-pms-ai-leaders/817439/) | [Hospitality Net – funding analysis](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/editorial/4131882/hospitality-tech-investment-topped-1-billion-pms-platforms-led-the-way-ai-forces-hotels-to-rethink-what-service-means)

**So what:** For PE-backed hospitality SaaS companies, the signal is clear: investors are backing platforms that sit close to essential operator workflows and compound in value via data — not standalone AI features. The two segments attracting the most capital (PMS control layer, guest experience AI) are both fundamentally data-ownership plays.

## Governance & Security


On June 2, 2026, the White House issued an Executive Order titled "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security."
 Terence Ronson of Pertlink Limited published the most operationally grounded interpretation for hotels. 
The paper translates the Order's federal-facing language into practical pressures for hotels across cybersecurity, AI governance, procurement, vendor strategy, workforce, and data sovereignty. The Order's significance lies less in any single clause than in what it collectively recognizes: that artificial intelligence is now a national strategic capability, a cybersecurity priority, an economic competitiveness engine, and a critical infrastructure dependency all at once.


The practical risk vector for operators is vendor chain exposure. 
Hospitality companies are accelerating adoption of AI — from dynamic pricing and marketing personalization to fraud detection, digital key platforms, call-center transcription, and biometric check-in. These deployments typically require massive volumes of customer and device data and often rely on third-party vendors, offshore development teams, and cloud-hosted model pipelines. Hospitality companies routinely touch at least four categories of sensitive personal data under DOJ's DSP rules — identifiers, geolocation, biometrics, and financial data.


Sources: [Hospitality Net – AI Security EO](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/opinion/4132771/when-national-ai-security-becomes-a-hospitality-imperative) | [Hospitality Technology – DOJ Data Security](https://hospitalitytech.com/beyond-hipaa-navigating-dojs-new-bulk-data-thresholds-hospitality-ai)

**So what:** Hotels running AI-enhanced pricing, digital keys, and biometric check-in may be inadvertently creating "bulk data" datasets that trigger DOJ Data Security Program review — including when those datasets appear de-identified or tokenized. An AI governance audit before the next vendor renewal is no longer optional; it is an underwriting requirement.
