AI Visibility & Discovery

The booking path for both hotels and restaurants is being rewritten by agentic AI in real time — and the moves this cycle came from the largest players in tech, not hospitality vendors.

Google introduced hotel booking through AI Mode in Search on September 1, 2026, allowing travelers to discover and book their stay "in one seamless conversation." English-speaking users in the U.S. can review and compare hotels, choose a room from integrated partners and complete the reservation with Google Pay. Google is partnering with 10 travel websites and hotel chains for the initial rollout, including Choice Hotels International, Hilton and Wyndham Hotels & Resorts.

"Travel discovery is evolving rapidly, and Choice Hotels is actively shaping what comes next," said Anna Scozzafava, Choice's chief data, AI and technology officer. "By working with Google as an early partner for hotel booking through AI Mode in Search, we're helping make it easier for travelers to find the right Choice hotel for their trip while positioning our franchisees to compete and win in the age of agentic commerce."

Google Maps added agentic features on August 6, including the ability to order food, book hotels, and find event tickets via the "Ask Maps" feature. With the new agentic food-ordering capabilities, users can ask something like "Where can I order vegan avocado toast and an oat milk latte near home?" After selecting a restaurant, users can tap "Order online" to place an order through supported platforms such as Square, Toast, or Uber Eats.

Confirmed partners in Google's hotel booking test include Booking Holdings, Expedia, Marriott, Wyndham, IHG, and Choice Hotels, with Amadeus serving as the technical infrastructure partner for lodging. Bookings coming through conversational AI agents currently represent less than 1% of total room nights industry-wide, according to Booking Holdings' own CFO — meaning this remains an early-stage shift rather than a fully transformed booking landscape.

IHG Hotels & Resorts launched a beta of AI-powered conversational search on IHG.com and the IHG One Rewards mobile app on July 28, 2026. The new functionality brings intuitive, customized trip planning directly into IHG's digital channels, making it easier for guests to move from inspiration and discovery to booking one of more than 7,000 hotels globally. IHG CEO Elie Maalouf framed it as a milestone "that builds on years of strategic investment in our digital foundations, including the unification of our data in the cloud."

On the restaurant side, the first joint DoorDash and SevenRooms report, drawing on 3,001 U.S. consumer surveys and data from 50M+ monthly DoorDash users, found that 22% of consumers have used AI to choose a restaurant — yet only 39% of operators have updated their listings to show up in those results.

Third-party delivery is now the top driver of new customer acquisition, cited by 30% of restaurant owners. AI tools like Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT, and Perplexity now pull restaurant recommendations from delivery platforms before a customer opens a map, with 41.6% of restaurant-related AI queries citing third-party platforms as a source.

Sources: Hotel Dive — Google AI Mode hotel booking · TechCrunch — Google Maps agentic features · IHG Press Release · QSR Magazine — DoorDash report

So what: If your hotel is not connected to Google's UCP via your CRS or OTA partners, you are invisible to the growing share of travelers booking via AI Mode. For restaurants, an incomplete DoorDash/Google profile is no longer just a missed cover — it is a structural absence from AI-generated recommendations. Fix listing completeness first; it costs nothing.

Vendor Moves

The reservation industry has undergone a full restructuring, and the arms race in restaurant data is escalating into delivery infrastructure.

American Express agreed on June 15 to buy TheFork, Tripadvisor's European restaurant-reservation platform, for $700 million in an all-cash deal expected to close by year-end. TheFork booked about $232 million in revenue and $28 million in adjusted EBITDA over the twelve months to March, across roughly 50,000 restaurants in 11 countries. TheFork is the third dining-reservation platform Amex has bought, after Resy in 2019 and Tock in 2024, taking Amex's bookable-dining network to 75,000 venues.

Amex CEO Stephen Squeri framed the deal around shared strengths across "dining, travel, and experiences." A payments company is buying its way upstream into discovery, in a category where it already owns the transaction but not yet the choice — and nothing about that logic is confined to restaurants.

American Express has also partnered with Anthropic to enable Claude users to book restaurant reservations directly through the AI model on the Resy platform. When a Claude user asks for dining suggestions, the model generates relevant recommendations, allowing users to connect to the Resy app for more information and view available tables.

Resy and Tock are merging under Resy this summer, roughly doubling Resy's inventory to more than 25,000 venues. The move will also bring some Tock features to Resy, such as tiered experiences and pre-paid bookings. After Tock's catalog is absorbed by Resy, its website and app will go dark, while its restaurant management software will continue operating as part of Resy.

As part of the new chapter, Resy is activating a major partnership with Toast combining Resy and Tock's guestbook capabilities with Toast's Digital Chits technology, allowing restaurant staff to view important customer information such as wine preferences and birthdays directly on Toast Go handhelds and POS terminals during service.

DoorDash introduced an in-app AI chatbot called Ask DoorDash, allowing customers to order food, build grocery carts, and book restaurant reservations using text prompts, photos, or voice inputs. The stakes are high for DoorDash, which is in the middle of a massive investment cycle following a $1.2 billion deal for SevenRooms and the nearly $4 billion acquisition of Deliveroo.

DoorDash's Q2 total orders increased 27% year-over-year to 970 million; Marketplace GOV increased 36% to approximately $33.1 billion; revenue increased 36% to $4.45 billion. New signed venues at SevenRooms increased more than 100% year-over-year during Q2, and reservations booked through DoorDash marketplaces increased more than 150% sequentially.

DoorDash is expanding its autonomous delivery efforts and expects its Dot ground robots to handle a high single-digit percentage of orders in the company's largest test market by the end of 2026.

On the hotel side, many of the HITEC 2026 announcements surfaced AI across core hotel workflows — embedded into platforms that already support front desk operations, guest engagement, pricing, connectivity, sales and back-office management. Grevon launched at HITEC 2026 with Grevon Kore, an MCP-based connectivity layer for hospitality. The platform powers Pulse, an AI booking agent for hotel websites; Echo, an AI voice agent for inbound calls; and Ops, a staff intelligence platform — pointing to an emerging competitive front in making official hotel content usable by AI agents.

Oracle Hospitality announced Oracle OPERA Cloud Assistant at HITEC 2026, a new set of AI-powered capabilities embedded directly into OPERA Cloud workflows, including AI-assisted room assignments, AI-generated rate descriptions, operational guidance, translation and more consistent execution across properties.

Sources: Hospitality.today — Amex/TheFork · Restaurant Business — Resy/Tock merger · Pulse 2.0 — DoorDash Dot robots · HTN — HITEC launches

So what: American Express is assembling the most complete restaurant discovery-to-payment stack in the world. Any operator on OpenTable, Resy/Tock, or TheFork now effectively sits inside an Amex ecosystem. For hotels, the Grevon/Oracle MCP moves signal a protocol standard emerging — ask your CRS vendor whether they are building toward UCP/MCP compatibility before your competitors do.

Operational AI

Restaurant AI is moving from answering the phone to understanding the whole business.

Palona AI launched what it calls a multimodal AI operating layer for physical businesses, beginning with restaurants. The launch comes as Palona closes its Series A financing, bringing its total funding to $20 million, including converted SAFE notes.

Palona's larger opportunity is to establish itself as an intelligence layer that sits above or alongside the restaurant's existing technology stack, connecting POS, digital ordering, reservations, catering, loyalty, labor, inventory, payments and guest feedback into a unified picture. At Cali BBQ, Palona reported Father's Day revenue increased 20% year-over-year after more than a year in production — though the company has not published enough methodology detail to independently verify attribution.

Toast now positions Toast IQ as an AI assistant for restaurants and food-and-beverage retailers. That direction became more visible with the launch of Toast IQ Grow, a marketing-focused solution introduced as part of Toast's Spring 2026 product release, with Toast describing the release as more than 20 updates across marketing, payroll, inventory and operations. At the center is an AI-powered Marketing Agent that can build audiences and automate campaigns across channels.

Toast also launched Toast Drive-Thru in 2026 as a unified solution for enterprise quick-service operations, bringing together hardware, POS-native software, AI voice ordering integrations and services.

Nesto, a Germany-based restaurant workforce platform, raised $12 million in growth equity from Expedition Growth Capital. Its platform analyzes historical sales data and combines it with external signals such as weather patterns, local events and seasonal trends, generating staffing forecasts weeks in advance and translating those into shift schedules that account for employee availability, labor laws, and cost targets.

The system is already deployed across more than 3,000 locations, with over 100,000 daily users. Nesto reports productivity gains of more than 10%, reductions in planning time of up to 80%, and forecast accuracy approaching 90% under optimal conditions.

On the hotel side, Actabl's AI Insights, used across more than 100 hotels, has demonstrated a measurable 13% reduction in overtime share and strengthened daily labor management — one of the cleaner operator-reported outcome claims in the recent cycle.

PAR Technology released its 2026 Industry Report: AI on the Diner's Terms, finding that consumer discomfort with restaurant AI fell from 41% in 2025 to 27% in 2026 — meaning nearly 3 in 4 diners are now open to AI playing some role in their experience.

CEO Savneet Singh: "Consumers are giving operators clear direction — they are open to AI when it makes their experience better. The operators who move now to build that trust will own the guest relationship. The ones who wait will be handing it to someone else." The report also found that 62% of consumers would order through an LLM given the right offer — including 73% of Millennials.

Sources: RTN — Palona AI $20M · RTN — Toast Spring 2026 · RTN — Nesto $12M · QSR Magazine — PAR report

So what: The PAR data point that 62% of consumers would order through an LLM is the single most commercially important consumer signal of this cycle. LLM-readiness — structured menu data, verified listings, frictionless handoff from AI to POS — is now a near-term operator requirement, not a 2028 project.

Funding & M&A

Capital is concentrating around platform infrastructure rather than point solutions, on both sides of hospitality.

Hospitality technology startups raised more than $1 billion across 40 companies between April 2025 and March 2026, with PMS and AI-led platforms attracting the largest share of investment. The sector's three biggest raises landed almost back-to-back in a 90-day burst: Mews ($300 million), Kindred ($125 million across two simultaneous rounds) and Limehome (€75 million).

Seven PMS companies raised a combined $408.1 million. AI-led guest experience platforms were another standout category, with Duve, Chatlyn, Conduit and Canary Technologies raising a combined $152.6 million. These platforms are targeting one of the industry's biggest pressures: the need to deliver fast, personalized service with leaner teams.

"PMS is increasingly becoming the control layer of the hospitality tech stack. As hotel and lodging operators push to simplify fragmented systems, the PMS is taking on more responsibility — connecting teams, revenue, guest journeys and data in one place," the Abode Worldwide report states. Mews' acquisitions of Flexkeeping and DataChat in late 2025 are cited as examples of this consolidation strategy.

In restaurant technology, RTN reported that Owner raised $240 million to expand its AI-powered technology and marketing platform for restaurants — the largest restaurant AI raise visible in the current cycle. On the hotel infrastructure side, Smartness closed a €47 million Series B for its agentic AI platform for hotels and short-term rentals, while Pricepoint raised a $6.6 million seed for AI revenue management.

Sources: Hotel Dive — $1B hospitality tech funding · Abode Worldwide Investment Index · RTN sidebar — Owner $240M

So what: Investors are backing platforms that become harder to displace the longer they run — not point solutions. For a PE-backed hospitality SaaS company, this is the moment to either become the platform or find a clear workflow lane that the platforms will pay to acquire. Mid-tier point solutions without a credible data moat are the most exposed.

Data & Integration

The critical infrastructure gap is not AI model quality — it is the entity layer underneath.

Every major hotel chain has committed publicly to an agentic AI roadmap — Marriott's $1B+ agentic mesh, Hilton's AI Planner, Hyatt and Accor inside ChatGPT, IHG's AI-enhanced CRM. All of them are bottlenecked at the same layer: the property management system was built when a guest was a stay at a property, not a relationship across the chain.

The integration question in 2026 has shifted. It used to be "Can your PMS talk to system X?" Now it's "Can an AI agent read across all your integrated systems and act on behalf of the front desk?" Three patterns are emerging: AI receptionists answering guest questions by querying PMS, CRM, and POS data simultaneously; conversational AI handling reservation changes that write back to the PMS automatically; and agentic upsell engines reading guest history and reservation context to surface relevant offers at the right moment.

On the restaurant side, Lavu's 2026 Restaurant POS AI Capabilities Report found that most restaurant AI on the market is built on a single data source — leaving multi-unit operators with serious blind spots in the areas where restaurants lose the most money. "The problems that cost multi-unit operators the most money — the ones worth seven figures a year — are cross-system problems. You will not find them inside any single platform," said Saleem Khatri, CEO of Lavu.

Sources: Apptad — Hotel PMS entity layer · HTN — HITEC launches · GlobeNewswire — Lavu POS AI Report

So what: For any operator evaluating AI tools: if you cannot pull a clean guest or customer record across booking, stay, loyalty, and messaging in under 10 seconds, buy that integration before you buy AI. For restaurant groups: the seven-figure losses Lavu describes come from payroll/POS/scheduling fragmentation that no single-platform AI product can see.

Governance & Security

For hotels operating in the European Union, the most immediate EU AI Act duties in September 2026 are transparency requirements for certain interactive AI systems, with many Annex III high-risk obligations now having a later December 2, 2027 application date after the AI Omnibus changes.

Even if a vendor markets a model as neutral, the hotel remains responsible for how it uses the output. A model that predicts risk partly from postcode, language, or device type can reproduce social patterns that have little to do with the legitimate hotel objective.

The PAR Technology consumer survey found that transparency is now the clearest trust signal for restaurant guests — ranked first by 43% of diners, nearly 3x any other factor, overtaking last year's top concern (AI replacing workers).

So what: EU operators need to audit AI features that vendors have switched on quietly in software updates — governance needs to cover post-contract feature additions, not just initial procurement. For US restaurant brands, framing AI transparency as a guest-facing promise (not a compliance obligation) is a competitive differentiator supported by PAR's data.
