HOSPITALITY AI BRIEFING — 7 September 2026

AI VISIBILITY & DISCOVERY

The traveler discovery funnel is being redrawn at pace, and this cycle delivered the most concrete proof yet.

Google expanded its AI Mode on August 27 to let users plan and book trips conversationally, moving beyond simply surfacing information to actually handling parts of the trip-planning and booking process. Users can now ask AI Mode to track flight prices, book hotels, and see the cost of flights and hotels in points or miles.

The hotel booking feature has rolled out in the U.S. with partners including Booking.com, Choice Hotels, Expedia, Hilton, Hotels.com, IHG, Marriott, Priceline, Trip.com, and Wyndham Hotels & Resorts. Google completes the booking via Google Pay, but the hotel or OTA remains the merchant of record for the charge, confirmation, changes, and support. Critically, Google's AI does not book autonomously — the agentic work happens behind the scenes as the system assembles options, but travelers still choose the room, review cancellation terms, and approve payment.

Despite the fanfare, Booking Holdings' own CFO said bookings coming through conversational AI agents — free or paid — account for less than 1% of total room nights, with no significant recent change in that share.

DoorDash published its own restaurant-side discovery data. 22% of consumers have used an AI tool like ChatGPT or Google Gemini to help choose a restaurant, and restaurant listing sites like DoorDash account for more than 41% of the sources AI tools cite when recommending restaurants.

Three-quarters of consumers say they're comfortable using AI for reservations, but only 28% of operators are using AI to manage calls and customer service, with an estimated 40% of reservation calls going unanswered industry-wide.

On the hotel side, 94% of hotels are invisible in AI search results, and 68% of Google searches now end without a click — largely due to AI Overviews — making visibility a cross-functional commercial issue rather than a pure marketing task.

Outrigger Hospitality Group's CRO Jenna Villalobos described discovery responsibility as currently divided across multiple teams with no single owner — and said the company is testing GEO through Curacity VISTA, which builds visibility in trusted editorial sources that inform AI-generated travel recommendations. "Curacity is already included in our budget for next year," she said.

For restaurants, OTAs score at the top of every AI recommendation signal — deep editorial coverage, massive YouTube footprint, brand search volume — advantages built for metasearch and Google that transferred into AI eligibility without OTAs having to rebuild anything. A traveler discovered by a hotel through ChatGPT who then books through an OTA means the hotel paid for the top of the funnel; the OTA collected at the bottom. Same as before. Different pipe.

Sources: TechCrunch, Skift, DoorDash Trends Report

So what: If you are not actively managing your structured data, reviews, and editorial presence right now, you are not on the shortlist being formed before your potential guest ever opens your website. For hotel operators, this is the early-SEO moment. For restaurant operators, the 41% DoorDash-citation finding means your listing hygiene directly feeds AI recommendations — menu accuracy, photo quality, and review cadence are now SEO for LLMs.

VENDOR MOVES

The most consequential structural reshaping in this cycle is happening in the restaurant reservation market, driven by three parallel moves.

American Express plans to begin bringing Tock venues into Resy this summer, creating a combined consumer platform of more than 25,000 businesses — including over 1,200 wineries. The company has also proposed paying $700 million for TheFork; if approved, the European platform would add more than 50,000 restaurants in 11 countries and expand the American Express dining network to approximately 75,000 bookable venues.

The Toast–Amex strategic partnership aims to combine Resy and Tock's guestbook capabilities with Toast's Digital Chits technology — allowing restaurant staff to view important customer information directly on Toast Go handhelds and POS terminals during service. CEO Pablo Rivero told Forbes the harder challenge is preserving the operational flexibility that made Tock valuable — specifically the deposit, prepaid, and tiered-experience controls — while routing Tock's inventory through Resy's larger consumer funnel.

DoorDash is in the middle of a massive investment cycle that includes a $1.2 billion deal for SevenRooms and the nearly $4 billion acquisition of Deliveroo, and is building a unified tech platform to house all its brands. In June, DoorDash launched "Ask DoorDash," a chatbot that lets users order food and groceries and make reservations with text prompts and photos.

The chatbot is designed for "moments when you don't" know the exact restaurant or table you're looking for, according to DoorDash. On the merchant side, DoorDash's new AI-powered self-serve onboarding can help merchants launch more than 35% faster by pulling information directly from a merchant's existing online presence — photos, store hours, and menu items.

OpenTable launched an AI-powered dining room configuration tool that helps restaurants shuffle bookings to open up additional seats. The competition is so heated that one Boston restaurateur reported being offered a six-figure signing bonus to switch platforms, and the race is now to become the dominant all-in-one digital concierge, marketing agency, and loyalty-reward tracker.

OpenTable's Visa and Chase partnership creates cardmember incentives; DoorDash's SevenRooms creates a delivery-to-dine-in flywheel.

On the hotel side, Oracle Hospitality announced Oracle OPERA Cloud Assistant — a new set of AI-powered capabilities embedded directly into OPERA Cloud workflows designed to support AI-assisted room assignments, AI-generated rate descriptions, operational guidance, translation, and more consistent execution across properties.

Innspire launched Guest Flows, a zero-download guest journey solution delivering personalized engagement from pre-arrival through checkout — supporting mobile check-in, digital key, AI concierge, F&B ordering, upgrades, ancillary revenue, and automated checkout through a guest's smartphone without requiring an app download.

Grevon debuted at HITEC 2026 with Grevon Kore, an MCP-based connectivity layer for hospitality — powering Pulse (an AI booking agent for hotel websites), Echo (an AI voice agent for inbound calls), and Ops (a staff intelligence platform).

Sources: Forbes / Rivero interview, Boston Globe reservation wars, CNBC DoorDash, Hotel Technology News HITEC roundup

So what: The restaurant reservation market has become a data war dressed as a booking war. American Express is assembling the largest premium dining dataset in the world — loyalty spend plus guestbook plus POS via Toast. DoorDash is trying to connect delivery identity to dine-in identity via SevenRooms. For independent operators, the question is no longer "which platform fills seats?" — it is "which platform lets me keep the guest relationship I built?" On the hotel side, Oracle embedding AI inside OPERA Cloud matters more than most standalone AI tool launches because it sits in the system of record every front-desk team already uses.

FUNDING & M&A

Owner.com raised $240 million in a Series D led by Goldman Sachs Alternatives, bringing its total valuation to $2.3 billion. Owner says it wants to be the "AI CMO and CTO for local businesses" — restaurants can ask Owner to promote a menu item and an AI agent will create the promotion, update the website, and run the campaign. "The world is racing to build AI to replace people's jobs. Owner is building AI to do the opposite," said co-founder and CEO Adam Guild.

Owner crossed $100 million in ARR in 2026; independent restaurant owners running on Owner are expected to process more than $1 billion in sales through the platform this year. The strategic threat to marketplaces is explicit: Owner's platform replaces DoorDash's value proposition — traffic, tech, and data — with a flat-rate AI subscription; restaurants on Owner see 40% direct channel revenue growth and 2x repeat orders.

Wonder secured $650 million in Series D funding at a pre-money valuation of $9 billion. The investment will fund ongoing expansion, marketplace growth, and investments in technology, robotics, and AI. Wonder is using Infinite Kitchen — acquired from Sweetgreen — and recently partnered with Zipline to bring drone delivery to select restaurants in Texas within the next year.

Wonder has also filed for an additional $600 million funding round and is plotting a 2027 IPO.

Sources: Restaurant Business Online, Restaurant Dive

So what: Owner.com at $2.3B is a direct structural challenge to DoorDash's commission-on-delivery model. If even 30–40% of a restaurant's repeat orders route through a flat-rate owned channel, the marketplace becomes customer acquisition rather than revenue infrastructure. For PE-backed restaurant platforms, Owner's ARR and direct-channel metrics are worth tracking closely as a comp for what AI-native alternatives can now deliver to independents.

OPERATIONAL AI

Toast launched Toast IQ Grow as part of its Spring 2026 product release — more than 20 updates across marketing, payroll, inventory, and operations. At the center is an AI-powered Marketing Agent that can build audiences and automate campaigns across channels. Toast also launched Toast Drive-Thru in 2026 as a unified solution for enterprise QSR operations, bringing together hardware, POS-native software, AI voice ordering integrations (including with Incept AI), and Speed of Service reporting.

The reimagined Toast Local app gives guests a consumer-facing way to discover and order from restaurants using Toast, connecting operator-facing tools with consumer demand generation.

Hard data on operational AI outcomes is arriving. Restaurant365's 2026 State of the Restaurant Industry Mid-Year Report — based on 420+ operators across ~10,000 U.S. locations — found that of operators currently using AI in at least one back-office function (62%), 61% reported reduced food costs and 62% saw reduced labor costs, while 88% said AI was "saving time every week." Nearly a third said AI helped reduce costs by at least 6%. Despite cost pressures, consumer traffic has improved: only 28% said traffic increased at the start of 2026, but that number rose to 46% by mid-year.

PAR Technology's 2026 consumer survey of 1,000 diners found a meaningful trust shift: consumer discomfort with restaurant AI fell from 41% in 2025 to 27% — meaning nearly 3 in 4 diners are now open to AI playing some role in their experience. 48% are comfortable with AI making operational decisions like adjusting labor based on demand. But 47% say AI goes too far when it replaces most human interaction.

These findings arrive as agentic AI moves from concept to deployment; PAR Intelligence is positioned to help multi-unit operators connect data to coordinated action in the use cases where consumer comfort is highest. "The operators who move now to build that trust will own the guest relationship," said Savneet Singh, CEO of PAR Technology.

On the hotel side, AI at HITEC 2026 was not presented as a new product category — instead it appeared across core hotel workflows, embedded into platforms that already support front desk operations, guest engagement, pricing, connectivity, sales, and back-office management. A notable operational data point: Actabl's AI Insights has been deployed at more than 100 hotels, which collectively reduced their overtime share by 13% while strengthening daily labor management.

Sources: Restaurant Technology News / Toast, Restaurant365 / QSR, QSR Magazine / PAR

So what: The Restaurant365 mid-year data is one of the strongest operator-outcome datasets this cycle — cross-functional cost reductions across food and labor with high sample size. The PAR consumer shift from 41% to 27% discomfort is also signal: the trust gap is closing faster than most operators expected, which compresses the window for differentiation through early AI adoption.

GOVERNANCE & SECURITY

On June 2, 2026, the White House issued an Executive Order titled "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security." On its face, the order is about federal systems, frontier-model security, and national cyber defense — but it signals that AI has crossed a threshold from experimental productivity tool to critical operational infrastructure that governments now treat as a matter of national security. Terence Ronson of Pertlink Limited translated its implications for hotels: future competitiveness in hospitality technology will increasingly depend less on feature velocity and more on cybersecurity maturity, AI governance, operational resilience, explainability, transparency, compliance readiness, and trusted infrastructure partnerships. Commodity "AI-overlay" vendors — those that bolted generative features on for marketing — will be the first to fail procurement and insurer review.

On the ground, biometric data at check-in is a specific exposure: facial recognition pilots are live at some properties under vendor agreements with no mention of biometric data governance, and most hospitality AI deployments involving biometrics have been initiated through procurement processes with no legal review of the data protection implications.

In the European Union, Article 50 transparency obligations started applying on August 2, 2026, meaning guest-facing interactive AI systems generally need to make clear that a person is interacting with AI. In the United States, California, Colorado, New York City, and the FTC create overlapping rules and enforcement risk.

A Mews survey of hoteliers illustrates the governance gap: 98% of surveyed hoteliers had used AI, yet 41% had no formal AI policy — even as AI was touching pricing, guest communications, operations, and leadership work.

Sources: Hospitality Net / Ronson, ASIS Security Management

So what: The combination of a White House AI Executive Order, EU Article 50 transparency obligations that went live August 2, and 41% of hoteliers operating with no formal AI policy is a procurement and insurance risk materializing now — not eventually. Operators with biometric check-in pilots and no data governance documentation are the most immediately exposed.
