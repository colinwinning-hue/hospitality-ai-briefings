Operational AI

Agentic, conversational AI met restaurant and hotel operations from multiple angles across 48 hours, with product launches from operators of all sizes pointing to the same underlying shift: AI assistants are being embedded directly into daily workflows rather than sitting alongside them.

Sweetgreen (18 Nov) — The LA-based fast-casual chain became one of the first restaurant brands to open agentic ordering natively through both Claude and ChatGPT. Sweetgreen launched agentic ordering through the AI platforms Claude and ChatGPT; consumers can ask the AI assistants for recommendations from Sweetgreen's menu for specific dietary goals or preferences. The technical enabler is deliberate data hygiene: the sweetgreen menu already provides the flexibility and detail that AI assistants need to make useful recommendations, including ingredients, nutrition, allergens, pricing and real-time availability at each restaurant. CTO Wouleta Ayele framed the shift pointedly: "We're seeing a real shift from searching to delegating. People now hand an AI agent a goal, like a dairy-free dinner or lunch that fits their training plan, and expect it to come back with the right recommendation. That only works if the agent has ingredient information it can trust. So we've made our menu machine-readable, with every ingredient, allergen and price accurate down to the individual restaurant." Checkout still completes on Sweetgreen's own app or website, preserving first-party payment and loyalty data.

Oscar AI (18 Nov) — The Washington-based multi-unit intelligence platform for restaurant operators launched a mobile app and AI-powered chat feature simultaneously. Oscar AI announced two new offerings: a mobile app that brings its desktop platform to operators on the go, and an AI-powered chat feature available across both desktop and mobile; together, the new capabilities help restaurant leaders monitor performance, access business insights and make informed decisions wherever they are. The company reports a claimed operational impact: customers realize approximately $16,000 per store annually in improved profitability through faster detection and correction of operational issues, deployed across thousands of restaurants including Taco Bell, Burger King, Dunkin', Popeyes and Sonic franchisee groups. Since early testing, chat conversations on the platform have tripled, driven by both active users and prompts per session.

Flipdish (18 Nov) — The Irish all-in-one restaurant platform announced two AI products in a single release. Flipdish launched Flipdish AI Marketing and Flippy AI, its conversational assistant available via the Flipdish App; Flipdish AI Marketing provides independent restaurants with an always-on AI growth engine that enables automated Google Ad campaigns attracting new customers and generating more direct orders.

Flippy AI acts as a command centre inside the Flipdish App, providing access to business insights through a single conversational interface where operators can ask their restaurant anything and take action immediately — including updating opening hours across owned channels and marketplaces, or updating the menu to reflect missing items. CEO Conor McCarthy said: "AI defines the future of successful restaurant businesses. Operators who adopt AI for their marketing as well as daily operation will be the most equipped to grow, maintain their margins and compete against the big players."

Sources: Nation's Restaurant News — Sweetgreen · GlobeNewswire — Oscar AI · Hospitality Ireland — Flipdish

So what: The pattern is convergent: operational AI is moving from reporting to action. Oscar AI and Flipdish both allow operators to trigger changes — not just read dashboards — through a conversational interface. For mid-market groups, the question is no longer whether to adopt AI reporting but whether the AI can close the loop from insight to execution without a human relay in between. Sweetgreen's machine-readable menu shows the upstream preparation this requires: clean, real-time, granular ingredient data is the prerequisite for any agentic ordering integration.

Data & Integration

POS data remains the critical choke point between loyalty intelligence and in-store guest recognition, and this cycle's most concrete move came from the restaurant technology stack.

Incentivio (19 Nov) — The Boston-based AI-native loyalty platform unveiled a native integration with SpotOn's point-of-sale system. By bringing Incentivio's predictive loyalty, marketing and gift card capabilities to SpotOn, the integration gives multi-unit operators one continuous view of every guest across in-store and digital channels; for many multi-unit brands, in-store visits remain the least visible part of the guest relationship, and extending loyalty enrollment, earning and redemption to the register turns those visits into known, reachable guests. On the AI side, Incentivio's AI recommendation engine suggests add-ons based on real guest behavior, raising average order value without adding friction, and operators can tune recommendations and track each one's performance from the dashboard. SpotOn CPO Bryan Solar noted: "Keeping a guest coming back is how an independent restaurant thrives, and we want every operator to have that power."

Sources: RestaurantNews.com — Incentivio/SpotOn

So what: Most AI recommendation layers in restaurants only see digital-channel orders; in-store visits remain a black hole unless the POS is fully integrated. This integration closes that gap for SpotOn customers, making in-store visits as visible as app orders and enabling win-back campaigns triggered by lapsed in-store regulars — a capability that until now required a separate CRM investment.

AI Visibility & Discovery

The week's highest-stakes development for hotel distribution arrived not from a hotel company but from a search engine: Google was spotted testing a UI change that could quietly subordinate direct hotel booking links to OTA rates surfaced through its AI Mode.

Google (19 Nov) — Google shows a "Continue on Google" button on hotel results, sending the user through the AI Mode booking flow with their Google account instead of the direct hotel booking links; in one observed instance, the popup showed Expedia's Continue on Google rate of $170 — which covered the hotel's own website showing $158, so the cheaper direct rate was hidden. The observation was flagged by digital marketer Lluc B. Penycate and reported on 19 November; Google has issued no statement on rollout scope or whether this is a permanent feature or test.

Wonder (18 Nov) — On the restaurant side, Wonder CEO Marc Lore unveiled Wonder Create, a platform that inverts the traditional route to restaurant ownership. Anyone can log onto the Wonder Create platform and input an idea for a virtual restaurant into an LLM chat box, and the AI will generate an entire ghost kitchen brand, complete with a name, branding, logo, menu and pricing; the creator can tweak the concept any way they like — adding or subtracting ingredients, uploading recipes and images, or changing the logo and AI-generated menu images.

Wonder Create is launching in Manhattan but will expand next year to Texas; the company will also begin delivering Create creations via drone within the next couple of months, and by end of 2027, Lore said, Wonder Create will span 30 locations serving 30 million people. Subscription costs start at $10 a month per location, with creators earning a revenue share on every order.

Sources: Adriaan Dekker — Google hotel AI Mode test · Nation's Restaurant News — Wonder Create

So what: The Google "Continue on Google" test is the most commercially significant observation in this cycle for hotel operators. If Google systematically routes users into the AI Mode booking flow — which surfaces OTA rates first — a hotel's direct booking rate and associated margin advantage can be suppressed at the discovery layer, before the guest ever reaches the hotel's own site. Hotels not feeding live rates directly to Google via Hotel Center are most exposed. The Wonder Create launch has a different implication for restaurant operators: it signals that the marginal cost of launching a branded digital restaurant concept is approaching zero, which is both an opportunity (new revenue lines from existing kitchen infrastructure) and a competitive threat (the market for virtual restaurant slots could become saturated quickly).

Vendor Moves

Two of the largest players in travel distribution made structural announcements that reflect a shared bet: whoever controls the AI booking layer controls the guest relationship.

Booking Holdings (18-19 Nov) — CNBC, which reported the reorganization on November 18, framed it against a harder question for the whole online travel sector this year: what happens to booking sites if AI assistants become the place trips start; Booking's move is to supply its travel inventory, technology and planned AI capabilities to banks, airlines and online travel companies that sell trips under their own brands. Starting in January, the company will merge Booking.com Strategic Partnerships and Rocket Travel by Agoda into a single Booking Partner Division, with Agoda CEO Omri Morgenshtern leading it. Booking Holdings plans to put $100 million into Vayo, a newly launched personal AI travel service, and on October 14 it opened a waitlist for a private beta that is free and by invitation only; Vayo collects confirmations from a user's inbox into a single itinerary and monitors for price drops and reservations that open up, while the traveler signs off on any action. The division will offer partners access to 4.7 million+ properties, 500+ airlines, and a planned AI "vertical agent" built for travel — framed against Expedia's comparable B2B unit, which reported $4.8 billion in revenue in 2025 at 18% growth.

Sabre (19 Nov) — The global distribution system announced its Mosaic Travel Connector for Grok Bot, still in private beta. Sabre spent years modernizing the shopping, booking, and servicing systems that AI applications can use; now it's pursuing the major AI platforms themselves, aiming to keep travel transactions flowing through its systems even as the customer relationship moves elsewhere.

Sabre says more than 80 partners are in pilot or production with its MCP services, and the company plans to extend the connector to ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini throughout 2027, though it gave no platform-specific dates; CEO Kurt Ekert said the agency incentives Sabre pays today would disappear when it sits behind an AI platform, replaced by "a revenue share of sorts with the LLM." Ekert's sharpest observation for hotels and airlines: "the consumer will not paginate through results — they're going to get back one, two, maybe three options and choose from that."

Sources: Skift — Sabre/Grok · MarketScale — Booking Holdings · CNBC via MarketScale

So what: Sabre and Booking Holdings are running parallel strategies: insert themselves as the invisible infrastructure layer behind AI assistants before the customer relationship migrates fully to those platforms. For PE-backed hospitality SaaS companies, the signal is that GDS and OTA incumbents are spending aggressively to remain the transaction rails even if they lose the front-end conversation. Any product that depends on traditional search-and-click discovery to drive bookings is structurally exposed. Sabre's MCP connector and Booking's AI vertical agent both point toward a world where "who gets the booking" is decided by API agreements struck now, not by consumer choice at search time.
