## Funding & M&A

The biggest capital story in both sectors this cycle is a fight to own the restaurant guest relationship, with platform-layer and back-office deals dominating.


**American Express** has announced a proposed acquisition of **TheFork** from Tripadvisor for **$700 million in cash**.
 
The proposed transaction would add TheFork's network of more than 50,000 restaurants across 11 European countries to American Express' growing dining ecosystem. Combined with existing platforms Resy and Tock, the acquisition would expand American Express' network to approximately 75,000 bookable venues worldwide, creating one of the largest restaurant reservation and dining engagement platforms in the industry.
 
The proposed transaction is expected to close before the end of 2026, subject to labor consultation and customary conditions, including regulatory approvals.


**MarginEdge** closed a significant back-office round. 
MarginEdge has secured $80 million in Series D financing, bringing its total funding to $162 million, nearly doubling the capital raised since its founding. Schooner Capital and Ten Coves Capital jointly led the financing.
 
MarginEdge now supports more than 13,000 restaurants and says it has processed more than 40 million invoices representing approximately $28 billion in purchasing volume.
 Critically, the company made a data interoperability move the week before the raise: 
On August 4, the company launched a Model Context Protocol connector that allows authorized MarginEdge data to be accessed through compatible AI platforms including ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini — described as the first MCP connector from a restaurant management and bill payment platform.


**Palona AI** closed its Series A. 
Palona AI has launched what it calls a multimodal AI operating layer for physical businesses, beginning with restaurants and positioning the technology as something broader than another voice-ordering system. The launch comes as Palona closes its Series A financing, bringing its total funding to $20 million, including converted SAFE notes.
 
Palona says its platform is already deployed with operators including Din Tai Fung, Mountain Mike's Pizza, Giordano's, Rooted Hospitality Group and Cali BBQ.
 Operator evidence is early but directional: 
At Cali BBQ, Palona reported Father's Day revenue increased 20 percent year over year and that Palona became the restaurant's highest average-order-value channel as the deployment expanded to support catering and large orders.


On the hotel-tech side, the broader funding picture established earlier in the year continues to inform vendor consolidation. 
Hospitality technology startups raised more than $1 billion across 40 companies between April 2025 and March 2026, with PMS and AI-led platforms attracting the most investment. The sector's three biggest raises landed almost back-to-back: Mews ($300 million), Kindred ($125 million) and Limehome (€75 million).
 
Four AI-powered guest experience platforms raised a combined $152.6 million: Duve, Chatlyn, Conduit, and Canary Technologies — the largest at $80 million, which also acquired mobile key platform OpenKey in February 2026.


Sources: [BusinessWire/AmEx-TheFork](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260614939103/en/American-Express-Announces-Proposed-Acquisition-of-TheFork-a-Leading-European-Restaurant-Booking-Platform) | [Restaurant Technology News/MarginEdge](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/08/marginedge-raises-80-million-as-competition-for-the-ai-powered-restaurant-back-office-intensifies/) | [RTN/Palona AI](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/08/palona-ai-expands-beyond-voice-ordering-with-new-restaurant-operations-platform-and-20-million-in-funding/)

So what: AmEx now controls Resy, Tock, TheFork (pending), and a Toast integration — an end-to-end diner data stack from discovery through payment. For mid-market restaurant groups, this isn't a reservation vendor decision; it's a data sovereignty decision about which ecosystem controls your guest profile. MarginEdge's MCP connector is the most practically important plumbing move of this cycle for multi-unit operators: it means AI assistants can query live invoice and cost data, turning back-office records into an actionable conversational layer.

## Vendor Moves

The reservation market is undergoing the most visible consolidation in its history, with data ownership as the real battle line.


American Express is combining its two reservation platforms into one under Resy this summer, bringing Tock's inventory of fine-dining restaurants and wineries to Resy for the first time. The move will approximately double Resy's library to more than 25,000 venues, creating a single platform to compete against market leader OpenTable, which lists more than 60,000 restaurants worldwide.
 
Resy will activate a major partnership with POS provider Toast that will include connecting Resy guestbook data with Toast handheld devices to allow servers to view customer information such as wine preferences and birthdays. Resy will also integrate more closely with American Express cards and membership programs.


**SevenRooms** (DoorDash-owned) launched a direct countermove. 
The company unveiled Channel Connect by SevenRooms, a desktop app that aggregates reservations from various booking channels into a single system, while also automatically updating a restaurant's inventory across those channels.
 
The program will pull in bookings from reservation apps, search engines, social media, review sites, the restaurant's website, AI agents, and hotel concierges, and inject them all into the restaurant's preferred system of record. Notably, the company is offering Channel Connect for free under an open license, meaning it will be available to any restaurant, even those that don't use SevenRooms.
 This is a direct response to OpenTable's April 2026 policy: 
In April, OpenTable began requiring restaurant clients to make it their system of record and make all of their inventory available on OpenTable. Channel Connect seems to be at least partially a response to that strict policy — SevenRooms says "Channel Connect was built so that no single platform has to be the gatekeeper to your operations."


From the ground, the competition has reached cash-incentive intensity. 
Booking platforms have rolled out new features and partnerships which they say enhance the reservation experience. One Boston restaurateur reported they've been offered six-figure signing bonuses to switch platforms. After purchasing Resy in 2019, American Express in 2024 paid $400 million for Tock. DoorDash bought SevenRooms for $1.2 billion.
 
SevenRooms co-founder Joel Montaniel framed the data dimension: "Delivery and dine-in have typically been siloed data sets. So if a customer has ordered six times, and they're coming into the restaurant for the first time, are they a first-time customer or a seventh-time customer?"


**Delivery Hero** launched its own platform move for smaller operators. 
Delivery Hero unveiled a new agentic AI assistant for neighbourhood shops and restaurants via WhatsApp. Shop and restaurant owners can interact through text, voice notes and images. If the owner allows, the assistant can also make suggestions independently.
 
The AI assistant currently supports over 40,000 restaurants across Delivery Hero's global brands and markets, an early slice of the approximately 1.5 million partners on the company's platform.


Sources: [NRN/SevenRooms Channel Connect](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology/sevenrooms-launches-a-reservations-aggregator-for-restaurants) | [Boston Globe/Reservation Wars](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/08/17/lifestyle/reservation-platforms-sevenrooms-opentable-resy/) | [RTN/AmEx-Toast-Resy](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/06/american-express-expands-restaurant-technology-ambitions-with-700-million-thefork-acquisition/)

So what: Every mid-market restaurant operator needs to make an explicit platform choice now — not as a feature decision but as a data strategy decision. OpenTable offers the largest discovery network but is tightening control of inventory. Resy/Tock offers premium cardholder access and a growing link to Toast POS data. SevenRooms/DoorDash offers cross-channel guest data continuity but puts you inside a delivery ecosystem. TheFork (AmEx, pending) extends that logic to Europe. The question "who owns my guest?" is no longer rhetorical.

## AI Visibility & Discovery

The most structurally consequential development of this cycle: Google moved agentic booking from restaurants into hotels, and the mechanism it is using changes the commercial structure of discovery.


Google announced on August 6 that Google Maps' "Ask Maps" feature is gaining new agentic capabilities, including the ability to order food, book hotels, and find event tickets. The tech giant is also bringing Personal Intelligence to Ask Maps, allowing the AI tool to personalize its responses by drawing on information from a user's Gmail and Google Calendar.
 For food ordering specifically, 
users can ask a question like "Where can I order vegan avocado toast and an oat milk latte near home?" — the tool will surface nearby restaurants, and after selecting one, users can tap "Order online" to place an order through supported platforms such as Square, Toast, or Uber Eats.


The hotel booking test, confirmed August 7, goes further. 
A Google spokesperson confirmed that a limited test of AI agent hotel booking is currently underway in the United States, with Amadeus as technical partner. The feature, called Ask Maps, now returns real prices and availability. Booking Holdings CEO Glenn Fogel confirmed during the company's quarterly earnings presentation that a portion of his group's traffic already runs through this agentic booking, and that Booking Holdings counts among Google's first partners.
 
In May 2026, Google gave this ambition a technical framework at Google I/O by extending its Universal Commerce Protocol to hotels — allowing a conversation with the AI to be converted into a booking executed directly within Google's interfaces, while leaving the hotel as the party responsible for the transaction.


However, the gap between traffic signals and actual booking shifts is important to flag. 
Booking Holdings CFO said in Q2 2026 that bookings coming from conversational agents account for less than 1% of the group's room-nights. Phocuswright projects a stable split in the United States through 2029 between OTA and direct bookings at 52% and 48% respectively. The loss of referral traffic is real and documented; the shift in bookings remains, for now, a hypothesis.


For hotel chains specifically, the competitive response is underway at scale. 
Hilton's AI Planner sits on the brand side. Google, OpenAI, and the major hyperscalers have all positioned agentic AI as a 2026 priority. Phocuswright reports that more than 60% of travel businesses are experimenting with or scaling agentic AI. Agentic bookings remain a small share of total volume. Most agent flows still require human approval at the booking step.


**DoorDash** disclosed the state of its own agentic ambitions from Q2 earnings. 
DoorDash CEO Tony Xu said that agentic order volume from AI partners remains low, with the gap tracing back to most AI platforms prioritizing enterprise customers and coding tools over physical fulfillment. He framed the coming years as a fight between two forces: the battle for attention among AI platforms, and "the battle for atoms" — the physical infrastructure needed to deliver something. DoorDash is betting its future on winning the second fight regardless of who wins the first.
 DoorDash's own tools are already producing results: 
computed consumer memory improved grocery checkout conversion by approximately 24%, increased basket sizes by 17%, and reduced conversational turns by 7% during a seven-day evaluation. For restaurant discovery, the assistant achieved a 15% higher conversion rate on open-ended queries.


Sources: [TechCrunch/Google Maps Agentic](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/06/google-maps-adds-agentic-features-including-food-ordering-and-hotel-bookings/) | [Hospitality-On/Google Hotel Test](https://hospitality-on.com/en/distribution/google-launches-ai-agent-hotel-booking-test-united-states) | [PYMNTS/DoorDash](https://www.pymnts.com/news/delivery/2026/doordash-says-agentic-traffic-is-low-but-ai-gains-are-not/)

So what: Google's August 6-7 moves are the most commercially important development this cycle for hotel and restaurant operators. Ask Maps with Personal Intelligence pulling Gmail and Calendar data creates a preference-aware discovery layer that hotels cannot see into and cannot negotiate with, unlike OTAs. The Universal Commerce Protocol + Amadeus partnership is the plumbing that turns discovery into transacted bookings. Any operator not yet ensuring their inventory is machine-readable, schema-marked, and API-accessible is losing discoverability today — not in 2028.

## Operational AI

Restaurant back-office AI is consolidating from experimentation into a competitive moat race, with data depth — not feature sets — emerging as the differentiator.

Consumer readiness for AI in the dining experience shifted meaningfully this year. 
In 2025, 41% of surveyed consumers said they'd choose a restaurant that avoided AI altogether. One year later, average discomfort with restaurant AI has fallen to 27% — meaning nearly 3 in 4 diners are now open to AI playing some role in their experience, depending on how it's used.
 
62% of surveyed consumers would order through an LLM given the right offer, with Millennials (73%), Gen Z (70%), and parents (74%) showing the most enthusiasm. 43% rank transparency first when it comes to trusting AI usage at restaurants, nearly 3x any other factor.



"Consumers are giving operators clear direction — they are open to AI when it makes their experience better," said Savneet Singh, CEO of PAR Technology. "The operators who move now to build that trust will own the guest relationship. The ones who wait will be handing it to someone else."


On the back-office competition, MarginEdge's $80M raise is a signal that investors see restaurant operational data as a durable asset. 
Restaurant365 currently reports more than 50,000 restaurants using its platform and introduced R365 AI in May with dashboards, AI Advisor, and AI Scheduling. Toast also competes directly for the restaurant intelligence layer through Toast IQ, analyzing activity from more than 125,000 U.S. restaurant locations in Q1 2026. MarginEdge has more room to differentiate with restaurant groups that operate multiple concepts, POS systems or accounting environments.


For the hotel sector, the operational gap between deployment and workforce readiness remains the friction point. 
PYMNTS Intelligence found that 37% of Labor Economy workers, including those in hospitality, said their employer had introduced new automation or AI tools in the previous 12 months. Yet nearly 60% of workers affected by those tools said they received no training, suggesting deployment is moving faster than workforce readiness.


Sources: [QSR Magazine/PAR Technology Report](https://www.qsrmagazine.com/news/par-technology-releases-industry-report-on-ai-in-restaurants/) | [RTN/MarginEdge](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/08/marginedge-raises-80-million-as-competition-for-the-ai-powered-restaurant-back-office-intensifies/) | [PYMNTS/Hotel AI](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/ai-is-becoming-the-hotel-industrys-new-manager/)

So what: The PAR Technology consumer data is actionable right now: the majority of diners are ready for operational AI (labor adjustment, inventory optimization, service consistency), and personalized offers are gaining acceptance fastest among families. The 43% who rank transparency as the #1 trust factor suggests that operators who disclose AI usage proactively will outperform those who keep it invisible. For PE-backed hospitality SaaS companies, the back-office AI race is entering its consolidation phase — Toast, Restaurant365, and MarginEdge are each trying to become the system of intelligence, not just record.

## Governance & Security

Two concurrent regulatory shifts are converging on the same operators: US congressional scrutiny of Chinese AI models, and EU AI Act enforcement now live for chatbots.

**DoorDash and Chinese AI models.** 
Prominent US lawmakers have requested information from DoorDash on its use of Chinese artificial intelligence models after its co-founder disclosed that they had experimented with a model from Chinese start-up Moonshot AI, developer of the Kimi models. House Select Committee on China chairman John Moolenaar and House Committee on Homeland Security chairman Andrew Garbarino sent a letter to DoorDash co-founder and CEO Tony Xu, requesting information by August 14 on each Chinese model DoorDash uses and any security tests conducted on those models. The letter notes that "those practical considerations do not eliminate the need for risk-based safeguards or diminish the national security concerns associated with growing dependence on models developed by entities subject to PRC jurisdiction."


The DoorDash/Kimi K2.6 probe is a canary for the broader restaurant-tech supply chain. Any operator or vendor using open-weight Chinese LLMs — whether for drive-thru ordering, menu optimization, or back-office analytics — faces the same scrutiny risk, even if they are not publicly traded.

**EU AI Act chatbot transparency requirements.** 
EU AI Act transparency obligations for chatbots under Article 50 now apply from 2 August 2026. High-risk AI obligations under the Act apply from August 2026. California's Automated Decisionmaking Technology access and opt-out rights take effect January 1, 2027. Hospitality operators need to map their AI systems to these frameworks.


The **White House Executive Order on AI** (June 2, 2026) has hospitality-specific implications that are beginning to propagate through procurement. 
Future competitiveness in hospitality technology will increasingly depend less on feature velocity and more on cybersecurity maturity, AI governance, operational resilience, explainability, transparency, compliance readiness, and trusted infrastructure partnerships. The market is likely to fragment: trusted infrastructure vendors and governance-mature providers will consolidate enterprise deployments, while commodity "AI-overlay" vendors will be the first to fail procurement and insurer review.


Sources: [SCMP/DoorDash Kimi](https://www.scmp.com/news/china/diplomacy/article/3362616/us-lawmakers-investigate-doordashs-use-moonshot-ais-kimi-k26-model) | [Hospitality Net/National AI Security](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/opinion/4132771/when-national-ai-security-becomes-a-hospitality-imperative)

So what: For any hospitality operator procuring AI tools — voice ordering, chatbots, dynamic pricing, revenue management — the EU AI Act disclosure clock has now started. Properties in EU markets need to be able to answer: "Does the guest know they are talking to AI?" For US operators using vendor AI stacks that include open-weight models, the DoorDash investigation is a preview of the audit exposure that follows. The practical step: build an AI inventory — what systems you're running, which models they use, what data they touch, and whether disclosures are in place.

## WHO TO WATCH

**Tony Xu (DoorDash CEO)** — His Q2 framing of agentic commerce as a "battle for atoms vs. a battle for attention" is the most commercially useful mental model for understanding where AI in food delivery is actually heading. His acknowledgement that external agentic traffic is "still low" while citing real internal gains (24% checkout lift, 17% basket increase) is the opposite of agentwashing — worth tracking whether DoorDash's physical-infrastructure bet holds as Google's Ask Maps food ordering scales.

**Savneet Singh (PAR Technology CEO)** — His quote "The operators who move now to build that trust will own the guest relationship" is well-supported by PAR's own survey data (27% AI discomfort, down from 41%; 62% willing to order via LLM). His call that LLMs will become the new ordering interface is proving out faster than expected. Track PAR Intelligence's rollout and whether its multi-unit AI coordination capability produces operator-attributed outcomes.

**Joel Montaniel (SevenRooms CEO/Co-Founder)** — Channel Connect, launched for free under an open license, is an unusual competitive move: giving away infrastructure to prevent any single platform (read: OpenTable) from becoming the gatekeeper. The "one book for restaurants" positioning frames the data-ownership debate more clearly than any vendor press release this cycle. Watch whether Channel Connect's open-license structure produces the network effect Montaniel is betting on.

**Glenn Fogel (Booking Holdings CEO)** — His confirmation on the Q2 earnings call that a portion of Booking Holdings traffic "already runs through" Google's agentic hotel booking test is the most significant commercial disclosure from the hotel distribution world this cycle. He is simultaneously a partner to Google's UCP rollout and existentially threatened by it. How Booking Holdings navigates being both inside and underneath the agent layer will define OTA strategy for the next three years.

**Rafa Marquez (President, International Card Services, American Express)** — The architect of the Resy + Tock + TheFork global dining stack. His comment that "dining is one of the most important ways people engage with our brand" is strategy, not marketing — American Express is building the premium end of a closed-loop dining ecosystem (cardmember spend → reservation → POS data via Toast → loyalty reward). European restaurant operators in particular should be assessing what TheFork integration means for their data posture before the acquisition closes.
