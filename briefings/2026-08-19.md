## AI Visibility & Discovery

The most consequential shift of this news cycle: AI-mediated discovery has moved from theory to live product across both hotel and restaurant sectors simultaneously, with Google's agentic booking test confirmed this week and a cascade of restaurant-side data quantifying how much LLMs are already reshaping where guests choose to eat.

**Google** confirmed to Skift on August 7 that it has begun a limited U.S. test of agentic hotel booking inside Search's AI Mode. 
Google has launched a limited U.S. test of agentic hotel booking within Search's AI Mode, with Booking Holdings among the first partners and Expedia, Marriott, Wyndham, and IHG also collaborating. Google's new Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP) could allow bookings to be completed directly inside a chat session — Amadeus is its first B2B foundational partner for lodging, with Sabre advising on the air side.
 The infrastructure dimension is the most telling detail: 
Amadeus says it is the first infrastructure partner Google has wired into the new protocol it built to let an AI agent complete a hotel booking. Sabre's Kurt Ekert was blunter: Google "does shop very well. It doesn't do book or service" — and his company, he said, could hand it that ability "en masse tomorrow." It is the first time Google has tied its agentic hotel booking, on the record, to the global distribution systems — the rails that already carry hotels into airline, corporate and travel-agent channels.
 Hospitality.today's analysis is sharp on who benefits and who doesn't: 
The companies Google has worked with on agentic travel booking are chains and OTAs — Booking, Expedia, Marriott, Wyndham, IHG. The independent hotel is not among them.


**Booking Holdings** reported Q2 earnings on August 4 with a clear-eyed picture of the LLM transition in progress. 
Google's AI Overviews are pressuring Booking's organic search (SEO) results, but the impact is limited since SEO is a small part of the business and direct traffic in the mid-60% range remains stable. Booking is accelerating its AI strategy across customer service, AI discovery, and LLM visibility, while raising its transformation-program savings target to $650 million in annual run-rate savings by 2027.
 The critical counterweight to agentic hype: 
Booking Holdings CFO Ewout Steenbergen said in the second quarter of 2026 that bookings coming from conversational agents, free or paid, account for less than 1% of the group's room-nights, with no significant recent change in that share.
 Booking is also consolidating its three brands' separate B2B businesses into a single platform called Booking Partner Services, with Priceline B2B partner migration already underway.

**Radisson Hotel Group and Accenture** launched a hotel discovery application within ChatGPT in July 2026. 
Travellers can use conversational queries to search more than 1,000 Radisson properties across more than 100 countries. Results can incorporate rates, inventory, amenities and location information before directing customers to the hotel group's website to complete a reservation.


**American Express / Resy** announced a partnership with Anthropic in April 2026 to make U.S. Resy restaurants discoverable in Claude. 
Claude users can now explore U.S. Resy restaurants directly in their conversations when deciding where to eat. Whether it's a low-key dinner in the neighbourhood or an all-out special occasion, Claude connects to Resy to help diners browse available tables in real time and secure a table seamlessly.
 This follows the company's 2026 shareholder letter commitment to AI: 
In 2026, for U.S. consumers, American Express plans to introduce enhanced dining discovery and booking features within the American Express app, enable new AI-powered experiences and discovery capabilities in its Resy and Amex Travel apps, and pilot real-time, geolocation-based capabilities for Amex Offers.


**DoorDash** published its 2026 Restaurant Industry Trends Report, surfacing restaurant-side data on AI discovery at scale. 
22% of consumers have used an AI tool like ChatGPT or Google Gemini to help choose a restaurant. According to Yext research, restaurant listing sites like DoorDash account for more than 41% of the sources AI tools cite when recommending restaurants. Three-quarters of consumers say they're comfortable using AI for reservations, but only 28% of operators are using AI to manage calls and customer service. An estimated 40% of reservation calls go unanswered industry-wide.


**Hotel Management Network** published an August 13 analysis worth reading in full on AI discovery objectivity risk: 
Experimental research into AI hotel recommendations suggests that operators should not assume these systems are entirely objective. A 2026 audit covering 12 AI models found that guest ratings and price had particularly strong effects on hotel selection. It also identified an influence from list position, showing that factors unrelated to a hotel's underlying quality can affect AI-generated recommendations.


Sources: [Skift – Google Confirms Agentic Hotel Booking Test](https://skift.com/2026/08/07/google-confirms-hotel-agentic-booking-is-now-in-testing/) | [Hospitality.today – Analysis](https://www.hospitality.today/article/google-just-put-agentic-hotel-booking-into-testing) | [Skift – Booking Holdings Q2 Earnings](https://skift.com/2026/08/04/booking-holdings-saw-pressure-from-googles-ai-overviews/)

**So what:** AI-mediated discovery is live, not theoretical — but it is generating awareness, not yet bookings at scale. The structural fact for independent hotels is that they arrive in Google's agent only through pipes they don't own: an OTA or a GDS. For restaurant operators, the immediate job is ensuring your listing data is clean, current, and machine-readable across every aggregator — not optimising for a human reader alone. The 41% share DoorDash claims of AI citation sources gives listing-quality hygiene direct commercial consequence.

## Vendor Moves

Two watchlist companies made substantive product moves since late spring that define this cycle's restaurant-tech story: DoorDash attempting to become an AI-native commerce platform, and Toast consolidating its grip on restaurant operations from drive-thru to marketing.

**DoorDash** launched its "Ask DoorDash" conversational AI chatbot on June 11. 
The new chatbot, called Ask DoorDash, allows users to search the app for what they're looking for in their own words instead of having to scroll through restaurants and stores to build a cart. DoorDash announced it's launching the chatbot in select markets for restaurant search, grocery shopping, and DoorDash Reservations.
 The early performance data is concrete: 
Nearly half of all restaurant orders placed through the tool were from a place the customer had never ordered from before. Carts assembled through the chatbot ran more than 35% higher in value than a standard grocery order, with users completing them five times faster than through the regular app interface.
 Strategically, 
DoorDash intends to license the technology to outside partners — grocers, restaurants, and other retailers — who would deploy it under their own branding, a move that sharpens its rivalry with Instacart.
 The financial context: 
DoorDash is in the middle of a massive investment cycle that involves the creation of a unified tech platform following acquisitions including a $1.2 billion deal for SevenRooms and the nearly $4 billion acquisition of Deliveroo. Its stock is down 33%, compared to the Nasdaq's roughly 8% gain.


**Toast** launched two significant products in spring 2026. First, **Toast Drive-Thru** (April 14) — an enterprise-grade unified solution combining Delphi hardware, POS-native software, and AI voice ordering integrations with partners including Incept AI. 
"In the drive-thru lane, every second matters. As one of the important service models for QSR profitability, brands are constantly being pushed to balance speed and guest convenience with efficiency. At the same time, AI is giving brands new opportunities to impact this equation," said Steve Fredette, President and Co-Founder of Toast.
 Second, **Toast IQ Grow** (May 5) — 
Toast debuted Toast IQ Grow, a complete marketing solution, alongside a reimagined Toast Local consumer app featuring integrated Resy reservations, increasing the number of locations available for booking to more than 20,000.
 The pilot results use a small baseline but are specific: 
In the Toast IQ Grow pilot, restaurants working with a Marketing Success Manager saw sales grow more than 8% on average compared to similar Toast restaurants, and the top 25% of performers saw +40% growth in the first month.
 Business momentum underlying these launches: 
Engineering coding velocity increased more than 60% year over year, helping the company launch its marketing agent three months earlier than planned. About 40% of customer-support interactions were resolved by AI. First-quarter 2026 annualized recurring run-rate (ARR) rose 26% to $2.2 billion.


**American Express** is in the middle of restructuring the Resy/Tock ecosystem. 
This summer, American Express is folding Tock into Resy, doubling its venue library to over 25,000 restaurants. That merger brings Tock's prepaid booking model and tiered experience features directly into the Resy platform, creating a significantly larger single destination for diners seeking premium restaurant bookings.
 Separately, Amex announced a multi-year partnership with Toast to create more personalized hospitality experiences across the Resy, Tock, and Toast restaurant networks.

**Palona AI** raised $20 million and expanded beyond voice ordering into a broader restaurant operations platform (August 2026, per Restaurant Technology News). The company was previously focused on AI phone ordering; the expansion toward operations is a watch signal for where the voice-AI-to-platform conversion is heading.

**MarginEdge** raised $80 million as competition for the AI-powered restaurant back office intensifies (August 2026, per Restaurant Technology News). The round signals investor conviction in back-office AI specifically — consistent with the Restaurant365 survey finding that operator adoption surged from 25% to 69% in just six months.

## Data & Integration

The single most actionable research of this cycle — and a useful corrective to vendor marketing — is the finding that most restaurant AI is built on a single data source, leaving the highest-cost problems invisible.

**Lavu** published its 2026 Restaurant POS AI Capabilities Report in March, benchmarking seven major platforms. 
The report found that most restaurant AI on the market today is built on a single data source — leaving multi-unit operators with serious blind spots in the areas where restaurants lose the most money. The report evaluated platforms across six areas: cross-platform data connections, automatic profit leak detection, labor compliance tracking, real-time problem alerts, AI chat assistance, and overall intelligence depth. "The problems that cost multi-unit operators the most money — the ones worth seven figures a year — are cross-system problems. You will not find them inside any single platform," said Saleem S. Khatri, CEO, Lavu Inc.


**Toast IQ** — independently verified by RestaurantTools.ai — is the only major POS AI that both analyzes and acts. 
Toast IQ is the most capable POS AI right now — the only major assistant that both answers questions and executes actions (edit menus, 86 items, fix shift clockouts from chat). It draws on data from roughly 148,000 customer locations plus local weather and events — it can benchmark your Tuesday against the market's Tuesday. A POS trained only on your own data can describe your business but cannot tell you whether a 12% labor cost spike is your problem or everyone's problem.


**Restaurant365** documented the performance gap from AI adoption in its mid-year survey of 420+ operators. 
Operators using AI for back-office reporting and analytics jumped from roughly 25% at the start of 2026 to 69% by mid-year. Among active AI users, 61% report reduced food costs and 62% report reduced labor costs.
 The obstacles among non-adopters: 
37% of non-adopters cited data privacy and security concerns, 34% cited output accuracy issues, and 29% pointed to cost as the key hurdle.


**Mews** continues to demonstrate what data integration at the PMS layer looks like in practice. 
PMS is increasingly becoming the control layer of the hospitality tech stack. As hotel and lodging operators push to simplify fragmented systems, the PMS is taking on more responsibility — connecting teams, revenue, guest journeys and data in one place. Mews' acquisitions of Flexkeeping and DataChat in late 2025 are examples of this build-through-acquisition strategy.


Sources: [Restaurant Technology News – Lavu Report](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/05/lavu-publishes-restaurant-pos-ai-capabilities-report-finding-most-platforms-lack-cross-system-intelligence/) | [Restaurant365 – Mid-Year Report](https://www.restaurant365.com/guides/2026-state-of-the-restaurant-industry-mid-year-report/) | [Hotel Dive – $1B Funding Index](https://www.hoteldive.com/news/hospitality-tech-attracts-1b-in-funding-pms-ai-leaders/817439/)

**So what:** The data gap between single-source POS AI and cross-system intelligence is the real competitive moat being contested right now. For PE-backed restaurant groups, demanding cross-system AI — not just POS-native chat — should be the bar for any new tech evaluation. For hotel operators, the same principle applies: a PMS that can't share preference data with F&B, revenue management, and guest messaging is not the AI-ready control layer investors are pricing in.

## Operational AI

Restaurant back-of-house AI is moving fastest, with a critical cautionary signal from Starbucks alongside accelerating adoption data.

**McDonald's** is reported to be unifying data from nearly 220 million loyalty users as its global AI strategy takes shape (August 2026, per Restaurant Technology News). The scale of that loyalty data set is a meaningful competitive moat — the AI advantage in personalisation and predictive ordering scales directly with profile depth.

**Starbucks** reportedly pulled the plug on its AI inventory system (August 2026, per Restaurant Technology News). The story is a genuine signal, not hype: a large-scale operator deployed AI for inventory management and determined it wasn't delivering. The specifics are not yet in trade press, but the headline matters — it confirms that AI adoption does not equal AI success, and that back-of-house AI needs workflow fit, not just capability.

**Chili's** is investing in 23,000 iPads and 9,000 kitchen screens (August 2026, per Restaurant Technology News) as it takes a "selective approach" to AI. The framing is notable — the chain is investing in hardware foundation before AI layers, which is the disciplined sequencing that operators who have succeeded with AI recommend.

**Yum! Brands / Taco Bell** provides the strongest scale evidence for drive-thru voice AI: 
Yum! Brands has processed more than two million drive-thru orders via voice AI across 300-plus locations, achieving over 90% order containment — which frees employees from the headset to focus on food prep, accuracy at the window, and hospitality.


**Restaurant365 mid-year survey** quantifies the back-of-house AI surge precisely: 
Back-of-house AI adoption has surged since the start of 2026 in ways that were not widely anticipated heading into the year. Operators using AI for back-office reporting and analytics jumped from roughly 25% at the start of 2026 to 69% by mid-year. This is where the Restaurant Profitability Gap begins to take shape.


Sources: [Restaurant365 Mid-Year Report](https://www.restaurant365.com/guides/2026-state-of-the-restaurant-industry-mid-year-report/) | [Restaurant Technology News](https://restauranttechnologynews.com) | [The Hospitality Hangout – Drive-Thru AI Guide](https://www.thehospitalityhangout.com/blog/ai-drive-thru-ordering-in-2026-what-restaurant-operators-need-to-know-now/)

**So what:** The Starbucks pullback is the most important restaurant AI story of the week — not because AI inventory management doesn't work, but because it didn't work there, at that scale, in that deployment. Operators evaluating back-of-house AI should demand case studies with attribution methodology, not category claims. The Restaurant Profitability Gap data is credible because it comes from a vendor with direct access to P&L data across 10,000 locations — treat the 61%/62% cost-reduction figures as operator-reported, not third-party verified, and benchmark accordingly.

The 12-month funding cycle through Q1 2026 crossed $1 billion; Q2 was lighter on rounds but heavier on acquisitions, with Lighthouse's buy of an AI-visibility tool signalling where the category is heading.

**Abode Worldwide's Hospitality Tech Investment Index 2026** is the definitive dataset: 
Forty global hospitality technology startups raised more than $1 billion in the past year, with property management systems and artificial intelligence-led platforms attracting the most investment. The three companies with the largest raises included Mews at $300 million; Limehouse at €75 million; and Kindred at $125 million across two funding rounds.
 
AI-led guest experience platforms were another standout category, with Duve, Chatlyn, Conduit and Canary Technologies raising a combined $152.6 million. These platforms are targeting one of the industry's biggest pressures: the need to deliver fast, personalised service with leaner teams.


**Q2 2026** (Abode Worldwide roundup): 
Three disclosed rounds closed in the quarter, all AI-native by description. Smartness closed the largest, a €47 million Series B for its agentic AI platform for hotels and short-term rentals. Pricepoint raised a $6.6 million seed for AI revenue management. Besty AI raised a $3.75 million seed for its AI automation tool on the STR side.


**Lighthouse** made the most strategically telling acquisition of Q2: 
Lighthouse, a hospitality intelligence platform that has raised $370 million in growth equity, acquired Hotelrank, a tool that measures where hotels show up across AI platforms, from a startup founded in 2025.
 The signal: a major commercial intelligence platform is treating AI visibility measurement as a core product capability, not a side project.

**MarginEdge** raised $80 million in August 2026, at a moment when the AI-powered restaurant back office category is accelerating. The scale of the round reflects investor belief that the profitability gap between AI adopters and non-adopters creates a durable TAM.

**Jessica Gillingham, CEO of Abode Worldwide**, on the structural logic: 
"AI can't compensate for scattered data, disconnected systems or poor processes. If a hotel's underlying systems are fragmented, AI will only make operational problems more visible." The investment picture reflects a market where investor confidence is centred on core systems hospitality operators use to run their businesses, especially platforms that centralise operations, improve automation, and generate the data foundations needed for AI.

## Governance & Security

The White House AI Executive Order (June 2, 2026) has generated the sharpest governance analysis aimed specifically at hotels, and the DOJ Data Security Program is creating a compliance exposure most hospitality operators haven't mapped yet.

**The White House Executive Order** (June 2, 2026) prompted a detailed white paper from Terence Ronson at Pertlink Limited for Hospitality Net. The core message: 
The Order is about federal systems, frontier-model security, and national cyber defense. For hospitality, the relevance is not theoretical. The modern hotel has quietly become a dense, interconnected intelligent environment — binding together identity, payments, guest data, building systems, IoT, energy orchestration, and AI-driven workflows.
 The vendor market implication is pointed: 
Trusted infrastructure vendors and governance-mature providers will consolidate enterprise and flagged-brand deployments. Commodity "AI-overlay" vendors — those that bolted generative features on for marketing — will be the first to fail procurement and insurer review. The era of superficial "AI feature" marketing is beginning to fade, replaced by demands for accountability, clarity on liability, assurances of resilience, and security validation.


**The DOJ Data Security Program** creates a compliance exposure the hospitality industry has largely not mapped [PAYWALLED/BLOCKED — sourced from search snippets only]: Hospitality AI deployments — dynamic pricing, biometric check-in, call-center transcription, digital key platforms — typically create "bulk" datasets of sensitive personal data. DOJ's DSP rules restricting foreign access to bulk U.S. sensitive data cover covered personal identifiers, precise geolocation, biometric identifiers, and personal financial data; hospitality routinely touches at least four of these categories. An AI governance program with defined screening processes and contract controls is key to compliance.

**Data privacy compliance for biometrics** is an emerging pressure point: 
Biometric identifiers used for access control, timekeeping, drive-through, or kiosks constitute sensitive "personal data." Comprehensive state privacy laws now exist in 21 states, with Oklahoma becoming the 21st after enacting SB 546. Many state privacy laws treat biometrics as sensitive data, requiring explicit consent, risk assessments, and heightened security.


**Hotel cybersecurity** deepfake threat is becoming an operational concern, not just a theoretical one: 
Deepfakes will move from being celebrity hoaxes to operational fraud — imagine a voice message that sounds convincingly like your general manager asking to approve a wire transfer, or that video call from a "vendor representative" confirming a change in payment. This will become cheap and scalable with the release of free-to-use AI tools.


Sources: [Hospitality Net – White House EO Analysis](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/opinion/4132771/when-national-ai-security-becomes-a-hospitality-imperative) | [Fox Rothschild – Data Privacy & AI](https://dataprivacy.foxrothschild.com/2026/03/articles/general-privacy-data-security-news-developments/what-hospitality-hr-operations-and-leadership-need-to-know-about-data-privacy-and-ai/)

**So what:** Procurement teams at hotel brands and PE-backed SaaS companies should have two new standard questions for AI vendors: "Is any guest data used for model training without explicit consent?" and "Can you demonstrate purpose-binding controls for AI agents that have access to PMS, payment, or identity systems?" The governance maturity gap is widening, and cyber insurers will start pricing it.
