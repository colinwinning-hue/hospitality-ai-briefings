## Vendor Moves

The reservation and dining data layer is consolidating at pace, with three platform moves landing in the past 90 days that collectively reshape how restaurants are discovered, booked, and known.

**DoorDash** turned its $1.2 billion SevenRooms acquisition into a live product this month, launching restaurant reservations in Miami and New York through its app for 42 million active users. 
Via SevenRooms, DoorDash is also offering restaurants voice AI that can take reservations over the phone and event management capabilities including group bookings and private dining.
 The strategic play is explicit: 
"Delivery and dine-in have typically been siloed data sets," SevenRooms co-founder Joel Montaniel said. "So if a customer has ordered six times, and they're coming into the restaurant for the first time, are they a first-time customer or a seventh-time customer?"
 On the competitive response side, 
SevenRooms unveiled Channel Connect, a desktop app that aggregates reservations from various booking channels into a single system, while also automatically updating a restaurant's inventory across those channels. SevenRooms said Channel Connect will free operators from having to use multiple tablets to manually reconcile double bookings.
 Notably, 
this includes reservations from "major booking platforms," which presumably means SevenRooms' chief rivals, OpenTable and Resy. Channel Connect is able to access that data "through mechanisms those platforms already provide, using credentials and the data the restaurant controls."
 The move is partly a counter to OpenTable's April decision to require restaurants to make it their system of record.

**American Express** has spent the summer consolidating its dining empire on two fronts. Its multi-year partnership with Toast — announced in 2025 but rolling out products through 2026 — 
plans to build tools for restaurants to create more seamless and personalized hospitality experiences combining the power of Resy and Tock's guestbook capabilities and Toast's Digital Chits technology, which allows restaurant staff to easily view important customer information directly on Toast Go handhelds and POS terminals during service.
 Simultaneously, 
American Express agreed to acquire restaurant reservation management platform TheFork from Tripadvisor for $700 million. TheFork serves more than 50,000 restaurants in 11 European countries, offering a restaurant management, booking and customer engagement platform.
 This follows its earlier acquisitions of Resy and Tock. 
The acquisition would extend American Express' broader dining push and take its network to an estimated 75,000 bookable venues.
 Operationally, 
as of August 1, 2026, the Resy app and website now display a badge to let cardmembers know which restaurants are eligible for dining credits. Amex has added this label as it builds out its dining platform, with a specific callout that Tock restaurants will be added later this year.


**Toast** reported the strongest quarter in its history alongside a raft of ecosystem moves. 
Toast is working to capture more business from time-strapped customers by using artificial intelligence tools to assume more of a restaurateur's workflows. Toast's first AI-enabled workflow product — a digital marketing tool called Toast IQ Grow — is poised to become the company's fastest-growing product, with executives expecting it to generate $10 million in annual revenue since its May introduction.
 CEO Aman Narang framed the roadmap: 
over time, Toast will deploy "agentic products" atop its software for various services a restaurant needs to operate, including demand forecasting, labor schedules, income tax help, food costs and availability. "That's an incredibly exciting future, especially in an industry known for slim margins and long hours," Narang said.
 
In Q2, recurring gross profit streams grew 28%, GAAP Operating Income margins expanded to 26%, and Toast added a record 9,500 net locations.
 The company also 
deepened its ecosystem reach through BWH Hotels' endorsement of its POS platform across the U.S. and Canada and an expanded integration with Google's Ask Maps, positioning the company at the center of emerging AI-driven food ordering standards.


**Delivery Hero** announced on August 13 an agentic AI assistant for its restaurant and shop partners. 
Shop and restaurant owners interact with the assistant through chats on WhatsApp. They can use text, voice notes and images to ask the assistant for help directly, or, if the owner allows, it can also make suggestions independently.
 Johannes Bruder, CPO at Delivery Hero, framed it: 
"Small business owners have a lot to juggle and our new AI assistant helps them grow faster without adding to their workload. What makes it different is that it doesn't just advise. It acts on behalf of business owners, the moment they approve, right inside a messaging app they already use."
 
The AI assistant currently supports over 40,000 restaurants across Delivery Hero's global brands and markets, an early slice of the approximately 1.5 million partners on the company's platform.
 A Bahraini restaurant owner offered a rare operator quote: "I like how the assistant makes suggestions, but I can always decide and approve before anything changes, so I stay in control. It's helped me bring in more orders without adding to my workload."

**Lighthouse** acquired Hotelrank.ai, a tool that measures where hotels appear in AI platform recommendations. 
Lighthouse, a hospitality intelligence platform that has bought several companies in recent years and raised $370 million in growth equity, acquired Hotelrank, a tool that measures where hotels show up across AI platforms, from a startup founded in 2025.
 This is a direct product bet on AI visibility becoming a commercial discipline.

Sources: [Restaurant Dive – Toast Q2](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/toast-aims-to-drive-ai-into-dining/827503/) | [NRN – SevenRooms Channel Connect](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology/sevenrooms-launches-a-reservations-aggregator-for-restaurants) | [PYMNTS – Amex/TheFork](https://www.pymnts.com/acquisitions/2026/amex-looks-to-spend-700-million-to-buy-thefork-from-tripadvisor/) | [RTIH – Delivery Hero](https://retailtechinnovationhub.com/home/2026/8/13/delivery-hero-launch-ai-assistant-for-local-shops-and-restaurants-with-roll-out-due-across-platform)

So what: The reservation layer is becoming the locus of the full guest data relationship — delivery history, dining preferences, financial identity. For mid-market restaurant operators, choosing a reservation platform now means choosing an ecosystem (DoorDash/SevenRooms, AmEx/Resy-Tock, OpenTable/Uber). The data portability fight is happening now. Get it in writing before signing long-term terms.

## AI Visibility & Discovery

The hotel and restaurant discovery funnel has structurally shifted, with AI intermediating search before any OTA or direct booking channel is reached — and the commercial evidence is now concrete enough to require a budget response.


On August 7, 2026, a Google spokesperson confirmed that a limited test of AI agent hotel booking is currently underway in the United States, with Amadeus as technical partner. The feature, called Ask Maps, now returns real prices and availability rather than ranges or estimates.
 The same day, 
during its quarterly earnings presentation, Booking Holdings CEO Glenn Fogel confirmed that a portion of his group's traffic already runs through this agentic booking, and that Booking Holdings counts among Google's first partners.



Google Maps will now be able to order food, buy event tickets, book hotels, and give even more personalized recommendations. For the food ordering feature, Google says Ask Maps will find open restaurants along your route that serve what you're craving and will rely on partnerships with food industry payment processing platforms Square and Toast at first launch, and will later include Uber Eats.


Google and OpenAI are diverging on transaction strategy. 
While Google is expanding direct transaction capabilities inside its AI ecosystem, OpenAI has reportedly stepped back from enabling direct purchases inside ChatGPT.
 Google's UCP for Lodging architecture is explicitly designed to keep hotels as Merchant of Record: 
UCP is an open standard for agentic commerce that now unifies digital hotel booking. It enables direct, instant reservations, reducing friction and abandonment. You remain the Merchant of Record, maintaining full control over your customer relationships and the post-booking experience.


The traffic data is stark. 
SparkToro's analysis of clickstream data found that 68% of U.S. Google searches now end without a click to any website — up from 60% just two years earlier, the fastest acceleration the firm has recorded. AI Overviews are driving most of that shift, and their presence alone cuts click-through rate by roughly 60%.
 Yet the actual booking impact remains minimal: Booking Holdings CFO Ewout Steenbergen said in Q2 2026 that bookings coming from conversational agents account for less than 1% of the group's room-nights.

Hotel chains are positioning for the agent era regardless. 
Marriott launched its Ask Bonvoy assistant in June 2026, limited to its own portfolio and internally verified data. IHG and Wyndham opened their own apps on ChatGPT during spring 2026.
 The independent hotel challenge is severe: 
independent and regional lodging brands have less room for error. If they're not surfaced when travelers research destinations, resorts, or experiences, they may never enter the conversation.


Sources: [Hospitality ON – Google hotel booking test](https://hospitality-on.com/en/distribution/google-launches-ai-agent-hotel-booking-test-united-states) | [Gizmodo – Google Maps agentic](https://gizmodo.com/google-maps-adds-agentic-feature-that-orders-food-and-books-hotels-for-you-2000795579) | [Skift – AI visibility hotels](https://skift.com/2026/07/31/ai-driven-travel-discovery-hotel-visibility/)

So what: The window to do nothing is closing. Hotels that haven't structured their rate rules, attribute data, and cancellation policies for machine readability are already invisible to some agent queries. For PE-backed hospitality SaaS, GEO tooling is now a product category — Lighthouse's acquisition of Hotelrank confirms it.

## Funding & M&A

The hospitality tech funding picture for 2026 shows consolidation logic running faster than new capital deployment.


Hospitality technology startups raised more than $1 billion across 40 companies between April 2025 and March 2026, with PMS and AI-led platforms attracting the largest share. That 90-day burst between December 2025 and February 2026 saw Mews ($300 million), Kindred ($125 million), and Limehome (€75 million) land almost simultaneously.
 
The best-funded PMS businesses are using this position to build broader platforms through product development and acquisition, rather than relying on third-party integrations. Mews' acquisitions of Flexkeeping and DataChat in late 2025 are one example of this strategy.


Q2 2026 showed the acquisition side running ahead of new rounds. 
Three disclosed rounds closed in the quarter, all of them AI-native by description: Smartness closed the largest, a €47 million Series B for its agentic AI platform for hotels and short-term rentals; Pricepoint raised a $6.6 million seed for AI revenue management; Besty AI raised a $3.75 million seed for its AI automation tool on the STR side.
 
Smartness started in 2020 as Smartpricing, a dynamic pricing tool for hotels, and has since expanded into a broad platform spanning pricing, guest communications, CRM, payments, and property management. Its €47 million Series B is set partly toward acquisitions across Europe.


Jessica Gillingham, CEO of Abode Worldwide: 
"Investors are concentrating capital in the platforms that hospitality businesses increasingly depend on every day, especially PMS and AI-led systems that unify operations, improve automation and strengthen the data layer underneath the business."


On the restaurant side, the DoorDash/SevenRooms ($1.2B) and American Express/TheFork ($700M) deals are the two largest dining-tech acquisitions in recent years, both driven by data consolidation logic rather than feature acquisition.

Sources: [Abode Worldwide – Hospitality Tech Q2 2026](https://abodeworldwide.com/hospitality-tech-funding-q2-2026/) | [Hotel Dive – $1B funding](https://www.hoteldive.com/news/hospitality-tech-attracts-1b-in-funding-pms-ai-leaders/817439/)

So what: The PMS layer is becoming the control surface for AI — whoever owns it owns the data needed for agentic workflows. For SaaS founders in adjacent categories (upselling, messaging, revenue management), the bar is now: solve a specific problem better than an expanding PMS can, with clean API integration, or face acquisition pressure.

## Operational AI

Consumer readiness for AI in restaurants has shifted materially and the data now supports broader deployment investment — though the NRA Show consensus remains that use cases should stay narrow.


In 2025, 41% of surveyed consumers said they'd choose a restaurant that avoided AI altogether. One year later, average discomfort with restaurant AI has fallen to 27%. This means nearly 3 in 4 diners are now open to AI playing some role in their experience, depending on how it's used.
 PAR Technology CEO Savneet Singh: 
"Consumers are giving operators clear direction — they are open to AI when it makes their experience better. The operators who move now to build that trust will own the guest relationship. The ones who wait will be handing it to someone else."
 The PAR report (fielded June 2026, n=1,000) found 
48% are comfortable with AI making operational decisions like adjusting labor based on demand or optimizing menus based on inventory. Comfort rises to 51% when AI is used to improve service consistency and shorten wait times.


Restaurant tech executives at the NRA Show (May 2026) pushed back hard on the hype. 
Brendan Sweeney, CEO of Popmenu, argued that overadoption of large language models rests on shaky fundamentals. The cost of AI tokens required to operate coding services can exceed the salaries of the workers the technology purports to replace. The slow pace of data structure construction and rising cost of electricity make it unlikely that AI services will see a decline in cost.
 Kelly Esten, Toast CMO: 
"It can't be another thing to do. It has to actually do some of the work for you."


On hotels, 
every major hotel chain has now committed publicly to an agentic AI roadmap — Marriott's $1B+ agentic mesh, Hilton's AI Planner, Hyatt and Accor inside ChatGPT, IHG's AI-enhanced CRM. All of them are bottlenecked at the same layer: the property management system was built when a guest was a stay at a property, not a relationship across the chain.


Sources: [QSR Magazine – PAR Technology report](https://www.qsrmagazine.com/news/par-technology-releases-industry-report-on-ai-in-restaurants/) | [Restaurant Dive – NRA Show AI overdependence](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/restaurant-technology-executives-warn-against-ai-overdependence/821024/)

So what: The consumer resistance to restaurant AI has dropped faster than most operators assumed. But the NRA Show verdict — "AI at its best serves up an option to an operator to make an informed decision" — is still the right deployment principle. Toast IQ Grow's early $10M ARR trajectory is the clearest proof point that AI marketing automation, not AI ordering, is where restaurant operators are currently willing to pay.

## Governance & Security

Two regulatory instruments went live in August that directly affect hospitality operators deploying AI-facing systems.


Article 50 of the EU AI Act applies from 2 August 2026, with Commission guidelines adopted on 20 July 2026. Penalties run to fifteen million euros or 3% of worldwide annual turnover. If a chatbot or voice agent talks to guests, people have to be told they are dealing with AI. That design duty sits with the provider, meaning your vendor, so make it a procurement question and get the answer in writing.


The June 2026 White House Executive Order on AI Innovation and Security carries downstream implications for hospitality that are not yet widely understood. 
The Order is about federal systems, frontier-model security, and national cyber defense, but it signals that artificial intelligence has crossed a threshold — from an experimental productivity tool to a critical layer of operational infrastructure that governments now treat as a matter of national security.
 Terence Ronson (Pertlink Limited), translating the order for hotel operators: 
"The smartest move available to any hotel, vendor or integrator today is unglamorous: know where your AI already lives, govern it deliberately, and make trust a feature you can prove."


The DOJ's Data Security Program (DSP) creates a separate but acute exposure for hospitality AI deployments. 
Hospitality companies are accelerating adoption of AI — from dynamic pricing and marketing personalization to fraud detection, digital key platforms, call-center transcription, and biometric check-in. These deployments typically require massive volumes of customer and device data, and they often rely on third-party vendors, offshore development teams, and cloud-hosted model pipelines. Hospitality companies routinely touch at least four of the DSP's sensitive data categories — identifiers, geolocation, biometrics, and financial.


Sources: [Hospitality Net – White House EO analysis](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/opinion/4132771/when-national-ai-security-becomes-a-hospitality-imperative) | [Hospitality Technology – DOJ bulk data](https://hospitalitytech.com/beyond-hipaa-navigating-dojs-new-bulk-data-thresholds-hospitality-ai) | [Tableview – EU AI Act restaurants](https://www.tableview.com/blog/ai-in-restaurants/)

So what: EU AI Act Article 50 is live now — if your restaurant or hotel tech vendor runs a guest-facing chatbot or voice agent and has not told you how it discloses AI interaction to guests, that is a compliance gap that sits with you as the deployer. Make it a written clause in your next renewal.

## WHO TO WATCH

**Aman Narang (Toast CEO)** — His Q2 earnings framing — that AI allows Toast to "take on more of a restaurant's work" and that Toast IQ Grow is already tracking to $10M ARR — is the most operationally specific AI revenue claim made by a publicly traded restaurant tech company this cycle. His roadmap toward agentic demand forecasting, labor scheduling, and food cost management is worth tracking for which elements achieve product-market fit versus which stall at pilot.

**Joel Montaniel (SevenRooms CEO)** — Channel Connect, released under an open license with explicit interoperability across OpenTable and Resy, is the boldest competitive move in the reservation market this year. His framing — "no single platform has to be the gatekeeper to your operations" — is a direct attack on OpenTable's system-of-record requirement. Whether independent restaurants adopt Channel Connect in volume will determine whether DoorDash's data consolidation thesis holds.

**Savneet Singh (PAR Technology CEO)** — His prediction that LLMs become the new ordering interface is now supported by his own consumer data (62% willing to order via LLM given the right offer). Worth tracking whether PAR Intelligence converts that consumer readiness into revenue at multi-unit operators before Toast or Olo captures the same opportunity.

**Glenn Fogel (Booking Holdings CEO)** — His Q2 confirmation that a portion of Booking Holdings traffic already routes through Google's agentic booking was the most commercially significant disclosure in this news cycle. It answered the "will OTAs cooperate with Google agents?" question: yes, at least at first. The commission structure of that arrangement remains undisclosed and is the most important number in hotel distribution for 2027.

**Jenna Villalobos (Outrigger Hospitality Group CRO)** — Her candid admission that no single function at Outrigger owns AI visibility — and that the company is now formalizing that with its analytics team and building Curacity VISTA into the 2027 budget — is the clearest operator signal that GEO is graduating from experiment to budget line. Independent and regional brands should watch how Outrigger measures attribution before building similar programs.
