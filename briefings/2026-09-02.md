## AI VISIBILITY & DISCOVERY

AI has become a live distribution channel for both hotels and restaurants, not a future-state risk. Two explosive product launches in the past week confirm this is no longer theoretical.

**Google** launched hotel booking inside AI Mode in Search on September 1, 
allowing travelers to discover and book their stay "in one seamless conversation." English-speaking users in the U.S. can review and compare hotels, choose a room from integrated partners, and complete the reservation with Google Pay. Launch partners include Choice Hotels International, Hilton, Wyndham Hotels & Resorts, IHG, Marriott, Booking.com, Expedia, Priceline, Hotels.com, and Trip.com.
 Three weeks earlier, on August 6, 
Google Maps' "Ask Maps" feature gained agentic food-ordering capabilities, including the ability to order food, book hotels, and find event tickets. With the new agentic food-ordering function, users can ask a question like, "Where can I order vegan avocado toast and an oat milk latte near home?" — then place an order directly through supported platforms such as Square, Toast, or Uber Eats.
 Critically, 
Google's AI Mode passes only the minimum data needed to complete a booking (name, payment, occupancy, property), withholding broader chat context, inspiration, and decision signals from partners — even in aggregate. The hotel or OTA remains the merchant of record, handling the charge, confirmation, changes, and support. Google positions itself only as a transaction facilitator, not an OTA.


**Booking Holdings** confirmed it is among Google's first agentic booking partners. 
Google's AI Overviews are pressuring Booking's organic search results, though the impact is limited since SEO is a small part of the business and direct traffic in the mid-60% range remains stable. Booking is accelerating its AI strategy across customer service, AI discovery, and LLM visibility, while raising its transformation-program savings target to $650 million in annual run-rate savings by 2027.
 Despite the AI disruption, 
Booking Holdings processed 325 million room nights in Q2 2026, a 5% increase from the prior year — yet bookings from conversational AI agents account for less than 1% of total room nights, with no significant recent change in that share.


**Lighthouse** presented data-dense research at Luminate 2026 that quantifies what "AI invisible" actually means. 
A study was built on over 4,500 ChatGPT prompts across nine global destinations and five traveler personas — luxury, business, family, budget and generic — designed to answer what hotels AI recommends, why those and not others, and where it all comes from.
 
ChatGPT now has over 900 million users. Across all 4,545 prompts, ChatGPT mentioned hotels by name 49,707 times. Guest review scores are not what drives AI recommendations — the correlation between a hotel's Booking.com score and its ChatGPT visibility was weak. Star rating tells a clearer story: four and five-star properties dominated even on generic prompts where travelers gave no guidance, with three-star hotels nearly absent from that discovery pool.
 
Research from Curacity and Cornell shows 94% of hotels are invisible in AI search results, and SparkToor data shows 68% of U.S. Google searches now end without a click, largely due to AI Overviews — making visibility a cross-functional commercial issue spanning revenue, distribution, CRM, business intelligence, and content.


On the restaurant side, 
DoorDash's 2026 Restaurant Industry Trends Report found that 22% of consumers have used an AI tool like ChatGPT or Google Gemini to help choose a restaurant, making AI a new discovery signal. According to Yext research, restaurant listing sites like DoorDash account for more than 41% of the sources AI tools cite when recommending restaurants. Operators are already responding by updating menu information (39%), managing reviews (34%), and improving photo quality (32%).
 
Three-quarters of consumers say they're comfortable using AI for reservations, but only 28% of operators are using AI to manage calls and customer service, with an estimated 40% of reservation calls going unanswered industry-wide.


Sources: [Hotel Dive — Google AI Mode Hotel Booking](https://www.hoteldive.com/news/google-introduces-ai-mode-hotel-booking/829265/) · [Skift — Google Agentic Hotel Booking](https://skift.com/2026/08/27/googles-agentic-hotel-booking-tool-comes-to-ai-mode/) · [Lighthouse — AI Recommendations](https://www.mylighthouse.com/resources/blog/ai-recommendations-as-new-battleground-for-hotel-distribution)

So what: The shift from discovery to booking-in-one-step is now live, not a roadmap slide. Hotels and restaurants that lack consistent structured data across OTA profiles, Google Business, and direct channels are losing revenue they cannot measure — because they're not in the AI recommendation pool at all. GEO (generative engine optimisation) is now a revenue-management function, not a marketing experiment.

## VENDOR MOVES

The reservation platform wars intensified significantly this cycle as major platforms completed acquisitions and launched AI features, while Google became a direct booking layer atop all of them.

**DoorDash** launched "Ask DoorDash," 
a new AI chatbot that lets users order food and groceries with text prompts and photos. Users can tell the chatbot "a filling dinner for a family of 4," narrow results with queries like "Show me kid-friendly vegetarian spots with mild options," and ask Ask DoorDash for Reservations to find "a table for two downtown for a date-night dinner around 8 PM," with the app then surfacing restaurants with availability. The chatbot is rolling out on iOS in select regions.
 This follows DoorDash shutting down its standalone AI discovery app Zesty in April and 
embedding it into the core platform, where it can shape decisions at scale and connect them to a broader set of services — blurring the lines between discovering a restaurant, booking a table, and placing an order.
 DoorDash also launched AI merchant tools in May that 
enable new AI-powered self-serve onboarding that can help merchants launch more than 35% faster by pulling information from existing online presence automatically.


**Toast** launched Toast IQ Grow on May 5, 
a complete marketing solution that takes on the day-to-day work of marketing a business. It includes the Marketing Agent — an AI that builds audiences and automates campaigns across channels — and a reimagined Toast Local consumer app featuring integrated Resy reservations, increasing the number of locations available for booking to more than 20,000. Bundled at $499/month, Toast IQ Grow offers an alternative to purchasing point solutions. In the pilot, restaurants working with a Marketing Success Manager saw sales grow more than 8% on average compared to similar Toast restaurants, and the top 25% of performers saw +40% growth in the first month.
 On its August Q2 earnings call, 
Toast IQ Grow is expected to become the company's fastest-growing product, with executives expecting it to generate $10 million in annual revenue since its May introduction.
 Toast also reported 
Q2 net income of $154 million, nearly double the $80 million from 2025, with revenue rising 23% to $1.91 billion, and the company added a record 9,500 new locations, bringing its total to about 180,000.


**American Express** agreed to acquire TheFork for $700 million, 
highlighting the growing value of restaurant technology, customer data, and dining ecosystems.
 This adds a platform serving more than 50,000 restaurants across 11 European countries to Amex's portfolio that already includes Resy and Tock, 
and this summer American Express is folding Tock into Resy, doubling its venue library to over 25,000 restaurants, bringing Tock's prepaid booking model and tiered experience features directly into the Resy platform.
 The combined dining network is expected to encompass approximately 75,000 bookable venues worldwide. Amex also launched its 
Agentic Commerce Experiences (ACE) developer kit, designed to embed cards into AI-driven purchasing flows, along with a purchase protection framework tailored to agentic transactions.


**OpenTable** (Booking Holdings) 
launched a ChatGPT integration in 2025; OpenTable's Concierge AI now answers 80% of pre-booking diner questions inside the platform.
 **Marriott** launched its Ask Bonvoy assistant in June 2026, limited to its own portfolio. 
IHG and Wyndham opened their own apps on ChatGPT during spring 2026.


Sources: [TechCrunch — DoorDash Ask DoorDash](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/11/doordashs-new-ai-chatbot-lets-you-order-with-prompts-and-photos/) · [Toast IQ Grow Press Release](https://pos.toasttab.com/news/toast-debuts-toast-iq-grow-spring-release-2026) · [FoodChain Magazine — Amex TheFork](https://foodchainmagazine.com/how-american-express-plans-to-expand-its-dining-business-through-thefork/)

So what: The reservation and ordering platform layer is consolidating rapidly into three mega-ecosystems: DoorDash/SevenRooms (delivery-to-dine-in), Amex/Resy/Tock/TheFork (premium dining across 75,000 venues), and Booking Holdings/OpenTable (global discovery). A mid-market restaurant group or PE-backed operator must now choose which ecosystem's data flywheel to feed — or invest in direct booking infrastructure before the window closes.

## OPERATIONAL AI

The restaurant sector's AI adoption story moved from anecdote to hard data this cycle.

**Restaurant365** released its 2026 State of the Restaurant Industry Mid-Year Report on July 16, covering more than 420 operators representing nearly 10,000 U.S. locations. 
Among operators actively using AI: 61% report reduced food costs since implementation, 62% report reduced labor costs, and 88% report AI is saving time every week. Nearly a third report cost reductions of at least 6%.
 
A striking 88% of AI-using operators said the technology saves them time every week. And 62% of operators have now implemented or plan to implement AI in at least one back-office function — more than double the adoption level reported at the start of the year.
 "Our research suggests that the operators who are consistently pulling ahead aren't simply adopting AI, they're using it to make faster, better decisions every day," said Tony Smith, CEO of Restaurant365. 
Restaurant turnover stands at approximately 74% as of March 2026, and for the first time in Restaurant365's research, better training programs have overtaken pay increases as the most frequently cited employee retention strategy.


On the hotel side, the Hotel Tech Report Q2 2026 from HITEC San Antonio documented a clear shift. 
A year ago, the best hotel AI could tell a revenue manager what to do. This quarter, it does the thing itself and leaves the judgment calls for a person — completing the task rather than handing a person the answer. The software that used to summarize reviews and suggest a rate now answers the guest, works the lead, and moves the rate on its own.
 On revenue management specifically, 
Jeff Wermager, VP of sales and revenue management at My Place Hotels of America, described the IDeaS partnership this way: "I'd describe it as AI owns the high-volume, repeatable pricing decisions; our revenue management team owns the strategic exceptions — new property ramp-up, market disruptions, and negotiated rates."
 
Chris Ellison of Brittain Resorts noted that "every revenue management system on the market today has some type of AI or machine learning built into it," with the most important next step being overcoming integration challenges.


**Wendy's FreshAI** continues scaling: 
in a 2026 product update, Google Cloud reported that Wendy's FreshAI was expanding across 24 U.S. states and handling about 50,000 orders per day, with a reported 95% success rate.


Sources: [Restaurant365 Mid-Year Report — PRNewswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/restaurant365-research-identifies-a-new-restaurant-profitability-gap-operators-using-ai-are-pulling-ahead-302825987.html) · [Hotel Management — Revenue Playbook](https://hotelmanagement.mydigitalpublication.com/publication/?i=868825&article_id=5195912) · [Hotel Tech Report Q2 2026](https://hoteltechreport.com/news/q2-2026-hotel-ai-trends-report)

So what: The Restaurant Profitability Gap is now measurable and documented. For PE-backed operators, AI back-office adoption has a clear ROI case — operators using it are pulling ahead on food costs, labor costs, and traffic. The barrier to entry isn't technology availability; it's data cleanliness and workflow integration.

## GOVERNANCE & SECURITY


Regulators are increasing scrutiny of hotel AI governance, as artificial intelligence becomes embedded in guest personalisation, pricing, and travel discovery. Hotel operators are being pushed toward more structured AI governance frameworks — including clearer documentation of how guest data is collected and used, stronger vendor oversight, and improved data standardisation across systems. Technology providers are also under pressure to provide greater transparency in how their AI models process hotel and guest information.



The European Commission states that Article 50 of the EU AI Act applies from August 2, 2026 and includes transparency obligations for certain interactive and generative AI systems — meaning restaurants and hotels cannot assume that a friendly bot name is enough. Disclosure should be clear in the context where the guest encounters the system.



Facial recognition at check-in, lobby surveillance, or access control can process biometric data. Under GDPR Article 9, biometric data used to uniquely identify individuals is a special category of personal data subject to the regulation's strictest processing restrictions. In practice, most hospitality AI deployments involving biometrics have been initiated through procurement processes with no legal review of the data protection implications.



In 2026, the EU's NIS2 directive and Cyber Resilience Act will spur a global rollout of even tighter cybersecurity and privacy regulations, introducing a patchwork of requirements impacting vendors, connected devices, and digital services for hotels operating in multiple regions.
 
Deepfakes will move from being celebrity hoaxes to operational fraud — a voice message that sounds convincingly like a general manager asking to approve a wire transfer, or a video call from a "vendor representative" confirming a change in payment, will become cheap and scalable with the release of free-to-use AI tools.


Sources: [Hotel Management Network — Regulators](https://www.hotelmanagement-network.com/news/regulators-turn-attention-to-hotel-ai-governance/) · [ASIS Security Magazine — Data Risk](https://www.asisonline.org/security-management-magazine/articles/2026/08/hospitality-data-risk/) · [allainews — EU AI Act Restaurant Guide](https://allainews.net/ai-for-restaurants/)

So what: The gap between deployment velocity and governance readiness is where the exposure sits. For operators and SaaS vendors with EU customers or biometric deployments, August 2026 is not a future date — EU AI Act Article 50 obligations are live now. Treating AI governance as a compliance box-tick rather than an infrastructure decision is the single fastest way to lose enterprise contracts.
