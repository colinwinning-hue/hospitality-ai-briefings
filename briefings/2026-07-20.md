## Funding & M&A

The reservation stack is consolidating into three ecosystem plays, and a fourth (Uber) is now entering the delivery side of the same market.

**American Express** is acquiring **TheFork** from Tripadvisor in an all-cash deal at 
$700m, adding a booking network of more than 50,000 restaurants across 11 European countries to the dining business it has been quietly assembling for years.
 
TheFork is the third piece: Amex already owns Resy and Tock, and says the three together will push its dining network past 75,000 bookable venues. The logic is to own the reservation layer end-to-end — in the US and now across Europe — so that booking a table, paying for it, and earning rewards all happen inside the Amex ecosystem.
 
TheFork generated $232 million in revenue and $28 million in adjusted EBITDA during the 12 months ending with the first quarter of 2026; the $700 million transaction value equals approximately three times trailing revenue and 25 times trailing adjusted EBITDA.


**DoorDash** completed its **SevenRooms** acquisition ($1.2B) earlier in the cycle and has since moved quickly. 
SevenRooms unveiled Channel Connect, a desktop app that aggregates reservations from various booking channels — including rivals — into a single system while automatically updating a restaurant's inventory across those channels. In a blog post, SevenRooms stated "Channel Connect was built so that no single platform has to be the gatekeeper to your operations,"
 a direct shot at OpenTable's recent policy requiring restaurants to make it their system of record. 
Via SevenRooms, DoorDash is also offering restaurants voice AI that can take reservations over the phone and event management capabilities including group bookings and private dining.


**Uber** announced its intention to acquire Germany-based **Delivery Hero** for $14.8 billion (NRN, July 16), a deal that, if cleared, would reshape the European delivery market and put a third major platform in position to cross-sell reservations alongside delivery in major international markets. Deliveroo had no material AI product news this cycle.

**Besty AI** raised $3.75 million to advance guest service and operations automation for short-term rental operators (Hotel Technology News, July 2026) — a small funding round that signals continued VC interest in AI-native operators targeting the non-hotel lodging tier.

Sources: [Restaurant Technology News](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/06/american-express-expands-restaurant-technology-ambitions-with-700-million-thefork-acquisition/), [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/american-express-acquires-thefork-tripadvisor-700-million), [Nation's Restaurant News](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology), [Hotel Technology News](https://hoteltechnologynews.com/2026/07/recent-hotel-technology-launches-show-ai-moving-from-standalone-tools-into-connected-hotel-operations/)

**So what:** Restaurant operators now have to choose an ecosystem, not just a booking tool. DoorDash/SevenRooms offers data continuity across delivery and dine-in; Amex/Resy/Tock/TheFork controls premium cardholders and European reach; OpenTable/Booking Holdings maintains the widest discovery network. Sitting on all three simultaneously is a transitional strategy at best — pick which guest data model you want to own.

## Vendor Moves

The defining theme of HITEC 2026 and the weeks following it: vendors competing to become the intelligence layer across hotel operations, not just a better module within it.

**Oracle Hospitality** launched OPERA Cloud Assistant on June 16, 
a new suite of AI-powered capabilities embedded directly within familiar OPERA Cloud workflows. Wyndham, with more than 2,100 properties on OPERA Cloud, described the addition as building on a multi-year effort to integrate AI across its ecosystem.
 
The features enable hoteliers to automate guest room assignments, generate AI-driven rate descriptions, and allow staff to ask questions such as "How do I run a report?" or "How do I resolve this guest issue?" and receive real-time guidance.
 The capabilities are available at no additional cost to OPERA Cloud customers globally. Oracle VP of consumer industries Laura Calin said: "AI has the potential to transform hotel operations when it is seamlessly integrated into associates' daily work."

**Toast** published real-world usage data on its AI assistant on July 14, the clearest operator-behaviour signal this cycle. 
Toast IQ launched in October 2025. In the first quarter of 2026, operators at more than 125,000 restaurants started millions of threads with Toast IQ. Their most common questions were about sales and revenue (47%), followed by menu and inventory (34%) and guests and marketing (32%).
 
Fine-dining locations had 29% more threads with Toast IQ than fast casuals; the most common single prompt was "Create a short, easy-to-read daily briefing for my restaurant."
 On the product side, Toast's Spring 2026 release included 
Toast IQ Grow, a marketing-focused solution with an AI-powered Marketing Agent that can build audiences and automate campaigns across channels, supported by a reimagined Toast Local app.
 Goldman Sachs upgraded Toast stock this week, citing the AI platform expansion (July 12).

**Mews** laid off 15% of its staff in what founder Richard Valtr described on LinkedIn as a "difficult" decision reflective of a pivot to an "AI-native" future (Hotel Dive, July 8). The company raised $300 million in January 2026 and is the named PMS of AAHOA, representing roughly 60% of US hotels. The layoff repositions Mews as a leaner AI-native builder, though the claim warrants scrutiny (see Agentwashing below).

**Revinate** introduced **Ivy**, 
an AI decision-intelligence layer built across its platform and designed to understand a hotel's guests, property and brand voice. Revinate also used HITEC to highlight Ivy-powered capabilities for the reservations call floor and guest inbox, including automated call scoring and Revinate Chat.


**Grevon** debuted at HITEC 2026 with Grevon Kore, 
an MCP-based connectivity layer for hospitality powering Pulse, an AI booking agent for hotel websites; Echo, an AI voice agent for inbound calls; and Ops, a staff intelligence platform.


**Radisson Hotel Group** launched AI-powered price matching as part of a direct booking push (Hotel Technology News, July 2026), putting an AI distribution tool directly in the hands of a mid-market chain operator.

Sources: [Oracle](https://www.oracle.com/news/announcement/new-ai-capabilities-in-oracle-opera-cloud-supercharge-hotel-operations-2026-06-16/), [Hotel Technology News](https://hoteltechnologynews.com/2026/07/recent-hotel-technology-launches-show-ai-moving-from-standalone-tools-into-connected-hotel-operations/), [Nation's Restaurant News / Restaurant Business](https://www.nrn.com/independent-restaurants/here-s-how-restaurants-are-using-toast-s-ai-chatbot)

**So what:** Oracle and Toast are the biggest moves this cycle because both embed AI into the system of record operators already use. A PMS or POS AI that operators ask questions of daily is categorically different from a standalone AI tool they have to log into separately. That usage data from Toast IQ is gold for PE-backed restaurant tech companies positioning against Toast — the bar for "embedded AI" has been set in concrete numbers.

## AI Visibility & Discovery

The competition to become the front door for hospitality discovery is accelerating across multiple platforms simultaneously.


Two weeks after launching agentic bookings in AI Mode to reserve restaurants, buy event tickets and make beauty and wellness appointments, Google confirmed it is developing similar capabilities for hotel and flight reservations. "Our goal is that you'll be able to also book flights and hotels directly in AI Mode," said Julie Farago, Google's vice president of engineering for travel and local search. Google is working on building out the experience with industry partners including Booking.com, Expedia, Marriott International, IHG Hotels & Resorts, Choice Hotels International and Wyndham Hotels & Resorts.
 Google confirmed it 
will not serve as merchant of record; partner companies will manage transactions and service bookings.
 Restaurant bookings via AI Mode in the US are live now without a Labs opt-in.

At Google I/O 2026, 
Gemini Spark joined the ranks of ChatGPT, Claude and Le Chat as a task-execution assistant capable of drafting emails, making restaurant reservations and booking hotels, with the significant advantage of native integration across Gmail, Calendar, and Maps. Crucially, unlike a booking made through an OTA, the hotel retains Merchant of Record status and full ownership of the guest relationship.



OpenAI launched apps in ChatGPT with Expedia and Booking.com among its first partners. "By bridging the gap between planning in ChatGPT conversations and booking on Expedia, we see enormous potential to create seamless traveler experiences," said Ariane Gorin, CEO of Expedia Group.



A July 11 analysis summarised why Google still matters more than ChatGPT for current booking volume, how AI results rely heavily on crawled website content rather than live data, and why strong SEO remains more important than chasing GEO shortcuts for hotels.


**OpenTable** responded to the DoorDash reservation push by giving top-tier loyalty members access to the most exclusive restaurant inventory (NRN, July 9), leaning into its discovery network advantage as the competitive differentiation it can credibly maintain.

Sources: [PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/google-agentic-travel-booking-ai), [Hospitality ON](https://hospitality-on.com/en/distribution/google-brings-its-full-firepower-claim-central-role-hotel-distribution), [Hotel Revenue Insights](https://www.hotelrevenueinsights.com/july-11-2026/)

**So what:** For mid-market hotels without structured data and machine-readable inventory, the Google agentic travel booking rollout is the most consequential distribution event since metasearch. Hotels whose content is locked in PDFs and non-structured CMS pages will not appear in agentic results at all. Review your content architecture now — before the hotel/flight booking layer goes live.

## Operational AI

The PYMNTS deep-dive published July 14 synthesises the strongest operational AI signal this cycle — AI is becoming the management coordination layer in hotels, not primarily a guest-facing novelty.


Labor accounts for 51.7% of hotel operating expenses and 32.4% of total revenue at U.S. hotels, per CBRE Group's "Trends in the Hotel Industry" report. Hotels paid 11.9% more in total labor costs while operating with 5.9% fewer employees than in 2019. In North America, 65% of hotels reported staffing shortages in 2025, BCG reported, citing the American Hotel and Lodging Association.



AI platforms are beginning to replace the coordination layer by connecting occupancy forecasts, IoT sensor data and guest activity into a single operational system. The Ritz-Carlton San Francisco implemented an AI system synchronizing room-cleaning schedules with checkout patterns, cutting room preparation time by 20%, BCG reported. IHG Hotels & Resorts has deployed predictive housekeeping models that anticipate peak cleaning times and allocate resources accordingly. Predictive maintenance programs can reduce maintenance costs by 25% to 30% and cut equipment downtime by 35% to 45%, according to the U.S. Department of Energy.


On the restaurant side, 
Taco Bell's drive-thru voice AI expanded to nearly 900 U.S. restaurants through a partnership with Omilia (NRN, July 8), making it one of the largest voice AI deployments in quick-service.
 This is a significant scale milestone for drive-thru AI after Taco Bell earlier had to rethink adoption following inconsistent performance.


PAR Technology's head of AI published analysis (Restaurant Dive, July 14) arguing that AI tools with a holistic view of restaurant operations can make operators more efficient, improve margins and drive same-store sales growth
 — though PAR's specific outcome evidence was not available from the search snippet alone.

Sources: [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/ai-is-becoming-the-hotel-industrys-new-manager/), [Hotel Technology News](https://hoteltechnologynews.com/2026/07/recent-hotel-technology-launches-show-ai-moving-from-standalone-tools-into-connected-hotel-operations/), [Nation's Restaurant News](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology)

**So what:** The measurable ROI cases are in housekeeping, predictive maintenance, and operational coordination — not guest-facing AI. Operators chasing chatbot headlines while ignoring IoT-fed maintenance scheduling are working the wrong end of the ROI table. For PE-backed hotel portfolios, the BCG data on predictive maintenance cost reduction (25–30%) provides a defensible capital allocation case.

## Data & Integration

The central theme of HITEC 2026 was the PMS evolving from system of record to orchestration layer — and the gap between vendors claiming that shift and those actually executing it.


A series of recent hotel technology announcements shows how AI is moving from standalone applications into the core operating systems hotels use every day. Vendors are less focused on isolated tools and more focused on intelligence layers that connect the PMS, guest communications, network infrastructure, revenue management, procurement, access control, building operations and commercial sales workflows.



Grevon Kore's MCP-based connectivity layer points to an emerging competitive front in hotel distribution: making official hotel content, rates, availability and booking paths usable by AI agents — as travelers rely more heavily on conversational search and AI assistants to plan trips.
 Zucchetti North America similarly released an MCP connector suite at HITEC allowing hotels to accept direct AI platform bookings, bypassing OTA distribution costs.


Agoda, a Booking Holdings subsidiary, found that AI-assisted automation drove a double-digit year-over-year reduction in customer service costs per booking.
 This is one of the cleaner attributable outcome claims in the current cycle.


SevenRooms co-founder Joel Montaniel articulated the data integration opportunity directly: "Delivery and dine-in have typically been siloed data sets. So if a customer has ordered six times, and they're coming into the restaurant for the first time, are they a first-time customer or a seventh-time customer?"
 The DoorDash/SevenRooms integration attempts to answer that.

Sources: [Hotel Technology News](https://hoteltechnologynews.com/2026/07/recent-hotel-technology-launches-show-ai-moving-from-standalone-tools-into-connected-hotel-operations/), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/25/doordash-resy-opentable-restaurant-reservation-wars.html), [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/ai-is-becoming-the-hotel-industrys-new-manager/)

**So what:** The MCP-for-hospitality wave means hotels without structured, machine-readable content and live-rate APIs are invisible to agentic booking flows. The architecture question is no longer "what PMS do we have" but "can AI read cleanly across our entire data layer." That's a different procurement conversation and most mid-market operators aren't having it yet.

## People & Skills

Two converging data points define this cluster: hospitality is simultaneously the most AI-exposed industry and the least prepared workforce.


A 2026 workforce analysis by Resume Now found hospitality ranks as the most exposed industry for AI workforce gaps, followed by healthcare, financial services, and logistics. These sectors rely heavily on frontline roles, where training is harder to scale and daily operations leave little room for upskilling.



PYMNTS Intelligence found that 37% of Labor Economy workers, including those in hospitality, said their employer had introduced new automation or AI tools in the previous 12 months. Yet nearly 60% of workers affected by those tools said they received no training, suggesting deployment is moving faster than workforce readiness.



HSMAI Foundation research found hospitality students rated their confidence in applying AI to work tasks at 3.24 out of 5, while rating their program's preparation at 2.78 out of 5 — implying students are self-teaching AI through experimentation rather than through structured curriculum.
 HSMAI CEO Brian Hicks said: "Students are entering the workforce with established AI habits and confidence, but employers must equip future talent with the training, governance, and decision-making frameworks needed to apply AI tools successfully in real-world hotel operations."

Keryn McNamara, CIO of Aimbridge Hospitality, offered the most concrete framing at HITEC 2026: 
"I'm not a coder, but what I personally have been able to achieve with Claude is unimaginable, and I wouldn't have thought it possible a year ago. We have the ability to put that power into our general manager's hands and into our frontline associates' hands — that's why I think we're going to see drastic change."


Sources: [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/ai-is-becoming-the-hotel-industrys-new-manager/), [Allwork.Space](https://allwork.space/2026/04/hospitality-ranks-least-prepared-for-ai-as-workforce-gaps-widen/), [PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/news/technology/hospitality-tech-future-hitec-2026)

**So what:** The 60% no-training figure is an operator liability problem, not just an HR problem. If a housekeeper is relying on an AI scheduling system she doesn't understand, and it routes her wrong, the failure mode is a guest complaint, not a help desk ticket. Operators deploying AI without parallel training programmes are compounding their operational risk.

## Governance & Security


On June 2, 2026, the White House issued an Executive Order titled "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security." On its face, the Order is about federal systems, frontier-model security, and national cyber defense. Read carefully, it is one of the clearest signals yet that artificial intelligence has crossed a threshold — from an experimental productivity tool to a critical layer of operational infrastructure that governments now treat as a matter of national security.



The Order directs the Treasury to form an AI cybersecurity clearinghouse — a voluntary collaboration with the AI industry and infrastructure operators to coordinate vulnerability scanning, validation, and patch distribution. It extends AI-enabled cybersecurity tooling to operators of critical infrastructure.
 Terence Ronson of Pertlink, translating the Order for hospitality (Hospitality Net), argued that 
the Order's significance lies less in any single clause than in what it collectively recognizes: that artificial intelligence is now a national strategic capability, a cybersecurity priority, an economic competitiveness engine, and a critical infrastructure dependency all at once.



As hospitality businesses increasingly rely on digital tools, automation, biometrics, and AI-enabled services, their collection and use of personal data has expanded significantly. Biometric identifiers used for access control, timekeeping, drive-through, or kiosks are now included in the personal data footprint. The data controller remains responsible for legal compliance — including when vendors or franchisors dictate the technology used.


The Colorado AI Act takes effect June 30, 2026, and California's ADMT compliance obligations trigger January 1, 2027 — two near-term compliance gates that hospitality operators using automated decision tools (pricing, scheduling, hiring screening) need to document now.

Sources: [Hospitality Net](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/opinion/4132771/when-national-ai-security-becomes-a-hospitality-imperative), [Fox Rothschild](https://dataprivacy.foxrothschild.com/2026/03/articles/general-privacy-data-security-news-developments/what-hospitality-hr-operations-and-leadership-need-to-know-about-data-privacy-and-ai/)

**So what:** The governance ask is now commercial, not just compliance. Buyers are beginning to add AI governance questionnaires to RFPs, and cyber insurers are tightening requirements around AI agent auditing. For PE-backed hospitality SaaS companies, inability to demonstrate data handling transparency and agent containment is increasingly a deal-stopper, not a year-two problem.
