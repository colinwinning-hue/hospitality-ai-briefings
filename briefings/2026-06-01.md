## AI VISIBILITY & DISCOVERY

Hotels face a new distribution layer — AI search engines — that requires fundamentally different optimization strategies, and the gap between visible and invisible properties is widening fast.


**Lighthouse** acquired **Hotelrank.ai** on May 28, 2026, adding an AI visibility measurement layer to its Connect AI solution — giving hotels the ability to "manage their appearance, ranking and performance" across AI travel platforms.
 This is the clearest signal in this cycle that hotel commercial teams are beginning to treat AI search presence as a managed distribution channel, not just an SEO afterthought.


**Philip Warthen**, CEO of First Wave AI, articulated the structural shift: "This is becoming a new distribution layer. In the past, hotels optimized for search rankings. Now, they need to optimize to be recommended, summarized and trusted by AI systems." He noted that many hotels are excluded because their data footprint is fragmented, that AI models "prioritize confidence," and that many AI crawlers do not execute JavaScript — meaning content embedded in booking engines may be invisible to LLMs entirely.



**SiteMinder** linked hotel inventory to AI booking channels via MCP, with DirectBooker as the first partner supporting this pathway. SiteMinder's Changing Traveller Report 2026 found that eight in 10 travelers want AI assistance during the booking process.



**IHG CEO Elie Maalouf** offered the clearest chain-level framing in Q1 2026 earnings: "It's more important to ready technology and content for AI searches to get the right visibility with the right content... Launching an app store is actually pretty easy. What is the content that it's pulling? Is your content in the cloud? Is the data structured in the right way to respond?" IHG's new content platform highlights hotels' features across multiple languages and formats including video and VR.



**Wyndham** unveiled a native ChatGPT app while **Choice Hotels** unveiled a series of internal AI tools in the same week in early May — confirming AI discovery readiness as a board-level priority at major chains.


Sources: [PhocusWire – Lighthouse/Hotelrank.ai](https://www.phocuswire.com/), [PhocusWire – Hotels risk invisibility](https://www.phocuswire.com/hotels-risk-invisibility-ai-reshapes-travel-discovery), [PhocusWire – Hotel groups mull app vs AI-ready](https://www.phocuswire.com/news/technology/hotels-ai-apps-content-ai-ready)

**So what:** If your hotel's content isn't structured, multilingual, and crawlable by LLMs right now, you're invisible in the emerging AI booking layer. The distribution battle has shifted from SEO keywords to data quality and schema markup — and OTAs already hold that advantage. The window to establish a first-party AI presence before aggregators lock it up is closing.

## DATA & INTEGRATION

The PMS is becoming the control layer of the hotel tech stack, and MCP is emerging as the protocol standard for connecting hotel data to AI platforms.


**Agentic Hospitality** launched its TravelOS MCP Server (March 2026), connecting hotel reservation systems directly to AI platforms. The system enables hotels to surface real-time availability, rates, and inventory inside AI assistants while maintaining full control over pricing and guest relationships. Alongside it, the company launched an Agentic Booking Engine that overlays existing internet booking engines, enabling conversational booking flows across websites and AI surfaces while routing transactions through the hotel's existing CRS.



CEO Brad Brewer's architecture argument is the right counter-positioning to OTA aggregation inside AI: "No traveler is going to download a random aggregator they've never heard of. But they will engage with a hotel brand they recognize. That's what we've enabled inside AI." Aggregator-style MCP platforms, he argues, repeat OTA dynamics — hotels reduced to interchangeable inventory competing on ad spend rather than data quality.



**Cendyn** launched AI Connect, pushing hotel ARI (Availability, Rates, Inventory) directly into ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini via MCP in partnership with DirectBooker. The company's argument is pointed: "Before now, most of this information has been surfaced to AI tools from OTAs. By taking advantage of MCP solutions, more hotels can be seen at the exact moment travelers are making decisions."



**Sirma Group** is debuting its "Travel & Hospitality 360 Connect" platform at HITEC (June 15–18). The European software group, having completed over 350 integrations across 6,000+ hotels since 2016, is pitching enterprise-wide AI orchestration across operations, maintenance, and revenue under a single secured enterprise data layer — addressing the "familiar architectural headache: vendor fragmentation" created by rapid AI adoption.


Sources: [Hospitality Net – Agentic Hospitality TravelOS](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4132086/), [Cendyn – Year of MCP](https://www.cendyn.com/blog/year-mcp-2026/), [Hospitality Technology – Sirma/HITEC](https://hospitalitytech.com/news-briefs)

**So what:** The PMS-as-control-layer thesis is now investment consensus. Mid-market operators and PE-backed portfolio companies should pressure-test every vendor on whether their stack is MCP-ready and whether their PMS exposes data in a structured, queryable format for AI agent consumption. If not, the AI distribution layer being built right now will route around you.

## VENDOR MOVES

A cluster of platform-level moves signals that the "AI everywhere" phase is giving way to "AI in the stack" — embedded rather than bolted on.


**Mews** continues its acquisition-led platform buildout following its $300M Series D at a $2.5B valuation — the largest funding round in hospitality software history. The funding is explicitly aimed at "embedding agent-driven systems across the platform to automate complex workflows." Mews now processes 42.3 million checked-in reservations annually (3.2 million via kiosk) and claims $537 million in additional revenue created for hoteliers through Mews Spaces. Late 2025 acquisitions of Flexkeeping and DataChat have been absorbed into the platform.



**Cendyn** is rolling out an expanded Managed Services model alongside its MCP distribution play, pivoting from passive software renewals to proactive account management and commercial consultation tied to revenue outcomes. Early adopters including Outrigger Hospitality Group and ONYX Hospitality Group "report that the leadership shift has already accelerated direct-channel growth." Cendyn serves 32,000+ properties across 150 countries.



**Wonder** (restaurant) announced **Wonder Create**, a system that uses AI to design and launch a restaurant brand "in under a minute." CEO Marc Lore described it as "a Shopify front-end with an AI prompt": "You type in what kind of restaurant you want to build. It builds the restaurant — AI does — in under a minute. It does the name, branding, description, pictures, pricing, health information, and all the recipes for your restaurant." Wonder currently has 120 "programmable cooking platforms" operating as up to 25 restaurant brands simultaneously, expanding to 400 next year.
 
Ghost kitchens tried something similar and struggled to build customer loyalty — MrBeast Burger vividly illustrated the challenge of inconsistent quality. Wonder's added layer of automation is designed to solve exactly that problem, but the model is still unproven at scale.


Sources: [Hotel Dive – Mews $300M](https://www.hoteldive.com/news/mews-series-d-300-million-funding/810338/), [TechCrunch – Wonder/Marc Lore](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/05/marc-lore-says-that-ai-will-soon-enable-anyone-open-a-restaurant/), [Hospitality Technology – Cendyn HITEC](https://hospitalitytech.com/news-briefs)

**So what:** Platform consolidation is accelerating. PMS vendors with clean data architectures and MCP readiness are pulling away from point-solution competitors. For restaurant operators, Wonder's model is worth watching not as a competitive threat but as a template for what AI-enabled commissary cooking could mean for ghost kitchen economics — and as a distribution question: do you want to be on Wonder's platform or build your own?

## OPERATIONAL AI

The NRA Show marks a shift from AI theory to operational proof. Starbucks' AI reversal is the counter-signal that deserves equal weight.


The 2026 National Restaurant Association Show (McCormick Place, May 16–19) attracted more than 2,200 exhibitors. According to Restaurant Technology News, nearly 90% of operators expect food and labor costs to continue increasing this year, while 80% of consumers say overall value remains their top priority. AI discussion at the show "has become noticeably more practical compared to previous years — vendors are increasingly focused on targeted operational applications that can help solve specific pain points."



Eight major software players — NCR Voyix, Toast, Square, Kluck, Cloudhood, Nory, Restaurant365, and SpotOn — showcased unified commerce platforms combining business management modules with self-service hardware. The show floor confirmed that "AI is no longer just hype. Restaurants are already reaping the benefits of conversational voice orders, biometric identification verification, digital menu management, robotic food preparation, robotic waiters, robotic baristas and more."



**Starbucks** eliminated its computer vision AI inventory counting system — which employees called "unreliable" — in favor of traditional stock-keeping methods.
 This failure, at scale and with full enterprise resources, is the single most important counter-signal in this briefing. Computer vision back-of-house automation has been one of the most hyped categories for three years. No vendor has specified the failure mode, but the reversal matters.


On the hotel side, h2c's global study cited by Cendyn confirms the execution gap: while 78% of hotel chains use AI, "this implementation often remains at surface level, with solutions such as chatbots and partially automated marketing. Integration challenges are a barrier for 45% of hotels. An unclear strategy is an issue for 51%."


Sources: [Restaurant Technology News – NRA Show 2026](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/05/ai-automation-and-cost-pressures-take-center-stage-at-the-2026-national-restaurant-association-show/), [Restaurant Dive – Starbucks AI](https://www.restaurantdive.com/), [Cendyn – Year of MCP](https://www.cendyn.com/blog/year-mcp-2026/)

**So what:** The Starbucks computer vision failure is a direct red flag for any operator evaluating back-of-house AI vision systems. Demand proof-of-concept results at similar volume and operational complexity — not controlled pilots. For hotel operators, the 51% "unclear strategy" statistic is the real barrier; AI investment without a unified data architecture first is money poorly deployed.

## PEOPLE & SKILLS

The hotel industry now has a formal body attempting to coordinate AI adoption, while restaurant operators have their first quantified AI hiring outcome data.


**Ira Vouk** launched the **AI Hospitality Alliance (AIHA)** in April 2026, positioning it as an independent global platform targeting stakeholder groups "that have traditionally operated in silos" — hoteliers, technology partners, researchers, and investors. Her stated motivation: "I just wanted to turn those discussions into actions, and that just wasn't happening on a large scale in the industry before and there was no coordination between different efforts."



On the restaurant side, **Chipotle** provided the clearest quantified outcome of the cycle: AI-powered hiring tools reduced its application-to-start date from 12 days to 4, nearly doubled applicant flow, and pushed application completion rates from roughly 50% to nearly 85%, per National Restaurant Association data.



Miami-based **MAJC**, launched in February 2026, combines AI-powered job-matching, training, and community tools into a single workforce engine for hospitality professionals, framed around an industry where the average annual restaurant turnover rate tops 75%, with QSRs regularly exceeding 130%.


Sources: [Hotel Dive – AI Hospitality Alliance](https://www.hoteldive.com/news/new-alliance-uniting-hotel-industry-artificial-intelligence/819935/), [PYMNTS – Restaurants and AI Hiring](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/restaurants-cannot-find-workers-ai-says-it-can/)

**So what:** The AIHA is worth tracking as a potential standards and procurement guidance body. For PE-backed operators, the Chipotle hiring data is the rare specific outcome number in this cycle — AI hiring tools cut time-to-start by 67%. That's actionable ROI for any high-turnover portfolio, and the methodology is citable.

## FUNDING & M&A

Investment volume is at a cycle high, with capital concentrating around PMS platforms and AI-native guest experience tools. The Caesars deal is the headline M&A event.


Forty global hospitality technology startups raised more than $1 billion in the past year, with property management systems and AI-led platforms attracting the most investment, according to Abode Worldwide's Hospitality Tech Investment Index 2026. The three largest raises were Mews ($300M), Kindred ($125M across two rounds), and Limehome (€75M).



AI-led guest experience platforms were also a standout, with Duve, Chatlyn, Conduit and Canary Technologies raising a combined $152.6 million. Canary notably acquired OpenKey in February to expand its access to door lock providers, confirming the strategic intent to own the full guest journey from pre-arrival to room access.



Abode Worldwide CEO Jessica Gillingham put the investment thesis plainly: "Investors are concentrating capital in the platforms that hospitality businesses increasingly depend on every day, especially PMS and AI-led systems that unify operations, improve automation and strengthen the data layer underneath the business."


**Lighthouse** acquired **Hotelrank.ai** on May 28, 2026 [see AI Visibility & Discovery cluster above] — the most recent M&A move of this cycle, directly targeting the AI search visibility gap.


Nearly half of the 40 companies tracked were raised at pre-seed, seed, or Series A, and more than half were founded after 2020, suggesting the market is "still being built."
 On the restaurant side, **Dishio** raised $2.5 million to help restaurants turn guest data into repeat revenue — a micro-round but part of a broader pattern of seed capital flowing into first-party guest data capture for independent operators.

The **Caesars/Fertitta Entertainment $17.6B deal** (announced May 28) is the week's dominant M&A headline. The combination brings Caesars' resort portfolio and digital gaming platform under the same roof as Fertitta's Golden Nugget Hotels & Casinos and Landry's restaurant brands, under a "go-shop" stipulation. Caesars has been an aggressive data-and-loyalty AI investor among US chains; integration with Fertitta's operational portfolio introduces execution risk for that AI roadmap.

Sources: [Hotel Dive – Hospitality Tech $1B Funding](https://www.hoteldive.com/news/hospitality-tech-attracts-1b-in-funding-pms-ai-leaders/817439/), [PhocusWire – Lighthouse/Hotelrank.ai](https://www.phocuswire.com/), [Hotel Dive – Caesars/Fertitta](https://www.hoteldive.com/news/caesars-fertitta-entertainment-acquisition/821357/)

**So what:** For PE-backed hospitality SaaS companies, this investment concentration confirms that "data moat" is the correct pitch — not "AI-powered." Investors are rewarding platforms with embedded workflows and high switching costs. The Caesars deal also introduces a data integration wildcard: watch what happens to Caesars' AI infrastructure under new ownership.

## WHO TO WATCH

**Adam Harris (Cloudbeds CEO)** — 
His argument at the Skift Data + AI Summit is that most hotel AI platforms being adopted today are built on probabilistic architectures that will soon be outdated: "Hotel CEOs who sign multi-year platform contracts in 2026 on the strength of AI capability demos are committing to architecture that will be obsolete within three years." By 2029, he argues, today's AI tools will look like fax machines.
 The deterministic vs. probabilistic AI framing is the sharpest procurement diligence question of this cycle. Worth tracking whether it reshapes buying criteria at scale.

**Ira Vouk (Founder, AI Hospitality Alliance)** — 
She frames the moment bluntly: "If we snooze through it again like we did 30 years ago in the beginning of the internet, then it's going to get worse."
 Whether AIHA develops enough cross-stakeholder credibility to function as a neutral standards voice — rather than another thought-leadership vehicle — will determine whether it becomes structurally important or fades in 18 months.

**Brad Brewer (Chief AI Officer, Agentic Hospitality)** — Running the sharpest infrastructure play in the MCP space by positioning TravelOS as a hotel-side alternative to OTA aggregation inside AI platforms. 
His distinction is direct: "In most models, the guest is still being routed through someone else's storefront. In our model, the guest interacts directly with the hotel brand."
 Claim is correct in architecture; needs booking volume data to be confirmed as Signal rather than Watch.

**Elie Maalouf (CEO, IHG)** — 
Q1 earnings comments revealed a board-level clarity most chain CEOs haven't stated publicly: "That's actually the most important thing. Launching an app store is actually pretty easy. What is the content that it's pulling? Is your content in the cloud? Is the data structured in the right way?"
 IHG's content platform investment backs the claim. Watch whether other chains match this level of AI infrastructure specificity in their earnings commentary.

**Marc Lore (CEO, Wonder)** — 
Wonder Create lets anyone launch a restaurant in under a minute via AI prompt, going live across Wonder's growing network of "programmable cooking platforms" — currently 120 locations, targeting 400 next year.
 The model combines AI brand generation, robotic cooking, and Grubhub distribution. If Wonder can crack consistent food quality at scale, it redefines what "opening a restaurant" means for a generation of food entrepreneurs and brand licensees. The ghost kitchen caveat remains real.

**Saleem S. Khatri (CEO, Lavu / Founder, Marty)** — 
His "painkiller vs. vitamin" framework in Restaurant Technology News cuts directly at the agentwashing problem: ask vendors "What data did you train on? What happens to my product if OpenAI raises prices 10x tomorrow?" If the answer involves "we leverage cutting-edge LLM technology," they're reselling someone else's product at a markup.
 The most operationally grounded vendor-interrogation checklist published this cycle. Any multi-unit restaurant operator evaluating AI tools should run it before signing.
