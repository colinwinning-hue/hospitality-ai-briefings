## Funding & M&A

American Express is consolidating the global restaurant reservation stack into a single card-linked dining ecosystem, while DoorDash completes the first visible product integrations from its 2025 acquisition spree.


**American Express** announced on June 15 the proposed acquisition of TheFork from Tripadvisor for $700 million in cash, subject to regulatory approval expected before year-end. TheFork connects millions of diners with more than 50,000 restaurants across 11 European countries. Combined with existing platforms Resy and Tock, the acquisition would expand American Express' dining network to approximately 75,000 bookable venues worldwide.



**Resy/Tock merger** (announced February 2026) is scheduled to complete this summer, adding roughly 8,000 venues — including 1,200 wineries — to Resy's platform, roughly doubling its inventory. The combined platform is designed to be the primary Amex dining property, with DoorDash's $1.2 billion acquisition of SevenRooms sharpening the competitive pressure Amex is responding to.



**Resy** reported that in the three weeks following the announcement of a $400 annual Platinum card dining credit, there was a 36% increase in Resy reservations made by Platinum cardholders, with linked users spending on average 25% more.



**Resy** separately confirmed a partnership with Anthropic: Claude users can now explore U.S. Resy restaurants directly in conversations, browse available tables in real time, and secure a booking. This makes Resy one of the few reservation platforms with documented live integrations across multiple AI interfaces.



**Hospitality tech investment** crossed $1 billion across 40 startups in the 12 months to March 2026, led by a concentrated 90-day burst: Mews ($300M), Kindred ($125M), Limehome (€75M). AI-led guest experience platforms Duve, Chatlyn, Conduit, and Canary raised a combined $152.6 million. Abode Worldwide's Jessica Gillingham: "The companies attracting the strongest investor backing share a common trait. They sit close to essential operator workflows and become more valuable over time."


**Sources:** [RTN – TheFork Acquisition](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/06/american-express-expands-restaurant-technology-ambitions-with-700-million-thefork-acquisition/) · [Fast Company – Resy/Tock Merger](https://www.fastcompany.com/91496951/amex-resy-tock-restaurant-reservation-wars) · [Abode Worldwide via Hotel Dive](https://www.hoteldive.com/news/hospitality-tech-attracts-1b-in-funding-pms-ai-leaders/817439/)

**So what:** American Express is building what no pure restaurant-tech vendor can replicate — a reservation and data platform attached to billions in cardholder spending. For operators on Resy, Tock, or TheFork, this consolidation means more powerful demand generation but also tighter dependency on an Amex-controlled data relationship. Independent operators should clarify now what guest data they own versus what lives on the platform.

## Vendor Moves

The DoorDash/SevenRooms/Deliveroo ecosystem delivers its first live product integration; Mews launches its most ambitious platform yet; Toast creates a live-operator product lab.


**Deliveroo UAE** launched Deliveroo Reservations in Dubai on June 4, powered by SevenRooms, giving customers real-time table availability inside the Deliveroo app. The launch represents the first major product integration since DoorDash's acquisitions of Deliveroo and SevenRooms in 2025.
 
Nick Price, Deliveroo Middle East GM: "By expanding beyond delivery and into the dine-out space, we are creating new opportunities for our restaurant partners to drive in-store sales, optimise table occupancy, and connect with new audiences." The service has initially launched in Dubai, with plans to expand across the UAE.



**Mews** unveiled five new products at Unfold 2026, completing what the company calls an "AI-native Operating System" — unifying PMS, RMS, messaging, automations, and accounts receivable on a single data model. Mews RMS runs 150 million pricing calculations a day and executes rate changes against live demand without manual intervention, lifting total revenue per square meter 13.7% on average against a matched control group. Richard Valtr, Mews founder: "PMS, RMS, CRS, CRM, POS — these aren't real things. They're acronyms invented by software companies to sell more software. Inside Mews, those categories don't exist as separate systems. They are one workflow." Independent IDC research cited by Mews: hotels on the platform achieve 8.7% revenue growth and a 476% three-year ROI with a four-month payback.



**Toast** announced Toast Lab on June 16, an initiative to co-fund and co-develop a new restaurant in Boston with a selected operator, with applications running through July 14. The selected operator receives strategic capital, mentorship from Toast's executive team, access to its full product suite, and real-time customer support — in exchange for co-developing new products alongside Toast engineers.
 Kelly Esten, Toast CMO and COO: 
"Toast Lab is a formal return to that instinct, inspired by our belief that the best restaurant technology gets built when operators and product teams are working side-by-side to create something new together."


**Sources:** [Gulf Business – Deliveroo Dubai](https://gulfbusiness.com/en/2026/lifestyle/from-takeaway-to-restaurant-reservations-deliveroo-launches-new-dubai-service/) · [Hospitality Net – Mews Unfold](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4132634/mews-unveils-the-operating-system-for-hospitality) · [Restaurant Business Online – Toast Lab](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/technology/toast-wants-help-open-restaurant-boston)

**So what:** The Deliveroo/SevenRooms Dubai integration is the most operationally concrete watchlist development this cycle. A unified delivery-and-reservations surface, deployed by a company that now controls SevenRooms' CRM data, is the architecture DoorDash said it would build when it made the acquisitions. For restaurant groups in MENA markets, this is no longer theoretical. Mews' Unfold claims demand scrutiny against the IDC methodology — but a four-month payback figure with disclosed causal inference is better sourced than most vendor ROI claims.

## AI Visibility & Discovery

Google, DoorDash, Wyndham, and Agentic Hospitality all moved to position themselves in the AI discovery layer — while Hospitality.today's freshest analysis this week argues that the hotels investing most in AI eligibility may be funding a funnel whose bottom belongs to OTAs.


**DoorDash** launched "Ask DoorDash" on June 11, a conversational AI chatbot that lets users order food and groceries with text prompts and photos, rolling out on iOS in select regions for restaurant search and grocery shopping, and within DoorDash Reservations. Users can tell the chatbot what they're in the mood for, share a recipe link to find ingredients, or describe the reservation they're looking for.
 
Early testing showed that nearly half of all restaurant orders placed through the tool were from a place the customer had never ordered from before. The company said carts assembled through the chatbot ran more than 35% higher in value than a standard grocery order. DoorDash co-founder Andy Fang said the product draws on AI models built by OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google, supplemented by open-source options.



**Google** introduced agentic capabilities within AI Mode beginning with restaurant reservations in the UK, allowing users to ask Google's AI to handle the entire reservation process — identifying suitable restaurants, checking availability, and confirming bookings — without navigating multiple links or interacting with third-party platforms. Google is now developing hotel and flight booking through the same architecture, working with Booking.com, Expedia, Marriott, IHG, Choice, and Wyndham. Details on booking flow and payments are still being finalized.



**Wyndham** launched the first native hotel app from a major U.S. economy-midscale franchisor within OpenAI's ChatGPT ecosystem in May, allowing map-based browsing and conversational search across its 8,400-hotel portfolio. Wyndham says the most engaged hotels using its AI platforms averaged more than $60,000 in incremental revenue last year, while the highest-performing property exceeded $200,000 — addressing a key franchise owner ROI question.
 
Since 2018, Wyndham has invested more than $450 million in technology modernization, and in 2020 became the first major hotel company to fully migrate its systems to the cloud.



**Agentic Hospitality** launched its TravelOS MCP Server and ChatGPT app framework, connecting directly to a hotel's CRS and PMS so all availability, pricing, and inventory originate from the system of record. The platform captures the full conversation — from initial inquiry to booking intent — and passes it downstream into hotel systems. Brad Brewer, founder: "The guest interacts directly with the hotel brand. They're not downloading an intermediary; they're effectively downloading the brand." The company explicitly positions itself against aggregator-style platforms that, in Brewer's framing, are "trying to become the next Expedia."



**OpenAI** quietly scaled back plans to enable direct purchases inside ChatGPT in March 2026, shelving a vision in which travelers could discover, compare, and book entirely within the chat interface. Booking Holdings shares jumped over 8.5% on the news day. Hospitality.today's Markus Busch argued: "Building full transaction infrastructure inside a conversational AI interface turns out to be genuinely hard. Payments, inventory synchronisation, merchant agreements, dispute resolution, cancellation logic — these aren't product features. They are operational businesses."



**The structural warning** (Hospitality.today, June 24): On April 28, Booking Holdings CEO Fogel told analysts the company was "working closely with leading external partners — OpenAI and Google named explicitly — to ensure Booking's supply is discoverable wherever travelers begin their journey." On May 8, Expedia CEO Ariane Gorin said the AI checkout pullback reinforced Expedia's view that AI should handle discovery while booking is best handled by "a trusted, scaled provider" — and if AI evolves toward paid advertising at the discovery layer, "that's a space Expedia knows well and considers a net positive." What it produces is a more legible hotel in the discovery layer of a system whose conversion layer OTAs already own.


**Sources:** [DoorDash official](https://about.doordash.com/en-us/news/ask-doordash) · [RTN – Google AI Booking](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/04/google-introduces-ai-powered-restaurant-booking-as-search-evolves-into-action/) · [Hospitality.today – OTA harvest](https://www.hospitality.today/article/hotels-are-building-the-ai-discovery-layer-otas-are-harvesting-it) · [Hospitality.today – OpenAI](https://www.hospitality.today/article/openai-just-saved-booking-com)

**So what:** The DoorDash 35%-higher cart value and 50% new-restaurant discovery rate are the most concrete consumer AI commerce figures in this cycle — for restaurant operators, that's the gap between "AI feature" and "revenue channel." For hoteliers, OpenAI's checkout retreat bought time but not safety: the Expedia and Booking earnings quotes are the most important strategic reads of the month. Building AI eligibility without also building a direct conversion path is funding the top of a funnel you don't control.

## Data & Integration

MCP (Model Context Protocol) is emerging as hospitality's next infrastructure battleground, with early movers competing to determine whether AI booking flows route through brands or intermediaries.


**Agentic Hospitality** launched the TravelOS MCP Server as an infrastructure layer that connects hotel reservation systems directly to AI platforms. The server connects to a hotel's CRS and PMS, which remain the system of record, without duplicating inventory, scraping rates, or creating separate booking systems. Brad Brewer: "Hotels have invested heavily in the systems that run their operations. Our approach is simple. We extend those systems into AI platforms rather than replacing them."



**Booking Holdings** is actively deploying AI across its portfolio. Agoda found that AI-assisted automation drove a double-digit year-over-year reduction in customer service costs per booking. OpenTable launched an AI Concierge in July 2025 that initially answered 80% of diners' questions and is now evolving into a broader discovery tool with capabilities to help restaurant partners gain actionable insights. Priceline's Penny AI travel agent is showing "noticeable uplift" in early conversion testing.



**The PMS architecture argument**: IDC's 2026 forecast holds that discovery, comparison, booking, and service will all be mediated by AI agents acting on guests' behalf. Stripe, Visa, Mastercard, AWS, OpenAI, and Google have all launched payment infrastructure specifically for AI agents in recent months. "Amazon's announcement this month named hotel bookings as a primary use case. The rails of the agentic economy are being laid right now, by the largest payment and technology companies on earth."


**Sources:** [Hospitality Net – Agentic Hospitality](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4132086/agentic-hospitalitys-travelos-mcp-chatgpt-app-is-putting-brands-not-intermediaries-at-the-center-of-ai-bookings) · [PYMNTS – Booking Holdings AI](https://www.pymnts.com/earnings/2026/booking-holdings-ai-assistants-slash-costs-and-boost-bookings/) · [Hospitality Net – PMS era ending](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/opinion/4132382/the-pms-era-is-ending)

**So what:** The practical question for any mid-market operator right now: does your PMS vendor expose a documented, real-time MCP endpoint? If not, you are invisible in the layer being built. Ask your vendor specifically — not about "API availability" in general, but MCP server support. The answer will tell you whether they're building for 2024 or 2027.

## Operational AI

Restaurant operators at the 2026 NRA Show have moved past "does AI work" — the new question is how to govern and replicate it across large franchise networks without each location becoming a separate environment.


**The NRA Show (Chicago, May 2026)** drew 55,000 professionals from 112 countries. The session "The rise of the agentic restaurant: From hype to reality" centered on a practical infrastructure problem: most restaurant and retail edge AI initiatives struggle to move beyond pilot programs. According to Deborah Matteliano Simeoni (AWS) and Justin Swagler (Spectro Cloud), many brands continue treating each restaurant location as a separate environment instead of building standardized systems capable of supporting AI deployments across entire networks.
 Swagler: 
"If you don't have a governance structure, you're going to stumble on how you scale AI use cases across your entire network." Yum Brands — managing AI-enabled systems across more than 45,000 locations globally — was cited as the primary scale challenge.



**Toast IQ** was adopted by more than 125,000 U.S. restaurant locations in Q1 2026 alone, following its October 2025 launch. Fine dining used it 29% more than fast-casual. Operators most frequently initiated conversations about sales and revenue (47% of restaurants), menu and inventory (34%), and guest and marketing (32%). The most common prompt: "Create a short, easy-to-read daily briefing for my restaurant."



**Operator skepticism on AI**, from a live NRA panel: Bruce Nelson, CFO at Tempo Hospitality Group, described AI's value as identifying small inconsistencies across multiple P&L statements that might otherwise go unnoticed during manual review — but added: "AI doesn't innovate, it just imitates." The panel agreed that operational utility requires human oversight.


**Sources:** [SmartBrief – NRA Show](https://www.smartbrief.com/original/ai-grows-up-at-the-2026-national-restaurant-association-show) · [Rutland Herald / Toast IQ 90-day data](https://www.rutlandherald.com/news/business/90-days-with-toast-iq-how-restaurant-operators-are-using-toast-iq-to-find-time/article_d512dcaa-62be-5e85-9d30-78ad13d724ca.html)

**So what:** Toast IQ at 125,000 locations in one quarter is the fastest adoption curve in restaurant AI this cycle and is a genuine signal, not noise. But the NRA's governance message is more important for multi-unit operators: if your edge infrastructure isn't standardized across locations, no AI product will replicate at scale. Fix the infrastructure first.

## People & Skills

Labor pressure is the structural enablement condition for restaurant AI adoption — and over a third of operators say staffing and skills gaps are their largest barrier to data strategy, not technology cost.


**NRA CEO Michelle Korsmo's keynote** framed the industry's central challenge: with 15.7 million workers employed across the restaurant industry, and the percentage of young workers in the U.S. workforce projected to shrink further by 2034, the labor pipeline is narrowing. Over one-third of operators cite staffing and skill shortages as their biggest barrier to adopting a meaningful data strategy.



**The labor data**: the restaurant sector employed 15.9 million workers in 2025 and average annual turnover topped 75%, with quick-service restaurants regularly exceeding 130%. An industry association's 2026 report found 80% of operators were short at least one position in 2025, the average operator carried five open roles (up from 3.8), and average training cost per hire was $3,037.



**Booking.com and OpenAI** launched an SME AI Accelerator running March through June 2026, targeting 20,000 independent hospitality SMEs across France, Germany, Italy, Poland, Ireland, and the UK with hands-on workshops and virtual AI skills training. The program was built to address a specific bottleneck: 88.2% of hotel workers expressed interest in training, but the barrier is practical enablement, not motivation.


**Sources:** [SmartBrief NRA](https://www.smartbrief.com/original/ai-grows-up-at-the-2026-national-restaurant-association-show) · [Let's Data Science / PYMNTS labor data](https://letsdatascience.com/news/restaurants-turn-to-ai-to-solve-staffing-shortages-d0ef18bc) · [EU Tourism Platform – Booking/OpenAI SME](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu/tourism/support-resources/bookingcom-and-openai-launch-sme-ai-accelerator)

**So what:** AI workforce tools (scheduling, forecasting, training) are solving real problems at the margin, but the structural labor gap — 80% of operators short-staffed, $3,037 per hire — is not a technology problem. The Booking.com/OpenAI SME program is the most interesting skills initiative in this cycle because it targets the specific enablement bottleneck: operators want training, they just can't access it.
