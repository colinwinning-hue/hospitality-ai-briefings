## Vendor Moves

The dining reservation market is being restructured by platform consolidation at a pace that has no recent precedent.

**American Express** is the biggest mover of this cycle. 
The proposed $700 million acquisition of TheFork from Tripadvisor would add the platform's network of more than 50,000 restaurants across 11 European countries to Amex's growing dining ecosystem. Combined with existing platforms Resy and Tock, the acquisition would expand Amex's network to approximately 75,000 bookable venues worldwide. American Express first entered the reservation technology market through its acquisition of Resy in 2019; the acquisition of Tock in 2024 significantly broadened Amex's capabilities in reservations, ticketed events, prepaid dining experiences and hospitality operations.


**Resy and Tock** are simultaneously merging. 
American Express is combining its two reservation platforms into one under Resy this summer, bringing Tock's inventory of fine-dining restaurants and wineries to Resy for the first time. The move will approximately double Resy's library to more than 25,000 venues. The merger also activates a major partnership with POS provider Toast that will connect Resy guestbook data with Toast handheld devices to allow servers to view customer information such as wine preferences and birthdays.
 The commercial stakes are clear: 
in the three weeks following the Amex Platinum dining credit announcement, there was a 36% increase in Resy reservations made by users with a U.S. Platinum card linked to their account — and a five-times increase in the daily average number of accounts being linked. People using the Resy credit are spending, on average, 25% more.


**DoorDash** launched "Ask DoorDash" on June 11. 
The company launched a new AI chatbot that lets users order food and groceries with text prompts and photos. The chatbot, called "Ask DoorDash," allows users to search the app for what they're looking for in their own words instead of having to scroll through restaurants and stores to build a cart.
 Early testing results are substantive: 
nearly half of all restaurant orders placed through the tool were from a place the customer had never ordered from before. The company also said carts assembled through the chatbot ran more than 35% higher in value than a standard grocery order, with users completing them five times faster than they would through the regular app interface. DoorDash co-founder Andy Fang said the product draws on AI models built by OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google, supplemented by open-source options.


**Mews** launched its Business Intelligence product following its January $300 million Series D. 
Mews BI is embedded directly into the Mews operating system, offering hoteliers a "single source of truth" for tracking revenue, occupancy and bookings across their portfolios. Features include AI-powered performance summaries, custom dashboards, automated reporting, and external source data combining Mews' data with Google Ads and OTAs. Mews BI has been adopted by 1,000 early customers.
 "With Mews BI, we're giving hoteliers a single, reliable platform to turn their data into actionable insights," said Pepa Starýchfojtů, Mews CPTO.

So what: The dining data layer — who owns a guest's discovery-to-payment journey — is now a battleground for Amex (Resy+Tock+TheFork+Toast), DoorDash (SevenRooms+Ask DoorDash+Zesty), Booking Holdings (OpenTable), and Google. Mid-market restaurant groups sitting on OpenTable or a standalone CRM should ask which platform will deliver net-new covers rather than commissions on covers they'd fill anyway. Hotel groups should watch whether Booking/OpenTable's Connected Trip strategy creates a genuine cross-sell engine.

## AI Visibility & Discovery

The discovery funnel for both hotels and restaurants is being restructured by AI agents that move users from intent to booking without leaving the chat interface.

**IHG Hotels & Resorts** launched an IHG app in ChatGPT on June 3. 
As more than half of U.S. travelers now use AI to plan trips, IHG's ChatGPT app lets users search, compare and explore IHG's more than 7,000 hotels in over 100 countries. ChatGPT recommends IHG hotels based on travelers' preferences, surfacing real-time availability, pricing, interactive maps and amenities, helping guests move naturally from discovery to comparison. Once a hotel is selected, users are guided to IHG's direct booking channels to complete their reservation.
 IHG CPTO Jolie Fleming: "Pairing AI with the warmth of human hospitality ultimately helps us deliver a more exceptional live experience — one that feels personal, responsive and connected to each guest in the moment."

**Google** deployed agentic restaurant reservations in its AI Mode in the UK, then expanded to the U.S. without a Labs opt-in. 
The new functionality allows users to ask Google's AI to handle the entire reservation process, including identifying suitable restaurants, checking availability and confirming bookings.
 
The strategic direction is clear: Google is moving toward a model in which search functions as a control layer for real-world actions. Restaurant reservations represent an early step in that evolution.
 Flight and hotel booking in AI Mode has been announced but not yet launched; 
the named launch partners include Booking.com, Choice Hotels, Expedia, IHG, Marriott, and Wyndham.


**OpenAI** pulled back from direct commerce transactions. 
OpenAI is shifting its shopping strategy to focus on discovery and research, leaving checkout to third-party app developers. An OpenAI spokesperson confirmed the shift: "We're prioritizing making ChatGPT search and product discovery great, with the Agentic Commerce Protocol serving as the infrastructure that connects users to merchants across the full shopping journey," with Instant Checkout moving to apps.
 This is a material reprieve for OTAs and reservation platforms — it means ChatGPT will surface and recommend, but not process the transaction.

**DoorDash's Zesty** was shut down and absorbed into the main app in April. 
Restaurant discovery is already deeply fragmented, and consumer habits are firmly established across Google Maps, Yelp, Instagram, and TikTok. DoorDash winding down the standalone product and integrating its AI-powered recommendation and conversational search capabilities into its core platform addresses that issue directly. The company's acquisition of SevenRooms last year marked a significant step into on-premise dining.


Sources: [IHG Press Release](https://www.ihgplc.com/en/news-and-media/news-releases/2026/ihg-hotels-and-resorts-advances-conversational-ai-search-capabilities) | [RTN – Google AI Booking](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/04/google-introduces-ai-powered-restaurant-booking-as-search-evolves-into-action/) | [Skift – OpenAI Commerce Walkback](https://skift.com/2026/03/05/openai-chatgpt-checkout-walkback/) | [RTN – DoorDash/Zesty](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/04/doordash-shuts-down-zesty-and-brings-restaurant-ai-discovery-into-core-app/)

So what: Any hotel or restaurant group that isn't structured for AI discoverability — clean, machine-readable data on menus, inventory, availability, and amenities — is already invisible in the fastest-growing discovery channel. GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) is no longer a future initiative. The operators who own their first-party booking flow within these AI environments (via MCP server or branded ChatGPT app) will pay dramatically lower distribution costs than those who become interchangeable inventory inside an aggregator.

## Operational AI

Agentic AI is crossing from demo to deployed infrastructure in restaurant operations, with credible outcomes data now emerging.

**PAR Technology and Square** both launched AI agents for restaurant operators in April. 
PAR Technology and Square unveiled AI products that can assist operators with their business and even complete tasks on their behalf, joining Toast and Thanx which had launched similar tools over the past year. Square's Managerbot is proactive — it surfaces insights and metrics the restaurant needs to know, and can draft employee schedules, create and run email campaigns, and generate purchase orders for supplies.
 PAR's outcome data is specific: 
Charter Foods, a Taco Bell franchisee, tested PAR's agent, asking it to identify stores that could benefit by staying open later. Late-night sales increased 20% as a result of its recommendations.
 PAR's positioning: "PAR Intelligence is designed to make your best people even better — faster decisions, clearer priorities, less time in dashboards and spreadsheets."


AI agents will only be as good as the data they are given to work with, which means they'll need to be fully integrated across a restaurant's existing tech stack. Square noted that Managerbot can make mistakes, and urged operators to check its responses for accuracy.


**Booking Holdings / OpenTable** reported early operational gains from AI. 
Booking.com's AI-driven capabilities have grown to include enhanced natural language search and dynamic discovery features; agentic service flows for complaints and cancellations have reduced customer service contacts and improved the post-booking experience. Agoda found that its AI-assisted automation drove a double-digit year-over-year reduction in customer service costs per booking.
 
CEO Glenn Fogel called AI an area of "notable opportunity," noting early positive signs in conversion, faster search, shorter path to booking, lower cancellation rates, and positive customer satisfaction — though the sample sizes remain limited.


Sources: [NRN – PAR + Square AI Agents](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology/the-hot-new-restaurant-tech-trend-ai-agents) | [PhocusWire – Booking Holdings Q1 2026](https://www.phocuswire.com/news/online/booking-holdings-earnings-q1-2026)

So what: The line between an AI assistant (answers questions) and an AI agent (takes action) has been crossed by PAR, Square, Toast, and Managerbot. For multi-unit restaurant operators, the question is no longer whether to deploy agentic tools but which data infrastructure can support them — agents are only as good as the connected systems underneath. Fragmented POS-inventory-labor stacks will block the productivity gains before they arrive.

## Data & Integration

Platform consolidation is accelerating the shift toward unified data layers as the foundational bet in hospitality tech.


Seven PMS companies raised a combined $408.1 million in the past year, more than any other category, according to Abode Worldwide's Hospitality Tech Investment Index 2026. PMS is increasingly becoming the control layer of the hospitality tech stack — as hotel and lodging operators push to simplify fragmented systems, the PMS is taking on more responsibility, connecting teams, revenue, guest journeys and data in one place.


**Mews** is positioning its $300M raise explicitly around "agentic orchestration." 
Hotels have spent the last decade moving core infrastructure to the cloud, but many are still running on a patchwork of legacy PMS platforms, bolt-on integrations, and manually maintained workflows. Mews argues that autonomous AI agents cannot thrive in that environment because the data is fragmented, the permissions are inconsistent, and the system lacks the API-native plumbing required for software to reliably take action.


**American Express + Toast** are building a data bridge between reservations and POS. 
Amex Card Members spent over $87 billion on dining in the U.S. alone in 2024. The partnership will build tools combining the power of Resy and Tock's guestbook capabilities and Toast's Digital Chits technology, which allows restaurant staff to easily view important customer information directly on Toast Go handhelds and POS terminals during service.
 Toast CEO Aman Narang: "When you book and check in, what Toast can do is create a personalized experience for you — everything from allergies, notes, birthdays but also be able to recommend menu items. Empowering the staff, the host and server, with that data is really valuable."

Sources: [Hotel Dive – Abode Hospitality Tech Index](https://www.hoteldive.com/news/hospitality-tech-attracts-1b-in-funding-pms-ai-leaders/817439/) | [HTN – Mews $300M](https://hoteltechnologynews.com/2026/01/mews-secures-300-million-to-accelerate-agentic-ai-for-autonomous-hotel-management/) | [Restaurant Business – Toast/Amex](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/technology/toast-teams-american-express-leverage-reservations-data)

So what: Operators evaluating PMS or POS replacements now need to ask not just "does this connect to X?" but "can an AI agent act across all my connected systems in real time?" Jessica Gillingham's framing is the right one: the companies attracting capital are those whose platforms compound in value as they centralize data. Switching costs are about to rise dramatically — choose the data layer carefully.

## Governance & Security

A regulatory and operational governance crisis is quietly building underneath AI adoption acceleration in hospitality.


On June 2, 2026, the White House issued an Executive Order titled "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security." The Order is about federal systems, frontier-model security, and national cyber defense — but it represents one of the clearest signals that AI has crossed a threshold from an experimental productivity tool to a critical layer of operational infrastructure that governments now treat as a matter of national security. The modern hotel has quietly become a dense, interconnected intelligent environment binding together identity, payments, guest data, building systems, IoT, energy orchestration, and AI-driven workflows.



The Order directs the Treasury to form an AI cybersecurity clearinghouse — a voluntary collaboration with the AI industry and infrastructure operators to coordinate vulnerability scanning, validation, and patch distribution — and extends AI-enabled cybersecurity tooling to operators of critical infrastructure.


The DOJ's Data Security Program adds a second pressure layer. 
Hospitality companies are accelerating adoption of AI — from dynamic pricing and marketing personalization to fraud detection, digital key platforms, and biometric check-in. These deployments typically require massive volumes of customer and device data and often rely on third-party vendors, offshore development teams, and cloud-hosted model pipelines. Hospitality companies routinely touch at least four of the sensitive personal data categories covered by DOJ's DSP rules: identifiers, geolocation, biometrics, and financial.
 The compliance trap: 
DSP coverage applies even where data is "de-identified." The safest approach is to treat AI vendor access as an important regulatory compliance issue that may touch upon national security matters.



EU AI Act Article 50 transparency requirements for chatbots apply from August 2, 2026. High-risk AI obligations under the Act apply from August 2026. California's Automated Decisionmaking Technology access and opt-out rights take effect January 1, 2027.


Sources: [Hospitality Net – White House EO for Hotels](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/opinion/4132771/when-national-ai-security-becomes-a-hospitality-imperative) | [Hospitality Technology – DOJ Bulk Data](https://hospitalitytech.com/beyond-hipaa-navigating-dojs-new-bulk-data-thresholds-hospitality-ai) | [WitnessAI – Conversational AI Security](https://witness.ai/blog/conversational-ai-hospitality/)

So what: Any hotel deploying AI chatbots into guest-facing flows before August 2 should confirm EU AI Act Article 50 compliance — failure to disclose AI interaction is an active enforcement risk, not a future one. Any operator using offshore AI vendors for processing guest data should conduct a DSP screen immediately. The White House EO is not a hotel regulation, but it is a leading indicator of where compliance expectations are heading.
