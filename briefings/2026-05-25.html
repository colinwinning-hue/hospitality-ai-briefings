AI VISIBILITY & DISCOVERY

Hotel brands are diverging sharply on how to win in AI-mediated search: build native LLM apps, or restructure content for AI readability.

Wyndham Hotels & Resorts launched a native app within ChatGPT on May 7 — described as the first from a major U.S. economy and midscale franchisor — allowing travelers to browse its 8,400-property portfolio through map-based browsing, conversational prompts, amenity filters, and interactive hotel cards before being routed to WyndhamHotels.com to complete bookings.

Since 2018, Wyndham has invested more than $450 million in technology modernization; in 2020 it became the first major hotel company to fully migrate to the cloud. According to the full article, AI-powered call-center tools have reduced average handle times by 7%, and Wyndham Connect's most engaged hotels averaged more than $60,000 in incremental revenue last year, with the top property exceeding $200,000. CEO Geoffrey Ballotti described connecting data to several LLMs as costing $100,000 — "nominal." The ChatGPT launch is Wyndham's second LLM integration in two years, following a 2025 Anthropic Claude launch; Google AI Mode integration is next.

IHG chose a different path. CEO Elie Maalouf said in Q1 2026 earnings that "it's more important to ready technology and content for AI searches to get the right visibility with the right content," backing a new content platform to highlight hotels' features in semantically rich ways rather than building a standalone app. Choice Hotels took the internal route, unveiling Charlie — designed to be a "teammate" guiding employees through the company's core platforms — alongside Business Direct, EasyBid, and RAISE.

Accor's AI and data science chief Nicolas Maynard acknowledged the scale of the problem at a recent industry conference: "We've been trying for a year already to understand how to make ourselves more relevant... and be more visible." He noted that Accor's systems do not currently classify properties by semantic attributes — "The biggest challenge is to understand vague requests like 'I want a romantic hotel in the south'" — and that the group must "adapt our systems to take semantics into account."

A HotelWorld AI analysis of 131,000 properties in 30 countries found that only about 16% of global hotel supply is visible in ChatGPT, Google's AI, and Perplexity results, with chain-affiliated properties significantly outperforming independents. "AI models prioritize confidence. They surface hotels they can clearly understand and validate across multiple sources," said Philip Warthen, CEO of First Wave AI.

Google launched agentic restaurant booking capabilities within AI Mode in the UK (April 10), allowing users to ask Google's AI to handle the entire reservation process — identifying suitable restaurants, checking availability, and confirming bookings — without interacting with third-party platforms. The timing reflects growing momentum around consumer-facing AI agents, and restaurant bookings offer a contained use case where the risks of automation are relatively manageable.

The implications for OpenTable, Resy, and TheFork are significant: if Google's AI can manage the reservation process within search, it may reduce the need for users to engage with those platforms directly.

Sources: Hotel Technology News (Wyndham) · PhocusWire (hotel visibility) [PAYWALLED/BLOCKED] · RTN (Google booking)

So what: The window for passive AI visibility is closing fast. Independent hotels and mid-market restaurant groups without semantically structured data risk falling out of the discovery funnel entirely — potentially as consequentially as failing early SEO. IHG's content-first approach may deliver better short-term ROI than native LLM app builds; Google's UK rollout is the most concrete evidence yet that agentic booking will disintermediate existing reservation platforms.

VENDOR MOVES

A dense product-launch period shows the hospitality software market converging on integrated platforms that eat adjacent categories.

Mews launched Mews Business Intelligence on April 16 — embedded directly into its PMS operating system — offering AI-powered performance summaries, custom dashboards, automated reporting, and external-source data combining PMS figures with Google Ads and OTA metrics. The product has been adopted by 1,000 early customers; the 42-room Adara Hotel in Whistler used the room performance dashboard to recategorize inventory and introduce targeted strategies to boost ADR. "We had data before, but it was manual and not detailed," said GM Rhys Davies. "With Mews BI, everything is in one place."

Mews has made several acquisitions including Flexkeeping (housekeeping automation) and DataChat (agentic AI platform) in late 2025, building toward a broader operating system.

Shake Shack unveiled Project Catalyst (April 11), partnering with Qu — a cloud-native unified commerce platform — to upgrade POS and kitchen orchestration capabilities. Artificial intelligence is a core component: "Shake Shack is building proprietary AI capabilities aimed at delivering real-time operational insights and supporting decision-making at both the restaurant and enterprise level." The initiative also includes the company's first loyalty platform and a unified data layer — positioning AI as an operating layer across systems rather than a standalone feature, as the brand targets 1,500 company-operated locations.

Wonder (Marc Lore) revealed Wonder Create — an initiative letting anyone use AI to design and launch their own restaurant brand in under a minute. "You type in what kind of restaurant you want to build. It builds the restaurant — AI does — in under a minute. It does the name, branding, description, pictures, pricing, health information, and all the recipes for your restaurant," Lore explained at the WSJ Future of Everything conference.

The company has 120 locations today and aims to have 400 next year and 1,000 by 2035.

Sources: Hotel Dive (Mews BI) · RTN (Shake Shack) · TechCrunch (Wonder)

So what: The integrated-platform thesis is winning. Mews BI, Shake Shack's Project Catalyst, and the Abode funding data all point the same direction: point solutions are being absorbed into — or displaced by — systems that unify the data layer. Operators buying standalone AI tools without a clear integration path are paying twice.

OPERATIONAL AI

Measurable outcomes from deployed AI are consolidating into usable benchmarks for mid-market operators.

BCG's AI-First Hotels report documented hotels seeing RevPAR gains up to 15% after implementing AI-powered pricing systems, and the Ritz-Carlton San Francisco reported a 20% increase in room-cleaning speed via an AI system that optimizes housekeeping schedules based on checkout timing, guest preferences, and staffing levels.

At the Four Seasons Peninsula Papagayo, AI waste-tracking tools yielded a 50% reduction in food waste in eight months. BCG categorized 25% of hospitality firms as "AI-scaling" — with a strategy starting to produce real returns across multiple operational areas.

Loman AI (voice AI for restaurants) reported that pizza and high-volume takeout categories that deployed first are "12-18 months ahead, seeing 26%-plus phone revenue increases," said CEO Christian Wiens.

Otelier is building natural-language querying for hotel portfolio analytics — "Why did ADR drop in my urban hotels in April?" — on top of a Snowflake-powered normalized data model. CEO Rob Lawrence's argument: "AI is only useful when it sits on trusted, unified data."

At the NRA Show (May 18), the new hot application was AI assistants sitting atop restaurants' data and providing insights and action items — for instance, telling an operator that a certain location is busier than usual or that they've run out of ketchup. Several of the big POS companies were offering a version of this.

Toast CMO Kelly Esten: "Restaurant people wear a ton of hats. How can we start to take some of that work that's not core to why they got into running a restaurant off their plate with AI?" Meanwhile, some operators are building their own. "The AI that we're seeing is not necessarily solving the immediate problem," said Khara Mangiduyos, owner of Kalei's Kitchenette in San Diego. "That's why you end up creating your own."

Sources: PhocusWire/BCG (measurable gains) [PAYWALLED/BLOCKED] · Restaurant Business (NRA Show) · QSR Web (Loman AI)

So what: The BCG benchmarks (15% RevPAR, 20% housekeeping speed, 50% food waste reduction) are now the numbers to stress-test vendors against. If a vendor can't point to comparable case studies in similar property types, the "AI-powered" label deserves scrutiny. The NRA Show operator building their own AI to solve problems vendors can't is an early warning signal for the vendor community.

FUNDING & M&A

Capital is consolidating around platforms closest to daily operator workflows, not toward standalone features.

Abode Worldwide's Hospitality Tech Investment Index 2026 (April 14) documented $1 billion raised by 40 companies between April 2025 and March 2026. PMS companies were the clear funding leader: seven raised a combined $408.1 million, with Mews ($300 million), Kindred ($125 million), and Limehome (€75 million) in a 90-day burst from December 2025 to February 2026.

Four AI-led guest experience platforms — Duve, Chatlyn, Conduit, and Canary Technologies — raised a combined $152.6 million, targeting the need to deliver fast, personalized service with leaner teams. Canary's round included the acquisition of OpenKey.

Abode CEO Jessica Gillingham: "Investors are concentrating capital in the platforms that hospitality businesses increasingly depend on every day... Unified systems generate more data, better automation and higher switching costs. This is a compounding dynamic."

Shahar Goldboim, co-founder and CEO of AI-native PMS Boom: "Investors saw the shift from software-as-a-service to business-as-a-service, where the system doesn't just support the operator — it becomes an active participant in running the business." Hospitality Investor also surfaced a note from United Ventures: "Agentic AI represents one of the most consequential shifts in enterprise software since the move from on-premise to cloud."

Sources: Hotel Technology News (Abode) · Hotel Dive (Abode) · Hospitality Investor (Agentic PMS) [PAYWALLED/BLOCKED]

So what: For PE-backed hospitality SaaS companies, the Abode data confirms the consolidation imperative: become the control layer or be absorbed by one. Pure-play point solutions in messaging, scheduling, and revenue management face structural margin pressure unless they have deep workflow integration or proprietary training data. The $152.6M into guest experience platforms signals the second consolidation tier forming around the PMS.

GOVERNANCE & SECURITY

AI cost escalation and mislabeled capabilities are attracting serious analyst scrutiny.

PhocusWire's AI cost trap investigation [PAYWALLED/BLOCKED — captured via search snippet] documented AI talent costs rising ~30% per year in travel/hospitality, with HRS's Biermann noting that "recruiting specialist firms say these in-demand roles now take 50 times longer to recruit." RMS CMO Sandrine Zechbauer described an emerging "hospitality engineer" archetype — someone who can bridge service operations with technology — as becoming a new but expensive hiring category.

A Gartner report cited in the piece predicts more than 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by end of 2027 due to escalating costs. "Many solutions labeled as AI agents are actually extraction or classification tools or workflow automation with simple rules," said Romy Abbrederis, CEO of LobbyAI.

A Restaurant Technology News op-ed by Lavu CEO Saleem Khatri identified the underlying diagnostic test: "Did they build a model trained on restaurant operational data, or did they plug into someone else's general-purpose technology? A generic AI wrapper is not proprietary intelligence." He argued the industry has an "AI-washing problem — vendors are slapping 'AI-powered' onto tools that haven't materially changed in years."

Sources: PhocusWire (AI cost trap) [PAYWALLED/BLOCKED] · RTN (AI washing op-ed)

So what: Budget for AI talent and governance frameworks, not just tooling licenses. The "hospitality engineer" gap will widen faster than most operator HR plans account for. Operators who define which decisions AI can suggest, execute, or escalate before deployment will avoid the expensive restarts Gartner's 40% figure predicts.

WHO TO WATCH

Nicolas Maynard (AI & Data Science Chief, Accor) — His admission that Accor's systems "do not currently classify properties by [semantic] attributes" is the most candid public disclosure from a major hotel group about how far legacy data infrastructure lags behind AI-search requirements. Michael Mahar (SVP & Head of Commercial Technology, Wyndham) — Architect of Wyndham's multi-LLM distribution strategy (ChatGPT, Claude, Google AI Mode) and its owner-centric framing: "How do we add value for owners and offset that cost with cost savings?" His "MCP is not an expensive lift" positioning reframes AI distribution from a capital problem to an infrastructure-readiness problem. The most operationally grounded AI commercial narrative in the hotel sector right now.

Marc Lore (CEO, Wonder) — Wonder Create is either the most disruptive reinvention of the ghost-kitchen model or an elaborately packaged pivot from the category's troubled history. The combination of 120 programmable kitchens, Grubhub's 250 million annual deliveries, Spice Robotics' bowl automation, and AI brand generation is an unusual infrastructure convergence. The end-of-year Wonder Create launch will be the first real test of whether "anyone can make a restaurant" resolves into durable consumer brands or MrBeast Burger 2.0.

Kelly Esten (CMO, Toast) — Her NRA Show framing — not robots, not voice AI, but "taking work that's not core to why they got into running a restaurant off their plate" — is the most commercially honest positioning statement from a major restaurant POS vendor this cycle. Toast's decisions about what AI to build natively versus surface from partners will shape how tens of thousands of mid-market operators experience AI operationally. Jessica Gillingham (CEO, Abode Worldwide) — Author of the $1 billion hospitality tech funding analysis, her read — "unified systems generate more data, better automation and higher switching costs — this is a compounding dynamic" — is the clearest articulation this cycle of why PMS is winning the AI platform war.
