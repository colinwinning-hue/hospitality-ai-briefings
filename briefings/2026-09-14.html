HOSPITALITY AI BRIEFING — 11–14 September 2026

AI Visibility & Discovery

The booking front door is being rebuilt in real time: Google moved hotel booking in AI Mode from test to live product on September 1, restaurant agentic booking rolled out through AI Mode the same week, and new benchmarks show 83% of restaurant locations are structurally invisible to the AI engines now mediating consumer decisions.

Google officially launched hotel booking through AI Mode in Search on September 1. Google introduced hotel booking through AI Mode in Search, allowing travelers to discover and book their stay "in one seamless conversation." English-speaking users in the U.S. can review and compare hotels, choose a room from integrated partners, and complete the reservation with Google Pay. Google is partnering with 10 travel websites and hotel chains for the initial rollout, including Choice Hotels International, Hilton, and Wyndham Hotels & Resorts.

Ten partners are live at launch: Booking.com, Choice Hotels International, Expedia, Hilton, Hotels.com, IHG Hotels & Resorts, Marriott International, Priceline, Trip.com, and Wyndham Hotels & Resorts. Notably, Google is not the merchant of record — the hotel or booking platform handles that side of the transaction. Google is the discovery and handoff layer. Anna Scozzafava, Choice Hotels' Chief Data, AI & Technology Officer, said: "Travel discovery is evolving rapidly, and Choice Hotels is actively shaping what comes next."

Google simultaneously rolled out agentic restaurant booking in AI Mode. Agentic booking for restaurants is rolling out this week on AI Mode in the U.S. (no Labs opt-in required), so users can easily make plans for family dinners, date nights, holiday brunches and more. AI Mode will search across multiple reservation platforms and websites to find real-time availability, showing a curated list with direct links so users can finalize their booking through partners like OpenTable, Resy, Tock, and others.

Booking Holdings disclosed material SEO pressure from AI Overviews while simultaneously functioning as a Google UCP partner. CEO Glenn Fogel told analysts that Google's AI Overviews "definitely put some pressure on SEO," though Booking's direct traffic in the mid-60% range "hasn't gone down at all."

Booking Holdings CFO Ewout Steenbergen said in the second quarter of 2026 that bookings coming from conversational agents, free or paid, account for less than 1% of the group's room nights, with no significant recent change in that share. The traffic disruption is accelerating even as the transaction volume remains nascent.

Radisson Hotel Group launched @RadissonHotels in ChatGPT on July 28 in collaboration with Accenture. The app enables travelers to search for Radisson Hotels properties across over 100 countries through natural conversation. The launch marks an important step in Radisson Hotel Group's move toward agentic commerce. According to Accenture's Consumer Pulse Research, 87% of travelers are open to collaborating with an AI-powered travel agent to find the best option, and 71% of travelers say that at least half of their spend for hotels or airlines will be influenced by AI over the next 12 months. Gianni Di Fede, Radisson's chief commercial officer, said the group is seeing organic and direct web traffic giving way to new touchpoints like ChatGPT.

On the restaurant side, BrightEdge documented restaurants going from a 10% AI Overviews trigger rate in early 2025 to 78% by February 2026, one of the fastest-growing verticals they track. BrightLocal's 2026 consumer survey found 45% of consumers now use AI tools for local business recommendations — up from 6% one year earlier.

DoorDash's 2026 Restaurant Industry Trends Report (3,000 U.S. consumers) found 22% have used AI to choose a restaurant.

SOCi's 2026 Local Visibility Index found AI is 3–30x more selective than traditional local search: only ~1.2% of locations are recommended by ChatGPT versus 35.9% appearing in Google's local 3-pack.

Sources: Hotel Dive, Sept. 1 | Google Blog, Nov. 2025 (updated) | Accenture Newsroom, Jul. 28 | Inverisla Daily Brief, Sept. 11

So what: Google is not yet an OTA, but it has become the ambient discovery layer above both OTAs and hotel brand sites. Mid-market hotel operators should treat Google Business Profile completeness and UCP integration as distribution priorities now. For multi-location restaurant groups, the 83% AI-invisibility figure is existential margin exposure: at 22% consumer AI adoption for restaurant discovery and growing, invisible restaurants are absent from a material and fast-growing slice of the decision funnel.

Vendor Moves

The reservation stack is consolidating into three corporate ecosystem bets while platform providers push deeper into adjacent workflows — and one key data layer player used an acquisition to measure its own AI discoverability problem.

American Express is assembling the largest dining reservation network in the industry through three simultaneous moves. American Express has proposed acquiring European restaurant reservation platform TheFork from Tripadvisor for $700 million in cash. TheFork connects millions of diners with more than 50,000 restaurants across 11 European countries. American Express estimates that TheFork, Resy, and Tock would together create a dining network of approximately 75,000 bookable venues.

American Express / Resy partnered with Anthropic so that Claude users can now explore U.S. Resy restaurants directly in their conversations — with Claude connecting to Resy to browse available tables in real time and secure a reservation seamlessly.

American Express is also folding Tock into Resy this summer, doubling its venue library to over 25,000 restaurants. A Resy partnership with point-of-sale provider Toast is also underway. Boston operator Pajo Bruich put the underlying dynamic plainly: "It's less about the reservations, and it's all about the data."

DoorDash is deploying a conversational ordering agent with independently documented production results. DoorDash published the engineering architecture behind Ask DoorDash in a three-part deep dive, describing how it built the system using large language models, specialized AI agents, Model Context Protocol (MCP)-based tooling, persistent consumer memory, and automated evaluation infrastructure. DoorDash reported early production results showing that computed consumer memory improved grocery checkout conversion by approximately 24%, increased basket sizes by 17%, and reduced conversational turns by 7% during a seven-day evaluation. For restaurant discovery, the assistant achieved a 15% higher conversion rate on open-ended queries.

DoorDash's new AI-powered self-serve onboarding experience can help merchants launch more than 35% faster, pulling information directly from a merchant's existing online presence.

OpenTable dropped its largest-ever product suite on August 26. On August 26, 2026, OpenTable announced its largest suite of new and updated product features to date — more than 20 new and enhanced tools, many powered by AI and automation.

OpenTable now partners and integrates with leading AI and search platforms including Google (Search, Maps and Gemini), ChatGPT, Microsoft's Copilot, Perplexity, and Amazon's Alexa+ AI assistant.

LLM integrations drove 17x more seated diners year-over-year, bringing in guests who spent on average 20% more than other channels. To date, OpenTable has seated 3 million diners across its voice AI partners, a 270% increase in 2026 versus the same period in 2025. POS partner adoptions grew 40% year-over-year. Sagar Mehta, OpenTable's CTO, said: "We're applying AI and automation where they can make the biggest difference — driving demand, unlocking revenue and helping teams deliver more personalized hospitality without adding more complexity."

Toast presented at Goldman Sachs Communacopia on September 9 with a business that has grown beyond its POS roots. Toast used the Goldman Sachs Communacopia + Technology Conference 2026 to outline a business that has grown well beyond its original point-of-sale roots. The company now commands a market capitalization of $18.98 billion and posted revenue of $6.8 billion over the last twelve months as of Q2 2026, representing revenue growth of 23%.

Toast IQ Grow, a marketing-focused solution introduced as part of Toast's Spring 2026 product release, features an AI-powered Marketing Agent that can build audiences and automate campaigns across channels. Toast CEO Aman Narang told conference attendees: "When you think about an SMB business, 40% sales growth [from IQ Grow]. When incremental demand is so expensive, it is a huge deal." The company also launched Toast Drive-Thru in 2026 as a unified solution for enterprise quick-service operations, bringing together hardware, POS-native software, AI voice ordering integrations, and services. Features include a drive-thru-specific POS mode with vehicle ID and lane tagging, AI voice ordering through integrations with companies such as Incept AI, and speed-of-service reporting.

Mews launched POS in North America in September 2026, extending its platform beyond PMS into point-of-sale — a continuation of the acquisition-and-expand strategy that saw Mews acquire Flexkeeping and DataChat in late 2025.

Lighthouse acquired Hotelrank.ai in Q2 2026. Lighthouse, a hospitality intelligence platform that has raised $370 million in growth equity and has bought several companies in recent years, acquired Hotelrank, a tool that measures where hotels show up across AI platforms, from a startup founded in 2025. This is the most architecturally honest move of the cycle: a hotel commercial intelligence platform buying visibility into its own AI discoverability gap.

Sources: CNBC, Feb. 2026 | RTN, Jun. 15 | Food & Beverage Magazine, Aug. 26 | Boston Globe, Aug. 17 | Investing.com, Sept. 9

So what: The reservation stack has consolidated into three ecosystem bets — American Express (Resy + Tock + TheFork, Amex card network, Anthropic AI distribution), DoorDash (SevenRooms, delivery data, conversational MCP ordering), and OpenTable (Visa/Chase, discovery scale, Toast POS partnership). For an independent restaurant, the platform choice is now a decision about which corporate data layer you operate inside for the next five years. Toast's drive-thru launch signals it intends to own the full QSR operating stack — meaningful for PE-backed QSR roll-ups evaluating technology consolidation. Lighthouse's Hotelrank acquisition is the shrewdest signal of the cycle: the company best positioned to sell AI commercial intelligence bought a tool to measure whether hotels appear in AI answers.

Operational AI

A measurable and widening profitability gap has opened between AI-adopting restaurant operators and those that haven't deployed, while hotel adoption is near-universal but governance lags badly.

Restaurant operators are crossing a threshold from experimentation to measurable financial impact. Restaurant365's 2026 State of the Restaurant Industry Mid-Year Report, drawing on survey responses from more than 420 operators, found that 62% of those using AI for back-office functions reported reduced labor costs. Restaurant365's data shows back-office AI adoption among operators more than doubled in the first half of 2026 — from roughly 25% to 69%. Many chains are still working to integrate new technology into their operations. Shake Shack is using AI to identify and alert managers to potential inefficiencies. Culver's began rolling out computer vision to measure service execution and vehicle flow in its drive-thrus. But at the National Restaurant Association Show, restaurant tech leaders warned that overadoption of large language models rests on shaky fundamentals — the cost of AI tokens can exceed the salaries of workers the technology purports to replace. Brendan Sweeney, CEO of Popmenu, captured the nuance: "What people are getting wrong is [thinking that] it's either going to be nothing or it's going to be everything, and [they should] treat it like any other innovation."

On the hotel side, Mews reported that 98% of surveyed hoteliers had used AI across operations in the prior six months, showing how quickly the technology has become part of ordinary hotel work. Yet 41% of surveyed hoteliers still have no formal AI policy — even as AI touches pricing, guest communications, operations, and leadership work.

Sources: Restaurant365, 2026 Mid-Year Report | Restaurant Dive, May 26 | Mews / Canary Technologies, May 2026

So what: The Restaurant365 data is the strongest commercial case for back-office AI this cycle — survey-based across nearly 10,000 US locations, showing consistent cost reductions. The profitability gap between AI-adopting operators and others is widening in real time. The 41% of hoteliers with no formal AI policy represents both risk exposure (regulatory, data, security) and a product opportunity for governance tooling.

Funding & M&A

Investor conviction has crossed the billion-dollar mark in hotel tech and is now reaching for comparable scale in restaurant AI, with capital concentrating around data control layers rather than feature tools.

Hospitality technology startups raised more than $1 billion across 40 companies between April 2025 and March 2026, with PMS and AI-led platforms attracting the largest share. The biggest raises landed almost back-to-back in a 90-day burst: Mews ($300 million), Kindred ($125 million), and Limehome (€75 million). Jessica Gillingham, CEO of Abode Worldwide, said: "The companies attracting the strongest investor backing share a common trait — they sit close to essential operator workflows and become more valuable over time."

In Q2 2026, three disclosed funding rounds closed, all AI-native by description. Smartness closed the largest, a €47 million Series B for its agentic AI platform for hotels and short-term rentals. Pricepoint raised a $6.6 million seed for AI revenue management. Besty AI raised a $3.75 million seed for its AI automation tool on the STR side. On the acquisition side, Smartness started in 2020 as a dynamic pricing tool and has since expanded into a broad platform spanning pricing, guest communications, CRM, payments, and property management. Its Series B is set partly toward acquisitions across Europe — a former specialist now funded to consolidate.

On the restaurant side, Owner raised $240 million in a Series D funding round led by Goldman Sachs Alternatives, bringing its total valuation to $2.3 billion. Owner positions itself as the "AI CMO and CTO for local businesses." Separately, Lighthouse raised approximately $370 million in Series C funding to expand its commercial intelligence platform.

Sources: Hotel Dive, Apr. 14 | Abode Worldwide, Q2 2026 | Inverisla Daily Brief, Sept. 11

So what: The investor thesis is clear — buy the data layer, not the feature. PMS companies capturing 40%+ of all hospitality tech funding signals that operators who haven't consolidated onto a modern cloud PMS are making a structurally disadvantaged technology bet. Owner's $2.3B valuation is the most significant restaurant AI funding signal this cycle — VCs now believe "AI-native operator platform" is a category that can reach large scale for independents, not just chains.

Governance & Security

EU AI Act Article 50 is live and creating immediate legal exposure for any hospitality business with a guest-facing AI interface. This is not a future deadline.

The Attorney General filed proposed Automated Decision-Making Technology rules on August 11, 2026, with a formal comment process continuing into October. In Europe, EU AI Act Article 50 transparency obligations for guest-facing interactive AI took effect August 2, 2026; fines can reach €15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover. Any hotel or restaurant in the EU operating a guest-facing chatbot that does not disclose its AI nature is in breach now.

On the competition law front, Atlantic City hotel operators MGM Resorts, Caesars Entertainment and others must face a US antitrust case accusing them of using algorithms provided by Cendyn to raise and fix prices for rooms, according to a federal appeals court ruling in July 2026. The Third Circuit ruling is the clearest judicial signal yet that AI-enabled pricing coordination carries legal risk distinct from the technology question.

So what: For PE-backed hospitality SaaS, Article 50 is simultaneously a product risk (platforms enabling non-compliant deployments) and a market opportunity — governance tooling is an unbundled product waiting to be built on top of the PMS control layer. The Cendyn antitrust ruling should accelerate explainability requirements for revenue management AI across mid-market hotel groups.
