Vendor Moves

The restaurant and hotel tech stack is consolidating at an accelerating pace. Three large platform moves defined the closing days of November.

OpenTable (24 Nov) — Parent Booking Holdings agreed to acquire Australian hospitality technology company me&u in a deal worth $450 million, announced on 24 November with completion expected in H1 2027 subject to ACCC and potentially FIRB approvals. Me&u processes more than one million orders a week across thousands of venues in Australia, New Zealand, the UK and the US; its technology spans QR code ordering, payments, reservations, guest marketing, event management and voice AI. In August 2026, me&u had launched an AI-enabled reservations platform, putting it in direct competition with OpenTable, Resy and SevenRooms just months before its acquisition. OpenTable CEO Debby Soo said: "Me&u has built an exceptional platform for fast-paced and complex casual dining environments, and welcoming their team and technology allows us to offer hospitality operators an even broader set of connected solutions." Me&u CEO Kim Teo said the deal expands global reach without disrupting existing customers: "For our customers, it's business as usual, same team, same products, same pricing, same people on the other end of the phone. What changes is how far we can go." Dealroom notes this follows OpenTable's purchase of Canadian reservation platform Libro in April, "pointing to a broader push to assemble restaurant tools across markets rather than focus on bookings alone," and contextualises the deal alongside American Express's $700 million agreement to buy European booking service TheFork and DoorDash's SevenRooms ownership.

IHG (24 Nov) — The hotel group selected Amadeus Delphi as its global Groups & Events sales and catering standard, connecting event workflows with reservations, property management and revenue management. Automatic updates to group room blocks and availability are intended to reduce the handovers that make meetings business administratively demanding. Three configurations will accommodate different hotel needs, with Delphi Foundations expected mid-2027. IHG also intends to adopt MeetingBroker's Email to RFP functionality, converting email enquiries and attachments into structured requests for proposals. No portfolio-wide completion date or measured response-time improvement was supplied.

Sources: Startup Daily / SmartCompany, Dealroom, TravelPRNews (24 Nov recap)

So what: OpenTable's me&u acquisition converts a reservation platform into a full-stack restaurant OS covering ordering, payments, marketing and voice AI — the most consequential stack expansion since Booking Holdings' original OpenTable purchase. Operators running me&u should expect eventual platform consolidation; operators choosing between competing stacks should weight platform-owner ambition and regulatory timeline as heavily as current feature sets. IHG's Amadeus Delphi selection shows that established hotel groups are standardising group-and-events infrastructure as a prerequisite before layering AI-assisted enquiry processing on top.

Funding & M&A

Restaurant tech M&A is not letting up entering the holiday period.

Wonder (23 Nov) — The food hall company is reportedly exploring a sale of Grubhub, the delivery platform it acquired from JustEat in 2024 for $650 million, per Nation's Restaurant News. Wonder had positioned Grubhub as the delivery backbone for its "super app for mealtime" strategy; a sale would signal a retreat from that vision. No acquirer or price was disclosed at time of reporting.

Sources: Nation's Restaurant News

So what: Grubhub has changed hands for a fraction of its peak valuation twice already. If Wonder exits, it sharpens the question of whether a standalone restaurant delivery marketplace can survive without the scale and data advantages that DoorDash and Uber Eats have built. For operators: watch who acquires it — a strategic buyer with loyalty infrastructure (Instacart, a media company, a retailer) would change the data picture meaningfully.

Operational AI

Robot-staffed hotel lobbies moved from pilot curiosity to named deployment this cycle.

YYForce (24 Nov) — The Singapore-based AI workforce and robotics company (NASDAQ: YFOR) announced the launch of a proprietary-trained humanoid robot for live hotel concierge operations, with the first unit — named "Six Seven" by the hotel team — deploying at Novotel Singapore on Stevens. Six Seven is built on NVIDIA Jetson edge AI computing and NVIDIA Isaac Sim simulation workflows and is integrated into YYForce's Workforce OS platform. It is trained to handle full hotel and dining concierge functions, local travel guidance, and interactive storytelling for guests and families. CEO Mike Fu said: "With Six Seven built on NVIDIA Jetson and Isaac Sim within our Workforce OS platform, we are accelerating the commercial rollout of embodied AI, empowering hospitality partners to elevate guest experiences while addressing persistent frontline staffing challenges."

Sources: PRNewswire APAC

So what: This is a single property in Singapore, not a rollout. The operational test is not whether a humanoid can answer questions in a lab — it is whether it can handle ambiguous, multilingual, emotionally charged real-world lobby interactions without failure modes that embarrass the brand. Watch for independent performance data, not vendor press releases, before including humanoid robotics in budget discussions.

Governance & Security

Global Hospitality AI Coordinating Council (23 Nov) — HFTP, HSMAI and AHLA, including the latter's HTNG community, announced the formation of a Global Hospitality AI Coordinating Council designed to align AI guidance while preserving each association's existing programmes. Announced on 23 November, the collaboration seeks to reduce conflicting advice across commercial operations, finance, technology and policy. It is a coordination mechanism rather than a binding standard. An inaugural Hospitality AI Exchange is planned for 2–3 February 2027 in the Dallas area.

Sources: Hospitality Net, TravelPRNews

So what: Operators have been receiving fragmented AI guidance from HFTP, HSMAI and AHLA — sometimes contradicting each other on procurement, security and governance questions. A single coordinated framework reduces the homework burden for GMs and CIOs running vendor evaluations. Watch for the February 2027 Exchange to surface the first unified framework language that can feed directly into contract negotiations.

AI Visibility & Discovery

The Phocuswright Conference 2026 closed on 24 November with new research projecting that AI agents could account for 15–50% of airline ticket bookings within two years — the first public estimate at that scale from a credible travel research body. The same session highlighted that travel companies lag behind their own travelers on AI readiness, with Booking Holdings simultaneously completing the unification of its B2B operations — Booking.com partnerships and Rocket Travel by Agoda — into a single Booking Partner Division, whose CEO said partners want customised AI technology rather than blanket inventory access.

Sources: Hospitality Net / Phocuswright

So what: A 15–50% AI-mediated booking share within two years would restructure the entire commission and data-ownership conversation. Even the bottom of that range is enough to justify immediate action on rate-feed connectivity, structured property data and direct booking channel investment — the channels that Google, Muse and similar surfaces already need to surface a hotel at all.
