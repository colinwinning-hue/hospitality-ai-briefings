## AI Visibility & Discovery

The hotel industry's central distribution battle of 2026 is now concrete: whether AI interfaces own the discovery moment, handle the transaction, or hand it off — and what each outcome means for direct bookings.


**IHG Hotels & Resorts** confirmed this week it is participating in Google's agentic AI booking pilot, disclosed alongside its H1 2026 earnings. The pilot enables guests to book IHG hotels as direct bookings within Google's AI Mode experience, with loyalty members also redirectable to IHG's website for member rates. IHG is simultaneously part of Google's Direct Offers pilot, which surfaces promotions within AI-assisted trip planning.
 
IHG CEO Elie Maalouf said the company's cloud-based PMS is expected to reach 4,000 hotels by end of 2026. He declined to put a percentage cost saving on AI initiatives, saying it was "too early for anyone in the industry" to do so, calling it an "evolving technology."
 
IHG reported total revenue of $2.7 billion for H1 2026, up 5.6% year over year, with operating profit up 10% to $665 million.


**Radisson Hotel Group**, in partnership with Accenture, launched the `@RadissonHotels` app inside ChatGPT on July 28. 
Available via @RadissonHotels at the start of a ChatGPT prompt, travelers can search across more than 1,000 Radisson Hotels properties in over 100 countries through natural conversation, with live inventory, rates, location context, and amenities returned.
 
The app connects directly to Radisson's existing systems, delivering real-time information; over time, the companies plan to expand to in-chat booking, loyalty recognition, reservation management, and AI concierge capabilities.
 
According to Accenture's Consumer Pulse Research, 87% of travelers are open to collaborating with an AI-powered travel agent, and 71% say at least half of their hotel and airline spend will be influenced by AI over the next 12 months. Radisson noted organic and direct web traffic channels "are giving way to new touchpoints like ChatGPT."


**OpenAI's** counter-move, however, is not transaction ownership. 
Buying or booking directly inside ChatGPT has become vaporware — OpenAI shifted its shopping strategy to focus on discovery and research, leaving checkout to third-party app developers. Only around a dozen Shopify merchants had gone live with native ChatGPT checkout before OpenAI moved the feature to apps. Expedia and Booking Holdings shares rose on the announcement, as the move alleviates competitive pressure on travel booking giants.
 
The market is evolving into a mix of interface-led, ecosystem-led, and transaction-led models rather than a single unified solution; OpenAI's strategic shift reflects a prioritisation of enterprise and infrastructure over owning the transaction layer.


Sources: [PhocusWire — IHG H1 2026](https://www.phocuswire.com/news/online/ihg-google-agentic-ai-booking-pilot) | [Accenture/Radisson Newsroom](https://newsroom.accenture.com/news/2026/radisson-hotel-group-and-accenture-redefine-travel-discovery-on-chatgpt) | [Skift — OpenAI Checkout Walkback](https://skift.com/2026/03/05/openai-chatgpt-checkout-walkback/)

**So what:** Google AI Mode is quietly becoming a new booking channel — and IHG processing those reservations as direct bookings is a signal worth tracking. Independent and mid-market hotels need structured, machine-readable data and PMS API openness before AI agents will recommend them at all. The Radisson model (discovery now, in-chat booking later) is the realistic 2026 playbook: get the data right first.

## Vendor Moves

The restaurant reservation market is mid-consolidation, and the central question is no longer which platform books more covers — it's which ecosystem owns the diner relationship.


Acquisitions and credit-card partnerships have added new artillery. After purchasing Resy in 2019, American Express paid $400 million for Tock in 2024; those two platforms will be unified under Resy this summer. DoorDash bought SevenRooms for $1.2 billion, while UberEats and Booking Holdings' OpenTable announced a partnership to integrate reservations on Uber's app.



**American Express** has also partnered with Anthropic to make U.S. Resy restaurants discoverable in Claude. When a user asks Claude where to eat, Claude surfaces relevant restaurant recommendations; if the user wants to check availability or make a reservation at a Resy restaurant, Claude can prompt connection to the Resy app, where users can view available tables and be redirected to Resy.com or the mobile app.
 
The SevenRooms integration meanwhile gives DoorDash and its restaurants access to more diner data. SevenRooms co-founder Joel Montaniel: "Delivery and dine-in have typically been siloed data sets. If a customer has ordered six times and is coming in for the first time, are they a first-time customer or a seventh-time customer?"



The real prize operators are naming is data. "It's less about the reservations, and it's all about the data," said Pajo Bruich, president of Umami Riot Experience (O Ya, Hojoko). "When I look at the DoorDash move, that's what I see — them trying to get access to more data. What they do with it is going to be really interesting."
 
On August 19, NRN reported Square and Toast also deepened ties to reservations partners.


**Toast** delivered its Q2 earnings on August 10. 
Toast is working to capture more business from time-strapped customers by using AI tools to assume more of a restaurateur's workflows. Toast IQ Grow — a digital marketing tool — is poised to become the company's fastest-growing product, with executives expecting it to generate $10 million in annual revenue since its May introduction, CEO Aman Narang said on an August earnings call.
 
Toast also launched Toast Drive-Thru in 2026 as a unified enterprise QSR solution, bringing together hardware, POS-native software, AI voice ordering integrations, and vehicle ID and lane tagging.



**Delivery Hero** unveiled on August 13/14 a new agentic AI assistant for neighbourhood shops and restaurants, deployable via WhatsApp. Shop and restaurant owners can use text, voice notes, and images to ask for help, or — with owner approval — the assistant can make suggestions independently and implement changes directly. CPO Johannes Bruder: "Small business owners have a lot to juggle and our new AI assistant helps them grow faster without adding to their workload."
 
The assistant currently supports over 40,000 restaurants across Delivery Hero's global brands, an early slice of the approximately 1.5 million partners on the platform.


Sources: [Boston Globe — Reservation Wars](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/08/17/lifestyle/reservation-platforms-sevenrooms-opentable-resy/) | [Restaurant Dive — Toast AI](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/toast-aims-to-drive-ai-into-dining/827503/) | [RTIH — Delivery Hero](https://retailtechinnovationhub.com/home/2026/8/13/delivery-hero-launch-ai-assistant-for-local-shops-and-restaurants-with-roll-out-due-across-platform)

**So what:** DoorDash is now a reservation platform. AmEx/Resy is in Claude. OpenTable requires exclusivity. For a mid-market operator, picking a reservation platform is now a decision about which data ecosystem and which AI discovery channel your guests will find you through. The platform that combines first-party diner data with AI-native discovery (Resy/Claude, DoorDash/SevenRooms) wins the long game over pure cover volume.

## Operational AI

The strongest restaurant AI signal of the week comes with a named person, a specific outcome, and an honest admission that it is a "learning journey."

**Yum Brands** global chief digital and technology officer Jim Dausch spoke to Fortune on August 19. 
Dausch noted voice AI ordering has been rolled out to more than 900 Taco Bell U.S. restaurants to boost order sizes, improve accuracy, and increase customer satisfaction. He acknowledged the system has been a "learning journey," requiring tweaks so the handoff between AI and human workers is smoother. Digital kiosks have been rolled out to around two-thirds of global locations and consistently produce higher check averages than in-person orders.
 
Yum's automation system has led to an 85% reduction in "stockouts" through its Byte platform — a proprietary SaaS system that consolidates ordering, POS, kitchen, delivery, inventory, and labor management across 63,000 locations.
 
Yum has authorised enterprise licences for OpenAI's ChatGPT for district managers and franchise leaders, who are also mandated to take courses through an "AI Academy" and are encouraged to develop more than 400 AI agents. Dausch added: "I don't know anyone that has ever just selected a restaurant to go to based on the technology."


**Deliverect** launched its commercial AI agents platform on April 9 (PRNewswire). 
Deliverect AI — a digital workforce of autonomous agents serving 95,000+ locations across 78 countries — continuously rewrites digital menus to increase revenue, resolves technical issues before they cost operators money, and replaces labour-intensive manual tasks. The Autonomous Menu Agents analyse live purchasing data at the individual location level and automatically reconfigure menu layout and visibility on an ongoing basis as new orders come in.
 Prior to launch, a separate Deliverect agent autonomously designed, deployed, and optimised a KFC marketing promotion that produced a 118% sales increase with no human involvement. 
Deliverect processes over 30 million API calls daily, has powered more than one billion orders, and is valued at over €1 billion.


Sources: [Fortune — Yum Brands/Dausch](https://fortune.com/2026/08/19/how-kfc-and-taco-bells-top-technologist-is-embracing-ai-and-automation-across-63000-restaurants/) | [PRNewswire — Deliverect AI](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/deliverect-launches-ai-agents-and-smart-assistants-to-supercharge-restaurant-sales-protect-digital-revenue-and-maximise-efficiency-302737268.html) | [Restaurant Technology News — Toast Platform](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/06/toast-advances-restaurant-operations-with-connected-pos-ai-and-guest-engagement-technology/)

**So what:** Yum's 85% stockout reduction on Byte is the most independently credible restaurant AI outcome this cycle. The honest admission that voice AI handoffs need ongoing tuning is a useful signal for any operator evaluating QSR voice ordering: 900-location rollouts still require active workflow management. For independent and emerging-group operators, Deliverect's 118% KFC sales uplift on a zero-human-involvement agent is the number to stress-test — ask the vendor for methodology before citing it.

## Funding & M&A

Two different capital stories are running simultaneously: a blockbuster M&A cycle in hospitality assets and a tighter, more selective funding environment for hospitality tech startups.


In the first half of 2026, the U.S. travel, leisure, and hospitality sector saw $39.6 billion in M&A activity, a 106.8% increase in deal value year over year, according to a new KPMG report published August 12. However, the number of deals (402) fell 7.6%, as buyers pursued "bigger, higher-conviction" deals prioritising assets with "customer ownership, loyalty ecosystems, distribution control, pricing power, and scalable operating models."
 
KPMG principal Daniel Fischer warned: "The true value of these large deals won't be measured on signing day, but by a company's ability to seamlessly transform the business." Underfunding renovation, technology, and workforce initiatives after closing is called out as a key execution risk.


On the tech funding side, 
hospitality technology startups raised more than $1 billion across 40 companies between April 2025 and March 2026, with PMS and AI-led platforms attracting the largest share, according to Abode Worldwide's Hospitality Tech Investment Index 2026.
 
In Q2 2026, disclosed rounds were thin and acquisitions ran ahead: three rounds closed, all AI-native. Smartness closed the largest, a €47 million Series B for its agentic AI platform for hotels and short-term rentals. Pricepoint raised $6.6 million for AI revenue management. Besty AI raised $3.75 million for STR automation. Total disclosed funding in Q2 2026 was around $60 million versus approximately $135 million in Q2 2025.



Smartness began in 2020 as Smartpricing and has since expanded into pricing, guest communications, CRM, payments, and property management. Its €47 million Series B is partly set toward acquisitions across Europe — a former specialist now funded to consolidate.
 Also notable in the period: Lighthouse acquired Hotelrank.ai (AI visibility measurement across AI platforms) and MarginEdge raised $80 million in August 2026 amid intensifying competition for the AI-powered restaurant back office.

Sources: [Hotel Dive — H1 2026 M&A/KPMG](https://www.hoteldive.com/news/hospitality-mergers-acquisitions-H1-2026-kpmg/827622/) | [Abode Worldwide — Q2 2026 Funding](https://abodeworldwide.com/hospitality-tech-funding-q2-2026/) | [Hotel Tech News — $1B Index](https://hoteltechnologynews.com/2026/04/research-hospitality-technology-tops-1-billion-across-40-startups-with-pms-and-ai-platforms-capturing-the-largest-share/)

**So what:** PE buyers are prioritising data-rich assets with loyalty ecosystems — not just bricks and rooms. That's a direct parallel to what's happening in restaurant reservations (DoorDash, AmEx) and hotel tech (Mews buying Flexkeeping and DataChat). For PE-backed operators evaluating hospitality tech acquisitions: the Abode Worldwide finding that investors are concentrating on platforms that generate their own data flywheel is the governing logic for valuation premiums.

## Governance & Security

Two legally grounded risks emerged this cycle that operators need to act on now, not schedule for later.

**EU AI Act Article 50** became applicable on August 2, 2026. 
Article 50 includes transparency obligations for certain interactive and generative AI systems. A restaurant should not assume a friendly bot name is sufficient disclosure — the disclosure must be clear in the context where the guest encounters the system. Operators launching voice agents, web chat, automated reservation assistants, or synthetic promotional content across EU markets need to verify compliance jurisdiction by jurisdiction.


**Algorithmic pricing liability** entered US caselaw this cycle. 
The US Third Circuit Court of Appeals revived antitrust claims against Atlantic City hotel operators (MGM Resorts, Caesars Entertainment and others) and Cendyn Group over alleged price fixing via Cendyn's Rainmaker pricing tool. In a separate development, Google named IHG as a hotel partner for paid offers in AI-powered trip planning, and Booking.com faces an expanded class action in the Netherlands with almost 7,700 hotels joining a lawsuit over pricing clause restrictions.


Sources: [Foster Garvey — Circuit Split Algorithmic Pricing](https://www.foster.com/newsroom/blog/duff-on-hospitality-law/a-circuit-split-emerges-over-ai-enabled-pricing/) | [AllAI News — EU AI Act for Restaurants](https://allainews.net/ai-for-restaurants/)

**So what:** If you operate AI-driven pricing tools (RMS, revenue management SaaS) or guest-facing AI in any EU market, both issues require immediate legal review. The Cendyn/Atlantic City ruling means operators — not just vendors — face exposure when algorithms produce coordinated pricing. Get your vendor's documentation on how their model generates recommendations before any antitrust inquiry forces the question.
