AI VISIBILITY & DISCOVERY

The guest-discovery layer is becoming the active battleground, with major brands racing to insert themselves into AI-mediated trip planning before Google's agentic booking goes live.

IHG Hotels & Resorts launched a native ChatGPT app on June 4, aimed at helping travelers "search, compare and explore" IHG's portfolio. ChatGPT will suggest properties according to guest preferences, and the app will display availability, pricing, maps and amenities, "helping guests move naturally from discovery to comparison," per IHG.

IHG is also adding AI-powered conversational search to its website and rewards app. "Pairing AI with the warmth of human hospitality ultimately helps us deliver a more exceptional live experience — one that feels personal, responsive and connected to each guest in the moment," said Jolie Fleming, IHG's chief product and technology officer. Travelers are increasingly relying on AI to research and plan travel — 56% of U.S. travelers are using AI for trip planning, booking, or in-destination assistance, per a March report from Phocuswright. Critically, the app leads users back to IHG's own booking channels for transactions — a discovery play, not a booking disruption, yet.

Wyndham Hotels & Resorts launched a native ChatGPT app in May, positioning it as "the first native hotel app from a major economy and midscale franchisor in the U.S." Wyndham reports that since launching AI-powered tools in its call centers, they have aided in reducing average handle times by 7%, while simultaneously helping improve agent training and conversion rates.

The most engaged Wyndham Connect hotels last year averaged more than $60,000 in incremental revenue, with the highest-performing hotel exceeding $200,000. Wyndham's disclosure that it has invested more than $450 million in technology since 2018 — and completed full cloud migration in 2020 — makes this the most documented franchise-level AI deployment in the economy/midscale segment.

Google confirmed it is developing agentic hotel and flight bookings for AI Mode, working with Booking.com, Expedia, Marriott, IHG, Choice Hotels, and Wyndham. Google will not serve as merchant of record — partner companies manage transactions. Farago said agentic travel booking is an area Google wants to be "super thoughtful" about, given that purchases like flights and hotel bookings tend to be large and infrequent. Google has already expanded agentic restaurant reservation booking to AI Mode users in the U.S. without a Labs opt-in.

OwlTing Group announced the upcoming launch of OwlPay Booking Engine for Agent Checkout, a hospitality booking engine designed to support AI agent-initiated bookings and payments, with rollout expected in June 2026. The service integrates booking confirmation, AI agent-authorized payment, and cross-border supplier settlement into a single pathway. OwlTing plans to onboard its existing 2,800-plus hotel clients through its OwlNest PMS at launch. This is the most operationally specific agentic payment move from a PMS vendor this cycle.

Sources: Hotel Dive – IHG ChatGPT, PR Newswire – Wyndham ChatGPT, PhocusWire – Google AI Mode, PhocusWire – June 5 briefs

So what: Hotels without structured, AI-readable content and clean direct-booking APIs are already invisible in the discovery layer that will precede agentic booking. Operators should audit their data quality for LLM compatibility now — before Google's travel booking goes live at scale.

OPERATIONAL AI

The commercial and back-office AI layers are compressing from dashboards into agents that act. Both hotel and restaurant tech vendors shipped this cycle.

Lighthouse launched Ernest, an AI assistant aimed at hotel commercial teams, allowing them to query and act on integrated data across revenue, marketing, sales, and distribution without switching between dashboards. Ernest connects with hundreds of hospitality platforms, tracks every action with built-in approval controls, and learns from team interactions to improve over time.

The launch follows Lighthouse's acquisition of Hotelrank.ai (May 28), adding AI visibility intelligence to its Connect AI offering. Skift's editorial position is candid: "Hotel teams already have too many dashboards. Ernest has to prove it can simplify commercial work, not become one more place to check."

Operto unveiled Operto ONE, a system of seven AI agents for independent hotels, handling tasks across distribution and front and back of house. The distribution agents include a GEO consultant to help hotels with visibility in AI platforms, and a marketing agent that monitors demand patterns and kicks off direct booking campaigns. Back-office agents cover housekeeping, maintenance, and workflow analysis. Every action is checked by a Supervisor agent with configurable thresholds. "No one has the time to constantly analyze how their property appears across generative search, track revenue leakage in real time, respond instantly to every guest question and coordinate tasks across departments all at once," said CEO Tim Major.

PAR Technology and Square unveiled AI products that can assist restaurant operators with their business and complete tasks on their behalf — joining Toast and Thanx, which launched similar tools over the past year. They are part of the growing field of agentic AI, or smart software that can act on its own, sometimes even without being asked.

PAR's agents are directly tied to its POS and marketing products, enabling what the company calls "one source of truth." Taco Bell franchisee Charter Foods began testing one of PAR's agents and asked it to identify stores that could benefit by staying open later — late-night sales increased 20% as a result of its recommendations. "In this industry, speed of analyzing data is real money," said Charter Senior Director John Rankin.

Square's Managerbot is proactive — it surfaces insights and other metrics the restaurant needs to know, and can draft employee schedules, create and run email campaigns, and generate purchase orders for supplies. Square noted that Managerbot, like any AI system, can make mistakes, and urged operators to check its responses for accuracy.

Sources: Skift – Lighthouse Ernest, NRN – PAR + Square agents, PhocusWire – Operto ONE

So what: PAR's franchisee outcome (20% late-night uplift) is the strongest operator-attributed ROI signal in restaurant tech this cycle. For hotels, Ernest's value proposition collapses if it requires the same fragmented data inputs it claims to consolidate — watch whether Lighthouse publishes workflow-reduction metrics at scale.

PEOPLE & SKILLS

The week's sharpest executive positioning on AI came from NYU IHIF (June 3–5) and Mews Unfold Amsterdam (June 1), and the two events pulled in opposite directions.

At NYU IHIF, Accor CEO Sébastien Bazin made the most explicit workforce disruption statement: "Probably a third, if not 40% of all my jobs under corporate, will be replaced by AI technology," warning that corporate workers will not be doing what they do today in 18 to 24 months. Hyatt CEO Mark Hoplamazian offered a counter: "We're looking to try to effectively use AI to amplify the humanity that's in hospitality," with AI focused on administrative tasks so workers can interact more thoughtfully with guests.

At Mews Unfold, EY Global Vice Chair Julie Linn Teigland reframed the debate. The reality on hotel floors today, she argued, is that it's the lack of AI, rather than the presence of it, that's making guest interactions feel transactional — staff are buried in administrative work, from invoice matching to responding to messages across a dozen channels. "Technology should be elevating us, making us better, bringing in the human touch." Mews co-founder Matt Archard made the same point: "We're not very often upfront with customers, and that really is one of the real pain points about why we're not creating magical moments."

At NYU IHIF, AI dominated conference floor conversations, and the discussion has matured well beyond hype. Hotels are deploying AI across revenue management, guest communications, security, and sales initiatives. This creates a new imperative of ensuring properties are visible not just on traditional OTAs, but in the AI-driven discovery environment and social media algorithms that are quickly influencing how people travel.

U.S. hotel performance is beating projections: CoStar and Tourism Economics project ADR gains of 1.0% and RevPAR gains of 2.2%, now expecting occupancy to grow rather than decline, driven by stronger demand from both group and transient segments, with room demand up more than eight million room nights year over year through April.

Sources: Hotel Dive – NYU IHIF CEO panel, Fortune – Mews Unfold, Hospitality Net – NYU IHIF insights

So what: Bazin vs. Hoplamazian is the live debate inside every corporate hotel team right now. Operators sitting between those poles — expecting AI to change roles without knowing the shape — need a workforce transition plan alongside their AI strategy, not after it.

GOVERNANCE & SECURITY

On June 2, the White House issued an Executive Order titled "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security." Hospitality consultant Terence Ronson (Pertlink Limited) published a white paper on June 3 translating the Order into operational priorities for hotels, covering cybersecurity exposure, AI governance, vendor strategy, and data sovereignty. The Order's significance, Ronson argues, is collective: artificial intelligence is now recognized as a national strategic capability, a cybersecurity priority, an economic competitiveness engine, and a critical infrastructure dependency all at once.

The practical implication for hotel operators: the Order creates an AI cybersecurity clearinghouse through the Treasury, extends AI-enabled cybersecurity tools to critical infrastructure operators, and prioritizes criminal enforcement against AI-assisted system access and fraud. Hotels that deploy AI as a cost-cutting measure — stripping out staff and calling it innovation — will likely prove the skeptics right, Teigland argued; that is a failure of leadership, not of the technology.

Ronson's key observation is structural: an improperly governed AI agent with access to hotel APIs could — in principle — unlock guestrooms, alter reservations, manipulate pricing, issue refunds, or expose guest data. The document names four immediate strategic risks operators should explicitly address: AI shadow operations (departments deploying AI tools without governance or visibility); autonomous operational drift (agents exceeding intended boundaries); data contamination (guest data absorbed into vendor model training); and cyber-physical convergence (digital attacks producing physical operational impact). The vendor landscape implication: the Order indirectly disqualifies "AI-overlay" vendors who bolted generative features on for marketing, rewarding governance-mature providers as procurement RFPs start to include AI governance questionnaires and cybersecurity maturity scoring.

Sources: Hospitality Net – White House EO analysis

So what: Cyber insurers are watching this closely. Hotels with opaque vendor AI integrations — particularly those touching PMS, payments, or IoT — should be conducting AI exposure assessments before likely tightening in insurer underwriting requirements.
