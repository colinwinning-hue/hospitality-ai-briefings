Wednesday 14 May 2026 | Lookback: ~7 days (May 7–14)

1. AI VISIBILITY & DISCOVERY

The new distribution race: MCP becomes the plumbing, hotel brands split on tactics

SiteMinder connected its hotel distribution platform — covering 53,000 hotels across 150 countries — to AI booking channels using Model Context Protocol (MCP), expanding both its Demand Plus solution into conversational environments like ChatGPT and Claude, and its Channels Plus product to allow AI-enabled OTA and intermediary platforms to access hotel inventory end-to-end. Both expansions are powered by MCP, which delivers live hotel data to AI systems in real time rather than relying on static information. DirectBooker is the inaugural AI demand partner.

Meanwhile, Wyndham launched what it claimed was the first native ChatGPT hotel app from a major economy and midscale franchisor in the U.S., while Choice Hotels unveiled CHARLIE — an AI-powered internal "teammate" guiding employees through the company's core platforms. IHG is charting a different course: CEO Elie Maalouf said in Q1 2026 earnings it is more important to ready technology and content for AI searches than to chase apps, building a new content platform that highlights hotel features across multiple languages and formats, including video and virtual reality.

A Cornell Center for Hospitality Research study (n=1,029 U.S. travelers, in partnership with Curacity) found that the earliest stages of travel planning — including inspiration, discovery, and comparison — are increasingly occurring within AI-powered tools, with 94% of hotels effectively invisible in AI search results. If AI platforms determine the initial shortlist, distribution no longer starts at checkout but at the recommendation stage — and brands filtered out upstream may never be converted by traditional channels.

Lighthouse's independent hotelier guide offers a useful calibration: 53% of travelers are comfortable letting AI suggest a hotel, but 66% say they would not trust AI to book on their behalf. Expedia Group's own data puts the share of travelers actively relying on AI platforms like ChatGPT or Gemini for trip planning at just 8% — suggesting the gap between inspiration and booking is still large.

Sources: SiteMinder/PhocusWire · PhocusWire hotel brands roundup · Skift/Curacity · Lighthouse

So what: MCP is fast becoming table-stakes infrastructure for hotel distribution. Mid-market operators without a channel manager that supports MCP are building a structural visibility gap into their stack. The IHG vs. Wyndham/Choice split — content-readiness vs. app-first — is the most important strategic fork in hotel distribution right now. For PE-backed SaaS companies, the channel manager's role is expanding from distribution to AI discoverability layer.

2. VENDOR MOVES

Choice Hotels drops the biggest franchise AI suite of the year; Expedia posts record numbers on the back of AI

At its 70th annual convention in Las Vegas (May 7), Choice Hotels International unveiled CHARLIE — an AI-powered virtual "teammate" designed as a 24/7 digital coach for hotel staff — alongside EasyBid (AI-enhanced group RFP tool), RAISE (next-generation rate management), and Choice Hotels Business Direct (a self-service SMB booking platform). CEO Patrick Pacious acknowledged the company has been using AI for over a decade: "It used to be called robotic process automation and all these other things. Today, we're getting the tools in the hands of the franchisees."

Notably, the CTO acknowledged AI's limitations on the convention floor: at the bottom of the CHARLIE screen, a notice reminded users they are "responsible for independently reviewing and verifying all AI-generated content." "You can't turn your hotel over to AI," the CTO said. "The humans are still in control." — That candor is rare and credible.

Choice's Chief Data, AI & Technology Officer said AgentCore and AgentForce "help Choice Hotels move beyond isolated AI use cases to an integrated, enterprise-wide approach," positioning the company to lead in "the emerging world of agentic commerce, where AI agents will increasingly research, compare and book travel on behalf of consumers."

On the OTA side, Expedia Group reported its highest Q1 profitability in company history (May 7), with adjusted EBITDA rising 83% year-over-year to $542 million. AI is impacting multiple layers: the company handles more than 250 million service interactions a year, with over half resolved through self-service and more than 30% powered by AI — a figure the CEO said "keeps increasing." AI also helped manage surging Middle East cancellation volumes, allowing human agents to focus on complex, time-sensitive issues.

Answer engine optimization is now Expedia's "fastest-growing channel." Traffic and bookings from AI-driven channels remain small, but the company is encouraged by the mix of new users, conversion, and average purchase size. Its two-thirds direct channel share may gradually shift as AI intermediaries expand.

Sources: Hotel Management / Choice Hotels · PhocusWire / Expedia Q1 · GeekWire / Expedia AI strategy

So what: Choice Hotels' suite is the clearest example of a major franchisor deploying AI to strengthen unit-level operator performance — directly relevant to mid-market franchisee operators who need tools, not just strategies. The RAISE rate tool (built with owner input) and EasyBid (built for hotels without dedicated sales teams) are the two to track closely. Expedia's numbers also signal that AI-optimized OTAs are extracting margin before hotels even see the guest.

3. DATA & INTEGRATION

OpenAI builds a travel-tested deployment army — AI becoming a long-term platform bet, not a vendor choice

OpenAI launched the OpenAI Deployment Company — a $4 billion venture — acquiring Tomoro, the Edinburgh/London-based firm behind Virgin Atlantic's AI concierge and Supercell's in-game support agent. OpenAI CFO Sarah Friar said Tomoro's "track record deploying AI in complex enterprise environments" will help the company "embed frontier AI engineers directly within organizations around the world." The model — Forward Deployed Engineers sitting inside client companies to redesign workflows — is a direct challenge to incumbent consultancies.

At Supercell, Tomoro launched an in-game support agent serving 110 million users in 12 weeks; it reduced the cost of resolving a support ticket by 90%, raised customer satisfaction scores by 20%, and delivers an average response time of seven seconds. Those are the benchmarks hospitality should be demanding.

Hospitality.today (May 12) drew the direct implication for hotels: OpenAI's acquisition of Tomoro gives the initiative an immediate travel-industry proof point. The development signals that AI adoption is increasingly becoming a long-term platform and ecosystem decision, not a matter of selecting isolated software tools. Long-term platform dependency is becoming a strategic issue, as hospitality companies adopting deeply integrated AI systems may become increasingly tied to the roadmap, pricing, and ecosystem priorities of a specific AI provider.

Days before OpenAI's announcement, Anthropic revealed its own enterprise deployment venture — a $1.5 billion entity backed by Blackstone, Hellman & Friedman, and Goldman Sachs — with essentially the same premise: embed engineers inside companies, redesign workflows around AI agents, and build durable systems that don't evaporate after the pilot phase.

Supporting the integration urgency: Amadeus' Travel Dreams 2026 report (500 hoteliers, GM level or above) found that 499 of 500 intended to invest in AI capabilities, with average planned spend of $319,000 per property in 2026 — one in five planning to spend more than $500,000. Hoteliers said AI was useful for planning (42%), inspiration (36%), booking (32%) and during travel (35%).

Sources: Skift/OpenAI-Tomoro · The Next Web · Hospitality.today · PhocusWire/Amadeus

So what: The OpenAI Deployment Company is the most important structural move in enterprise AI this week — and its travel proof point (Virgin Atlantic) puts hospitality in the crosshairs. PE-backed hospitality SaaS companies should model what this means for their position: if OpenAI can embed engineers inside your customers and redesign the workflows your software supports, the competitive threat is real. For operators, choosing between OpenAI, Google, Anthropic, and Microsoft ecosystems is becoming an infrastructure decision with decade-long consequences.

4. OPERATIONAL AI

Restaurant brands move from pilot to production; Wonder bets on AI-generated restaurant concepts

Marc Lore, founder of Wonder (currently 120 "programmable cooking platform" locations), announced Wonder Create — an initiative that would let anyone use AI to design and launch their own restaurant brand in under a minute. "You type in what kind of restaurant you want to build. It builds the restaurant — AI does — in under a minute. It does the name, branding, description, pictures, pricing, health information, and all the recipes for your restaurant," Lore said at the WSJ Future of Everything event.

Loman AI CEO Christian Wiens said voice AI crosses a critical threshold in 2026, moving from experimental technology to essential infrastructure. "The testing phase is over. Restaurants now demand platforms that integrate seamlessly with their existing systems and deliver utility-grade reliability." In the data: pizza and high-volume takeout categories that deployed first are now 12–18 months ahead, seeing 26%+ phone revenue increases.

The National Restaurant Association's 2026 report found 26% of operators are already using AI, with many more planning to follow.

Steven Fine, GM of the restaurant division at PolyAI, put it bluntly: "As we segue into 2026, we're going to see more of what I call 'AI adoption hitting escape velocity.' This means pilot projects turning into system-wide rollouts."

On the hotel side, VIVI's operator experience data shows AI logging guest interactions that reveal previously invisible operational patterns: which hours guest questions spike (often late evening, outside traditional service windows), and which maintenance issues are quietly generating guest complaints across multiple rooms before they ever surface in formal reviews — turning guest interaction data into a capital planning input.

Sources: TechCrunch/Wonder · QSR Web · Hotel Technology News/VIVI

So what: Voice AI in restaurants is showing its first clean ROI numbers (26%+ phone revenue increase for early QSR deployers). That's a commercial case for AI readiness advisory. Wonder Create is a bold bet on AI-native restaurant brands — if it works at scale, it challenges ghost kitchen economics and franchise brand models simultaneously.

5. PEOPLE & SKILLS

The "up-leveling" thesis vs. the restructuring reality

PhocusWire/Cloudbeds VP Sebastien Leitner argues that many hotels will never return to pre-pandemic staffing levels, making it critical to "up-level" existing staff. Modern AI training platforms are distinct because they understand intent — what a staff member is actually trying to accomplish — adapt content in real time based on role and experience level, and learn which interventions reduce errors, escalations, or call volume. The result is continuous learning environments that deliver just-in-time training when each employee needs it.

Mews' 2026 Hospitality Outlook found expert consensus that transactional processes — check-in, payments, routine questions — are likely to be increasingly automated, and that leading hotels will redesign roles around soft skills, empathy, and brand storytelling, not admin.

Expedia has cut new customer service agent onboarding time by about 60% through AI tools. The company has also cut hundreds of engineering, product, and technology jobs over the past two years, with outgoing CFO noting AI compute costs will rise in H2 2026 but are being funded through cuts elsewhere.

At FIU's TIED @ the Bay event (May 11), hospitality professor Dale Gomez announced plans to restructure his class so that "close to half will be based off AI because that's where the future is."

Sources: PhocusWire/Cloudbeds · Mews Outlook · GeekWire/Expedia · FIU News

So what: For mid-market operators, "up-leveling" is the narrative but restructuring is the reality at scale. The question is whether you get ahead of role redesign or let attrition and headcount cuts outpace capability building. For SaaS companies, embedded training modules may be a more defensible product surface than standalone features.

6. GOVERNANCE & SECURITY

Bot traffic corrupts analytics; ransomware targets PMS; Choice Hotels builds governance into launch

PhocusWire flagged a structural data problem: human-driven web traffic is declining across hospitality, while automated traffic from AI agents, OTA bots, and scrapers now represents a growing share of what platforms like Google Analytics record. The result: analytics aren't measuring what they used to. When bot activity is misclassified as genuine interest, hotels may overestimate demand, misjudge marketing performance, or make strategic decisions based on patterns that don't reflect real guest behavior.

Hotel cybersecurity predictions for 2026: malicious actors are using AI to craft sophisticated phishing messages that closely mimic internal communication styles. Ransomware groups are moving away from encryption toward disrupting operations — targeting property management systems, door locks, HVAC controls, and booking integrations. The aim is to halt operations with minimal effort, forcing a payout. Average cost of a hotel data breach was $3.86 million in 2024.

PhocusWire's AI crawlability debate adds another dimension: opening up to AI crawlers can boost visibility and potentially direct bookings, but questions remain over security and compliance risks. A robots.txt file may look simple, but operational security decisions are complex — and even ChatGPT can now reportedly pass "I am not a robot" verification, while Amazon has sued Perplexity over its agentic shopping tool allegedly disguising automated activity as human browsing.

Choice Hotels is rare among hotel brands in publicly baking governance into its launch: the CHARLIE interface explicitly reminds staff they are "responsible for independently reviewing and verifying all AI-generated content." AgentCore is positioned as a "secure, reusable foundation for intelligent agents" enabling governance at enterprise scale.

Sources: PhocusWire/Cloudbeds · Hotel Online/Nomadix · PhocusWire crawlability · Hotel Management/Choice

So what: The analytics corruption problem (bot traffic inflating demand signals) is an underappreciated operational risk right now — especially for revenue managers relying on channel performance data. Any AI readiness advisory should include an analytics audit as a first step. Ransomware-as-a-service targeting PMS infrastructure is no longer a theoretical risk.

7. FUNDING & M&A

OpenAI's $4B deployment arm is the week's defining move; Anthropic mirrors it

OpenAI launched the OpenAI Deployment Company as a committed partnership between OpenAI and 19 leading global investment firms, consultancies, and system integrators. The partnership is led by TPG, with Advent, Bain Capital, and Brookfield as co-lead founding partners. McKinsey, Bain & Company, and Capgemini also invested — making this simultaneously a consulting industry disruption event and an enterprise AI infrastructure play.

Days earlier, Anthropic revealed a $1.5 billion enterprise deployment venture backed by Blackstone, Hellman & Friedman, and Goldman Sachs — with the same embedded-engineer premise.

Traditional consulting firms' stock reacted immediately: Accenture fell 3%, Cognizant dropped 5%, Infosys declined 4%. UBS maintained its Accenture buy rating, arguing scale advantages in legacy infrastructure and regulated environments make the firms more complementary than competitive in the short term.

Wyndham's technology infrastructure context: since 2018, the company has invested more than $450 million in technology modernization; in 2020, it became the first major hotel company to fully migrate its systems to the cloud — creating the flexible infrastructure now enabling faster AI integrations.

Sources: OpenAI · The Next Web · Skift/OpenAI-Tomoro

So what: The OpenAI Deployment Company's $14B valuation and travel-sector beachhead (Virgin Atlantic) signals that hospitality will be an early target vertical for embedded AI deployment. PE-backed SaaS companies competing on implementation services should model this threat explicitly. For operators evaluating system integrators, the question of which AI ecosystem your integrator is aligned to is no longer secondary.
