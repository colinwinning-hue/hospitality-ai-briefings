CLUSTER 1 — AI Visibility & Discovery: MCP Becomes the New Distribution Rail

The most active story cluster of the week. A clear industry consensus is forming: Model Context Protocol (MCP) is the infrastructure layer hotels must sit on to remain discoverable as travel search migrates from Google to conversational AI platforms.

SiteMinder is connecting its hotel distribution platform to AI booking channels using Model Context Protocol (MCP), updating the platform to support AI-driven bookings via both supplier-direct and intermediary channels. Concretely, SiteMinder is extending Demand Plus — its demand generation solution across Google, Trivago and TripAdvisor — beyond traditional metasearch into AI-driven conversational environments including platforms such as ChatGPT and Claude, with DirectBooker as its inaugural AI demand partner. Separately, Lighthouse announced the launch of The Hotels Network app, the first direct booking app for hotels available inside ChatGPT, powered by its Connect AI engine built on the MCP standard — creating a live data bridge between hotels and AI platforms so that when a traveller asks ChatGPT for hotel recommendations, the response draws on accurate, real-time information directly from the property, not scraped summaries. The app is on a flat subscription, zero-commission model. Meanwhile, Aven Hospitality, the former Sabre hotel technology unit now owned by TPG, is integrating MCP into its SynXis central reservation system, enabling AI agents to interact directly with hotel inventory — with a planned early access program for select hotel chains in 2026.

On the demand side, IHG Hotels & Resorts is adding a natural-language search tool to its website and app, allowing guests to describe in their own words where they want to go and the hotel features that matter to them, across IHG's more than 7,000 hotels. CEO Elie Maalouf confirmed IHG is also working with Google, OpenAI, and Anthropic. In restaurants, Google is redefining the role of search by introducing "agentic capabilities" within AI Mode, beginning with restaurant reservations in the UK.

This raises important questions for platforms such as OpenTable, Resy, and TheFork — if Google's AI can manage the booking process within search, it could reduce the need for users to engage directly with those platforms.

According to Phocuswright research, 56% of travel companies have implemented standards such as MCP and Agent2Agent (A2A) protocols or are exploring doing so in the future.

Key sources: PhocusWire – SiteMinder MCP | PhocusWire – Lighthouse ChatGPT | Skift – Aven/SynXis MCP

So what: MCP is no longer experimental — it's the new distribution plumbing. A mid-market independent hotel that hasn't structured its data for machine-readability is already invisible to a portion of AI-led searches. For PE-backed hospitality SaaS: MCP integration is table stakes for your clients' renewal conversations. If you're not enabling this pathway, a competitor will.

CLUSTER 2 — Operational AI: Specific Outcomes Start to Surface

The signal-to-noise ratio improved this week as concrete case studies replaced generic promises.

AI sales platform Hivr, co-developed with Radisson Hotel Group, launched an agentic AI tool that automates one of the most tedious workflows in hotels: processing rooming lists for groups and meetings. The tool ingests guest data in any format and structures it directly into the hotel's PMS — Hivr estimates it saves roughly 50 minutes of admin time per 100-person booking, with greater savings as changes accumulate.

The tool is already deployed across Radisson's European portfolio.

MSC Cruises unveiled MSC Concierge on 7 May 2026 — a conversational AI assistant integrated into the MSC for Me app, available 24/7 in more than 90 languages, helping guests book spa treatments, restaurant reservations, shore excursions and check account balances.

The launch follows a pilot with more than 170,000 guests who exchanged more than 1 million messages with the tool, generating a 93% service satisfaction score. This is a meaningful signal — it replaces MSC's failed Zoe voice assistant, and the 93% satisfaction from a live population of 170k+ gives this genuine operator credibility.

On the performance side, BCG data shows hotels are seeing revenue per available room gains up to 15% after implementing AI-powered pricing systems that adjust prices in real time based on demand signals such as booking pace, competitor rates, and local events. The Ritz-Carlton San Francisco reported a 20% increase in room-cleaning speed via an AI system that optimizes housekeeping schedules.

Key sources: Skift Meetings – Hivr/Radisson | PhocusWire – MSC Concierge | PhocusWire – BCG Pricing Gains

So what: Three operator-backed proof points in one cycle — rooming lists, cruise concierge, revenue management — all with specific numbers attached. This is the commercial case content you need. Use these as benchmarks when scoping AI readiness assessments: do you have the PMS integration to deploy what Radisson just did?

CLUSTER 3 — Data & Integration: Clean Data as the Core Bottleneck

Minor Hotels is developing a global AI and data platform from the ground up to connect guest data, enabling improved personalization, marketing, and service across its portfolio of over 640 hotels and 12 brands. Unlike competitors layering AI onto legacy systems, Minor's clean-slate approach allows for faster iteration and deeper real-time guest understanding. The initiative, supported by Google Cloud, Salesforce, and OneTrust, aims for full deployment by the end of 2026.

"Many groups are layering AI onto systems that weren't built for real-time data, which limits both speed and impact," said Ian Di Tullio, Minor Hotels' CCO.

BCG is direct about the structural barrier: many hotel companies operate with fragmented technology systems, including separate platforms for property management, point-of-sale transactions, CRM, and loyalty programs. Hotels will need to integrate these systems and build a centralized data platform that creates a single source of truth. The Skift PE angle adds urgency: Vivek Bhogaraju, Executive In Residence at PE firms, argues: "Hotels have been held back for years by fragmented tech stacks, information silos, and subscale solution providers that kept digital transformation at scale out of reach. AI agents are the perfect antidote, stitching together disparate datasets to unlock revenue and boost profitability that were operationally impossible before."

In the restaurant sector, operators are pulling information from every direction — POS, invoices, payroll, reservations — but most of those systems speak completely different languages. Over the next year, data management will move from being a behind-the-scenes IT issue to a core operational priority. Larger groups may start appointing a data lead or chief data officer.

Key sources: Skift – Minor Hotels | PhocusWire – BCG Report | Skift – Agentic AI PE Angle

So what: Every operator engaging with AI is hitting the same wall — dirty, siloed data. This is the highest-value entry point for an AI readiness advisory. The Minor Hotels/Google Cloud model is the premium version; your mid-market client needs a lighter-weight equivalent before any agentic deployment is viable.

CLUSTER 4 — Funding & M&A: European Hospitality SaaS Heats Up

Smartness, a Trentino-based vertical SaaS company, closed a €47 million Series B — the largest round ever completed for an Italian vertical SaaS company. The round was led by United Ventures and CDP Venture Capital, with participation from Partech. The funding will support international expansion, product development, and acquisitions.

Alongside geographic expansion, Smartness reportedly delivers around 10% month-over-month organic growth and has grown more than 6x since its Series A. Its long-term vision is to build the world's first Agentic System for hotels and vacation rentals.

This sits within a broader 2026 pattern: Mews raised €255 million in Series D to further invest in AI and agent-driven workflows; Vuelo secured €64 million Seed for AI-native travel booking.

On leadership moves: Key Data CEO Jason Sprenkle will retire at the end of May, with president Dustin Downing set to take over as CEO in a planned transition. Duetto has promoted Michael Schäffner to chief sales officer from SVP of global sales, to lead global commercial operations.

Key sources: PhocusWire – Smartness Series B | BeBeez – European Funding Context

So what: European independent hotel SaaS is seeing serious capital inflows. Smartness targeting DACH and UK markets post-round creates competitive pressure on incumbents. For PE-backed hospitality SaaS: the M&A consolidation thesis is playing out — Smartness is already executing it. Watch for their UK moves in H2.

CLUSTER 5 — Vendor Moves: MCP Race and Restaurant Platform Bets

Oracle and NetSuite announced Oracle NetSuite Restaurant Operations, a unified platform designed to help restaurants increase operational efficiency by combining inventory, procurement, scheduling, production, and cash management data in a single AI-enhanced system with real-time visibility across locations.

Wonder founder Marc Lore announced Wonder Create, an AI initiative that would let anyone — from food entrepreneurs to social media influencers — use AI to design and launch their own restaurant brand in under a minute, described as "a Shopify front end with an AI prompt." The AI creates the name, branding, description, pictures, pricing, health information, and recipes. Wonder's network currently has 120 programmable kitchen locations expected to reach 400 next year.

On the agentic front: Wyndham's agentic AI deployments include allowing customers to book a room directly through an agent. AI also assists employees with the booking process, sharing proactive information like if a caller is a loyalty member or complained about noise during their last stay and should be offered a quieter room.

Key sources: Oracle Press Release | TechCrunch – Wonder Create | Fortune – Wyndham Agentic AI

So what: Oracle's restaurant back-office move is significant at the enterprise level — it closes the gap between operational and financial data in a single AI-enhanced platform. Wonder Create is a wilder bet; interesting as a stress test of whether AI-generated restaurant brands can maintain quality at scale. Wyndham's employee-assist model (loyalty context, noise complaints) is the most transferable playbook for mid-market operators today.

CLUSTER 6 — People & Skills: The Change Management Gap Is the Real Risk

The anxiety heard from hoteliers isn't "will AI replace my job?" — it's more subtle: "I don't fully understand what this system is doing, and I'm not sure when I should trust it." That's not a technology problem. That's a change management problem.

BCG puts a number on it: just 2.9% of full-time travel and tourism employees are skilled in AI, compared to 21% in technology and media. The Canary Technologies survey found that the top challenges hoteliers face around AI adoption are data and privacy issues, integration barriers, limited training bandwidth, and a lack of technical expertise.

Many hotels will never return to pre-pandemic staffing levels, making it critical to "up-level" existing staff by helping them learn faster, adapt to new roles, and deliver better guest experiences with fewer people. Modern training systems differ from traditional knowledge bases — they understand intent, adapt content in real time based on role and experience level, and learn which interventions reduce errors.

Key sources: PhocusWire – AI Skills Gap Opinion | PhocusWire/BCG – AI-First Hotels

So what: The skills gap is now quantified and mainstream. For advisory firms: workforce readiness assessment is a billable workstream alongside tech stack audits. This is especially acute at the GM and ops manager layer — these are the people who will either enable or kill an agentic deployment.

CLUSTER 7 — Governance & Security: The Quiet Constraint

As human-driven web traffic declines, automated traffic from AI agents, OTA bots, and scrapers represents a growing share of what platforms like Google Analytics record. Signal integrity is collapsing. When bot activity is misclassified as genuine interest, hotels may overestimate demand, misjudge marketing performance, or make strategic decisions based on patterns that don't reflect real human intent.

With data privacy presenting a major hurdle, hoteliers should consider finding solutions that seamlessly share data, rely on trusted sources, and meet global data protection and privacy standards.

In 2026, agentic AI in hospitality won't be judged by how fluent or "smart" it sounds but by how safely it can influence decisions across the business.

Key source: PhocusWire – 6 AI Trends 2026

So what: Analytics poisoning from bot traffic is an underreported risk. If you're helping a client build an AI readiness case, their baseline data may already be compromised. Audit first. The governance conversation is also a natural entry point when data privacy (43% of hoteliers cite it as a barrier) is the most common blocker to AI adoption.
