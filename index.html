HOSPITALITY AI BRIEFING — 24 August 2026

AI Visibility & Discovery

The agentic booking era moved from roadmap to live product this cycle, with Google, OpenTable, and DoorDash each executing a step that compresses the discovery-to-transaction funnel into a single interface.

Google confirmed on August 7 that it has launched a limited U.S. test of agentic hotel booking inside Search's AI Mode. Booking Holdings CEO Glenn Fogel referenced the tests on an earnings call, saying "We are one of the first with them," with Expedia, Marriott, Wyndham, and IHG also collaborating.

The feature, called Ask Maps, now returns real prices and availability rather than ranges or estimates, and is designed to handle complex queries — finding a hotel with a particular atmosphere, within walking distance of a gym and restaurants, for a conference in a given city center. Amadeus is the foundational B2B technical partner for lodging, with Sabre advising on the airline side.

Google's new Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP) could allow bookings to be completed directly inside a chat session , though Google insists it does not intend to become an OTA. Critically, the Booking Holdings CFO separately disclosed that bookings coming from conversational agents, free or paid, account for less than 1% of the group's room-nights, with no significant recent change in that share — the traffic erosion is real and documented, the booking shift remains a hypothesis for now.

Booking Holdings disclosed in its Q2 earnings that Google's AI Overviews are pressuring Booking's organic search results. CEO Glenn Fogel said "I do believe that some of the changes that were made in the display at Google definitely put some pressure on SEO," though direct traffic in the mid-60% range remains stable.

Booking is accelerating its AI strategy across customer service, AI discovery, and LLM visibility, while raising its transformation-program savings target to $650 million in annual run-rate savings by 2027.

The competitive context includes Expedia, which acquired Layla in 2026 — a planning app built on conversational AI — to capture travelers at the research phase.

OpenTable upgraded its AI Concierge from individual restaurant pages to the platform's full homepage. The platform simultaneously upgraded its AI Concierge dining assistant to the homepage, where it searches 65,000-plus restaurants using Perplexity and OpenAI. OpenTable has publicly identified Perplexity as the real-time web grounding layer and OpenAI as the underlying LLM inference engine, with the live-availability grounding being the architecturally significant part: the system cannot tell you that a restaurant is open when the reservation data shows it is full.

OpenTable's simultaneously published Top 100 Hotel Restaurants list (drawn from more than 10 million verified diner reviews) found 61% of Americans have chosen a travel destination specifically because of its restaurant scene, and 60% of respondents planning summer travel say they booked a hotel because of its on-site restaurant. The concurrent push into discovery-to-booking makes the list editorial content, acquisition tool, and data capture in a single move.

Sources: Skift — Google Agentic Hotel Booking, Hospitality ON — Google US Test, TechTimes — OpenTable AI Concierge

So what: Any mid-market operator not yet structured for machine-readable discovery — clean rate rules, consistent room/restaurant attributes across channels, live availability APIs — is already invisible to a meaningful slice of high-intent demand. This is not a future problem.

Vendor Moves

The reservation and ordering platform landscape has been reshaped by a chain of acquisitions that now means picking a reservations system is picking whose ecosystem you join.

DoorDash launched Ask DoorDash on June 11. The chatbot, called "Ask DoorDash," allows users to search the app for what they're looking for in their own words instead of having to scroll through restaurants and stores to build a cart. Users can ask for a "filling dinner for a family of 4" and the app will surface restaurants with a personalized blurb; for reservations, a query like "table for two downtown for a date-night dinner around 8 PM" will surface restaurants with availability.

Early user tests point to tangible business gains: nearly half of all takeout orders made with the tool were from a place the customer had never ordered from before, grocery baskets are more than 35% larger than typical orders, and customers build their cart five times faster.

The chatbot uses a combination of AI models from OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google, as well as open-source models to help reduce costs.

DoorDash intends to license the technology to outside partners — grocers, restaurants, and retailers — who would deploy it under their own branding. Meanwhile, U.S. House committees have launched a joint investigation: DoorDash disclosed it has been deploying Chinese AI model Kimi K2.6 for some workloads, with its AI research lab finding Kimi K2.6 vastly outperforms other Anthropic models at cheaper cost. Lawmakers requested information and documents relating to DoorDash's evaluation and deployment of AI systems from China. American Express announced a $700 million cash deal to acquire TheFork from TripAdvisor (announced June 15, 2026). The deal would give Amex a reservations trifecta with Resy and Tock, which are set to merge this summer. Together, the booking services would encompass 75,000 venues.

TheFork connects millions of diners with more than 50,000 restaurants across 11 European countries. Simultaneously, Resy and Tock are merging under the Resy brand this summer, doubling its venue library to over 25,000 restaurants. Resy CEO Pablo Rivero has framed the combined stack as "one that links AI-powered discovery and booking, table and guestbook management, content and curation, ticketing and transactions, check-level point of sale data, and American Express membership."

Toast and American Express also announced a strategic partnership (announced August 2025, rolling out in 2026). The companies plan to build tools combining Resy and Tock's guestbook capabilities and Toast's Digital Chits technology, which allows restaurant staff to view important customer information directly on Toast Go handhelds and POS terminals during service. The partnership also intends to give restaurants greater visibility by making their listings from Resy and Tock available on the Local by Toast app.

Alex Drummond, EVP and GM of American Express Membership Portfolio Services, noted that Amex cardholders spent more than $87 billion on dining in the United States in 2024.

Toast also co-developed the Universal Commerce Protocol for Food with Google. Toast expanded its partnership with Google to power agentic food ordering through Ask Maps, connecting AI agents directly to restaurant ordering systems that run on Toast, allowing users to place orders from within Google Maps. Toast is a co-developer of an open standard designed to govern how AI agents interact with restaurant commerce online.

Delivery Hero launched an agentic AI assistant for local shops and restaurants via WhatsApp. The AI assistant currently supports over 40,000 restaurants across Delivery Hero's global brands, an early slice of the approximately 1.5 million partners on the company's platform.

Shop and restaurant owners interact through chats on WhatsApp using text, voice notes, and images, and if the owner allows, the assistant can also make suggestions independently. "What makes it different is that it doesn't just advise. It acts on behalf of business owners, the moment they approve," said CPO Johannes Bruder. So what: The reservation market is now three ecosystems: Booking Holdings (OpenTable), American Express (Resy + Tock + TheFork), DoorDash (SevenRooms + Deliveroo). Mid-market operators choosing a reservations platform are choosing which of these data relationships to hand their guest history to. Commission-free direct booking and data ownership arguments sharpen considerably in this context.

Operational AI

Operators are getting tangible early signal from AI marketing and scheduling tools, with consumer sentiment shifting faster than most predicted.

PAR Technology released its "2026 Industry Report: AI on the Diner's Terms" (August 18). In 2025, 41% of surveyed consumers said they'd choose a restaurant that avoided AI altogether. One year later, average discomfort with restaurant AI has fallen to 27%. This means nearly 3 in 4 diners are now open to AI playing some role in their experience.

Nearly half of surveyed consumers (48%) are comfortable with AI making operational decisions like adjusting labor based on demand or optimizing menus based on inventory. Comfort rises to 51% when AI is used to improve service consistency and shorten wait times. A year ago, only 33% of consumers trusted AI to suggest personalized offers based on their dining history, but that number has risen to an average of 45%. On LLM ordering: 62% of consumers surveyed would order through an LLM given the right offer, with Millennials (73%) and Gen Z (70%) leading. CEO Savneet Singh said: "The operators who move now to build that trust will own the guest relationship. The ones who wait will be handing it to someone else."

Toast reported Q2 2026 earnings on August 5. Toast's first AI-enabled workflow product — a digital marketing tool called Toast IQ Grow — is poised to become the company's fastest-growing product, with executives expecting it to generate $10 million in annual revenue since its May introduction.

The company illustrated the strategy with a Louisiana restaurant, Spirits Food & Friends, that reduced its outside marketing costs by 70% while generating more than $100,000 in marketing-attributed sales in less than two months after consolidating onto Toast and adopting Toast IQ Grow. Toast said its access to customer transaction data gives it an advantage because it can directly connect marketing campaigns with completed orders.

Looking further out, CEO Aman Narang said Toast will deploy "agentic products" for demand forecasting, labor schedules, income tax help, food costs, and availability: "That's an incredibly exciting future, especially in an industry known for slim margins and long hours."

BCG published research showing Ritz-Carlton San Francisco implemented an AI system that synchronizes room-cleaning schedules with check-out patterns, guest preferences, and staff availability — speeding up the time it takes to clean and prepare guest rooms by 20%. IHG followed suit with predictive housekeeping models that anticipate peak cleaning times and allocate resources accordingly.

Nordic chain Scandic is rolling out Quinyx (an AI workforce management tool) to forecast demand and align schedules and labor costs with occupancy.

IHG Hotels & Resorts was also noted by Hotel Technology News as having launched conversational AI search across its website and mobile app — a recent product launch noted in that outlet's news ticker but without a full article to extract further detail.

Sources: QSR Magazine — PAR Consumer Survey, Restaurant Dive — Toast AI, BCG — AI-First Hotels

So what: The PAR data is the strongest consumer signal yet that the AI-averse diner is a shrinking minority. The Toast IQ Grow early case study — $100K+ in attributed sales with 70% reduction in outsourced marketing costs — is the kind of PnL line that moves mid-market operator decisions. But one case study is one case study; the platform is still pre-GA for most users.

Funding & M&A

A quieter funding quarter, but acquisition activity is running ahead as platforms absorb capabilities.

Across Q2 2026 (April to June), three disclosed funding rounds closed in the hospitality tech sector, all of them AI-native by description. Smartness closed the largest, a €47 million Series B for its agentic AI platform for hotels and short-term rentals. Pricepoint raised a $6.6 million seed for AI revenue management. Besty AI raised a $3.75 million seed for its AI automation tool on the short-term rental side.

Smartness is directionally significant: it started in 2020 as Smartpricing, a dynamic pricing tool for hotels, and has since expanded into a broad platform spanning pricing, guest communications, CRM, payments, and property management. Its €47 million Series B is set partly toward acquisitions across Europe. A former specialist is now funded to consolidate.

On the acquisition side, Lighthouse acquired Hotelrank.ai, an AI visibility measurement platform. Ixigo took a majority stake in hotel booking platform Brevistay. RealTime Reservation, an ancillary booking platform, acquired guest experience platform STAY.

For context: between April 2025 and March 2026, hospitality technology startups raised more than $1 billion in funding across 40 companies, with PMS companies leading at a combined $408.1 million raised across seven players, while AI-driven guest experience platforms (digital check-in, automated messaging, and upsell tools) brought in $152.6 million.

Jessica Gillingham, CEO of Abode Worldwide, said: "The companies attracting the strongest investor backing share a common trait. They sit close to essential operator workflows and become more valuable over time."

Sources: Abode Worldwide — Q2 2026 Funding Roundup, Hotel Dive — $1B Funding Report

So what: The "frankenstack" consolidation thesis is playing out in real time. Smartness's trajectory — from single-workflow specialist to platform-funded-to-acquire — is the playbook. PE-backed SaaS companies in hospitality should be asking which specialists are now funded to eat into their adjacencies.

Governance & Security

Two serious incidents from earlier in 2026 are still driving industry conversation, and a new regulatory layer took effect August 2 in Europe.

Chekin and Gastrodat (March 2026 breach): It took 527 stolen credentials to leak the data of over 5 million guests. CyberNews discovered that an attacker compromised credentials of accounts on Chekin and Gastrodat, hospitality platforms based out of Spain and Austria, and obtained plain-text copies of usernames, passwords, and tokens. The attacker extracted guest names, booking details, and even ID documents, streamed directly into their Telegram by a simple set of Python scripts.

Between January 2025 and February 2026, at least 20 separate security incidents have occurred across AI-powered applications. Nearly all had preventable causes like mishandled credentials or lax authentication. AI agents running Computer Use interfaces take screenshots of PMS pages (often with guest credit card data and PII visible), process them, and use that information to take next actions — with no guarantee of where that data ends up. The author's conclusion: "When a vendor offers instant, no-setup integration into all of your systems, that speed comes at the cost of security. Ask your AI vendors where your data goes, how their audit trail works, and who controls your information. Five million guests had their data leaked. The question is: are AI tools solving problems for my property, or are they just automating risk?"

EU AI Act Article 50 transparency obligations came into force August 2, 2026. The European Commission states that Article 50 applies from August 2, 2026 and includes transparency obligations for certain interactive and generative AI systems. Restaurants should not assume that a friendly bot name is enough; the disclosure should be clear in the context where the guest encounters the system.

Employment AI deserves a separate approval path. New York City already requires a bias audit and notices for covered automated employment decision tools.

DoorDash's Chinese AI exposure adds a new governance dimension. US lawmakers have formally requested information from DoorDash on its use of Chinese AI model Kimi K2.6, citing a post by DoorDash co-founder Andy Fang that details how the company is delegating lower-level AI work to Kimi K2.6, which is developed by Moonshot AI. This is the first documented instance of a major hospitality-adjacent company facing congressional scrutiny for open-weight model usage. [CNBC July 31 article — PAYWALLED/BLOCKED; snippet-sourced only.]

Sources: Hotel Tech News — AI Security, allainews.net — EU AI Act for Restaurants

So what: Hospitality operators handing PMS credentials to AI agents without auditable data trails are materially exposed. The Chekin/Gastrodat breach is a template for exactly the attack that poorly guardrailed agentic systems invite. The EU AI Act's August 2 effective date means any European-market chatbot or voice agent without clear AI disclosure is now in technical breach.

People & Skills

Only 5% of companies offer AI training at scale and BCG research shows just 26% have developed the capabilities to move beyond proofs of concept and generate tangible value. HBR cites approximately 80% failure estimates for AI projects, and MIT's 2025 report found approximately 95% of enterprise generative AI efforts showed no measurable P&L impact. Gartner projects that 40% of agentic AI projects started in 2024–2025 will be scrapped by 2027.

Front desk staff and reservation agents typically spend 40 to 60 percent of their time on repetitive enquiries — work that AI chatbots now handle automatically. That statistic alone should reshape what a front office module teaches. If AI is absorbing routine tasks, hospitality graduates need training in supervising those tools, interpreting the data they generate, and stepping in for complex guest situations that still require a human touch.

So what: The skills gap is compound — operators can't implement AI well because they can't evaluate vendors honestly, and they can't evaluate vendors honestly because their teams don't understand what AI is actually doing. Training investment is a prerequisite for ROI, not a nice-to-have.

WHO TO WATCH

Aman Narang (Toast CEO) — His Q2 call is the clearest articulation in the restaurant tech sector of AI moving from feature to agentic product platform. The IQ Grow trajectory — and the explicit roadmap toward agentic demand forecasting, labor scheduling, and food costing — positions Toast not as a POS with AI bolted on but as an operating system with AI at the core. Whether the Spirits Food & Friends numbers hold at scale across 180,000 locations is the question that determines whether Toast's valuation growth continues.

Glenn Fogel (Booking Holdings CEO) — His dual posture this earnings cycle — acknowledging Google AI Overviews are squeezing organic search while simultaneously confirming Booking is among Google's first agentic booking partners — is the sharpest example of a major OTA threading the needle between threat and adaptation. His framing that "no travel business has found the silver bullet yet in how to show up prominently on a consistent basis within major LLMs" is the most honest public acknowledgment from an OTA leader of the LLM discovery problem.

Pablo Rivero (CEO, Resy and Tock) — Managing the merger of two competing platform philosophies (Resy's subscription discovery model vs. Tock's prepaid ticketing model) while simultaneously building toward TheFork's European absorption and the Toast partnership integration. The vision he articulated — "AI-powered discovery and booking, table and guestbook management, content and curation, ticketing and transactions, check-level POS data, and American Express membership" — is ambitious enough to matter if executed; watch whether the combined data layer creates a genuine personalization moat.

Savneet Singh (PAR Technology CEO) — The PAR consumer survey he commissioned is the most methodologically credible sentiment shift documented in the restaurant AI space this cycle. His framing — "LLMs will become the new ordering interface" and "the field is pre-seeded" — is a more direct commercial bet than most vendor communications. Watch whether PAR's Intelligence platform can translate the survey's directional signal into platform-level ordering integrations.

Daniel Hersey (Caleta CEO) — His guest column on the Chekin/Gastrodat breach is the most operationally grounded security warning published in hospitality tech this cycle. The specific mechanism (CUA agents handling PMS login credentials, taking screenshots of guest PII during processing) describes exactly the attack surface that most AI agent vendors are not discussing in their marketing. His audit-trail framing deserves broader adoption as a vendor evaluation standard.
